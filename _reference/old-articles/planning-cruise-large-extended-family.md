A cruise for 30 or more relatives is less about the ship and more about running a small project. Start 12–18 months out, share the work among a few people, pick a port the family can reach easily, and book everyone into one group block. Then plan only a handful of whole-family moments.

## Why 30+ is different from a 12-person reunion
At a dozen people, one organised aunt can run everything from her phone. At 30 or more, you are juggling 12–18 cabins, several departure cities, toddlers and great-grandparents, accessible cabins and a payment schedule nobody can afford to miss. For smaller groups, [family reunion cruise planning for 10–30](/articles/family-reunion-cruise-planning/) is the simpler version.

## Share the work: a small planning committee

| Role | What they do |
|---|---|
| Lead organiser | The single contact with the line or agency; owns the group block |
| Money keeper | Tracks deposits and deadlines; collects shared-event money by e-transfer |
| Branch captains | One per household branch; passes on updates and follows up with their own people |
| Events lead | Organises welcome drinks, the family dinner, the group photo and one shared excursion |
| Care lead | Collects accessibility needs, dietary requests and children's document checks |

Branch captains are the key. Thirty people will not read every message, but they will answer a sibling or a cousin.

## Choosing a sailing that works from across Canada
### Western Canada
If most of the family lives in BC and Alberta, an Alaska sailing from Vancouver between May and September is hard to beat. BC relatives can drive or take transit, and Calgary or Edmonton is roughly a 90-minute flight. [Calgary to Alaska cruise costs](/articles/calgary-alaska-cruise-cost/) breaks down the budget.

### Ontario, Quebec and the East
Florida ports such as Fort Lauderdale, Miami and Port Canaveral are around three hours by air from Toronto and Montreal, with frequent nonstop flights, and Caribbean sailings run all year.

### A family spread coast to coast
Florida is often the easiest common point in winter, and Vancouver or Seattle in summer. Spring breaks don't line up across provinces, so families with school-age kids in different provinces usually end up choosing July or August.

> **Good to know:** seven nights is the sweet spot when relatives are flying in from several provinces. Shorter sailings rarely justify the flights, and longer ones are hard for working adults to take off.

## The planning sequence
- **15–18 months out:** send a short survey on dates, budget comfort, mobility needs and who is likely to come.
- **12–15 months:** the committee shortlists two sailings, the family votes, and the group block opens with refundable deposits.
- **9–12 months:** pick cabin numbers as a cluster, lock in accessible cabins and submit dining requests.
- **6–9 months:** remind everyone about insurance and passport expiry dates.
- **3–4 months:** final payment and names locked; shared-event money collected.
- **Final month:** circulate the cabin list, the event schedule and an emergency contact sheet.

Clustering is explained in [cabins for large groups](/articles/large-group-cabins-stay-connected/), and the payment dates in [group cruise deadlines](/articles/group-cruise-deadlines-deposits/).

## Keeping 35 people from becoming chaos
- **Two chats:** one for announcements from the committee only, one for everything else.
- **One shared document** with the cabin list, flight arrivals and key dates.
- **Three anchor events:** welcome drinks on night one, a family dinner mid-week and a group photo on formal night. Seating tips are in [seating a group of 20 or more](/articles/group-dining-cruises-20-people/).
- **Freedom on port days.** Let branches do their own thing rather than moving 35 people through one port.
- **A daily meeting spot,** such as one lounge at 5 pm, for anyone who wants company.

> **Example:** a family of 34 living in Halifax, Ottawa and Calgary books 15 cabins on a 7-night Caribbean sailing from Fort Lauderdale in July. Everyone flies in the day before, the committee books a block of hotel rooms near the port, and the family's first get-together is breakfast before boarding.

## Insurance and documents
Every household should buy its own travel medical and cancellation cover, since ages and health vary widely across a family this size; [group cruise insurance](/articles/group-cruise-insurance-one-policy-everyone/) explains why one policy rarely fits all. Check passport expiry dates at the survey stage, as renewals take time.

## Budgets across many households
Each household pays for its own cabin and onboard spending. Shared costs, such as the welcome drinks or a family excursion, should be agreed and collected in advance. If budgets vary widely, see [managing different budgets in a group](/articles/multi-family-friend-group-cruise-budget-tiers/).

## A framework, not a schedule
A family this large doesn't need every hour planned. It needs a clear structure, a few people sharing the work, and enough free time that everyone comes home wanting to do it again.
