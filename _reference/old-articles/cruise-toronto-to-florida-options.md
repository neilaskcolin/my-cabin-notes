No cruise sails from Toronto to Florida, because ocean cruise ships can't get to Lake Ontario. If the real goal is avoiding the airport, there are workable alternatives: start from New York, Boston or Baltimore, or sail from Montreal or Quebec City. Most Ontarians still fly to Florida, and it is easier than it sounds.

## Why there's no Toronto–Florida sailing
To get from Lake Ontario to the Atlantic, a ship has to pass through the St. Lawrence Seaway's locks, which limit vessels to about 225 m long and 24 m wide. The ships that sail to the Caribbean are typically 290 m or more and much wider. The only cruise ships visiting Toronto are small Great Lakes expedition vessels, and they don't run to Florida.

## Your realistic options compared
| Option | How you reach the ship | Typical length | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fly to Fort Lauderdale or Miami | 2.5–3 hour nonstop | 7-night Caribbean plus a pre-night | Most people, especially with limited vacation days |
| Round trip from New York or Baltimore | Drive about 8 hours, train or short flight | 5–10 nights | Airport-averse travellers |
| Autumn repositioning to Florida | Start in New York or Boston, fly home from Florida | 5–12 nights | Flexible schedules, retirees |
| Canada–New England from Montreal or Quebec City | Train or drive | 7–10 nights | Fall colours rather than beaches |
| Drive to Florida and sail | 2–3 days each way | Two weeks or more | Snowbirds already heading south |

### Round trips from New York and Baltimore
New York has year-round sailings to the Bahamas and Caribbean, and some itineraries call at Florida ports along the way. Reach it by Amtrak's daily Maple Leaf from Toronto, about 12–13 hours including a customs stop at the border, or by an 8-hour drive. Baltimore, also a day's drive, has sailings much of the year. Expect two or more sea days each way before the warm weather, which some people love and others find slow.

### Autumn repositionings
Each fall, ships that spent summer in New England and Canada move south for the winter, selling one-way sailings from New York or Boston to Fort Lauderdale or Miami, often with Bermuda or Bahamas stops. Per-night fares can be low; the catch is a one-way flight home. How these fares work is in [what a repositioning cruise is](/articles/repositioning-cruises-explained/).

### Canada–New England from Quebec
September and October sailings from Montreal or Quebec City to Boston or New York are the closest a Toronto family gets to an ocean cruise without a long flight. VIA Rail reaches Montreal in about five hours. They don't reach Florida, but they are covered in [New England and Canada fall cruises](/articles/new-england-canada-fall-cruises/).

> **Good to know:** every option that touches the US needs a valid passport. Coming home after more than 48 hours away, each person can bring back up to C$800 of goods duty-free.

## How to choose
1. **Count your vacation days.** With a week, flying to Florida for a 7-night Caribbean cruise is hard to beat.
2. **Decide how much you dislike flying.** If it is a firm no, a New York round trip by train or car is the cleanest option.
3. **Check the calendar.** Repositionings and Canada–New England sailings run in autumn; Caribbean sailings from Florida peak in winter.
4. **Compare total cost.** A repositioning plus a one-way flight home often costs less than a Caribbean week with return flights, but uses more days. For Caribbean costs, see [Caribbean cruise cost from Canada](/articles/caribbean-cruise-cost-canada-budget/).

## The flight most people take
Toronto to Fort Lauderdale or Miami takes about two and a half to three hours, with frequent service and fares from around C$180–350 return at recent prices. Fly in the day before, especially in winter, when snow and de-icing delays at Pearson are common (see [snow delay risk on winter flights](/articles/snow-delay-winter-flights-caribbean-cruise/)). Step-by-step plans are in [Toronto to Fort Lauderdale cruise flights](/articles/toronto-fort-lauderdale-cruise-flights/) and [Toronto to Miami for a Caribbean cruise](/articles/toronto-miami-caribbean-cruise/).

## Choosing between them
If you have the days and hate airports, New York by train is the most relaxing route to warm water. If you have one week, fly. If you love sea days, an autumn repositioning gives you the most ocean for the money.
