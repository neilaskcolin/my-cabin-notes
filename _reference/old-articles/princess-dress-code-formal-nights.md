Princess runs two evening dress codes: smart casual on most nights, and formal on one or two nights per sailing. A 7-night Alaska cruise usually has two formal nights. The codes apply in the dining rooms in the evening; the buffet and casual spots stay relaxed every night.

## What does smart casual mean on Princess?
Smart casual is the default. Think trousers or chinos with a collared shirt or a neat sweater, or a dress, skirt or smart top with trousers. Swimwear, tank tops, flip-flops and gym gear are out in the dining rooms after dark. Many guests wear smart jeans on these nights.

Princess keeps a slightly more traditional evening feel than the bigger party lines, in keeping with a guest mix that leans towards couples over 50. That does not mean stiff. It means most people make a small effort for dinner.

## How formal are Princess formal nights?
The line suggests a suit, or jacket and tie, for men and an evening gown or elegant dress for women. In practice a jacket without a tie, or dress trousers with a collared shirt, is common and accepted. Some guests go all out with tuxedos and gowns, particularly on longer voyages and transatlantic crossings.

Formal nights tend to be the social high point of the week. Many sailings include a captain's welcome event and the champagne waterfall in the Piazza, the ship's central atrium, which is a Princess tradition and a popular photo spot. The general picture across lines is in [formal nights on cruises](/articles/formal-nights-what-to-wear/).

| Where you eat | Smart casual night | Formal night |
|---|---|---|
| Main dining rooms | Collared shirt, dress or smart separates | Suit or jacket; evening or cocktail dress |
| Crown Grill, Sabatini's | Smart casual | Formal expected |
| Buffet | Neat casual | Smart casual |
| Pizza, burgers, room service | Anything reasonable | Anything reasonable |

## Is the dress code relaxed on Alaska sailings?
Yes, in practice. On a Vancouver round trip you will see plenty of dark trousers with a sweater, and simple dresses with a wrap, alongside a few tuxedos. Formal nights usually fall on sea days or scenic cruising days such as Glacier Bay, so you are not changing in a rush after a long port day. More on what people actually wear is in the [Alaska cruise dress code](/articles/alaska-cruise-dress-code/) guide.

Daytime is a different matter. You will live in layers, a waterproof shell and good shoes, as covered in [what to pack for Alaska in May vs September](/articles/alaska-packing-may-vs-september/).

## Packing for a 7-night Princess cruise
1. Choose one formal outfit you can wear twice, and change only the shirt, tie or accessories for the second night.
2. Add a blazer or smart cardigan. It works on formal nights and lifts a smart casual outfit too.
3. Pack four or five smart casual evening outfits that mix and match.
4. Bring one pair of dress shoes that are comfortable enough for the walk from your cabin, which on the larger ships can be long.
5. Leave the tuxedo at home unless you love wearing one. Nobody will turn you away from dinner without it.

> **Example:** a couple flying from Toronto to Vancouver with one checked bag each can cover both formal nights with a dark suit and a single dress, plus a scarf and a change of tie. That keeps the formal kit to a small corner of each bag.
