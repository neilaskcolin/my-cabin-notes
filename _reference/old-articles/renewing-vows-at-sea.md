Yes, you can renew your vows on a cruise, and it is simpler than a wedding because nothing is legally binding. Most lines offer packages booked before sailing, ranging from a short, officer-led ceremony to a full celebration with photos and a reception.

## What a shipboard vow renewal involves
Because you are already married, there is no paperwork, licence or legal officiant. The ceremony is purely about the occasion. A ship's officer or staff member usually leads it, using a standard script that you can personalise with your own words.

Ceremonies usually take place at a scenic spot on board, such as a forward lounge, the chapel on ships that have one, or an open deck. Some lines can also arrange ceremonies ashore in certain ports through local partners.

## How much does it cost?
Packages and prices vary by line. These are typical ranges at recent prices; confirm for your ship.

| Package level | Usually includes | Rough cost per couple |
|---|---|---|
| Simple | Short ceremony, officiant, certificate, basic decor | US$200–400 (C$275–560) |
| Mid-range | Above plus bouquet, champagne, small cake, some photos | US$500–900 (C$690–1,250) |
| Full celebration | Above plus photographer, reception space, canapés, more flowers | US$800–1,500+ (C$1,100–2,100+) |

Some premium and luxury lines will arrange a simple ceremony at little or no cost for milestone anniversaries. It is worth asking before buying a package.

> **Example:** a Winnipeg couple celebrating 40 years on a 7-night Caribbean cruise might choose a mid-range package on the first sea day, then book a specialty restaurant that evening. Allowing about US$700 for the ceremony and US$150–200 for dinner and drinks, the celebration adds roughly C$1,200 to the trip.

## How to book it, step by step
1. **Tell the line at booking** that you are celebrating an anniversary, and ask whether vow renewals are offered on your ship.
2. **Contact the celebrations or weddings department** well before sailing. Slots are limited, and packages often close a set number of days before departure.
3. **Choose your day and time.** A sea day is easiest. Port days are busy, and on embarkation day the crew is stretched.
4. **Confirm the details in writing:** location, officiant, what is included and the photo arrangements.
5. **Check in with guest services on day one** to confirm everything is still on.

## Making it feel personal
### Your own words
Write your own vows, or ask to add a reading or a song to the standard ceremony. Keep a printed copy, as phones can be awkward on deck.

### The right moment
Ask about timing that suits the itinerary: a sunset on a Caribbean sea day, or a calm stretch of the Inside Passage on an Alaska sailing. On glacier days, senior officers are often busy on the bridge, so another day is usually better.

### What to bring
- Outfits in a garment bag, plus layers if the ceremony is outdoors.
- Rings, if you plan to exchange them again.
- Small personal touches, like photos or a family keepsake.

Most ships do not allow candles or loose confetti, so check before packing decorations.

## Inviting others
In most cases only people sailing with you can attend, so if you want family there, they need to book the same cruise. Planning a bigger family trip around the occasion is covered in [silver or golden anniversary cruises with family](/articles/silver-golden-anniversary-cruise-family-invite/).
