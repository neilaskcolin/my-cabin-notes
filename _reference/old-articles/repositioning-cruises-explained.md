A repositioning cruise is the trip a ship makes between seasons, sold as a one-way voyage. Because the ship must sail anyway, the fares are often among the lowest per night you will find, as long as you can handle plenty of sea days and a one-way flight.

## Why ships reposition
Cruise ships follow the weather. Alaska ships leave the Pacific Northwest each fall for warmer waters, and Caribbean ships cross to Europe each spring for the summer season, then return in the fall. These connecting voyages, known as "repos", are sold like any other cruise.

The routes are often unusual too, with port combinations no regular schedule offers, such as the Azores on a transatlantic or a string of Pacific coast cities on the way south from Vancouver.

## Common repositioning routes for Canadians
| Route | When | Typical length | Why it matters to Canadians |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vancouver to Hawaii | September–October | 10–18 nights | Sail from home, fly back from Honolulu |
| Vancouver to California | Late September–October, reverse in spring | 2–5 nights | Short, cheap trial cruise from Canada Place |
| Vancouver to Asia or Australia | Fall | 2–3+ weeks | A long voyage without a long flight out |
| Florida to Panama Canal to Vancouver | Spring | 15+ nights | Ends at home for BC and Alberta |
| New York or Boston to Florida | October–November | 5–10 nights | Easy start for Ontario and Quebec |
| Florida to Europe | April–May, reverse October–November | 12–16 nights | The classic transatlantic |

Exact sailings change every year, so treat this as a guide to what to look for rather than a schedule. ## What a repo is like on board
Expect a different crowd and pace from a summer week. Children are scarce outside school holidays, and many guests are retirees or seasoned cruisers. Ships keep every restaurant, show and bar open, and many add guest lecturers, cooking demos and classes for the sea days. If that sounds appealing, see [enrichment lectures at sea](/articles/enrichment-at-sea-lectures/).

### The honest trade-offs
- **One-way flights.** You finish far from home, and the flight back from Honolulu, Barcelona or Sydney is the real cost. It can eat half the fare saving.
- **Long runs of sea days.** Five or more in a row on crossings is bliss for readers and a slog for port collectors.
- **Season's-edge weather.** The North Pacific and North Atlantic in spring and fall can be rough. Pack seasickness remedies.
- **Dates that don't repeat.** Each route usually runs only once or twice a year.
- **Time-zone changes.** Clocks move an hour on many nights, which is easier heading west.

> **Example:** a Kelowna couple boards a fall Vancouver to Honolulu sailing, so there is no flight out. If the fare is about US$100 per person per night for 12 nights and the one-way flight home costs C$500–800 each, the trip still often comes in well below a Hawaii round trip with return flights. Price it both ways using our [Hawaii cruise cost from Canada](/articles/hawaii-cruise-cost-canada/) guide.

## Who repositioning cruises suit
They suit retirees and anyone with flexible dates, sea-day lovers, solo travellers watching for reduced supplements, and anyone who has always wanted a long voyage or an ocean crossing. Families tied to the school calendar and travellers who want a new port every day are usually better served elsewhere.

## How to judge a repo fare
Add up the whole trip, not just the cruise: fare, one-way flights, any hotel nights at either end, gratuities for every night, and travel insurance. Divide by the number of nights to compare with a regular sailing. Many repos still win comfortably; the false bargains are the ones where an expensive flight home wipes out the saving.

> **Good to know:** gratuities are charged per night, so a 15-night repo carries twice the tip bill of a week. Prepaying them in Canadian dollars where the line allows it keeps that cost predictable.

## Catching the right one
Repositioning sailings appear in narrow windows and the best-value cabins go early, often a year or more ahead. If you live near Vancouver, watch the fall departures from Canada Place first, since sailing from home removes half the flight problem.
