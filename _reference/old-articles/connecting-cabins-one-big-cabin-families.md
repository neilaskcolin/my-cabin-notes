For most families of four or more, two connecting cabins win on space, privacy and bathrooms, and the price gap is often smaller than people expect. One larger cabin wins when the children are small, when budget is tight, or when you simply want everyone in one room.

## Connecting, adjoining and family cabins: what's the difference?
These terms get mixed up at booking, and the difference matters.

- **Connecting cabins** have an interior door between them, so you can move between rooms without going into the corridor.
- **Adjoining or adjacent cabins** sit side by side with no internal door. On some ships, the cabin attendant can open the divider between neighbouring balconies on request.
- **Family cabins** are single rooms designed for four to six, often with bunks, a sofa bed or a curtain to split the space. Some lines also sell two-bedroom family suites.

Always confirm the cabin numbers are a true connecting pair on the deck plan. [How to read a cruise ship deck plan](/articles/reading-cruise-ship-deck-plans/) shows the symbol to look for.

## How do the options compare?

| | Two connecting cabins | One family cabin | One standard cabin for four |
|---|---|---|---|
| Space | Two full cabins | Larger than standard | Standard size, tight |
| Bathrooms | Two | Usually one, sometimes split | One |
| Privacy for parents | Close the door | Curtain at best | None |
| Price | Two double-occupancy fares | Premium for the category | Lowest, with cheaper 3rd and 4th berths |
| Availability | Limited, sells out early | Very limited | Wide |
| Kids' age rules | Line minimums apply | No issue | No issue |

## Running the numbers
Cruise fares are priced per person, assuming two people in a cabin. The third and fourth guests in the same cabin usually pay much less, which is why one cabin for four can undercut two cabins. Taxes, port fees and daily gratuities are per person either way, so they don't change the comparison.

> **Quick math:** on a 7-night Caribbean sailing where an inside cabin costs US$900 per person for two, two connecting insides for a family of four come to about US$3,600 before taxes. The same four in one cabin might pay US$900 + US$900 + two reduced fares of around US$400 each, about US$2,600. That's roughly C$1,350–1,400 more for the pair, buying a second bathroom and a door that closes.

Kids-sail-free promotions tilt this further towards one cabin, because they only apply to the third and fourth berths. [Do kids really sail free?](/articles/kids-sail-free-catch/) explains the fine print.

### When the pair is worth it
- Children aged roughly eight and up, or teenagers who want their own space.
- Families with a 6:30 a.m. riser and a night owl.
- Two bathrooms for four or five people getting ready for dinner at the same time.
- Grandparents travelling with the family, covered in [multigenerational cruise planning](/articles/multigenerational-cruise-planning-cabins/).

### When one cabin makes more sense
- Babies and toddlers who'll sleep in your room anyway (see [cruising with a baby or toddler](/articles/cruising-with-baby-toddler/)).
- Tight budgets, particularly on port-heavy itineraries where you're rarely in the cabin.
- Families who'd rather spend the difference on excursions.

## The age and noise catch
Most lines require an adult in each cabin unless the children's cabin connects to the parents', and they set a minimum age for kids sleeping in their own room. Rules differ, so check before booking. Also note that connecting doors aren't as soundproof as walls. If you book a connecting cabin next to strangers, you may hear them, which is one reason light sleepers avoid them (see [noisy cabin complaints](/articles/cruise-cabin-noise-complaints-what-to-do/)).

## Book early for school holidays
Connecting pairs and family cabins are a small share of any ship, and they go first for March break, Christmas and July and August, when Canadian schools are out. For those dates, booking nine to twelve months ahead is sensible. Guarantee fares, which assign your cabin later, can't promise a connecting pair, so avoid them if the door matters.
