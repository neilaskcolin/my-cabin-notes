Alaska cruise ads love a headline fare. Here is what a Calgary couple realistically spends on a 7-night Inside Passage sailing from Vancouver, so nothing ambushes your credit card.

## What does an Alaska cruise from Calgary cost in total?
Prices below are per couple, at recent prices, for a 7-night round trip from Vancouver. Cruise costs are charged in US dollars on most bookings, so they are shown with a rough Canadian-dollar equivalent.

| Item, per couple | Inside cabin | Balcony cabin |
|---|---|---|
| Cruise fare | US$1,400–2,200 (C$1,900–3,100) | US$2,400–4,000 (C$3,250–5,600) |
| Taxes and port fees, if not in the fare | about C$540–840 | about C$540–840 |
| Calgary–Vancouver return flights | C$300–700 | C$300–700 |
| One hotel night in Vancouver | C$250–450 | C$250–450 |
| SkyTrain to and from the terminal | C$20–40 | C$20–40 |
| Gratuities | C$340–490 | C$340–490 |
| Drinks without a package | C$270–560 | C$270–560 |
| Wifi, if you need it | C$0–280 | C$0–280 |
| Two excursions each | C$810–1,400 | C$810–1,400 |
| Travel insurance | C$150–500 | C$150–500 |
| **Rough total** | **C$4,500–8,500** | **C$6,000–11,000** |

> **Example:** a couple who find an inside cabin at US$800 each in May, fly on a seat sale, skip wifi and book one excursion each will land near the bottom of that range. The same couple in a July balcony with a drink habit and a helicopter tour can pass C$10,000.

## The fare itself
Inside cabins run roughly US$700–1,100 per person in shoulder season (May and September), and balconies about US$1,200–2,000. Peak summer, when Alberta schools are out, pushes both higher. [When Alaska cruise prices drop](/articles/when-do-alaska-cruise-prices-drop/) covers timing.

Alaska is the one itinerary where the balcony argument is strongest, because the scenery is the destination. The [best cabins for Alaska scenery](/articles/best-cabins-alaska-cruise-scenery/) page explains which side and deck pay off.

## Getting from Calgary to the ship
The flight to Vancouver is about an hour and a half, and you gain an hour crossing into Pacific time. Even so, fly the day before: a delayed morning flight is the most common way Albertans miss a ship (see [flying the day before vs same day](/articles/fly-day-before-cruise/)).

From YVR, the Canada Line SkyTrain runs to Waterfront station, a short walk from Canada Place, for around C$10 each including the airport add-fare. Downtown hotels near the terminal are expensive in summer, and booking early or staying near a Canada Line station helps. Step-by-step options are in [Calgary to the Vancouver cruise terminal](/articles/calgary-to-vancouver-cruise-terminal/).

### What about driving or Seattle?
The drive is roughly 10–11 hours each way, which usually means an overnight stop plus port parking. It is covered in [driving from Calgary to an Alaska cruise](/articles/drive-from-calgary-to-alaska-cruise/). Seattle sometimes has cheaper fares but adds a border crossing and pricier flights, compared in [Seattle vs Vancouver from Calgary](/articles/calgary-vs-seattle-vs-vancouver-alaska/).

## The onboard spend nobody budgets
Gratuities run about US$18–25 per person per day depending on the line, or roughly US$250–350 per couple for the week. A latte here and a beer at the glacier there adds US$200–400 without a package. Wifi is US$100–200 for the week if you need it.

> **Canadian tip:** onboard charges post in US dollars. A no-foreign-transaction-fee credit card saves around 2.5% on everything you sign for, which on a typical onboard bill is C$30–60.

## Shore excursions: the big variable
This is where budgets explode. Whale watching in Juneau runs about US$150–250 per person, the White Pass railway in Skagway about US$150–200, and helicopter-and-glacier trips US$400 and up. Two excursions each is a normal spend of US$600–1,000 per couple.

Walking towns on your own is free and genuinely good in Ketchikan and Skagway. Try the [free Ketchikan walking plan](/articles/ketchikan-free-walking-plan/), and use [are Alaska shore excursions worth it](/articles/alaska-shore-excursions-worth-it/) to pick the one or two that matter most.

## Insurance and documents
Every Alaska port is in the US, and your Alberta health plan covers very little out of country. Buy travel medical insurance at minimum, and consider trip cancellation cover given the flights involved. Bring valid passports, as explained in [passports for an Alaska cruise from Vancouver](/articles/passport-alaska-cruise-vancouver/).

## How to cut the bill
1. Sail in May or September rather than July or August.
2. Book flights early, or watch for seat sales on the Calgary–Vancouver route.
3. Prepay gratuities in CAD where the line allows it.
4. Pick one standout excursion each and do the rest on foot.
5. Skip the drink package unless the maths clearly works.

## Where the money hides
Two identical cabins on the same sailing can differ by hundreds of dollars depending on when and how they were booked, and whether the fare came with prepaid gratuities, onboard credit or a drink package. On the budget above, US$300 of prepaid gratuities plus US$200 of onboard credit removes a meaningful chunk of the hidden costs. Ask what is bundled with any quote, and compare totals, not headline fares.
