A cruise is one of the easiest holidays to take grandchildren on without their parents: meals, entertainment and supervised kids clubs are all on board. The extra work is mostly paperwork before you leave and pacing once you're there.

## The documents you need
Get these sorted at least a month before sailing:

1. **The child's passport.** It's the simplest document for any international cruise, including Alaska sailings from Vancouver that stop in US ports. See [passports, NEXUS and ID for cruising](/articles/passports-nexus-id-cruise-documents/).
2. **A consent letter from the parents.** The Government of Canada publishes a free consent letter template for children travelling abroad without one or both parents. Both parents, or whoever has legal custody, should sign it, and having it witnessed or notarised adds weight.
3. **The cruise line's own form.** Many lines have a minor-consent or parental-authorisation form for children sailing without a parent. Use it as well as the Canadian letter, not instead of it.
4. **Medical authorisation.** A signed note letting you approve emergency treatment, plus the child's provincial health card copy, allergies, medications and doctor's details.
5. **Supporting proof if surnames differ.** A copy of the child's birth certificate showing the parents' names helps explain the relationship.

> **Canadian tip:** your grandchild's provincial health plan won't cover care on board or abroad. Add them to your travel insurance, or ask the parents to buy a policy, and make sure it names you as the travelling adults.

## Choosing a ship and itinerary
### Kids clubs matter more than ever
A good kids club gives the grandchildren friends their own age and gives you an afternoon off. Most run from about age 3 to 17 in age bands, often free for the main hours. Register on day one and make sure you're listed as the responsible adults. [Kids clubs on cruise ships](/articles/kids-clubs-cruise-ships-ages-costs/) covers ages, hours and costs.

### Good first itineraries
- **Alaska from Vancouver:** no flight for many BC families, whales and glaciers kids remember, and calm Inside Passage water.
- **A short Caribbean or Bahamas sailing:** 4 or 5 nights is a good test run for a first trip together.
- **Family-focused lines:** if the grandchildren are young, [Disney Cruise Line](/articles/disney-cruise-line-honest-guide-canadians/) or another family-heavy line makes things easy.

## Cabins: together or connecting?
One cabin is cheapest, but sharing a small room for a week can wear on everyone. Connecting cabins give you evening privacy with the children a door away. On many lines a child can sleep in the connecting cabin as long as an adult aged 21 or older is next door. See [connecting cabins vs one big cabin](/articles/connecting-cabins-one-big-cabin-families/) and [how to pick your first cabin](/articles/pick-first-cruise-cabin/).

## Pacing two generations
Grandchildren run on a different battery. A rhythm that works for many families:

- one active outing in the morning, then lunch and a rest
- kids club in the afternoon while grandparents nap, read or visit the spa
- early dinner, a family show, and bedtime routines kept roughly the same as at home
- one port day with no excursion at all

> **Watch out:** grandchildren may be seasick, homesick or both, especially on night one. Pack children's motion-sickness remedies the parents approve, and read [seasickness in kids](/articles/seasickness-in-kids-what-to-do/).

## Agree on the rules with the parents first
Talk through screen time, bedtimes, treats, swimming without an adult, and how often you'll check in. A small wifi package makes a nightly photo to the parents easy, and [cruise wifi and roaming for Canadians](/articles/cruise-wifi-phone-roaming-canadians/) explains how to avoid roaming charges. Clarify whether children may join teen clubs or sign for purchases on their own cruise card.

If the parents come next time, the trip becomes a multigenerational one; see [cruising with adult children](/articles/cruise-with-adult-children-parents/) for that version.

## Planning a smooth skip-generation cruise
Sort the passport, consent letter and medical permission early, choose a ship with a strong kids club, and plan fewer activities than you think. Confirm your line's exact minor-travel rules before final payment, since requirements vary by line and itinerary.
