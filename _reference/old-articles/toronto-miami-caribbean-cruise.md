Toronto to Miami works smoothly if you do two things: confirm your ship actually sails from PortMiami, and land the day before. The flight is a three-hour nonstop, and US customs happens before you leave Pearson. The variable is Miami itself: a big airport, busy roads and a port with many terminals.

## Check which port your ship uses
Miami is the home port for many of the newest, largest ships from lines like Royal Caribbean, Carnival, Norwegian and MSC. Fort Lauderdale's Port Everglades, about 45 minutes north, hosts a different set of sailings. A few lines use both.

The port is printed on your booking confirmation and boarding documents. Read it before buying flights, and note your **terminal letter** too, because PortMiami has several terminals spread along the island.

## The flight from Toronto
Nonstops from Pearson to MIA take a little over three hours, with frequent daily departures in winter and fares similar to the Fort Lauderdale route. At recent prices, return economy often runs roughly C$300–550, with March break and Christmas at the top of that range or beyond.

> **Canadian tip:** Pearson's US preclearance means you go through US customs and immigration in Toronto. That's the part of Miami's reputation for long queues that doesn't apply to you. Allow extra time at Pearson instead, especially on winter Friday and Saturday mornings.

## Getting from MIA to PortMiami
| Option | Rough cost | Time | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Taxi | about US$30–40 with tip | 20–45 minutes | Couples, quickest door to door |
| Rideshare | varies with demand | 20–45 minutes, plus pickup-zone walk | App users travelling light |
| Cruise line coach | about US$20–35 per person | Waits to fill, then direct | First-timers, solo travellers |
| Hotel or private shuttle | varies | Depends on route | Groups and families |

Prices change, so confirm when you book. On embarkation day, the main delay is usually the queue of cars and coaches onto the port causeway around midday, not the highway.

## Where to stay the night before
- **Downtown, Brickell or near Bayside:** the closest area to the port, often a short taxi ride, with plenty of restaurants. Some hotels run cruise shuttles.
- **Near the airport:** convenient after a late flight, and usually cheaper, but you'll do the full transfer on cruise morning.
- **Miami Beach:** fun for an extra night, but further from the port and pricier. Better saved for a post-cruise stay.
- **Fort Lauderdale:** only if your ship sails from there. Don't stay in Fort Lauderdale and drive south on a cruise morning.

Hotels near the port typically run US$150–300 a night in winter at recent prices, roughly C$210–420. Booking early helps.

## Embarkation morning in Miami
1. **Check your boarding time** in the cruise line app. Most lines give you an arrival window, and turning up early rarely gets you on sooner.
2. **Check out and leave your hotel** with time to spare. Late morning is usually right for a midday boarding window.
3. **Tell the driver your terminal letter**, not just "the cruise port".
4. **Hand checked bags to porters at the kerb**, keeping your passport, medication and a swimsuit in your carry-on.
5. **Clear security and check-in**, then head aboard for lunch.

The [embarkation day mistakes first-timers make](/articles/embarkation-day-mistakes-first-timers/) list covers the rest.

## Miami or Fort Lauderdale?
Your ship decides. If it sails from PortMiami, fly to Miami. If the MIA fare is very high, Fort Lauderdale is a fallback with a longer drive. If your ship sails from Port Everglades, fly to FLL and enjoy the 15-minute transfer. ## Avoiding the port mix-up
The most common Miami mistake is booking flights and a hotel for the wrong port. Look up your departure port, terminal and boarding window before paying for anything else, and keep them in one note on your phone.
