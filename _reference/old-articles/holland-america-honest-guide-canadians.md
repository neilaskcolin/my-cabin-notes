Holland America is right for you if you want a calm, mid-size ship with traditional touches, strong Alaska itineraries and the option of very long voyages. It is not the line for toddlers, waterslides or nightlife. Most Canadians who love it are couples and solo travellers who value a good dining room, live music and a deck chair over a ropes course.

## What kind of cruise line is Holland America?
Holland America traces its roots to 1873 in the Netherlands and is now based in Seattle, part of the same parent company as Princess. That Dutch heritage still shows in ship names ending in "dam" and in a slightly formal, polished style of service.

Its biggest point of difference is size. Holland America has no mega-ships. Its fleet of around 11 ships carries roughly 1,400 to 2,700 guests each, so there are shorter lines, fewer crowds and less walking than on ships twice that size.

| Ship class | Ships | Approx. guests | What to expect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pinnacle | Koningsdam, Nieuw Statendam, Rotterdam | about 2,650 | Newest (2016–2021), most dining and music venues |
| Signature | Eurodam, Nieuw Amsterdam | about 2,100 | Late 2000s, a middle ground |
| Vista | Zuiderdam, Oosterdam, Westerdam, Noordam | about 1,900–2,000 | 2002–2006, classic layout, popular in Alaska |
| R class | Volendam, Zaandam | about 1,400 | Oldest, most intimate, often on longer itineraries |

> **Good to know:** ship age matters more on Holland America than on lines that refresh constantly. The Pinnacle-class ships have the newest cabins and venues, while the older ships trade some polish for a cosier feel and lower fares.

## The onboard style
Evenings lean traditional. Most nights are smart casual, with a few "dressy" nights per cruise, as the [Holland America dress code](/articles/holland-america-dress-code-dressy-nights/) page explains. Entertainment centres on live music, with several small venues on many ships, rather than big production shows or ice rinks.

Dining is a strength. The main dining room is the anchor, the Lido Market buffet covers casual meals, and specialty restaurants such as the Pinnacle Grill and Canaletto are a step up for a fee. Expect libraries, enrichment talks, cooking demos and quiet lounges.

## Holland America in Alaska
This is where the line is strongest for Western Canadians. Holland America sails from both Vancouver and Seattle, usually on 7-night Inside Passage round trips, with Glacier Bay on many itineraries. Seattle round trips usually call at Victoria, while Vancouver departures suit anyone who would rather start the trip in Canada.

It is also the line most associated with land-and-sea cruisetours that include Canada's Yukon as well as Denali, a genuinely distinctive option. [Alaska cruisetours explained](/articles/alaska-cruisetours-denali-rail/) and [Glacier Bay vs Hubbard vs Endicott](/articles/glacier-bay-vs-hubbard-vs-endicott/) help with the choices.

> **Canadian tip:** from Calgary or Edmonton, a short flight to Vancouver and a sailing from Canada Place keeps the trip simple, with no US airport or border line-up before boarding. You still need a passport, as [the Vancouver Alaska passport guide](/articles/passport-alaska-cruise-vancouver/) explains.

## Long voyages and the snowbird calendar
Holland America runs more long itineraries than most big lines: Panama Canal transits, transatlantics, Collectors' Voyages that link back-to-back sailings without repeating ports, Grand Voyages of a month or more, and a Grand World Voyage of around four months. For retired Canadians escaping winter, that is a real draw, covered in [longer cruises for retirees](/articles/longer-cruises-retirees-14-nights/).

In the fall, Canada and New England sailings bring the line closer to Ontario and Quebec, with some itineraries using Montreal or Quebec City. See [New England and Canada fall cruises](/articles/new-england-canada-fall-cruises/).

## Who Holland America suits, and who it doesn't
It tends to suit:
- couples and solo travellers in their 50s, 60s and beyond
- first-timers who want a calm introduction to cruising
- Alaska travellers who want Glacier Bay, Yukon options or a Vancouver departure
- anyone wanting 14 nights or more without changing ships

Consider alternatives if you are:
- travelling with young children, since the [kids club](/articles/holland-america-kids-club-ages/) is small and quiet
- after nightlife, waterslides or big-ship thrills
- looking for the newest ship design, where [Celebrity](/articles/celebrity-cruises-honest-guide-canadians/) may appeal more

## Questions to ask before booking Holland America
1. Which ship is it, and how old? A Pinnacle-class ship and an R-class ship feel quite different.
2. Is the fare cruise-only or Have It All, and which extras does each include?
3. For Alaska, does the itinerary include Glacier Bay, and is it a round trip or one-way?
4. For long voyages, does your Canadian insurance cover the full trip length?
5. What is the final payment date? Holland America has been moving many bookings to 120 days.
