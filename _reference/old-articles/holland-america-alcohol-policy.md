Holland America allows wine and champagne on board, in bottles of 750 ml or less, and charges corkage of about US$20 per bottle at recent policy. Beer, spirits and liqueurs are not allowed, and anything you buy in port or in the onboard shops is held until the last night.

## What Holland America lets you carry on
Wine is the one real exception to a broad "no outside drinks" rule. Holland America's current guidance says each bottle must be 750 ml or less, that limits apply, and that the ship or security can refuse a quantity it considers excessive.

For many years the line let each adult bring one bottle free and charged corkage only on extras. Its current FAQ simply lists a fee per bottle, and you will still see the free-bottle allowance quoted in older guides. Treat the free bottle as something to confirm, not assume.

### What's not allowed
- Beer, spirits and liqueurs, in carry-on or checked bags.
- Sodas and energy drinks, in any form.
- Plastic water bottles, even ones bought in port. A small amount of water per stateroom is allowed in cans or cartons.
- Open drinks in plastic containers, which have to be finished or thrown out before security.

Sealed distilled or purified water for a medical device such as a CPAP is a separate exception, packed in checked luggage with the device. The details are on the [CPAP on Holland America](/articles/holland-america-cpap-machine/) page.

## How the corkage fee works
Corkage is charged per bottle, around US$20 at recent policy, and is billed to your onboard account. Two things are exempt: wine you buy from Holland America itself, and one bottle per person bought on a Holland America winery excursion.

> **Quick math:** a couple bringing four bottles of Okanagan or Niagara wine on a 7-night Alaska sailing would pay about US$80 in corkage, roughly C$110. It pays off when those bottles would cost a good deal more on the wine list.

## Bottles bought in port or on board
Alcohol bought in a port of call or in the ship's shops is collected at the gangway and delivered to your stateroom shortly before disembarkation. On a Vancouver round trip that usually means it turns up on your last evening, ready to pack.

## Canadian customs on the way home
Bringing wine home after a cruise of more than 48 hours falls under the normal CBSA personal exemption: 1.5 litres of wine, or 1.14 litres of spirits, or 8.5 litres of beer, duty-free. Anything over that is taxed at provincial rates. The rules for shopping on board are covered in [cruise duty-free limits for Canadians](/articles/cruise-shopping-onboard-duty-free/).

> **Watch out:** Seattle sailings mean crossing the US border with your wine first. Keep it to a reasonable personal amount, declare it, and pack it where it will not break.

## Is bringing wine worth it on Holland America?
Holland America's guests tend to drink wine with dinner rather than cocktails by the pool, and the dining room staff are used to opening guests' bottles. On long Alaska, Panama Canal and Grand Voyage itineraries, a few bottles from home can trim a real chunk off the bar bill.

If you drink more than a glass or two a day, compare against the [Holland America drink package price](/articles/holland-america-drink-package-price/), which is also bundled into Have It All fares. Light drinkers usually do best paying by the glass, as the [guide to what drinks are included by default](/articles/cruise-drink-menu-what-included-basics/) explains.

## How Holland America compares
| Line | Wine you can bring | Corkage (recent) |
|---|---|---|
| **Holland America** | 750 ml bottles; limits apply (confirm any free bottle) | ~$20 per bottle |
| [Princess](/articles/princess-alcohol-policy/) | 1 free per adult; more with corkage | ~$20 for extra bottles |
| [Celebrity](/articles/celebrity-alcohol-policy/) | 2 bottles per stateroom | ~$25 in bars and restaurants |

All three lines are relatively relaxed about wine; the differences are whether a bottle comes free and where corkage applies. For alcohol rules for all eight lines, see [Cruise Line Alcohol Policies Compared](/articles/cruise-line-alcohol-policies-compared/).
