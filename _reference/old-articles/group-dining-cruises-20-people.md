A group of 20 can eat together every night of a cruise, but usually at two or three neighbouring tables rather than one long one. What decides it is the dining style you pick and asking when the group booking is made, not at the maître d' stand on day one.

## Can a cruise ship seat 20 people at one table?
Rarely in the main dining room. The largest tables on most big ships seat around 8 to 12, and they are the scarcest tables on board. A party of 20 is normally placed at two adjacent tables of 10, or a 12 and an 8, close enough that people can swap seats between courses.

A true single table for 20 usually means a private or semi-private room, booked for one or two nights rather than all week. Some carry a per-person charge or minimum spend.

## Which dining style works best for a big group?
The dining style matters more than the ship.

| Dining style | How it works for 20 | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Traditional fixed seating | Same tables, time and waiters every night, assigned before you sail | Groups who want to eat together most nights |
| Flexible dining (My Time, Anytime, Freestyle) | Reserve a time each night; big parties may wait or be split | Groups meeting up only a few nights |
| Private dining room | One table or room for everyone, often a set menu | A birthday, anniversary or reunion dinner |
| Specialty restaurant | Possible on quieter nights, booked ahead | One splurge night |
| Buffet | Pull tables together, no booking | Casual lunches and early kids' dinners |

Fixed seating is the reliable choice: the group keeps the same spot and service team all week, and the waiters learn the allergies fast. Norwegian's Freestyle model has no fixed seating, so a group of 20 on NCL should pre-book large-party reservations or expect a few nights split up. The basics of each system are in [how dining works on a modern cruise ship](/articles/how-dining-works-cruise/).

> **Good to know:** on some lines, suite guests have their own exclusive restaurant. They can still join the group in the main dining room, but make sure their names are on the group's table request so nobody is left off the seating plan.

## When should you book group dining?
As soon as the group block is created. Large tables go first come, first served, and leaving it until boarding usually means scattered tables or a late sitting nobody wanted.

1. **Agree on a sitting.** Early (around 5:30–6 pm) suits kids and grandparents; late (around 8–8:30 pm) suits adults who want a long afternoon. Times vary by ship.
2. **Link every cabin.** The request must list every booking number, including anyone who booked before the block existed.
3. **Ask for adjacent tables in one section.** Put "side by side, same waiter team" in writing.
4. **Send dietary needs once, for everyone,** as a single list.
5. **Book the special night now.** Reserve a private room or specialty restaurant for the celebration dinner at the same time.
6. **Reconfirm 30–45 days out** and again with the maître d' on boarding day.

## Making two tables feel like one
- Mix households across both tables so cousins and new partners actually meet.
- Keep one or two nights optional for the buffet or a quiet dinner for two.
- Tell the head waiter early about any birthday or anniversary so the cake and song happen on the right night.

> **Example:** a Toronto family of 20 on a 7-night Caribbean sailing from Fort Lauderdale books early fixed seating at two neighbouring tables, a private dining room for the grandparents' anniversary on night four, and leaves two nights open. Everyone eats together five times without feeling herded.

## What does group dining cost?
Main dining room dinners are included, so nightly group seating costs nothing extra. Private rooms and specialty restaurants cost roughly US$40–80 per person for a specialty dinner at recent prices (about C$55–110), before drinks and a service charge of around 18–20%. For 20 people, that is roughly C$1,100–2,200 for one night, so agree upfront who pays. [Main dining room vs buffet vs specialty restaurants](/articles/main-dining-buffet-specialty-restaurants/) compares the options.

## Which cruise lines handle big tables best?
Lines that still offer traditional fixed seating, including Royal Caribbean, Carnival, Princess and Celebrity, are the easiest for nightly group dinners. Check the specific ship. ## Lock the table in with the cabins
Put the dining request in writing when the block opens, name every cabin, and confirm it twice. Then dinner becomes the easy part of the week.
