The short answer: never from the ship, sometimes ashore, and only after some homework. Drone rules on a cruise come in three layers, the cruise line, the port's country, and the specific spot you want to launch from, and you need a yes from all three.

## Why can't you fly a drone from the ship?
Every major cruise line bans drone flights from its ships, balconies included. The reasons are practical: a crowded open deck, wind that changes as the ship moves, radar and communications masts, and a sea that swallows drones that misjudge a return. A ship is also a moving launch point, which makes "return to home" features unreliable.

Lines enforce this firmly. Flying one on board breaches the line's guest conduct rules, and crew can hold the drone until the end of the cruise.

## Can you bring a drone on the cruise at all?
Many lines allow drones in your luggage for use ashore, but this varies, and some lines hold them or restrict them further. Check the prohibited items list on your line's website, not a forum post from three years ago.

- Pack the drone in your carry-on, with lithium batteries in protective cases or with terminals taped.
- Bring proof of registration and your pilot certificate where required.
- Keep it out of sight on board so security has no reason to question it.

## Where can you fly at cruise ports?

### Alaska ports
Ketchikan, Juneau and Skagway follow US rules. Recreational flyers need to pass the FAA's free safety test, register drones over 250 g, stay under 400 feet and keep the drone in sight. Launching or landing is banned in national park sites, which includes Klondike Gold Rush National Historical Park in Skagway and Sitka National Historical Park. Flying close to whales or sea lions can count as harassment under US marine mammal law.

### Caribbean, Bahamas and Mexico
Rules vary island by island. Several countries, including the Bahamas and Bermuda, have required visitors to get advance permission, and some effectively don't allow tourist flights. Cruise lines' private islands generally ban drones outright.

### Vancouver, before you sail
Canadian rules apply at home too. Drones from 250 g to 25 kg must be registered with Transport Canada and flown by someone with a drone pilot certificate. Canada Place sits in controlled airspace beside the harbour's floatplane base, which needs an advanced certificate and NAV CANADA authorization, so a quick launch at the terminal is not an option.

> **Watch out:** "the rules didn't say no" is not a permit. In several Caribbean countries, flying without approval can mean the drone being seized at customs or in the field.

## A simple pre-trip drone checklist
1. Read your cruise line's prohibited items list for drones.
2. List every port and private island on your itinerary.
3. Look up each country's aviation authority rules for visitors.
4. Rule out national parks, airports and crowded beaches on a map.
5. Register and carry certification for any country that requires it.
6. Decide whether one or two legal flights justify carrying it.

A helicopter or floatplane tour gives you the bird's-eye glacier view legally, with no paperwork. ## Planning aerial photography within the rules
For most cruisers, the honest conclusion is that a drone is a lot of luggage and paperwork for a handful of flights. If aerial footage is the point of the trip, plan it port by port before you pack, or book the helicopter instead.
