Your fare covers the basics you'd expect at a hotel breakfast and a little more. Anything fizzy, frothy or alcoholic is usually extra. Knowing exactly where the line falls is the first step in deciding whether a drinks package is worth buying.

## What drinks are free on a cruise?
On virtually every mainstream line, these come with your fare:

- **Water:** tap water in restaurants and at buffet stations. Ship water is treated and safe to drink.
- **Regular coffee and tea:** brewed coffee, hot tea and often iced tea, at breakfast and at the buffet most of the day.
- **Juice at breakfast:** usually orange and apple, sometimes more.
- **Milk:** at breakfast and with coffee.
- **Lemonade, iced tea or fruit-flavoured water:** at the buffet on many ships.
- **Hot chocolate:** at the buffet on many ships, especially welcome on Alaska sailings.

## What costs extra, and roughly how much?
Prices are typical for mainstream lines at recent rates, in US dollars before the automatic service charge.

| Drink | Included? | Typical price |
|---|---|---|
| Tap water | Yes | Free |
| Brewed coffee and tea | Yes | Free |
| Breakfast juice | Yes | Free |
| Bottled water | No | About US$3–5 |
| Fountain soda | No, except on Disney | About US$3–4 a glass |
| Latte or cappuccino | No | About US$4–6 |
| Fresh-squeezed juice or smoothie | No | About US$4–8 |
| Beer | No | About US$8–11 |
| Glass of wine | No | About US$10–15 |
| Cocktail | No | About US$12–16 |

Most lines add a service charge of about 18–20% to bar purchases, so a US$14 cocktail is really about US$16.50. Line-by-line package prices are in [cruise line policies compared](/articles/cruise-line-policies-compared/).

> **Quick math:** two specialty coffees, a soda and two cocktails a day comes to roughly US$45–50 per person with the service charge. That's about C$62–68 a day, or roughly C$430–480 each over a 7-night cruise. Compare that with the drinks package price for your line before deciding.

## How lines differ
Premium bundles change the picture. Some fares on Celebrity, Princess, Holland America and Norwegian include a drinks package, and luxury lines often include house wine and spirits as standard. Disney includes fountain soda but sells alcohol separately. MSC sells drinks packages heavily, so expect the offer at boarding.

[What "all-inclusive" means on a cruise](/articles/what-does-all-inclusive-mean-cruise/) explains how these bundles work, since the label isn't used consistently.

## Can you bring your own?
Partly. Most lines allow a small amount of wine at embarkation, and some allow a limited number of sealed cans or cartons of soft drinks or water. Beer and spirits are banned on almost every line. The rules, which differ more than you'd expect, are in [cruise line alcohol policies compared](/articles/cruise-line-alcohol-policies-compared/) and [laundry, alcohol and water rules](/articles/cruise-laundry-cost-alcohol-water/).

## Check on day one
Ask at the buffet or read the app on embarkation afternoon to confirm what's free on your ship. Coffee shops on some ships include pastries but charge for drinks, and a few venues pour juice that's free at breakfast but charged at the bar later. A quick check prevents small surprises on the final bill.

## Know your baseline before buying a package
If free coffee, tea and water cover most of your day, a package is unlikely to pay off. If you drink soda, specialty coffee and a few cocktails daily, it might. [Are drink packages worth it on your first cruise](/articles/drink-packages-worth-it-first-cruise/) walks through the break-even point.
