MSC allows smoking and vaping, but only in a handful of marked places: outdoor smoking areas, indoor smoking spaces on some ships, and part of the casino. Your cabin and balcony are off-limits, and the penalties for ignoring that are steep.

## Where you can smoke on an MSC ship
- **Outdoor smoking areas.** Usually a section of an open deck, often near a bar. Signs and ashtrays define the space.
- **Indoor smoking spaces.** Some ships have an indoor smoking area or cigar lounge. Not every ship does.
- **The casino.** Most MSC ships allow smoking in a designated section of the casino.
- **Ocean Cay.** On MSC's private island in the Bahamas, smoking is limited to marked areas.

The MSC app and your ship's deck plan show the exact spots. They differ between World, Meraviglia and Seaside class ships, so don't assume your last MSC cruise applies.

## Where smoking and vaping are banned
Cabins and balconies, without exception, along with restaurants, theatres, bars outside the smoking zones, and children's areas. The balcony ban is about fire: a cigarette end blown back onto a lower deck is a real hazard at sea.

### Vaping counts as smoking
MSC treats e-cigarettes and vapes the same way as cigarettes, so they can only be used where smoking is allowed. Vaping on a balcony is treated like smoking on one. Bring devices in your carry-on, as lithium batteries shouldn't go in checked bags on your flight.

## What happens if you break the rules
MSC usually starts with a warning. Smoking in a cabin can bring a cleaning charge, which can run to several hundred dollars, posted to your onboard account. Repeat offences can lead to being put ashore at the next port at your own cost, which travel insurance won't normally cover.

> **Example:** a Halifax couple flying to Port Canaveral for a 7-night MSC Grandiosa sailing, one a smoker, should find the nearest outdoor smoking area on the deck plan before booking a cabin. Choosing a balcony on the same side and a few decks down makes the walk easy without sitting right below the smoke.

## Who notices MSC's smoking rules most
MSC's European roots show here. Its international guest mix includes more smokers than on many North American lines, especially on Mediterranean sailings, so the outdoor smoking areas can be busy and non-smokers may notice smoke around them. If that bothers you, avoid sun loungers downwind of the marked area and choose the non-smoking side of the casino.

## Cannabis and duty-free tobacco
Cannabis is banned on MSC ships and in most ports, even though it is legal at home in Canada. [Why you can't bring cannabis on a cruise](/articles/cruise-cannabis-canada-why-no/) explains the legal side.

If you buy duty-free cigarettes on board or ashore, remember that the CBSA personal exemption is 200 cigarettes or 50 cigars, and tobacco not stamped "duty paid Canada" can still attract a special duty at the border. The ship's prices may not be the bargain they look. [Onboard duty-free shopping](/articles/cruise-shopping-onboard-duty-free/) covers what to watch for.

## How MSC compares
| Line | Smoking in the casino | Cabins and balconies |
|---|---|---|
| **MSC** | Designated section on most ships | Banned |
| [Carnival](/articles/carnival-smoking-vaping-policy/) | Designated section on most ships | Banned |
| [Norwegian](/articles/norwegian-smoking-vaping-policy/) | Designated section on most ships; tighter on newer ships | Banned |

All three ban smoking in cabins and on balconies. MSC, Carnival and Norwegian also keep a smoking section in most casinos, though Norwegian is tighter on newer ships. Where MSC differs is the crowd: more smokers on board, particularly on European itineraries. ## Knowing the map before you sail
Smoking areas vary more by ship than by line on MSC. Smokers and non-smokers alike should check the deck plan for their ship before choosing a cabin.
