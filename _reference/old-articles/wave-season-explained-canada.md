Wave season is the stretch from about January to March when cruise lines run their biggest promotions of the year. For Canadians it lines up neatly with the coldest weeks, when many people are planning summer and next winter's getaways.

## Why the biggest sale happens in winter
After the holidays, travellers start planning, and cruise lines know a large share of the year's bookings are made in the first few months. They respond with their strongest offers, and the whole industry refers to the period as wave season. Black Friday in late November has become a second big moment, compared in [are Black Friday cruise deals real](/articles/black-friday-cruise-deals-real/).

## What wave season promotions look like
The headline is usually value rather than a slashed price. Common offers include:
- Onboard credit attached to the booking
- Fares that include drinks, wifi, gratuities or specialty dining
- Reduced or low deposits, making it cheap to commit early
- Discounts on the second guest, or free or reduced fares for third and fourth guests
- Cabin upgrades or bonus perks for loyalty members

Raw fares do fall on some sailings, especially ones that are filling slowly. But the typical pattern is the same fare with more attached. [Cruise line sales decoded](/articles/cruise-line-sales-decoded/) explains how to read the percentages.

> **Quick math:** a fare that includes drinks and wifi sounds generous, but it only saves you money if you would have bought them. A couple who would have bought one wifi package and the odd glass of wine might get US$300 of real value from a bundle advertised as worth US$1,500.

## A Canadian timeline for 2027 sailings
| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| October–December 2026 | Shortlist routes, months and two or three sailings, and note their prices as a baseline |
| Late November | Check Black Friday offers against your baseline |
| January–March 2027 | Compare wave season offers, using totals, and book or reprice |
| April–May 2027 | Choice thins for summer Alaska and Christmas sailings |
| Before final payment | Keep checking, and reprice if a better promotion appears |

## The Canadian playbook
1. **Price a baseline in the fall.** Write down the total for your preferred cabin on your shortlisted sailings, taxes included.
2. **Compare offers in totals.** In January, price the same cabins again and add up the value of extras you would actually use.
3. **Book on a refundable fare.** That keeps the option of moving to a later promotion.
4. **Reprice if something better appears.** Bookings before final payment can usually adopt a new offer, as explained in [price drops after booking](/articles/price-drop-after-booking-cruise/).
5. **Book flights early for peak dates.** Alaska in July, March break and Christmas fill flights from Calgary, Edmonton and Toronto as fast as cabins.

> **Canadian tip:** wave season is also when many Canadian families lock in next year's March break cruise. If your dates are fixed by the school calendar, the best cabins rarely wait for a better offer, and our guide to [booking 2027 now or waiting](/articles/booking-2027-now-vs-waiting/) covers that trade-off.

## Who benefits most
Wave season works best for people who are flexible about which promotion they take but fixed on the trip they want: summer Alaska from Vancouver, a Caribbean week in February or a Mediterranean sailing in spring. It matters less for last-minute bookers, who are playing a different game, and for very flexible travellers who can wait for unsold cabins closer to sailing, covered in [last-minute cruise deals from Canada](/articles/last-minute-cruise-deals-canada/).

### What wave season will not do
It will not make a sold-out sailing appear, discount taxes and port fees, or guarantee the lowest price of the year. For some sailings, prices fall later; for popular ones, they only rise. If Alaska is your target, see [when Alaska cruise prices drop](/articles/when-do-alaska-cruise-prices-drop/) for the pattern.

## Watching the offers
Wave season promotions can change week to week across a dozen lines, and the best ones sometimes run for only a few days. Having your baseline ready in the fall, and someone watching your booking until final payment, is what turns the noise into savings.
