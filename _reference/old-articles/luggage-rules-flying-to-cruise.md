faq1q: Do cruise lines limit how much luggage you can bring?
faq1a: Cruise lines rarely set strict limits. In practice you are limited by your cabin's storage and by what the airline lets you fly with, not by the ship. Porters take bags at the terminal and deliver them to your cabin.
faq2q: What should you pack in your carry-on for a cruise?
faq2a: Passports and travel documents, all medication, valuables, chargers and power banks, and one change of clothes including a warm layer. Checked bags can reach your cabin hours after boarding, or a day or more late if the airline misplaces them.
faq3q: Can you pack wine in your checked bag for a cruise?
faq3a: For the flight, yes: wine has to go in checked luggage because of the 100 mL carry-on limit. But most cruise lines require your wine allowance to be in your carry-on when you board the ship, so move the bottles into a carry-on bag after you collect your luggage. Leave hard liquor at home.

The airline decides what comes with you, not the cruise line. Pack to your fare's carry-on and checked-bag limits, hand your checked bags to the porters at the terminal, and keep anything you can't do without in the bag you carry yourself.

## What Air Canada and WestJet allow
At recent prices and rules for domestic economy fares. Both airlines change fees and fare rules often, so confirm on the day you book.

| | Air Canada | WestJet |
|---|---|---|
| Carry-on size | 55 × 40 × 23 cm | 53 × 38 × 23 cm |
| Personal item | Yes, small bag under the seat | Yes, small bag under the seat |
| First checked bag | About C$35 | About C$40 prepaid, more at the airport |
| Checked bag limit | 23 kg, 158 cm total dimensions | 23 kg, 158 cm total dimensions |
| Cheapest basic fares | No free checked bag; check carry-on rules | Higher bag fees on the lowest fare; check carry-on rules |

Overweight bags between 23 and 32 kg carry a steep extra fee each way, so weigh your suitcase at home. Ultra-low-cost carriers charge for almost everything beyond a small personal item, which is worth factoring in when comparing fares (see [the cheapest way from Edmonton to the cruise port](/articles/edmonton-to-vancouver-cruise-port-cheapest/)).

## What happens to your bags at the terminal
At Canada Place and the Florida ports, porters take checked luggage at the curb, and it is delivered to your cabin door, usually during the afternoon or early evening. Attach the cruise line's luggage tags, printed from your boarding documents, before you leave home. Tipping the porters a few dollars a bag is customary.

## The cruise-day carry-on rule
If an airline misroutes a checked bag, it may catch up with you at the first port, which on an Alaska sailing could be two days away. Pack your carry-on as if you might live out of it until then:

- passports, boarding documents and travel insurance details
- all medication, in original containers
- spare lithium batteries and power banks, which airlines don't allow in checked bags
- chargers, glasses and valuables
- a change of clothes, a warm layer and swimwear

If a bag does go missing, report it to the airline before leaving the airport, then tell guest services on board. The next steps are in [lost or delayed luggage on a cruise](/articles/lost-delayed-luggage-cruise/).

> **Watch out:** a bottle of spirits bought at the airport duty-free shop will normally be taken by ship security and returned on the last night. Wine within your line's allowance is fine: fly with it in checked luggage (carry-on liquids are limited to 100 mL), then move it into your carry-on before you board, since most lines only accept wine brought on by hand.

## Packing Alaska into 23 kg
Alaska packing is bulky: layers, a rain shell, decent walking shoes and something dressier for the evenings. A few tricks keep you under the limit:

- **Wear the heaviest items on the plane,** such as boots and your warmest jacket.
- **Skip the suit bag.** One smart-casual outfit covers most ships' dressier nights; see [what people actually wear in Alaska](/articles/alaska-cruise-dress-code/).
- **Plan a laundry load.** Most ships have paid laundry service and many have self-serve machines (see [self-service laundry on board](/articles/cruise-ship-laundry-self-service-walkthrough/)).
- **Use packing cubes** to compress layers and keep the cabin tidy; [packing cubes for a cruise](/articles/packing-cubes-luggage-organization-cruise/) has a system.

## Coming home with more than you left with
Leave room for souvenirs, and remember the return flight has the same limits. After more than 48 hours away, each Canadian can bring back up to C$800 of goods duty-free, with separate limits on alcohol and tobacco; [cruise duty-free limits for Canadians](/articles/cruise-shopping-onboard-duty-free/) explains what counts.
