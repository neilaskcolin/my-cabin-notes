faq1q: How cold is it on an Alaska cruise deck?
faq1a: Near glaciers, wind chill on deck commonly feels around 0–5°C even when the port towns feel mild, and early May can dip below freezing. Dress for the deck, not the port forecast.
faq2q: Do you need rubber boots for an Alaska cruise?
faq2a: No. Waterproof walking shoes or light hikers cover ports and most excursions. Specialist tours that need boots, such as some glacier walks or bear-viewing trips, typically provide them.
faq3q: Do I need a winter coat for an Alaska cruise in May?
faq3a: No. A fleece or light down jacket under a waterproof shell is warmer and more flexible than a parka. Add a second mid-layer and heavier gloves for glacier mornings in May.

May and September bracket the Alaska season with its best prices, and both reward a well-packed bag. The core kit is identical; what changes is whether you prioritize warmth or waterproofing. Most Canadians already own nearly all of it.

## The core system for both months
- **Base layers:** two sets of merino or synthetic tops, plus one pair of thermal leggings.
- **Mid layer:** a fleece or light down jacket. A second thin fleece gives you options.
- **Shell:** a genuinely waterproof jacket with a hood. This is the trip's most important item.
- **Bottoms:** quick-dry or hiking trousers and one pair of jeans for evenings.
- **Feet:** waterproof walking shoes, broken in, plus wool socks.
- **Extremities:** toque, gloves and a neck warmer. Deck wind finds every gap.
- **Extras:** binoculars, sunglasses, sunscreen, lip balm and a small day pack.

## May vs September: what to add
| | May | September |
|---|---|---|
| Typical port highs | about 10–14°C | about 10–14°C, cooling through the month |
| Main challenge | Cold, especially near ice | Rain, plus shorter days |
| Add for warmth | Heavier gloves, a second mid-layer | One warm layer for dark evenings on deck |
| Add for wet | Shell is usually enough | Rain trousers, waterproof gloves |
| Eyes and skin | Good sunglasses for snow glare; sunscreen | Sunscreen still, though there is less glare |
| Gear care | Spare camera batteries, which drain in the cold | A dry bag for the camera and phone |
| Footwear | Waterproof shoes | Waterproof shoes plus a second pair while one dries |
| Nice to have | Hand warmers for glacier mornings | A headlamp or small light for aurora watching |

### Why May feels colder
Snow sits low on the mountains, mornings are crisp and the air flowing off glaciers is cold. On a clear May glacier morning the deck can feel well below freezing in the wind, which is also when the views are sharpest. See [how cold it really is, port by port](/articles/alaska-cruise-temperature-guide-by-port/).

### Why September feels wetter
Rain becomes more frequent as the month goes on, and evenings get dark much earlier than in summer. Clear nights are your best chance for the [northern lights](/articles/northern-lights-alaska-cruise/), so pack enough warm layers to stand outside after dark. For the full seasonal picture, see [Alaska weather month by month](/articles/alaska-cruise-weather-by-month/).

> **Canadian tip:** you don't need to buy special gear. The outfit you would wear for a cool, wet October walk in Vancouver or a crisp spring morning in Calgary, a fleece, a rain shell, a toque and gloves, is exactly right.

## Evening clothes
Dinners are mostly smart casual. One or two outfits that clear "no shorts or ball caps" plus one dressier piece cover the whole week. [The Alaska dress code](/articles/alaska-cruise-dress-code/) has the detail for formal nights.

## What stays home
- Umbrellas: the wind on deck wrecks them.
- A heavy winter parka and snow boots: layers do the job better.
- Several pairs of shoes: two pairs, plus sandals for the cabin, are enough.
- "Just in case" extras: ships have laundry, and your airline's bag allowance is the real limit.

Flying on a basic economy fare from Toronto, Montreal or Winnipeg may mean a fee for every checked bag, so packing light pays off. [Luggage rules for flying to a cruise](/articles/luggage-rules-flying-to-cruise/) covers the airline side.

## A final check before you zip it up
Lay everything out by layer and check you could wear it all at once on a cold glacier morning. Then wear your bulkiest layers on the plane, keep the rain shell in your carry-on for embarkation day, and pack a spare pair of socks in your day bag for every port. Families can add the kid-specific items in [packing for kids on an Alaska cruise](/articles/alaska-cruise-packing-for-kids-specifically/).
