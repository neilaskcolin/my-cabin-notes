A cruise makes an easy honeymoon: one booking covers your room, meals and a different view every day. A few details at booking time make it feel like a celebration rather than just another sailing, and one of them can stop you boarding if you get it wrong.

## The names on the booking
This is the honeymoon detail that matters most. The names on your cruise booking and flights must match the passports you travel with.

> **Canadian tip:** if your passport is in your birth name, book in your birth name, even if you plan to change it. Applying for a new passport just before the trip means surrendering the old one and waiting on processing times. In Quebec, spouses keep their birth names by law, so there is nothing to change.

[Passports, NEXUS and ID for cruising](/articles/passports-nexus-id-cruise-documents/) covers what you need for each type of itinerary.

## What to flag when you book
1. **The occasion.** Ask for the honeymoon to be noted on the reservation, with your wedding date.
2. **Bed configuration.** Most cabins can be set as a queen or as two twins. Ask for the queen.
3. **Dining.** Request a table for two if you choose fixed dining. These are limited, so ask early, or pick flexible dining where tables for two are easier to get.
4. **Any celebration package.** Order decorations or champagne in advance, so they are in the cabin when you arrive.
5. **Dietary needs.** Note them now, especially if you plan specialty dinners.

## Free touches vs paid packages
Set expectations realistically. What lines give for free varies a lot, and some give nothing beyond a note on your file.

- **Sometimes free:** a card, a dessert or a mention at dinner.
- **Usually paid:** balloon or rose-petal cabin decorations, champagne and chocolate-covered strawberries, a photo session, a couples spa treatment, a specialty dinner.
- **Often better value à la carte:** if you only want one or two items, compare buying them separately against the bundled package.

Many lines let friends and family buy gifts that are added to your booking, such as onboard credit or spa treatments. If guests ask what you want, onboard credit is one of the most useful gifts. [Couples spa experiences at sea](/articles/couples-spa-experiences-cruise/) and [whether photo packages are worth it](/articles/cruise-photography-packages-worth-it/) help you decide which extras deserve the money.

## Choosing your cabin
A balcony gives you private outdoor space for breakfast, sunsets and quiet evenings, which suits a honeymoon well. It matters most on scenic itineraries. If budget is tight, an oceanview or inside cabin still works, since you will spend little time in it. [How to pick your first cruise cabin](/articles/pick-first-cruise-cabin/) explains the trade-offs, and a suite is the splurge if you want extra space and priority perks.

## Matching the itinerary to your wedding date
### Summer weddings
Canada's wedding season peaks in summer, which lines up with Alaska from Vancouver and the Mediterranean. Alaska suits couples who love scenery and the outdoors; [the most romantic Alaska cruise moments](/articles/best-alaska-cruise-proposal-engagement/) gives ideas.

### Autumn and winter weddings
The Caribbean is at its best from December to April, and the [Caribbean cost guide](/articles/caribbean-cruise-cost-canada-budget/) shows what to budget from Canada. Avoid the late-summer and early-autumn hurricane peak if you can.

### For a quieter atmosphere
Ships with [adults-only areas](/articles/adults-only-areas-cruise-ships/), or sailings outside school holidays, mean fewer families and calmer pools.

## A honeymoon that feels special
Get the names right, flag the occasion and your table for two, and choose the extras you will actually use.
