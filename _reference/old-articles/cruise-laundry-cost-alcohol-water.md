Yes, every cruise ship can wash your clothes, which means you can pack for about half the trip and do one laundry round midway. That's especially useful for fly-cruisers juggling airline baggage limits, or anyone packing bulky layers for Alaska. Here's what it costs, plus the rules on bringing your own wine and water.

## What are your laundry options on a cruise?

| Option | Typical cost (recent prices) | Where you'll find it |
|---|---|---|
| Per-item laundry service | About US$3–8 per item | Every line |
| "Fill the bag" flat rate | About US$25–40 per bag | Most lines, often promoted mid-cruise |
| Pressing only | A few dollars per item | Most lines |
| Self-service launderette | A few dollars per wash or dry | Most Carnival, Princess, Holland America and Disney ships |
| Free laundry as a perk | Included | Some suites and top loyalty tiers |

Royal Caribbean, Celebrity and Norwegian generally don't offer self-service laundry on their ships, so plan on the bag deal there. Prices are charged to your onboard account in US dollars.

### How the bag deal works
Your cabin attendant leaves a laundry bag and a form. Fill the bag with as much as fits, tick the bag-deal box, and leave it out in the morning. It usually comes back folded within a day or two. Many ships run the best bag offer on a particular day, often around the middle of the cruise, so check the daily programme.

### Using a self-service launderette
Launderettes sit on cabin decks and get busy on sea days and the last two days. Go early in the morning or during dinner. Machines take your cruise card or tokens, and detergent is either provided or sold nearby. The [self-service laundry walkthrough](/articles/cruise-ship-laundry-self-service-walkthrough/) covers the process step by step.

## A packing plan built around laundry
- Pack for four or five days on a 7- to 10-night cruise.
- Book one laundry round, ideally on a sea day.
- Choose quick-dry, wrinkle-resistant fabrics.
- Bring a few clothes pegs or a travel line for swimwear in the shower.
- Leave the iron and steamer at home.

> **Watch out:** irons and clothes steamers are confiscated at security on virtually every line because of fire risk. Use the pressing service for a formal night outfit instead. [What not to pack for a cruise](/articles/what-not-to-pack-for-a-cruise/) lists the other common confiscations.

## Can you bring your own wine?
Most lines allow a small amount of wine or champagne at embarkation, carried in your hand luggage. Beer and spirits are banned on almost every line, and anything over the limit is held until the last night. On most lines, drinking your own wine in your cabin is free and a corkage fee applies in a restaurant or bar; Norwegian charges corkage on every bottle as it comes aboard.

## Can you bring water and soft drinks?
Tap water on board is safe and free, so most people don't need to bring any. If you prefer bottled water, some lines allow a limited number of sealed cans or cartons of non-alcoholic drinks at embarkation, often around a dozen. Others restrict it or ban it. Check your line's current rules before hauling a case to the terminal, and see [what drinks are included in your fare](/articles/cruise-drink-menu-what-included-basics/) for what you'll get free on board.

## Alcohol rules by cruise line
Wine allowances and corkage fees differ more than most people expect: MSC's is the strictest, Norwegian sets no fixed limit but charges corkage on every bottle, and Disney's, once the most generous, was cut in June 2026 to one bottle of wine or six beers per adult.
