Hoping to book a sitter to stay in the cabin while you go to dinner? On mainstream cruise lines, that service doesn't exist. Ships run childcare as supervised group programmes, and the good news is that the group options cover most of what a babysitter would.

## Why there's no in-cabin babysitting
A ship is not a resort hotel with a list of local sitters. Crew work set roles and long hours, and lines don't let them take private childcare jobs. Insurance and safeguarding rules push childcare into staffed, purpose-built spaces with sign-in, sign-out and trained youth staff.

## What each line offers instead
Here's the picture at recent check. Ages and fees vary by ship, so confirm for your sailing.

| Line | Under 3s | Late-night care |
|---|---|---|
| Disney | Paid nursery, 6 months to 3 years, every ship | Kids club runs late at no charge |
| Royal Caribbean | Paid Royal Babies & Tots nursery, 6–36 months, many ships | Paid late-night party session |
| Carnival | No nursery, but Camp Ocean starts at age 2 | Paid Night Owls service for younger kids |
| Norwegian | Guppies, 6 months to under 3, parent stays | Paid late-night fun zone |
| MSC | Free parent-stay Baby Time, plus paid Baby Care drop-off from 6 months on many ships | Late sessions on some ships; ask about charges |
| Princess, Celebrity, Holland America | No drop-off care under 3 | Varies, limited on some ships |

The per-line detail is on pages like [Disney kids club ages](/articles/disney-kids-club-ages/) and [Royal Caribbean kids club ages](/articles/royal-caribbean-kids-club-ages/), and the overview is in [kids clubs on cruise ships](/articles/kids-clubs-cruise-ships-ages-costs/).

## How late-night group care works
This is the closest thing to a babysitter. After the regular kids club closes, younger children stay on in a quieter session with films, games and sometimes sleeping mats. Fees are usually charged per child per hour, roughly US$7–12 at recent prices, and some lines charge late pick-up fees.

> **Watch out:** nursery places on popular sailings, such as March Break and Christmas, can fill up. Where the line allows advance booking, reserve on the first day or before you sail.

## Planning a parents' night out with young kids
- **Book dinner early in the evening** and collect the kids before the late session's last hour.
- **Ask about pagers or the app.** Most lines contact parents through the ship's app or a phone if a child needs them.
- **Do a trial run** on a sea day afternoon so the first drop-off isn't at 9pm.
- **Check toilet-training rules.** Many clubs require children to be out of nappies for drop-off.
- **Consider a balcony or connecting cabin** so the evening can continue quietly after bedtime (see [connecting cabins for families](/articles/connecting-cabins-one-big-cabin-families/)).

If you're travelling with a baby, [cruising with a baby or toddler](/articles/cruising-with-baby-toddler/) covers cots, formula and age minimums. Grandparents along for the trip can be the in-cabin sitter, which is how many families solve it, as covered in [multigenerational cruise planning](/articles/multigenerational-cruise-planning-cabins/).

## Planning realistic childcare expectations for your cruise
If evenings alone matter to you, choose the line by its nursery and late-night care before you choose by price. A family with a toddler will get a very different week on Disney or Royal Caribbean than on a line where care starts at 3.
