Yes, you can bring a CPAP on Royal Caribbean, and plenty of guests do on every sailing. The line supplies an approved extension cord for medical devices and can usually arrange distilled water, so the job is mostly telling the Access Department in advance and packing the machine in your carry-on.

## How to set up your CPAP for a Royal Caribbean cruise
1. **Notify the Access Department after booking.** Royal Caribbean handles medical equipment through this team. Ask for the CPAP, the extension cord and distilled water to be noted on your reservation.
2. **Check your ship's cabin layout.** Look at photos of your exact cabin category to see where the outlets sit relative to the bed.
3. **Confirm the water arrangement.** Ask whether it comes via your stateroom attendant or must be pre-arranged, and whether there is a charge.
4. **Pack the machine in your carry-on,** with mask, tubing and a spare filter.
5. **Bring a night's worth of water,** in case the ship's supply takes a few hours to reach you on embarkation day.
6. **Check the cord is in your cabin** when you arrive, and ask Guest Services the same afternoon if it isn't.

> **Good to know:** step 1 is the one that matters. A request noted weeks ahead is waiting in your cabin; one made at 10 p.m. on embarkation night depends on what the ship has left.

## Royal Caribbean cabins and CPAP outlets
### Newer ships
Royal Caribbean's fleet runs from Vision-class ships built in the late 1990s to the Icon class launched in 2024, and cabin wiring follows the age of the hull. On Oasis-, Quantum- and Icon-class ships many cabins have outlets or USB ports near the bed, so some guests never need the cord.

### Older ships
On Vision- and Radiance-class ships, and some Voyager-class cabins, the nearest socket is often at the vanity desk, a few metres from the bed. Radiance-class ships are common on Alaska sailings, so if you are sailing north, treat the cord request as essential. Cabins carry standard North American outlets, so a Canadian plug fits without an adapter.

## The extension cord rule
Royal Caribbean prohibits guests' own extension cords and power bars fleet-wide, as most lines do, and security can hold them until the end of the cruise. The cord the ship supplies is approved for the job. The wider rule is explained in [can you bring an extension cord on a cruise](/articles/extension-cord-cruise-ship-rules/).

## Distilled water
Supply varies by ship: some deliver it through the stateroom attendant, others want it arranged ahead. Medical liquids are generally allowed through terminal security in reasonable quantities. What each line provides is compared in [distilled water for a CPAP on a cruise ship](/articles/cpap-distilled-water-cruise-ships/).

> **Canadian tip:** Air Canada and WestJet generally let you carry a CPAP as a medical device on top of your normal carry-on allowance. Confirm when you book, and keep it out of checked bags on the flight to Fort Lauderdale or Seattle, since delayed luggage is a real risk, as covered in [lost or delayed luggage on a cruise](/articles/lost-delayed-luggage-cruise/).

## How Royal Caribbean compares
| Line | Desk to contact | Distilled water |
|---|---|---|
| **Royal Caribbean** | Access Department | On request, often via stateroom attendant |
| [Carnival](/articles/carnival-cpap-machine/) | Special Needs team | Paid; pre-order, buy on board or bring your own |
| [Norwegian](/articles/norwegian-cpap-machine/) | Access Desk | On request, delivered to stateroom |

Carnival and Norwegian are the lines most often weighed against Royal Caribbean. All three welcome CPAP machines. Royal Caribbean and Norwegian supply an approved extension cord, while Carnival lets you bring your own plain cord without a surge protector; otherwise the difference is who you contact and how water reaches your cabin. On Royal Caribbean, the bigger variable is the age of your ship. The full side-by-side for all eight lines is in [Can You Bring a CPAP Machine on a Cruise Ship? ## Getting it noted on the booking properly
One message to the Access Department, or to whoever holds your booking, covers the cord, the water and a note on your cabin. Send it soon after you book, and keep the reply with your travel documents.
