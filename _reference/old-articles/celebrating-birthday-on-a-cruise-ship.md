A birthday at sea mostly takes two things: telling the line in advance, and deciding whether a free acknowledgment is enough or you want to pay for a cake, decorations or a special dinner. Here's how to get it right without overspending.

## How to make sure the ship knows
1. **Add it at booking.** Ask for the occasion to be noted on the reservation, the same way you would for an anniversary or honeymoon (see [what to flag when booking a honeymoon cruise](/articles/booking-cruise-honeymoon-what-to-flag/)).
2. **Check it during online check-in.** Many lines have a special-occasion field. Confirm the date of birth on the booking is correct too.
3. **Tell the dining room on day one.** If you have fixed seating, let your head waiter know which night. With flexible dining, mention it when you book the table.
4. **Order paid extras before sailing.** Cakes and cabin packages can usually be added on the line's website or app up to a few days out.

## What's free and what costs extra
Free gestures vary by line and change over time. At most, expect a song from the waiters, sometimes a small dessert, or a birthday card in the cabin. Don't count on a full cake.

Paid options typically include:

- **Celebration cakes:** roughly US$25–50, delivered to your table or cabin.
- **Cabin decoration packages:** balloons, banners and sometimes sparkling wine, from roughly US$50 to well over US$150.
- **A specialty restaurant dinner:** often the real treat. Book early, because popular nights fill up. [Main dining, buffet and specialty restaurants](/articles/main-dining-buffet-specialty-restaurants/) explains what each costs.
- **A photo session:** many ships sell portrait sessions, covered in [are cruise photo packages worth it](/articles/cruise-photography-packages-worth-it/).

All of these charge to your onboard account in US dollars, so a C$70 cake is closer to the real figure once converted.

> **Good to know:** the drinking age on most lines is 21 on North American sailings, including Alaska cruises from Vancouver. A 19th or 20th birthday that would be legal at home in Canada won't get a toast on board.

## Picking the right day
If the birthday falls on a port day with a long excursion, consider celebrating the night before or on a sea day instead. An Alaska glacier day or a quiet sea day makes a relaxed setting for a long dinner. On a Caribbean sailing, a sunset sail-away from a port can stand in for the party.

## Birthdays in a group
For family groups, ask the dining room to seat everyone together on the night and bring out the cake at the end. Matching shirts and door signs are popular, and most lines are relaxed about them as long as nothing damages the cabin. For 20 or more guests, private event spaces and group bookings come into play, covered in [organising a milestone birthday cruise for 20 people](/articles/milestone-birthday-cruise-20-people/).

## The short version
Flag the date twice, once at booking and again at check-in, then decide on one paid treat that matters to you, whether that's a cake, a specialty dinner or a decorated door. That combination makes a birthday feel marked without inflating the onboard bill.
