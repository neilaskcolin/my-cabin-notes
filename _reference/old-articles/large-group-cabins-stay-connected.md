For a large group, being "connected" on a cruise rarely means connecting doors. It means a run of cabins along one corridor, on one deck, picked by number when the group block is created. Cluster everyone first, then decide which households need an actual door between rooms.

## Connecting, adjoining or just close?
- **Connecting cabins** have an inside door between two rooms. Ideal for parents with young children next door, but there are few of them and they sell early. See [connecting cabins vs one big cabin](/articles/connecting-cabins-one-big-cabin-families/).
- **Adjoining or across-the-hall cabins** share a wall or face each other with no door between. Easy to get in a group block and nearly as convenient.
- **Clustered cabins** sit in the same stretch of corridor. This is what most large groups actually want.
- **Opened balcony dividers:** on some ships, the steward can unlock the partition between neighbouring balconies when both cabins ask. Treat this as a bonus, not a promise.

## Where on the ship should a big group stay?
### Choose a corridor, not just a category
On many ships, inside cabins line the inner side of the same corridors as balcony cabins. That makes insides and balconies the easiest mix to cluster. Suites tend to sit high or aft, and oceanview cabins are often on lower decks, so a group mixing those may end up split.

### Mid-ship, near but not beside the lifts
Mid-ship cabins feel the least motion and are a short walk from both ends of the ship. Cabins a few doors from an elevator lobby suit grandparents without the late-night lift traffic outside the door.

### Steer clear of noisy spots
Avoid cabins under the pool deck, buffet or theatre, above the nightclub, or beside crew service doors. A group of 20 stuck under a jogging track means a week of complaints. [Quietest cabins on a cruise ship](/articles/quietest-cabins-where-to-book/) lists what to check on the deck plan.

## How to get your cabins clustered
1. **Open the group block before anyone books.** Cabins booked separately can often be added to a group later, but they stay wherever they were assigned.
2. **Pick cabin numbers from the deck plan** when the block is created. Skip guarantee fares, which can put a household anywhere on the ship; see [what a guarantee cabin is](/articles/guarantee-cabin-should-you-book/).
3. **Hold one or two spare cabins** in the same stretch if the block allows, and release them before the release date.
4. **Put requests in writing,** naming which households need connecting doors or accessible cabins.
5. **Recheck the cabin list after final payment** in case anyone has been moved.

> **Watch out:** a family that books the cheapest guarantee fare to save US$100 (about C$140) can end up four decks and a ship's length from everyone else. For a group, choosing the actual cabin is usually worth the small premium.

## Staying in touch on board
Clustering solves the evenings. During the day, the group spreads across a ship the size of a small town.
- **Cruise line apps** on most big lines include onboard messaging over the ship's network, sometimes free and sometimes for a small per-device fee. [Using the cruise line app](/articles/using-cruise-line-app-before-during-trip/) explains the setup.
- **One fixed meeting point,** such as a lounge at 5 pm daily, saves dozens of messages.
- **A printed cabin list** with everyone's numbers, taped inside each cabin door.
- **Two-way radios** work for some families; keep them quiet in public areas.

> **Canadian tip:** don't rely on a Canadian mobile plan to text the group. At sea, phones can connect to the ship's maritime network, which many roaming add-ons exclude and which can cost several dollars a minute or megabyte. Switch off data roaming and use the app or ship wifi, as explained in [cruise wifi and roaming for Canadians](/articles/cruise-wifi-phone-roaming-canadians/).

## When a suite or connecting pair is worth it
Inside the cluster, one suite can become the group's evening hub for pre-dinner drinks, and connecting pairs suit families with toddlers. Run the numbers in [two cabins vs one connecting suite](/articles/booking-two-cabins-vs-one-connecting-suite/) before anyone commits.
