A 7-night Mediterranean cruise usually costs a Canadian couple between C$5,500 and C$10,500 all-in in an inside cabin. The fare can look a lot like a Caribbean week, but the overnight flight to Europe, a hotel before sailing and pricier shore days roughly double it.

## What does a Mediterranean cruise cost in total?
Here is a per-couple budget for a 7-night sailing from Barcelona or Rome, at recent prices. Fares are usually priced in US dollars, so they are shown with a rough Canadian-dollar equivalent at about 1.37.

| Item, per couple | Inside cabin | Balcony cabin |
|---|---|---|
| Cruise fare, taxes included | US$1,400–2,200 (C$1,900–3,100) | US$2,400–4,000 (C$3,300–5,500) |
| Return flights from Canada | C$1,800–3,600 | C$1,800–3,600 |
| Two hotel nights before sailing | C$500–900 | C$500–900 |
| Airport and port transfers | C$100–250 | C$100–250 |
| Gratuities | C$300–400 | C$300–400 |
| Drinks without a package | C$300–600 | C$300–600 |
| Shore excursions or tours | C$400–1,200 | C$400–1,200 |
| Travel insurance | C$200–500 | C$200–500 |
| **Rough total** | **C$5,500–10,500** | **C$7,000–13,000** |

> **Example:** a Toronto couple who book an inside cabin for early May, fly a nonstop on a seat sale and spend one night in Barcelona can come in near C$6,000. The same couple in a July balcony, flying from Calgary with a connection, adding three nights in Rome and a guided Vatican tour, can pass C$12,000.

## The fare itself
Inside cabins on a standard 7-night Mediterranean sailing run roughly US$700–1,100 per person, and balconies about US$1,200–2,000. A balcony earns its keep here more than in the Caribbean, because arrivals into places like Santorini, Dubrovnik or the Amalfi coast are part of the show.

Large-ship lines such as MSC, Norwegian, Royal Caribbean and Celebrity often have the lowest headline fares. On European sailings some lines run the onboard account in euros rather than US dollars, so check which currency your bill will be in.

## The flight: the biggest extra line
Return flights to Barcelona, Rome or Athens typically cost C$900–1,800 per person, and in peak summer that single line can exceed the cruise fare.

### Flying from your city
- **Toronto and Montreal:** the most nonstop choice, mostly overnight, with several routes seasonal.
- **Calgary, Edmonton and Winnipeg:** expect one connection, often through Toronto, Montreal or a European hub.
- **Vancouver:** a long day either way, usually via a European hub or eastern Canada.

Cruises billed as "Venice" often start from nearby ports such as Trieste or Ravenna now that large ships face restrictions in central Venice, so check the actual port before booking flights. Rome's cruise port is Civitavecchia, roughly an hour or more from the city, and Athens uses Piraeus.

> **Canadian tip:** fly into the embarkation city at least a day early. A missed connection in Frankfurt or London is far harder to recover from than a late flight to Vancouver, as explained in [what happens if your flight is delayed before a cruise](/articles/cruise-flight-delay-what-happens/).

## Pre- and post-cruise stays
Most Canadians add two to four days in Barcelona, Rome or Athens. It beats the jet lag and makes a long, expensive trip feel worth it. Budget around C$250–450 per night for a central hotel in summer, less in the shoulder months.

## Onboard spending and shore days
Gratuities run about US$16–20 per person per day depending on the line, or roughly C$300–400 per couple for the week. Drinks add C$300–600 without a package.

Shore days are where Mediterranean budgets differ most. Ship tours to Rome or Florence often cost US$100–200 per person because of the long drive from port. Barcelona, Palma, Valletta and Kotor are easy to walk on your own. ## How to keep the Mediterranean affordable
1. Sail in April, May or October instead of July and August.
2. Price flights first, since they can swing the total more than the cabin.
3. Choose an itinerary that starts where your best flight lands.
4. Pay for one or two guided days and do the rest independently.
5. Use a credit card with no foreign transaction fee for euros ashore and the onboard bill.

Check entry rules before you go: the EU has introduced a new entry-exit system and plans a travel authorization for visa-free visitors, so confirm current requirements for Canadians on official government sites.
