Princess's youth programme, Camp Discovery, starts at age 3 and splits children into three groups up to 17. Daytime sessions are included in your fare. There is no nursery for babies and toddlers, which is the single biggest thing for parents of under-3s to know before booking.

## Princess kids club age groups
| Club | Ages | What it is like |
|---|---|---|
| The Treehouse | 3–7 | Games, crafts and supervised play; children must be toilet-trained |
| The Lodge | 8–12 | Science activities, sports, games and themed events |
| The Beach House | 13–17 | A teen hangout with organised activities and later evening hours |

If siblings straddle two groups, ask the youth staff on day one whether they can join some activities together. Exact spaces and layouts vary by ship, so check the deck plan for yours.

## What makes Princess different for kids
Princess has a partnership with Discovery, so activities lean educational: science experiments, nature themes and talks pitched at children. On Alaska sailings, kids can work through a Junior Ranger programme tied to the national parks they sail past, including Glacier Bay. That makes Princess a strong pick for Alaska family trips from Vancouver, where what children do in the club connects to what they see from the deck.

Princess usually carries fewer children than the big family-focused lines, so the ships feel calmer. There are no waterslides on most of the fleet, which suits families who want scenery over thrills but may disappoint some 8 to 12 year olds. For a sense of which ages get the most from the trip, see [Alaska cruise with kids: what ages work](/articles/alaska-cruise-with-kids-ages/).

## Babies and toddlers on Princess
There is no nursery or drop-off care for under-3s on most ships, so parents look after the youngest themselves. Ask on board whether any parent-accompanied play times run in The Treehouse.

Infants generally need to be at least six months old to sail, and a year old on some longer itineraries with several sea days in a row, such as Hawaii and transatlantic sailings. Confirm the rule for your cruise. The practicalities are covered in [cruising with a baby or toddler](/articles/cruising-with-baby-toddler/).

> **Watch out:** toddlers under 3, or older children who are not yet toilet-trained, cannot join The Treehouse. If you are counting on club time for a 2-year-old, Princess is the wrong line for that trip.

## Hours and the paid evening option
### Daytime
On sea days the clubs open for much of the day, with breaks around lunch and dinner. On port days they usually open once the ship is alongside, since many families head ashore together.

### Late night
On many ships Princess offers late-night group care for younger children, often called Night Owls, for a modest hourly fee. It is the easiest way for parents to have a late dinner at Crown Grill or Sabatini's. Check whether it runs on your ship and sailing.

## Tips for Canadian families
- Summer Alaska sailings line up with Canadian school holidays from late June to Labour Day, so expect busier clubs in July and August.
- Register on day one during the open house, when staff walk you through sign-in, pick-up and allergies.
- On MedallionClass ships, the Princess app can help you see where family members are on board, which is handy with teens.
- Pack warm layers for kids even on club days; deck time in Glacier Bay is cold. The [Alaska packing list for kids](/articles/alaska-cruise-packing-for-kids-specifically/) covers the details.
