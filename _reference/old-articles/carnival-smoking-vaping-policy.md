If you smoke or vape on Carnival, the rule is about where, not whether. Both are allowed only in marked outdoor areas and a designated part of the casino on most ships, and the penalty for lighting up in a cabin or on a balcony is steep: US$500 and possibly the end of your cruise.

## Where you can smoke on a Carnival ship
Carnival keeps smoking to a few clearly signed places:

- **Outdoor smoking areas:** usually one side or one section of an open deck, often near a bar. The HUB app's deck plans and the signs on deck show where they are on your ship.
- **The casino:** most Carnival ships set aside a section of the casino for smokers who are playing, with the rest of the room smoke-free.
- **Cigars and pipes:** only in the designated outdoor areas, never indoors.

Vapes and e-cigarettes follow exactly the same rules as cigarettes. A vape on a balcony is treated the same as a cigarette on a balcony.

### Smoke-free everywhere else
Cabins and balconies are smoke-free on every ship, along with restaurants, bars, lounges, theatres, Camp Ocean and teen areas. Balcony fires are the main reason the industry tightened up, as [smoking and vaping rules on cruise ships](/articles/smoking-vaping-rules-cruise-ships/) explains.

## The US$500 charge and getting put off the ship
Carnival takes cabin and balcony smoking more seriously than most guests expect. Each violation adds US$500, roughly C$675–700, to your Sail & Sign account, and paying it doesn't give you permission to carry on.

Repeat or serious cases can see everyone in the cabin disembarked at the next port. You pay your own way home, and the unused part of the cruise isn't refunded. Stateroom attendants notice the smell, and neighbours report it to Guest Services.

> **Watch out:** cannabis is legal in Canada but banned on Carnival in every form, including medical cannabis, and it remains illegal under US federal law, which applies in Carnival's home ports. Leave it at home. [Why cruise lines ban cannabis even for Canadians](/articles/cruise-cannabis-canada-why-no/) explains the federal US rules behind this.

## Who this affects most on Carnival
Smokers who like the tables find Carnival easier than lines that have gone fully smoke-free indoors, because most ships keep that casino section. The trade-off for non-smokers is that you may notice smoke near the casino entrance on some ships, so pick a route to the theatre that goes around it.

Carnival's private destinations, including Celebration Key and Half Moon Cay, also have designated smoking spots, so check the map when you go ashore. [Private island cruise stops](/articles/private-island-cruise-stops-explained/) describes what's there.

## Buying tobacco on board and bringing it home
Carnival's Fun Shops sell duty-free cigarettes, usually delivered to your cabin near the end of the cruise. Canadian residents back after 48 hours or more can bring in 200 cigarettes, 50 cigars, 200 grams of tobacco and 200 tobacco sticks duty-free. Anything beyond that is taxed, and tobacco duty in Canada is high. [Duty-free shopping on a cruise](/articles/cruise-shopping-onboard-duty-free/) has the full allowances.

> **Canadian tip:** the allowance is per person and only applies after 48 hours away. If you're driving home from Baltimore or New York, keep tobacco receipts together for the CBSA officer at the land crossing.

## How Carnival compares
| Line | Smoking in the casino | Cabins and balconies |
|---|---|---|
| **Carnival** | Designated section on most ships | Banned |
| [Royal Caribbean](/articles/royal-caribbean-smoking-vaping-policy/) | Designated section on many ships | Banned |
| [MSC](/articles/msc-smoking-vaping-policy/) | Designated section on most ships | Banned |

All three ban smoking in cabins and on balconies. The difference is in the casino, and it's small: Carnival and MSC keep a smoking section on most ships, Royal Caribbean on many. On Carnival, the published US$500 charge and the rule on disembarking whole cabins are the parts to take seriously.
