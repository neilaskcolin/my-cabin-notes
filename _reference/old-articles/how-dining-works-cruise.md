On a cruise, most of your food is already paid for. The main dining room, the buffet and several casual spots are included, and the only real decision is how you want dinner to work: same time and table every night, or turn up when you like.

## Fixed seating vs flexible dining
### Fixed (traditional) seating
You get an assigned table and time, usually an early sitting around 5:30–6pm or a late one around 8–8:30pm. The same waiters serve you all week and learn your drink order and allergies. It suits families with young children, groups who want to eat together and anyone who enjoys routine.

### Flexible dining
You turn up within an evening window, often around 5:30–9:30pm, or reserve a time in the app. You may wait at peak times and you will get different waiters. It suits couples, long port days and anyone who hates watching the clock.

| Line | Flexible option name | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Royal Caribbean | My Time Dining | Choose at booking; reservations recommended |
| Carnival | Your Time Dining | Walk-in within the evening window |
| Princess | Anytime Dining | Reserve or walk in |
| Norwegian | Freestyle | No fixed seating on most ships |
| Disney | Rotational dining | Fixed time, but you rotate restaurants with your servers |

Other lines, including Celebrity, Holland America and MSC, offer fixed and flexible options in different forms. Check your booking to see which you have.

> **Good to know:** on an Alaska sailing in June, sunset in Juneau can be after 9:30pm, so a late fixed seating or flexible dining lets you watch the scenery first.

## A day of eating on board
Here is how food usually flows on a sea day:
- **Breakfast:** buffet or main dining room, plus a café for pastries and paid specialty coffee.
- **Lunch:** buffet, a poolside grill or the main dining room on sea days.
- **Afternoon:** pizza, ice cream, snacks and afternoon tea on many ships.
- **Dinner:** main dining room, buffet or a specialty restaurant.
- **Late night:** pizza, room service or a small late buffet.

Main dining room menus change nightly, with several courses. You can order two starters, two mains or two desserts at no extra cost.

## What costs extra
Specialty restaurants, such as steakhouses, sushi bars and chef's tables, charge a cover or à la carte prices, often around US$30–80 per person. Many people book one or two in a week. Room service usually includes a free continental breakfast, while hot food or late-night orders may carry a delivery fee depending on the line. The [main dining, buffet and specialty restaurants guide](/articles/main-dining-buffet-specialty-restaurants/) compares them in detail, and [is room service free](/articles/room-service-cruise-free-cost/) covers fees by line.

> **Canadian tip:** specialty restaurant prices are in US dollars plus, on some lines, a service charge. A US$55 steakhouse cover for two is roughly C$150–160 before drinks.

## What to do on day one
1. Check your dining time and table in the app or on your cruise card.
2. If it does not suit, see the maître d' on embarkation afternoon. Changes are often possible.
3. Ask for a table for two, or a shared table, if you have a preference.
4. Confirm any allergies or dietary needs with the head waiter, even if you declared them at booking. See [dietary needs at sea](/articles/dietary-needs-cruise-ships/).
5. Book specialty restaurants for the nights you want, avoiding formal nights if you prefer the dining room's dressier atmosphere then.

## Set it up with the booking
Dining time, table size, allergies and celebrations all attach to your reservation. Sort them when you book, then confirm on day one, and seven evenings run smoothly. If a birthday or anniversary falls on board, flag it too, as in [celebrating a birthday on a cruise ship](/articles/celebrating-birthday-on-a-cruise-ship/).
