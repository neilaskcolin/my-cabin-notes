Princess's internet is called MedallionNet, and at recent prices it costs roughly US$15–25 per device per day, less if you buy before sailing or add extra devices. If your fare includes Princess Plus or Premier, you already have it.

## How much does Princess wifi cost?
MedallionNet is sold per device, per day, for the length of the cruise. At recent pricing that is roughly US$15–25 for one device, with a lower daily rate for each additional device on the same booking. Prices vary by ship and sailing, so check yours.

> **Quick math:** a couple who both want to be online for a 7-night Alaska cruise would pay about US$210–350 for two devices, or roughly C$285–490. That figure is the one to set against the cost of Princess Plus.

### When wifi is included
MedallionNet is part of the Princess Plus and Princess Premier fare bundles, along with a drinks package and crew appreciation. At recent terms Plus covers one device per guest and Premier covers more. For anyone who would buy wifi and drinks anyway, the bundle often costs less than the pieces, as the [Princess drink package price](/articles/princess-drink-package-price/) page shows. Some promotions also add wifi to the fare, so check your dates before buying.

### What works without a package
The Princess app runs on the ship's own network without an internet plan. You can check the daily programme, see dining reservations, look at your account and order food or drinks through OceanNow. You cannot browse the web or use other apps.

## How fast is MedallionNet?
Princess now uses Starlink satellite internet across the fleet, which made a big difference to speeds. Most of the time you can stream, scroll and make video calls. Expect slow patches in narrow fjords and close to glacier faces, where steep walls block part of the sky, and at busy times when many guests are online. Coverage generally recovers quickly once the ship is back in open water.

If you plan to work from the ship, read [can you work remotely from a cruise ship](/articles/can-you-work-remotely-cruise-ship/) first, and [how cruise ship internet works](/articles/cruise-ship-internet-how-it-works/) explains why speeds vary.

## What should you buy?
1. Check whether your fare already includes Princess Plus, Premier or a wifi promotion.
2. Count the devices that genuinely need to be online. One shared phone is often enough for a couple on a scenery-heavy Alaska trip.
3. Buy before you sail through your booking, which is usually cheaper than the onboard price.
4. Once on board, log in through the Princess app. You can usually sign one device out and another in if you need to switch.
5. Put your phone in airplane mode with wifi turned on, so it cannot connect to the ship's expensive cellular network.

## Phone roaming for Canadians
At sea, phones can latch onto the ship's own cellular service, which is billed at high rates and is not covered by Canadian roaming add-ons. In Alaska ports such as Juneau and Ketchikan, your carrier's US roaming plan usually works once you are on shore. Along parts of the Inside Passage you may pick up Canadian towers, but do not count on it. The full picture is in [cruise wifi and phone roaming for Canadians](/articles/cruise-wifi-phone-roaming-canadians/).
