A future cruise credit is real money with house rules. Used well, it pays for a good chunk of your next sailing. Ignored, it quietly expires, and lines rarely send reminders.

## What is a future cruise credit?
An FCC is a voucher held on your loyalty or guest record that can be applied to a new booking with the same line. It is usually issued per person, in a set amount, with conditions spelled out in the email that announced it. That email is effectively the contract, so keep it.

## Where do credits come from?
- **The line cancels or makes a major change.** You are normally offered a refund or a credit, and the credit is sometimes worth more than you paid as an incentive. See [what happens if the line cancels your cruise](/articles/what-happens-cruise-cancelled-by-line/).
- **You cancel a non-refundable deposit fare.** Before final payment, some lines return the deposit as credit, occasionally minus a fee (see [refundable vs non-refundable fares](/articles/cruise-fare-classes-refundable/)).
- **Goodwill.** A missed port, a cabin problem or a rough sailing can lead to a partial credit.
- **Promotions.** Occasionally a credit is part of a booking offer.

An onboard "future cruise" deposit or certificate bought during a sailing is a different product. It reserves a discount or perk on a later booking rather than holding refunded money.

## The rules that eat value

### Deadlines
Most credits have a book-by date, a sail-by date, or both. Book-by means the new reservation must be made in time; sail-by means you must actually travel in time, which is far stricter. Popular sailings sell out, so a deadline you thought was distant can arrive with nothing suitable left.

### Name lock
Credits attach to a person, not a household. If two of you cancelled, each typically gets a credit in their own name, and both people usually need to be on the new booking to use both.

### What the credit can pay for
Many FCCs apply to the cruise fare only, not taxes, port fees, gratuities or extras. If a US$500 credit is applied to a fare of US$400, some lines forfeit the remaining US$100 and others reissue it. Always aim for a fare larger than the credit.

### Combining with promotions
Credits usually combine with standard sales but sometimes not with certain discounted fare types. Ask before choosing between two fares.

### Currency
Check whether the credit is held in US or Canadian dollars. If you would otherwise take a refund on a US-dollar booking, remember that converting back to CAD often loses a little, as explained in [how exchange rates affect cruise pricing](/articles/exchange-rate-cruise-pricing-canadians/).

## How to use an FCC without losing any
1. Save the issuing email and note the value, currency, book-by date, sail-by date and eligible fares.
2. Set calendar reminders six months and two months before the first deadline.
3. Shortlist sailings where each person's fare is higher than their credit.
4. Book early in the window, while cabin choice is still good, rather than in deadline week.
5. Ask for the credit to be applied at booking, and check it appears on the invoice.
6. If several family members hold credits, ask whether they can all go against one cabin.
7. If you use a travel agent, give them the credit details; most lines let agents apply FCCs to bookings they make.

> **Watch out:** if you later cancel a booking paid partly with an FCC, the credit portion may come back with its original expiry date, or be lost if you are inside the penalty period. Ask how the line treats it before cancelling, and ask your insurer how the credit portion of the trip is covered.

## Where a credit works hardest
A credit offsets the same dollars whatever the fare, so put it toward a sailing that is already good value, such as one booked during [wave season](/articles/wave-season-explained-canada/) with promotions attached. Combined with a refundable fare, you can still [ask for a reprice](/articles/cruise-price-protection-asking-for-it/) if the price falls.

## The one habit that matters
Treat a credit like a gift card with an expiry date. Write down the deadlines the day it arrives, book well inside them, and make sure each person's fare is larger than their credit.
