Points feel like free money, so it is tempting to throw a big balance at a cruise fare. Sometimes that is the right call. Often, though, the same points stretch further on flights, and paying cash for the cruise keeps promotions and extras that a points booking can lose.

## How do Canadians use points for a cruise?
There are three main routes, and they behave very differently.

| Redemption route | Typical value | Good for the cruise? | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Statement credit against a travel charge | Fixed rate, often around 0.5–1 cent per point | Yes, and you book wherever you like | Minimum redemptions; time limits on which charges qualify |
| Bank or card travel portal | Fixed or variable | Sometimes | Limited cruise choice, no agency extras, changes handled by the portal |
| Airline points for flights | Variable, often higher on long-haul or premium seats | No, but ideal for getting to the port | Seat availability and taxes |
| Gift cards or merchandise | Usually the lowest | Rarely | Poor value per point |

The statement-credit route is usually the most flexible. You pay the cruise with your card, wherever you booked it, then use points to cancel out the charge. Because the booking itself is normal, any onboard credit, repricing or help from a consultant still applies.

Booking through a bank's portal is different. The portal holds the reservation, so changes, refunds and price drops go through it, and extras attached to other channels are often missing. Compare the portal price with the same cabin elsewhere before committing.

Cruise line loyalty points are another thing again. They mostly build status and perks rather than paying fares, as covered in [cruise loyalty programs](/articles/cruise-loyalty-programs-worth-chasing/).

## How to work out what your points are worth
1. Find the cash price of the booking in Canadian dollars.
2. Find how many points the same booking needs, plus any cash still owed.
3. Subtract the cash still owed from the cash price, divide by the points, and multiply by 100. That gives cents per point.
4. Repeat for your next-best use, usually flights.
5. Add what a points booking gives up: a cash booking earns card rewards and keeps any promotion attached to the fare.

> **Quick math:** suppose two return flights from Toronto to Fort Lauderdale cost C$1,100 in cash, or 50,000 points plus C$150 in taxes. That works out to about 1.9 cents per point. If the same points only erase cruise charges at 1 cent each, they are worth almost twice as much on the flights.

Those numbers are illustrative; airline pricing moves constantly. The method is what matters. [Toronto to Fort Lauderdale cruise flights](/articles/toronto-fort-lauderdale-cruise-flights/) covers what those routes typically cost.

## When points make sense for the cruise itself
- Your points have one fixed value everywhere, so there is no better use waiting.
- You can redeem by statement credit, keeping the booking with the line or your agent.
- You have a large balance and no flights to buy, for example driving from Calgary or Edmonton to a Vancouver sailing.
- The cruise is a one-off splurge and cash flow matters more than squeezing value.

## When cash wins
- Your points are airline points that go further on flights.
- A strong promotion or attached extras would not survive a portal booking.
- The portal fare is non-refundable or harder to reprice than the line's own fare.
- Paying cash earns rewards on a large purchase you would otherwise give up.

> **Canadian tip:** many Canadian cards include trip cancellation or travel medical cover only when the trip is charged to the card. If you plan to erase the charge with points, put the booking on the card first and confirm the coverage still applies, including any age limits on card medical insurance. [Cruise insurance in Canada](/articles/travel-insurance-cruise-canadians/) explains what to check.

## The hybrid that often wins
For most Canadian cruisers, the strongest combination is points for the flights, cash for the cruise. The flights are where airline points shine, and paying cash for the cruise keeps the full menu of promotions, repricing and extras. If the fare is in US dollars, a no-foreign-transaction-fee card helps too (see [paying in CAD or USD](/articles/paying-cad-vs-usd-cruise/)).

Co-branded cruise cards are a separate question, covered in [cruise line credit cards and travel points](/articles/cruise-line-credit-card-rewards-points/).

## Before you redeem
Run the cents-per-point sum on both the cruise and the flights, and check what the points booking would lose in extras or flexibility. Five minutes with a calculator usually settles it.
