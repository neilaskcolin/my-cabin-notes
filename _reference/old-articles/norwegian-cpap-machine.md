Yes, you can bring a CPAP on Norwegian Cruise Line, and plenty of guests do. A few steps before you leave home make the first night easy.

## Register your CPAP with Norwegian's Access Desk
Norwegian handles medical equipment through its Access Desk. Contact it once you have booked, either directly or through whoever holds your reservation, and ask for three things to be noted: that you are bringing a CPAP, that you need a medical extension cord, and whether you want distilled water.

A note on the booking means the cabin can be set up before you arrive. Skip it and you may spend part of embarkation afternoon at Guest Services sorting it out.

## Extension cords: use the ship's, not yours
Like most large lines, Norwegian bans guest extension cords and surge-protected power bars because of fire risk at sea. The ship provides its own approved cord for medical devices instead, which matters on older ships where the nearest socket is at the desk rather than the bed.

> **Canadian tip:** cabin outlets on Norwegian ships take the same North American plugs used in Canada, so your CPAP plugs straight in with no adapter. Many cabins also have a European-style socket and, on newer ships, USB ports.

## Water for the humidifier
Norwegian can typically supply distilled water when you request it in advance, delivered to your stateroom. Ask about quantity and any charge when you register. If you would rather not depend on it, a 4 L jug bought near the terminal in Seattle, Vancouver or Miami covers most week-long sailings. See [CPAP distilled water on cruise ships](/articles/cpap-distilled-water-cruise-ships/) for how much to expect to use.

## Getting your CPAP from home to your cabin
1. Pack the machine, mask, tubing and any spare filters in their travel case.
2. Carry it on the plane. Air Canada and WestJet generally allow a CPAP as a medical device that doesn't count against your carry-on allowance, but confirm with your airline before flying.
3. Keep it with you through the cruise terminal instead of handing it to a porter. Checked bags can reach cabins late in the evening, as [lost or delayed luggage on a cruise](/articles/lost-delayed-luggage-cruise/) explains.
4. On boarding, check the cord and water are in the cabin, and ask Guest Services if not.

## What's different about a CPAP on Norwegian
Norwegian's freestyle approach, with no fixed dining times and late shows, means many guests keep irregular hours, and a CPAP fits that easily. The newer Breakaway, Breakaway Plus and Prima class ships generally have more cabin sockets and USB ports than the older Jewel-class ships, where the medical cord does more of the work.

On an Alaska sailing from Seattle or Vancouver, port calls often start early. Clean the mask and tubing and leave them to dry before you head ashore, so they are ready by bedtime.

### What to pack
- Spare mask cushion and filter, in case one fails mid-cruise
- A small towel or drying hook for the tubing
- A printed copy of your prescription or a letter from your doctor
- Battery pack, if you use one, in carry-on only

> **Good to know:** if your CPAP uses lithium batteries, airline rules on spare batteries apply on your flight to the port; check them with your airline.

## How Norwegian compares
| Line | Desk to contact | Distilled water |
|---|---|---|
| **Norwegian** | Access Desk | On request, delivered to stateroom |
| [Royal Caribbean](/articles/royal-caribbean-cpap-machine/) | Access Department | On request, often via stateroom attendant |
| [Carnival](/articles/carnival-cpap-machine/) | Special Needs team | Paid; pre-order, buy on board or bring your own |

All three lines welcome CPAP machines. Norwegian and Royal Caribbean supply an approved extension cord, while Carnival lets you bring your own plain cord without a surge protector, so the choice of line shouldn't hinge on it. The practical difference is who you contact: Norwegian's Access Desk, Royal Caribbean's Access Department or Carnival's Special Needs team.
