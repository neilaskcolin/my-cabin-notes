Use the buffet for speed, the main dining room for most dinners, and a specialty restaurant for one or two nights that deserve it. That is the mix most experienced cruisers settle on, and it keeps the food bill close to zero.

## How do the three compare?

| | Main dining room | Buffet | Specialty restaurant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost | Included | Included | About US$30–75 per person (roughly C$40–105) |
| Service | Waiter, multiple courses | Help yourself | Waiter, small room |
| Reservations | Fixed time or flexible window | None | Yes, book early |
| Dress | Smart casual; dressier on formal nights | Anything but swimwear | Smart casual |
| Pace | 75–90 minutes | As quick as you like | 90 minutes to 2 hours |
| Best for | Most dinners | Breakfast, port-day lunch, tired nights | Celebrations, foodies |

How seating times and flexible dining work is covered in [how dining works on a cruise](/articles/how-dining-works-cruise/).

## When the main dining room wins
For a sit-down dinner without a bill, nothing beats it. Menus change nightly, you can order two starters or two desserts at no extra charge, and on fixed seating the same waiter learns your preferences by night three. It is also where the kitchen handles allergies and dietary needs best, since your waiter can talk to the chef directly (see [dietary needs on cruise ships](/articles/dietary-needs-cruise-ships/)).

Breakfast and lunch are often served here too, usually on sea days. It is a calmer alternative to a packed buffet.

## When the buffet wins
Breakfast before an early excursion. A quick lunch on a port day. The night you're sunburnt and can't face a collared shirt. The buffet is fast and varied, and fussy young eaters can choose for themselves.

Its weak spots are crowds at peak times and food that has sat a while. Go just after it opens or late in a service, and look for the cooked-to-order stations, which are often the best food in the room.

### The other included options
Most big ships also have included pizza, burger or deli counters, and many offer a free continental breakfast in your cabin. [What's free with room service](/articles/room-service-cruise-free-cost/) explains where the fees start.

## When a specialty restaurant is worth paying for
Steakhouses, Italian, sushi and chef's table dinners are the usual line-up. You get better ingredients, a quieter room and more time with your server. They are worth it for:

- An anniversary, birthday or milestone night.
- Couples who want a quiet table for two without a big dining room's noise.
- A port-heavy week where you'd like one unhurried evening.
- Food lovers keen to try a line's signature venue.

They're less worth it if you're budget-focused, travelling with young kids who won't sit for two hours, or on a short cruise where the main dining room menu hasn't had time to repeat.

> **Quick math:** a couple booking two specialty dinners at US$55 each per person spends US$220, plus an automatic service charge of around 18–20% on some lines. That's roughly C$355–370 for the week. A dining package covering three meals can save money if you'd have booked three anyway.

## A week that works
On a 7-night Caribbean sailing from Fort Lauderdale, a typical pattern looks like this:

- **Breakfast:** buffet on port days, dining room on sea days.
- **Lunch:** buffet or a pool-deck counter.
- **Dinner:** main dining room five nights, buffet one night after a long port day, specialty restaurant once.

On an Alaska sailing from Vancouver, many people swap the specialty night onto a scenic cruising evening, when daylight lasts well past 9 pm in June.

## Booking specialty restaurants
Popular venues and the 7 pm slots sell out on busy ships. Book in the cruise line's app once reservations open, usually after you've paid in full and checked in online. Pre-cruise prices are sometimes discounted, and charges post in US dollars, so a card with no foreign transaction fee helps.

If your fare includes a dining package or onboard credit, use it here. The [best cruise lines for quiet dining](/articles/best-cruise-lines-quiet-dining-experience/) guide covers which ships make the included dining room feel like a specialty night.

## Dress and timing on the night
Specialty restaurants ask for smart casual, which in practice means trousers or a nice dress rather than shorts. On formal nights, the main dining room fills up and specialty venues can be easier to book. [What to wear on formal nights](/articles/formal-nights-what-to-wear/) covers the dressier evenings.
