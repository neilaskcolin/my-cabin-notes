On price alone, they're closer than most people think. A mainstream Caribbean cruise and a mid-range all-inclusive week often land in the same range for a Canadian couple once flights, drinks and tips are counted. The real difference is what you get for the money: several destinations and a floating resort, or one beach with the bar always open.

## What each one actually includes

| | Mainstream cruise | All-inclusive resort package |
|---|---|---|
| Flights from Canada | Not included; you book to Florida or the port | Usually included, often direct |
| Airport transfers | Extra | Usually included |
| Accommodation | Included | Included |
| Meals | Main restaurants and buffet | All restaurants, sometimes with reservations |
| Alcohol and soda | Extra, or a package | Included |
| Entertainment | Large production shows, live music | Evening shows, smaller scale |
| Destinations | Four or five ports in a week | One |
| Tips | Daily gratuities added to your account | Optional, at your discretion |
| Currency | US dollars on board | Packages usually priced in Canadian dollars |

## What does a week cost for a Toronto couple?
Rough totals for 7 nights in February, outside March break, at recent prices. Both exclude travel insurance.

**Caribbean cruise from Fort Lauderdale, inside cabin:**
- Cruise fare: US$1,400–2,400 (roughly C$1,900–3,300)
- Taxes and port fees, if not in the fare: C$300–550
- Toronto–Fort Lauderdale flights: C$400–800
- Hotel night before sailing: C$200–350
- Gratuities: C$310–390
- Drinks without a package: C$300–700
- Two or three excursions: C$300–800
- **Rough total: C$3,700–6,900**

**Riviera Maya all-inclusive, mid-range four-star package:**
- Package with flights and transfers: C$3,000–5,000
- Tips, if you leave them: C$100–200
- One or two excursions: C$200–600
- Small local tourist taxes in some destinations
- **Rough total: C$3,300–5,800**

> **Quick math:** a couple who'd each have four drinks a day would pay roughly US$80–100 a day between them on a ship without a package, about C$750–950 over the week. That alone can swing the comparison to the resort. A couple who have a glass of wine with dinner and nothing else may find the cruise costs about the same and shows them four islands.

## Where the cruise wins
- **Variety.** New scenery most mornings, without repacking.
- **Entertainment.** Big ships run full theatre productions, comedy and live music every night at no extra charge.
- **Families with children.** Kids clubs are included on most lines, which few resorts can match at that scale.
- **Alaska, the fjords and the Mediterranean.** There's no all-inclusive equivalent for seeing glaciers or several European cities in a week.
- **Light drinkers,** who effectively pay for alcohol they don't drink at a resort.

## Where the resort wins
- **Drinks.** If you'd use them, included cocktails are hard to beat.
- **Direct flights.** Canadian packages fly direct to Cancún, Punta Cana, Montego Bay and Varadero from many cities, while cruises from smaller airports usually mean a connection and a hotel night in Florida.
- **Winter flight risk.** A snow delay at an all-inclusive costs you a day at the beach. A delayed flight to a cruise can mean missing the ship, as explained in [winter flights and Caribbean cruises](/articles/snow-delay-winter-flights-caribbean-cruise/).
- **Pure relaxation.** Same beach, same chair, no all-aboard times.
- **Pricing in Canadian dollars,** with fewer extras appearing on a US-dollar bill at the end.

> **Canadian tip:** if you cruise, fly in the day before and use a credit card with no foreign-transaction fee for the onboard account. Both protect the budget in ways an all-inclusive package doesn't require.

## Timing matters for both
March break pushes both formats to their highest prices. Direct all-inclusive flights from Canada fill early for that week, and so do family cabins on Florida sailings. If your dates are flexible, late January, early February and late April are usually better value for either, though prices vary by year and destination.

## What about "all-inclusive" cruises?
Some premium fares, such as Celebrity's All Included fares or Princess Plus, bundle drinks and wifi, which closes much of the gap. Luxury lines go further and include almost everything, at a much higher price. The term isn't standardized, so check the inclusions line by line; [what "all-inclusive" means on a cruise](/articles/what-does-all-inclusive-mean-cruise/) explains the differences.

## How to compare for your own trip
Price both for the way you'd actually spend the week: your flights from your home airport, your realistic drink count, the excursions you'd book and the tips you'd leave. Compare those totals rather than the advertised prices, which leave out different things. [What's included in a cruise fare](/articles/whats-included-cruise-fare/) is a useful checklist for the cruise side.
