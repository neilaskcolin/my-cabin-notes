A hurricane in the forecast rarely means danger for a cruise ship. It does mean change. The ship will go somewhere else, and the bigger risks for Canadians are missed ports and disrupted flights to and from Florida, not the storm itself.

## When is hurricane season?
The Atlantic hurricane season runs from 1 June to 30 November. Storms can form at any point, but most activity falls between mid-August and mid-October.

| Month | Typical storm risk | What it means for a cruise |
|---|---|---|
| June | Low | Early-season storms are usually weaker |
| July | Low to moderate | Occasional itinerary changes |
| August | Rising | Reroutes become more common |
| September | Peak | The most likely month for changes |
| October | High early, easing later | Thanksgiving sailings can still be affected |
| November | Lower | Most of the month is usually settled |
| December to May | Outside the season | Hurricanes are very rare |

Canadian March break, Christmas and February escapes all fall outside hurricane season. The busiest risk window overlaps late-summer holidays and the Thanksgiving long weekend instead.

## How do cruise lines handle a storm?
Lines follow forecasts from the [National Hurricane Center](https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/) and their own weather teams, often days ahead. Ships are fast compared with a storm's slow, trackable path, so they simply go around it.

In practice, that might mean:

- Swapping an Eastern Caribbean route for a Western one, or the reverse.
- Skipping a port in the storm's path and adding a sea day.
- Changing the order of ports.
- Leaving a day early or returning a day late if the home port is about to close.

All of this is allowed under the cruise contract, which gives the line wide freedom to change itineraries (see [cruise contracts of carriage explained](/articles/cruise-contract-of-carriage-explained/)). Lines usually refund the port fees and taxes for a skipped port to your onboard account, but not part of the fare.

## The real risk: flights and home ports
Florida ports can close briefly before and after a storm. If your ship returns a day late, you'll need to change flights home to Toronto, Montreal or Calgary; if it leaves early, you'll need to be there in time. Lines sometimes help with costs, but don't count on it.

Fly in at least the day before, and book flexible or changeable fares if you can (see [flying the day before vs same day](/articles/fly-day-before-cruise/)). If your flight is delayed by weather and you miss the ship, [what happens if your flight is delayed](/articles/cruise-flight-delay-what-happens/) explains who pays.

> **Watch out:** insurance bought after a storm is named won't cover that storm. If you're sailing between August and October, buy at deposit time and check the weather and missed-connection wording (see [cruise insurance for Canadians](/articles/travel-insurance-cruise-canadians/)).

## What to do if a storm is forecast
1. Watch your email and the cruise line's app for updates; lines contact booked guests directly.
2. Don't cancel on your own. If you cancel, normal penalties apply, while a line-cancelled cruise is refunded or credited (see [what happens if the line cancels](/articles/what-happens-cruise-cancelled-by-line/)).
3. Call your insurer if your flights are affected, and ask what they'll cover before you pay for changes.
4. Pack an extra day of medication and a little extra money in case you return late.
5. On board, listen to the captain's updates and check the daily program for port changes.

## Should you avoid hurricane season?
Not necessarily. Fares in late August through October are often among the lowest of the year, and most sailings run as planned or with minor changes. It suits flexible travellers who don't mind a port swap.

If a specific port matters to you, or you can't be flexible about flights, sail between December and May. Itineraries to the southern Caribbean, such as Aruba, Bonaire and Curaçao, sit south of most storm tracks; [the southern Caribbean for a warm winter](/articles/cuba-southern-caribbean-warm-winter/) covers those. Or swap the Caribbean for a fall cruise closer to home, like [New England and Canada in autumn](/articles/new-england-canada-fall-cruises/).

## Cruising calmly in storm season
Book a flexible flight, buy insurance early and treat the itinerary as provisional. Do that, and a hurricane forecast becomes a change of scenery rather than a crisis.
