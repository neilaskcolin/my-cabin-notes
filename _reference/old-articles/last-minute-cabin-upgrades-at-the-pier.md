faq1a: Sometimes, if the ship still has unsold higher-category cabins close to departure. Offers are now more common through the app or email in the final days or at guest services after boarding, but a terminal offer does happen.
faq2q: Should you always accept a pier-side upgrade offer?
faq2a: Only if the price is clearly below what that category normally costs and you know which cabin you are getting. Accepting without a cabin number carries some of the same location risk as a guarantee booking.
faq3q: Do you have to pay more gratuities after an upgrade?

A last-minute upgrade is the line's way of earning something from a better cabin that would otherwise sail empty. It is different from the pre-cruise bidding programs covered in [cabin upgrades: bids, upsells and free bumps](/articles/cabin-upgrades-bids-explained/), where you submit an offer weeks ahead and wait.

## How do last-minute upgrades happen?
Close to departure, the line can see which higher categories are still unsold. Rather than leave them empty, it offers flat-price moves to guests already booked. These days the offer most often appears in the app or by email in the last few days, or at guest services on boarding day. A check-in agent at the terminal can also make one, though it is less common than it once was.

The price is usually quoted per person, in US dollars, and you decide on the spot.

## Is it worth taking?
Compare the offer with what the category would have cost to book. A move from inside to balcony for a modest per-night price can be excellent value, especially on scenic routes such as Alaska, where a balcony is where you watch the glaciers (see [best cabins for Alaska scenery](/articles/best-cabins-alaska-cruise-scenery/)).

Worth considering when:
- The price is well below the category's normal fare difference.
- You are moving to a cabin type that changes the trip, such as inside to balcony.
- The location is acceptable and you can see it on the deck plan.

Usually worth skipping when:
- You can't find out which cabin you would get.
- The new cabin sits under a nightclub, pool deck or galley (see [quietest cabins](/articles/quietest-cabins-where-to-book/)).
- The step up is small, such as one balcony grade to another.

## What to ask before you say yes
1. Which exact cabin number, and where is it on the [deck plan](/articles/reading-cruise-ship-deck-plans/)?
2. What is the total price for everyone in the cabin, including any taxes?
3. Does the daily gratuity rate change in the new category?
4. Will the onboard credit or other extras on my booking carry over?
5. How will my checked bags reach the new cabin if they are tagged with the old number?

> **Watch out:** luggage tags printed before you left home carry your original cabin number. If you move at the terminal, ask staff to note the change and confirm with your cabin steward that evening, or your bags may be delivered to your old room.

## Paying for it as a Canadian
The charge normally goes on your onboard account or card in US dollars. A US$150 per person upgrade for two is roughly C$410–420 before any foreign-transaction fee, so decide your limit in Canadian dollars before boarding day.

## Have a number in mind
Look up the price difference between your category and the one above it a week before sailing. If an offer arrives below that number and passes the five questions, take it with confidence.
