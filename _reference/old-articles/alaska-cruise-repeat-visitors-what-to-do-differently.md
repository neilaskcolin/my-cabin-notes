A second Alaska cruise on the same ship, route and month will feel like a rerun. The fix is to change at least one big thing: where you sail, which glacier you see, what size ship you are on, or when you go. Here is how to choose.

## Start with what your first trip was
Most first Alaska cruises are a 7-night round trip from Vancouver or Seattle, calling at Ketchikan, Juneau and Skagway, with one glacier day. Use this table to pick the change that will feel most different.

| If your first trip was… | Try this next | What feels new |
|---|---|---|
| Round trip from Vancouver | One-way to Seward or Whittier | The Gulf of Alaska, Hubbard Glacier or College Fjord |
| Tracy Arm or Endicott Arm | A sailing that enters Glacier Bay | A national park day with rangers aboard and several tidewater glaciers |
| A big ship in July | A May or September sailing | Snowy peaks in May; bears, fall colour and lower fares in September |
| Ketchikan, Juneau, Skagway | A longer sailing with Sitka or Haines | Russian-era history, a different totem park, eagle country |
| Cruise only | A cruisetour with Denali | The interior, the railway and the mountain |
| A mainstream ship | A small expedition ship | Smaller towns, kayaks and zodiacs, naturalists on board |

The routes are compared in [Inside Passage vs Gulf of Alaska](/articles/inside-passage-vs-gulf-of-alaska/), and the glaciers in [Glacier Bay vs Hubbard vs Endicott Arm](/articles/glacier-bay-vs-hubbard-vs-endicott/).

## Change the glacier
Glacier days differ more than first-timers realize. Glacier Bay is a protected national park, entered by a limited number of ships each season, with rangers boarding to narrate. Tracy Arm and Endicott Arm are narrow, steep fjords that end at a glacier face, though ice can stop big ships short. Hubbard Glacier is a huge, wide face on the Gulf of Alaska route. Check which one an itinerary includes before booking.

## Go smaller
Expedition ships carrying roughly 100 or fewer passengers reach places like Petersburg, Wrangell and quiet inlets with no cruise docks at all. Days revolve around kayaking, zodiac landings and wildlife rather than shops. Prices per night are often several times a mainstream balcony, so treat it as the special-occasion version of Alaska. See [best small-ship lines for wildlife and nature](/articles/best-small-ship-lines-wildlife-nature/) and [small ship vs big ship from Vancouver](/articles/small-ship-vs-big-ship-alaska/).

## Change the season
If you went in peak July, a shoulder month will feel like a different trip:

- **May:** snow low on the mountains, waterfalls at full flow, quieter ports and some of the season's driest odds.
- **September:** bears at salmon streams, fall colour, darker nights with a slim chance of the [northern lights](/articles/northern-lights-alaska-cruise/), and usually lower fares.

> **Good to know:** for Canadians, a September sailing after Labour Day avoids school holiday pricing and crowds, and the Vancouver weather at either end is often still pleasant.

## Upgrade the excursions
Skip the introductory tours you already did. Repeat visitors often get more from:
- Floatplane or helicopter flightseeing over Misty Fjords or the Juneau Icefield.
- A dedicated [bear-viewing excursion](/articles/bear-viewing-excursions-alaska/) in late July or August.
- A longer hike with a guide, such as a Chilkoot Trail day from Skagway.
- A small-group whale watch with a naturalist instead of a large boat.

## Change the cabin or the line
If you sailed in an inside cabin, a balcony makes the scenic days feel completely different. A different line also changes the experience: some lean into naturalist talks and local cultural programs, others into family entertainment.

## Planning a genuinely different second trip
Write down what you did, what you missed, and what you would happily skip. Then change one big variable and one or two small ones. That usually gives a trip that feels new without losing what made you want to return.
