The quietest ships are usually mid-size vessels from lines that spend their deck space on lounges, libraries and a walkable promenade instead of waterslides and splash zones. Pick the right ship, sail outside school holidays, and choose a longer itinerary, and you will rarely hear a pool-deck DJ.

## How can you tell if a ship will be quiet?
A ship's personality is built into its layout. Before you book, look at the deck plan and photos for these signs.

### Signs of a calm ship
- A wraparound teak promenade deck with loungers.
- Several small lounges with live piano or jazz, not one big party venue.
- A real library and a card room.
- A large spa and a lap-friendly pool, often with a covered or adults-only pool as well.
- More dining venues than entertainment attractions.

### Signs of a busy ship
- Waterslides, a water park or a splash zone on the top deck.
- Ropes courses, go-kart tracks, surf simulators or climbing walls.
- A big pool-deck stage and outdoor movie screen.
- A large kids' club and teen area listed near the top of the features page.

None of these features are bad. They simply tell you who the ship is designed for.

## Which kinds of ships tend to be quietest?
Rather than a single "quiet line", think in terms of ship types.

- **Adults-only lines.** Viking's ocean ships take guests 18 and over and have no casino. Virgin Voyages is also adults-only but deliberately lively.
- **Premium lines without waterparks.** Holland America, Cunard, Celebrity and Oceania generally build ships around dining and lounges rather than slides. Their fares and itineraries attract an older crowd.
- **Older, smaller ships in big fleets.** Many mainstream lines keep a few mid-size ships that pre-date the water-park era, often at lower fares than the newest flagships.
- **Boutique small ships.** The calmest of all, covered in [quiet luxury small-ship lines](/articles/quiet-luxury-small-ship-lines-overlooked/).

Features change with refits, so confirm what is on the specific ship you are booking, not just the line.

## Timing makes a big difference
The same ship has two personalities: term time and holidays. In Canada, avoid July and August, the Christmas break and mid-March, when most provinces have March break and many US schools have spring break too. US Thanksgiving week in late November is also busy on Caribbean sailings.

The quietest windows are usually late April to mid-June, September to mid-November, and January after New Year's. For Alaska from Vancouver, May and September sailings carry far fewer families than July.

> **Watch out:** a "quiet" ship on a short, cheap weekend sailing can still be loud. Three- and four-night cruises attract celebration groups regardless of the line.

## Length and itinerary filter the crowd
Sailings of 10 nights or more, repositioning cruises and itineraries with lots of sea days draw retirees and experienced cruisers. A longer September Alaska sailing on a premium line will feel very different from a 7-night July sailing on a big resort ship, even through the same scenery.

## A quick five-step check before booking
1. Look up the ship's passenger capacity and aim for roughly 2,000 or fewer.
2. Scan the top-deck plan for slides, splash zones and big stages.
3. Check for an adults-only pool or sun deck; see [adults-only areas on mainstream ships](/articles/adults-only-areas-cruise-ships/).
4. Avoid Canadian and US school holiday dates.
5. Choose your cabin carefully, using [where to book the quietest cabins](/articles/quietest-cabins-where-to-book/).
