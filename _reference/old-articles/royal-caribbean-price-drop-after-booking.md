If your Royal Caribbean fare drops before final payment, you can usually have the booking repriced to the new fare and keep your cabin. After final payment the money is locked in, and anything you get is goodwill. Separately, drink packages, wifi and excursions bought in the Cruise Planner can often be rebought when they go on sale.

## Before final payment: repricing your Royal Caribbean fare
Royal Caribbean generally allows a booking to move to a currently advertised lower fare for the same ship, sailing and cabin category. The fare has to be publicly bookable, not limited to new bookings or a closed group. You keep the cabin and booking number; only the price changes.

This works on non-refundable deposit (NRD) fares too, though the deposit stays non-refundable. It is the practical version of the price protection described in [what is cruise price protection](/articles/cruise-price-protection-asking-for-it/).

### How to ask for a reprice, step by step
1. Note your ship, sailing date, cabin category and the exact fare you paid.
2. Price the same category for two guests on Royal Caribbean's site and screenshot it, because promotions can vanish mid-call.
3. Compare the full package, including any onboard credit or perks on your current booking.
4. Ask whoever holds your booking, Royal Caribbean directly or your travel adviser, to reprice.
5. Check the revised invoice shows the new total and that your cabin number hasn't changed.

> **Example:** a couple from Calgary book a balcony on a Quantum-class Alaska sailing from Seattle for C$4,600 in the fall. During wave season the same category falls to C$4,100. Well before final payment, they ask for a reprice and save C$500, after checking their original fare didn't carry onboard credit worth more.

## The Best Price Guarantee window
Royal Caribbean has historically offered a short guarantee of around 48 hours after booking, during which a lower advertised price triggers a matching adjustment. It's useful if you spot a drop the day after booking, but most repricing happens through the pre-final-payment route above. Read the current wording before relying on it.

## After final payment
Once the balance is paid, cash refunds for later drops essentially stop. Some guests are offered onboard credit or a cheaper upgrade; others are told no. One polite request is reasonable. The general rules are in [cruise price drop after final payment](/articles/price-drop-after-booking-cruise/).

## Watch what you give up
Repricing can cost you more than it saves in some situations:
- **Lost perks:** Royal Caribbean sales often bundle onboard credit, reduced deposits or discounts for third and fourth guests. Moving to a new fare can mean surrendering what came with the old one.
- **Fare type:** if the cheaper fare is an NRD rate and you booked refundable, switching gives up your free cancellation.
- **Guarantee cabins:** a cheaper guarantee fare may not let you keep a specific cabin you chose.

## Repricing Cruise Planner add-ons
Royal Caribbean's Cruise Planner runs frequent sales on the Deluxe Beverage Package, VOOM wifi, specialty dining and shore excursions. Most pre-cruise purchases can be cancelled for a refund before sailing and rebought at the lower price. For a couple with a drink package, a mid-sized sale can be worth more than a small fare drop. See the [Royal Caribbean drink package price](/articles/royal-caribbean-drink-package-price/) page for what the package normally costs.

> **Good to know:** Royal Caribbean prices fares for Canadian residents in Canadian dollars. A lower fare you see quoted in US dollars may not be lower once converted, so compare like with like, as explained in [paying in CAD vs USD for a cruise](/articles/paying-cad-vs-usd-cruise/). ## What to have ready when you ask
Ship, sailing date, cabin category, the exact lower fare and a dated screenshot of it. Check fares every few weeks until final payment, especially during [wave season](/articles/wave-season-explained-canada/) from January to March, when Royal Caribbean's biggest sales usually run.
