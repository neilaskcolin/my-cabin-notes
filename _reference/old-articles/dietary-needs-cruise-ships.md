Special diets are much easier at sea than they used to be. The difference between a stressful week and an easy one is almost always notice: tell the line before you sail, then confirm in person on the first night.

## How to set up a special diet, in order
1. **At booking,** ask for your dietary needs or allergies to be added to the reservation. If you book through a travel advisor, ask them to do it and confirm in writing.
2. **Fill in the line's dietary or special-needs form** if it has one. For severe allergies, kosher meals or medical diets, contact the line's accessibility or special-needs team directly, ideally several weeks ahead or more.
3. **Repeat it at online check-in,** where there's usually a field for dietary requests.
4. **On embarkation day,** go to the main dining room and ask for the maître d' or head waiter. Many ships will introduce you to the person who'll look after your meals.
5. **Each evening,** review the next day's menu with your waiter so the galley can prepare your dishes separately.

This routine works best with consistent seating. If you pick flexible dining, tell the host each night, or ask whether you can keep a regular table. [How dining works on a modern cruise ship](/articles/how-dining-works-cruise/) explains the seating options.

## Which diets are easy, and which need planning?
| Diet | How well it's handled | Notice needed |
|---|---|---|
| Vegetarian | Options at nearly every meal | None, though it helps |
| Vegan | Good on most lines, best in the dining room | Recommended |
| Gluten-free | GF bread, pasta and desserts on request | Recommended |
| Celiac disease | Good if prepared separately, risky at buffets | Strongly recommended |
| Dairy-free or lactose-free | Plant milks and lactose-free milk widely available | Helpful |
| Nut, shellfish or other severe allergy | Separate preparation in the dining room | Essential |
| Diabetic or low-sodium | Sugar-free desserts common, lower-salt dishes on request | Helpful |
| Kosher | Often sealed, pre-prepared meals | Essential, often weeks ahead |
| Halal | Varies widely by line | Essential, confirm before booking |

## The buffet and specialty restaurants
Self-serve buffets are where allergy and celiac problems tend to happen: shared spoons, crumbs and items moved between trays. If your reaction is severe, eat in the main dining room, where dishes are made to order, or ask a buffet crew member to bring food from the kitchen. [Main dining room vs buffet vs specialty restaurants](/articles/main-dining-buffet-specialty-restaurants/) compares the venues.

Specialty restaurants handle diets well but need their own notice, so mention your needs when you book the table. Room service menus are more limited, and cooking classes need a heads-up too (see [cruise cooking classes](/articles/cruise-ship-culinary-classes-cooking-demos/)).

> **Watch out:** ships can't guarantee an allergen-free environment. Bring two epinephrine auto-injectors if prescribed, plus antihistamines, and know where the medical centre is. Its fees are charged privately, as explained in [cruise ship medical care costs](/articles/cruise-medical-care-costs/).

## Eating safely in port
Your ship's precautions stop at the gangway. On excursions that include lunch, tell the tour operator when you book. For independent port days, carry a printed card describing your allergy or celiac disease in the local language, which matters in Mexico, the Mediterranean or Japan.

> **Canadian tip:** sealed snacks from home make a useful backup, and most lines allow them (see [bringing your own snacks onboard](/articles/bringing-own-snacks-food-onboard/)). Don't carry fruit or other fresh food off the ship in US or Caribbean ports, and declare any food you bring back to Canada to the CBSA.

## Common mistakes
### Assuming the checkbox is enough
A note on the booking is the start, not the finish. Always confirm in person on day one.

### Waiting until you're hungry
Asking for a gluten-free pizza at 9 p.m. on a busy sea day rarely goes well. Pre-order where you can.

## Making sure your needs are covered
Tell the line early, confirm at check-in, and introduce yourself in the dining room on the first night. With those three steps, most travellers with dietary needs eat well all week, and travellers with severe allergies have a plan the whole crew understands.
