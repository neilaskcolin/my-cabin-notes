A reunion cruise needs about 12 months: enough time to track people down, let them save, and get past the stage where everyone says "sounds fun" and nobody books. The trick is putting a real price in front of classmates early and making the first commitment small.

## Why a reunion cruise needs a full year
A Saturday-night reunion needs a venue and a date. A cruise needs passports, time off work, flights and money spread over months. Classmates who have moved to Calgary, Halifax or Vancouver since graduation need time to plan flights, and parents need to work around school calendars. Twelve months also lets you book before popular sailings climb in price.

## The 12-month timeline

| Months before sailing | What to do | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 12–11 | Find classmates; send a poll with dates, rough prices and a partners question | A realistic headcount |
| 11–10 | Shortlist two sailings and share per-person price ranges | Something concrete to react to |
| 10–9 | Open the group block with low, refundable deposits | 8+ cabins committed |
| 9–6 | Share who has booked; run a roommate list for singles | Grow the block |
| 6–4 | Collect names and passport details; remind about insurance; plan events | Clean, complete bookings |
| 4–3 | Final payment, often 75–120 days out | Locked headcount |
| Last 2 months | Book the welcome party, print name tags, build the photo board | Ready to sail |

The payment dates are explained in [group cruise deadlines](/articles/group-cruise-deadlines-deposits/).

## Finding people and getting real commitment
Start with your class's social media group or alumni office, then ask one person in each old friend circle to spread the word. People respond to friends far more than to a group post.

Put a price in the very first poll. "A 4-night Bahamas cruise, about US$450–800 per person including taxes" gets honest answers. "Who's up for a cruise?" gets enthusiastic noise.

> **Watch out:** some fares come with a non-refundable deposit in exchange for a lower price. For a reunion, where plans change, stick with refundable deposits until final payment so nobody loses money for saying yes early.

## Making it affordable: payment plans
Most cruise bookings let guests pay any amount between the deposit and final payment. Suggest a simple monthly schedule: a US$1,000 balance over eight months is US$125 a month, roughly C$170. Let classmates pay the line or agency directly rather than through you, so you never handle anyone's money.

## Partners, singles and roommates
### Decide on partners early
Settle this in the first poll. Inviting partners usually raises the headcount and makes cabin sharing simple; just plan an icebreaker or two for those who didn't attend the school.

### Pair up singles
A solo cabin usually costs 150–200% of the per-person fare, so a roommate list saves real money. Some ships also have small studio cabins for solo travellers.

### Settle the kids question
Adults-only keeps the reunion feel and opens up cheaper non-holiday dates; inviting kids ties you to March break or summer.

## Choosing the sailing
A 4- or 5-night cruise from Florida keeps costs down and is an easy flight for classmates in Ontario and Quebec. For a Western Canadian class, an Alaska sailing from Vancouver in May or September is accessible and quieter than midsummer. If classmates are scattered across the country, pick a port with nonstop flights from most of their cities, and avoid the Caribbean's peak hurricane months of August to October.

## Planned moments on board
- **Welcome drinks on night one,** in private space arranged through the group block.
- **A yearbook dinner,** with old photos on the tables and a "then and now" board.
- **One group excursion,** such as a beach day or catamaran trip, with the rest of the port days free.
- **Simple icebreakers** for partners who don't know anyone; see [group cruise icebreaker ideas](/articles/group-cruise-icebreaker-activities-planning/).

> **Example:** a 25-year reunion of 22 classmates and partners fills 12 cabins on a 4-night Bahamas sailing from Port Canaveral in early May. They meet for welcome drinks, a yearbook dinner and one beach day, and leave the rest of the trip unplanned.

## When someone backs out
At least one person usually drops out. Recruit a cabin or two beyond the 8-cabin threshold and keep a waitlist, as explained in [what happens if someone drops out](/articles/what-if-someone-drops-out-group-cruise/). Optional [matching class shirts](/articles/group-cruise-matching-shirts-branded-items/) are a fun extra if the group wants them.

## Start with a price, not a promise
The reunions that sail are the ones that put a real number and a small, refundable first step in front of people early. Find your classmates, test the price, then let the timeline carry the momentum.
