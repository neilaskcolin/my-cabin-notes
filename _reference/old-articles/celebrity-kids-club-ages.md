Celebrity's kids club, Camp at Sea, takes children from 3 to 12 in three age groups, with a separate program for teens aged 13 to 17. Daytime sessions are included in the fare, late-night care costs extra, and there is no care at all for children under 3.

## How Camp at Sea splits by age
| Group | Ages | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| Shipmates | 3–5 | Must be fully toilet-trained; signed in and out by a parent |
| Cadets | 6–9 | Signed in and out by a parent |
| Captains | 10–12 | Can sign themselves in and out |
| Teens | 13–17 | Separate club and program; free to come and go |

Children join the group for their age. Camp at Sea runs on Celebrity's ocean ships but not on Celebrity Flora in the Galapagos. Celebrity Solstice has no dedicated teen room, so teen activities use other venues on board.

## Opening hours and late-night care
Complimentary sessions run on both sea days and port days, with extra lunch and dinner hours when the ship is in port, so parents can go ashore or eat alone. Teen hours vary by ship and time of year. Exact times are in the app and the daily schedule.

Late-night care runs from 10 pm to 1 am for a fee per child, per hour, bookable in the app before or during the cruise. It is how parents fit in a long dinner at Fine Cut Steakhouse or Eden on an evening chic night.

## Babies and toddlers
Celebrity has no nursery, no drop-off care for under-3s and no in-cabin babysitting. Babies must be at least 6 months old on the first day of the cruise, and some excursions and activities set 12 months as the minimum. Families with a toddler should compare options in [cruising with a baby or toddler](/articles/cruising-with-baby-toddler/) and [cruise babysitting, nurseries and kids clubs](/articles/cruise-ship-babysitting-in-cabin-service/).

> **Good to know:** Celebrity's youth spaces are smaller than the clubs on Royal Caribbean or Disney, matching a passenger mix that leans adult. Outside school holidays, your child may find only a handful of others their age.

## Is Celebrity right for your children's ages?
Celebrity works best for children of roughly 5 to 15 who are happy with a few hours of club time and enjoy food, scenery and port days. There are no waterslides, so kids who measure a ship by its water park will be happier elsewhere. Teens get their own space on most ships and a teen spa menu, and Alaska suits them well; see [Alaska cruise with teenagers](/articles/alaska-cruise-teenagers/).

> **Canadian tip:** July and August Alaska sailings from Vancouver and Seattle, plus Christmas and March Break departures from Fort Lauderdale, are when Camp at Sea is busiest and most social. Sail in May, June or September and the ship is mostly adults.

## Signing up on embarkation day
1. Register your children in the app before sailing if your ship offers it, or at the club on embarkation day.
2. Note allergies, medical needs and who is allowed to collect each child.
3. Check the first day's schedule so you know when sessions start.
4. Book late-night sessions early for evening chic and specialty dining nights.
5. Keep your phone handy so staff can reach you while your child is in the club.

## How Celebrity compares
| Line | Main club | Youngest age | Care for under-3s |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Celebrity** | Camp at Sea | 3 | None |
| [Princess](/articles/princess-kids-club-ages/) | Camp Discovery | 3 | None |
| [Royal Caribbean](/articles/royal-caribbean-kids-club-ages/) | Adventure Ocean | 3 | Paid nursery on many ships |

Princess and Royal Caribbean are the lines most often weighed against Celebrity. All three start their main club at age 3, but only Royal Caribbean, Celebrity's sister line, offers paid nursery care on many ships. With a toddler in tow, that tips the balance; with school-age children, Celebrity's smaller club and calmer ships can be the better fit. The full side-by-side for all eight lines is in [Kids Clubs on Cruise Ships: Ages, Hours and Costs](/articles/kids-clubs-cruise-ships-ages-costs/).
