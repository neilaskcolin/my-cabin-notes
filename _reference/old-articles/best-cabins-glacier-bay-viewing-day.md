For Glacier Bay, book a balcony on a mid-to-upper deck and don't worry much about port or starboard. The ship spends most of a day inside the national park and turns in front of the big glaciers, so outdoor space matters far more than which side you're on.

## Why Glacier Bay rewards the right cabin

Most glacier days give you a few hours at the ice. Glacier Bay gives you most of the day: the ship picks up park rangers near the park entrance in the morning, cruises roughly 100 kilometres up the bay to the tidewater glaciers, holds there, then retraces its route before dropping the rangers off in the afternoon.

That is a long time to stand on a public deck in cold wind. A balcony lets you step out for the highlights, duck inside to warm up, and never lose your spot. It is the single day of an Alaska cruise when the balcony earns its premium most clearly.

## How the day unfolds, and where your cabin fits

Timings vary by ship and season, but a Glacier Bay day usually runs like this:

1. **Morning, rangers board.** Commentary starts over the public address and, on many ships, on a cabin TV channel. Your balcony works well here, as the shoreline is close on both sides.
2. **Cruising up the bay.** Look for mountain goats on cliffs, brown bears on beaches and sea otters in the water. A lower or mid-deck balcony helps here, being closer to the waterline.
3. **Arrival at the glaciers.** At the head of the bay, Margerie Glacier is the usual star, beside the darker, rock-covered Grand Pacific. This is the moment for the bow or top deck, where you see straight ahead.
4. **The slow turn.** The ship holds at the face and rotates, giving each side its turn. When your side faces the ice, go back to your balcony for a private view of the calving.
5. **The return.** Smaller glaciers and coves you passed on the way in are now on your side of the ship. Afternoon is a good time to sit outside with a hot drink.

> **Good to know:** what you pass on the way in is on one side of the ship, and on the way out it's on the other. On a Glacier Bay day, both port and starboard balconies see roughly the same show.

## Which cabin position works best?

- **Mid-to-upper deck, midship.** The safe all-rounder: wide views over the ship's own structure and minimal wind.
- **Aft-facing balcony.** Excellent here, since you see the bay unfolding behind the ship on both sides at once.
- **Lower balcony deck.** Better for spotting wildlife along the shore, slightly narrower views of the peaks.
- **Forward cabins.** Fine, but windier, and you still can't see dead ahead from a side balcony.

Avoid balconies above lifeboats, which can block much of the view. The general rules for picking a clear cabin are in [the best cabins for Alaska scenery](/articles/best-cabins-alaska-cruise-scenery/).

## No balcony? You can still have a great day

The day is narrated throughout, and good public spots exist on every ship. Forward-facing observation lounges, such as the Crow's Nest on Holland America ships, give a warm, windowed view ahead. A promenade deck puts you near the water, and the top deck gives the full panorama once the ship turns. Holland America traditionally serves Dutch pea soup on deck on glacier days, which helps.

> **Example:** a Calgary couple in an inside cabin could spend the morning in a forward lounge, head to the top deck for the arrival and turn, then warm up with lunch at the buffet window. They will see everything a balcony couple sees, just with more walking and less privacy.

## Check that your sailing actually goes in

The National Park Service limits how many large ships enter Glacier Bay, so plenty of Alaska itineraries visit Hubbard Glacier or Endicott Arm instead. Before paying for a premium cabin to see Glacier Bay, confirm it is listed on your exact sailing, not just the ship's general route. ## Booking the cabin for this one day

Glacier Bay sailings on Holland America and Princess are popular, and the clear-view balconies, especially aft, go early for summer dates. Book once your itinerary is confirmed, pick a specific cabin rather than a guarantee, and pack layers and binoculars.
