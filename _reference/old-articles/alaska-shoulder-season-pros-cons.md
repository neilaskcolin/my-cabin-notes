faq1a: The core ports and main excursions run throughout the cruise season, but at the very edges, early May and late September, some tours, shops and seasonal services open late or close early. Denali park buses and some rail services also have shorter seasons than the cruises.
faq2q: Which is better: May or September Alaska cruise?
faq2a: May has drier odds, snowy peaks, full waterfalls and long days. September has bears at salmon streams, fall colour, a slim chance of the northern lights and often the lowest fares, but more rain. Pick by what you most want to see.
faq3q: Are shoulder season Alaska cruises good for families?
faq3a: Only if you can take children out of school. Canadian school years run into late June and restart around Labour Day, so most families are limited to July and August, with late June possible in some provinces.

The edges of the Alaska season sell the same ships, ports and glaciers at prices peak summer never touches. Whether that is a bargain or a compromise depends on what you want from the trip. Here is the honest trade.

## May vs September at a glance
| | May | September |
|---|---|---|
| Weather | Coolest mornings, best odds of dry days | Mild early, cooling and wetter as the month goes on |
| Daylight | Long and getting longer | Shrinking fast; dark evenings |
| Scenery | Snow low on peaks, waterfalls at full flow | Fall colour on hillsides and tundra |
| Wildlife | Whales arriving; bears out of hibernation but less concentrated | Bears at salmon streams; whales still feeding |
| Crowds | Quiet | Quiet after Labour Day |
| Extras | Spring prices on flights and Vancouver hotels | Small chance of northern lights |

## The case for the edges
- **Price:** fares are often well below the same cabin in July, and promotions such as onboard credit are more common. Check actual numbers for your dates.
- **Space:** glacier decks are less crowded, tables need less waiting, and ports feel half as busy.
- **Flights and hotels:** fares between Toronto, Montreal or Winnipeg and Vancouver can be lower outside summer, and Vancouver hotels near the terminal ease off.
- **Shorter queues:** with fewer ships in port on many days, shuttles, the Juneau tramway and popular viewpoints move faster, and last-minute excursion spots are easier to find.

## The honest costs
- **Weather:** May decks can be properly cold near ice; September brings the wettest weeks of the season.
- **Wildlife timing:** May whales are arriving rather than peaking, and salmon-run bears are a late-summer event. See [bear viewing in Alaska](/articles/bear-viewing-excursions-alaska/) if bears are your priority.
- **Operations:** at the very edges, some tours and shops run shorter hours or haven't opened. If one excursion matters, check its season before booking.
- **Daylight:** by late September, evenings come early and scenic cruising time shrinks.

> **Watch out:** if you are adding Denali, check land dates carefully. Park bus services and some lodges run a shorter season than the cruises, so a very early or very late sailing may limit what the land portion includes.

## Who each shoulder suits
**May suits** photographers who want snowy peaks and clear air, couples who want quiet ships, and anyone who prefers cold and dry to mild and wet. It also catches the Victoria Day long weekend for those who want to stretch a week off.

**September suits** value hunters, people hoping for bears or northern lights, and anyone who likes moody, misty scenery. It is ideal for retirees and couples without school-age children.

A September sailing also pairs well with a few days in Vancouver or the Rockies afterward, when summer crowds have gone but the weather is often still settled.

**Neither suits** wildlife maximalists, who should aim for late June to August, or families tied to school calendars.

> **Good to know:** the very first and last sailings of the season are sometimes repositioning cruises to or from California, Hawaii or Asia, with extra sea days. They can be excellent value, but check that the itinerary includes the Alaska ports you want. See [repositioning cruises explained](/articles/repositioning-cruises-explained/).

## How to find a good shoulder sailing
Compare two or three specific weeks rather than "May" or "September" in general, since the same cabin can carry onboard credit one week and nothing the next. Weigh the fare against your flights and hotel.
