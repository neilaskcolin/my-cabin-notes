October is foliage month, and the best seat for it is on a ship sailing the St. Lawrence and the Atlantic coast. Elsewhere, the Mediterranean is calming down after summer, west coast ships are heading south for the winter, and the Caribbean is still worth approaching with care.

## Your October options at a glance
- **Canada and New England:** peak fall colour, Canadian ports, and departures you can reach by train.
- **Mediterranean:** warm sea, softer crowds and shoulder-season fares.
- **West coast repositioning:** ships leave Vancouver and Seattle for Hawaii, California, Mexico and sometimes Asia.
- **Caribbean:** warm and quieter, but still in hurricane season.
- **Alaska:** closed. The final sailings depart in late September.

## The Canada and New England foliage cruise
This is the October classic. Itineraries typically run between Quebec City or Montreal and Boston or New York, or round trip from a US port, calling at places such as the Saguenay fjord, Charlottetown, Sydney, Halifax, Saint John and Bar Harbor. Colour usually peaks from late September to mid-October, arriving earlier in Quebec and later in southern New England.

For Ontarians and Quebecers, a Quebec City or Montreal departure can mean no flight at all, with VIA Rail or a drive to the pier. A one-way itinerary that ends in a US port still needs a return flight or train home, so price that in. [New England and Canada fall cruises](/articles/new-england-canada-fall-cruises/) covers the itineraries, and the [fall cruise cost guide](/articles/new-england-fall-cruise-cost-canada/) gives a realistic budget.

### How to pick a good foliage sailing
1. **Aim for the first half of October** for the best odds of colour along the whole route.
2. **Prefer itineraries with the Saguenay or a St. Lawrence day,** where the scenery from the ship is the point.
3. **Check embarkation and disembarkation ports,** since one-way routes change your travel costs.
4. **Bring a valid passport,** because these itineraries call at US ports.
5. **Book early.** This is a short season and the best sailings sell well ahead.

> **Canadian tip:** Thanksgiving falls on the second Monday of October. A sailing that spans the long weekend saves a vacation day, but those dates are the first to sell out, and you will miss turkey at home.

## The Mediterranean in October
October is one of the Mediterranean's most pleasant months: the sea is still warm, midday heat has eased and the summer crowds have thinned. Fares usually ease from summer highs, and flights from Toronto and Montreal follow. The late season also brings westbound transatlantic crossings for anyone wanting a slow trip home, described in [what transatlantic crossings are really like](/articles/transatlantic-crossings-what-like/).

## West coast sailings heading south
When the Alaska season ends, ships leave Vancouver and Seattle for their winter homes. That creates one-way Pacific coastal cruises, longer voyages to Hawaii and occasional Panama Canal or transpacific sailings. For Western Canadians, a Hawaii cruise that starts in Vancouver avoids the flight out, as explained in [Hawaii cruises from Vancouver](/articles/hawaii-cruises-from-vancouver/).

If you are set on seeing Alaska late in the season, look at the final September sailings instead. They offer low fares, autumn colour and an outside chance of the aurora, covered in [northern lights on an Alaska cruise](/articles/northern-lights-alaska-cruise/).

## The Caribbean in October
The Atlantic hurricane season peaks in September and stays active through much of October, easing later in the month. Ships reroute around storms rather than sail into them, but skipped ports and changed routes are more likely than in winter.

> **Watch out:** buy travel insurance when you pay the deposit, with trip interruption and delay cover. A policy bought once a storm is named usually excludes that storm. [What happens if a hurricane threatens your cruise](/articles/what-happens-hurricane-cruise-caribbean/) explains your rights.

## Choosing your October cruise
Foliage lovers should book a Canada and New England sailing early in the month. Travellers who want warmth without storm risk can choose the Mediterranean now, or wait for the Caribbean in December, covered in the [December guide](/articles/where-cruise-december-canadians/). And if Alaska was the plan, start pricing next year's late-September sailings now.
