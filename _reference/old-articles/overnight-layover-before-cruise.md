An overnight layover is fine before a cruise as long as it ends the day before you sail. Plan around three things: where you sleep, what happens to your checked bags, and how much buffer is left between your last flight and the ship.

## When does an overnight layover make sense before a cruise?
Most Canadians can reach Vancouver or Florida in one day. Overnights tend to come up if you start from a smaller airport such as Regina, Saskatoon, Thunder Bay, Kelowna or St. John's, if you're heading to a European or Asian departure port, or if the cheapest fare pairs a late arrival with an early departure.

The golden rule is to keep the layover ahead of your buffer day. Say a couple from Halifax is sailing from Fort Lauderdale on a Saturday. If they overnight in Toronto on Thursday and land in Florida on Friday, they're fine. If they fly on Friday, overnight in Toronto and land on Saturday morning, the whole cruise depends on one early flight. [Flying the day before vs same day](/articles/fly-day-before-cruise/) explains why that matters.

> **Watch out:** a layover that leaves you on a plane on embarkation morning is really a same-day arrival. If that flight is delayed or cancelled, you have no plan B.

## Choosing the layover hotel
### In or connected to the terminal
This is best for short nights. At YVR, the Fairmont Vancouver Airport sits inside the terminal building. At Toronto Pearson, the Sheraton Gateway connects to Terminal 3 by an indoor walkway. You pay more to skip the shuttle, but on a five-hour night that's usually worth it.

### Airport hotel with a shuttle
This is the value choice. Before you book, check when the first morning shuttle runs and whether it runs often. A 6 a.m. flight and a shuttle that starts at 5:30 don't mix well.

### Downtown hotel
This only makes sense with 12 hours or more and some daylight. In Toronto, the UP Express runs from Pearson to Union Station in about 25 minutes. In Vancouver, the Canada Line SkyTrain reaches downtown in under half an hour. Keep the plans modest: dinner and a walk, not a full sightseeing day.

## What happens to your checked bags?
- **One ticket:** bags are often checked through to your final airport, even overnight. That means you won't see them until you land, so look at the three-letter code on your bag tag before you leave the counter.
- **Separate tickets:** you always collect your bags and check in again, so you face the second airline's check-in cut-off and bag fees.
- **Heading to the US the next morning:** major Canadian airports such as Toronto, Montreal, Vancouver and Calgary have US preclearance, so you go through US customs before boarding. In winter peaks that line can be long, so arrive earlier than you would for a domestic flight.

## Pack an overnight carry-on
Pack it as though your checked bag might not reach you until the ship:
- Medication for the whole trip, plus passports and cruise documents
- A change of clothes and something to sleep in
- Toiletries in containers of 100 ml or less
- Phone and device chargers
- A swimsuit and a light layer, so day one on board isn't wasted

## A sample overnight that works
Here is how a traveller from Regina heading to a Saturday sailing from Fort Lauderdale might lay out the trip:

1. **Thursday afternoon:** fly Regina to Toronto, landing in the evening.
2. **Thursday night:** walk to a connected hotel, eat, and be in bed early.
3. **Friday morning:** back at the terminal two to three hours before departure to clear US preclearance.
4. **Friday midday:** land in Fort Lauderdale and check in near Port Everglades.
5. **Saturday:** a relaxed breakfast and an easy transfer to the ship.

## Insurance and missed connections
If you're on separate tickets and the first flight runs late, the second airline owes you nothing. Travel insurance with missed-connection and trip-interruption cover, bought soon after you pay your deposit, protects against exactly this. Some Canadian credit cards also include delay cover when you charge the flights to the card, so check the details. The trade-offs are compared in [cruise line air vs booking your own flights](/articles/cruise-line-air-vs-own-flights/), and [cruise insurance in Canada](/articles/travel-insurance-cruise-canadians/) covers what to look for in a policy.

## Make the stopover work for you
A well-placed overnight can take the stress out of a long journey. You arrive rested and your bags are sorted, and you might get a free evening in a new city. Just keep the extra day before the ship separate from the layover. For a trip with several flights, a licensed cruise consultant can route the flights and the hotel nights as one plan.
