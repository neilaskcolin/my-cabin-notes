A few weeks before sailing, an email arrives inviting you to bid for a better cabin. That is now the main way cruise lines sell their leftover balconies and suites. It can be a bargain, but only if you bid with a clear limit and accept that you lose control of where your new cabin is.

## How cruise upgrade bidding works
Royal Caribbean's RoyalUp, Celebrity's MoveUp and Norwegian's Upgrade Advantage are well-known examples, and other lines run similar schemes. The details vary, but the pattern is the same:

1. **You receive an invitation** if your booking and fare are eligible, usually by email or in the line's app.
2. **You choose categories and set an offer.** Some programs ask for a per-person amount for the whole cruise, others per person, per night. Read which before typing a number.
3. **The line decides close to departure**, often in the final days, based on what is unsold and what others offered.
4. **If accepted, your card is charged** and your booking moves to a cabin the line assigns.
5. **If not accepted, nothing is charged** and you keep your original cabin.

Accepted bids are generally non-refundable, and you may be able to change or withdraw an offer only until the line processes it. Confirm the rules for your program.

> **Watch out:** a winning bid moves you to a cabin the line picks. You could swap a quiet midship balcony for a suite above the theatre or at the very front of the ship. Suites can also carry higher daily gratuities, which adds to the real cost.

## Bidding with a clear limit
The only sensible yardstick is what the higher category costs to book outright today. Check the price on the line's site or ask your agent before you bid.

> **Quick math:** a couple in an Alaska balcony is offered a suite bid with a minimum of US$75 per person, per night. Over 7 nights that is US$1,050, roughly C$1,450. If booking that suite outright today costs US$1,200 more, the bid saves very little and you lose the choice of cabin. If the outright gap is US$3,000, a modest bid could be good value.

Bids tend to succeed where supply is soft: shoulder-season sailings, older ships, longer itineraries and categories with lots of empty cabins. Peak July sailings and holiday weeks rarely leave much to auction.

## The other ways up
| Route | What you pay | Do you choose the cabin? | How likely |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upgrade bid | Your offer, if accepted | No | Depends on unsold cabins |
| Paid upsell offer | A fixed price per person | Sometimes; ask | Occasional before sailing |
| Pier or onboard offer | A fixed fee on the day | Rarely | Hit and miss; see [last-minute upgrades at the pier](/articles/last-minute-cabin-upgrades-at-the-pier/) |
| Guarantee cabin bump | Nothing extra | No | Luck; see [should you book a guarantee cabin](/articles/guarantee-cabin-should-you-book/) |
| Loyalty or goodwill | Nothing extra | No | Rare, mostly top-tier members |
| Repricing before final payment | The fare difference | Yes | Best when the next category drops in price |

The last row is the one people overlook. If the price of a better category falls before final payment, you may be able to switch and pay only the difference, choosing your exact cabin. [Price drops after booking](/articles/price-drop-after-booking-cruise/) explains how.

## Canadian points to watch
Bids and upsells are almost always charged in US dollars. A card with no foreign transaction fee saves about 2.5% on the charge, which on a US$1,000 upgrade is about C$35. If you booked a promotion such as included drinks or onboard credit, ask whether it carries over to the new cabin before you bid.

## When not to bid
- You chose your cabin carefully for location, motion or noise.
- You are prone to seasickness and booked low and midship. See [the quietest cabins and where to book](/articles/quietest-cabins-where-to-book/).
- The minimum bid is close to the outright price.
- You are travelling with connecting cabins and a winning bid would split them.

## Getting the upgrade without the gamble
Upgrade value often starts at booking: pick a category priced close to the one above it, keep watching prices before final payment, and switch if the gap narrows. That way, any bid or bump that follows is a bonus, not a gamble.
