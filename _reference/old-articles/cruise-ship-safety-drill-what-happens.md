The muster drill is the one thing every passenger must do before the cruise really starts. On most big ships it's now quick and low-key: a video, a short walk and a scan. Here's exactly what happens and why it's taken so seriously.

## Why is the muster drill mandatory?
International maritime safety rules require every new passenger to receive safety instruction before or immediately on departure. Lines must be able to prove that everyone aboard knows where to go and what the alarm sounds like, which is why attendance is tracked person by person rather than assumed.

## Old drill versus new drill
Since around 2020, most big lines have switched from the traditional drill to a digital "e-muster".

| | Traditional drill | Digital e-muster |
|---|---|---|
| When | Everyone at once, at a fixed time | Any time after boarding, before a deadline |
| Safety talk | Live, in a crowded lounge or on deck | Video in the app or on cabin TV |
| Life jackets | Carried from your cabin | Usually stored at stations; shown on video |
| Check-in | Roll call or card scan | Card or app scan at your station |
| Time taken | 30–45 minutes | About 10–20 minutes |

## The digital drill, step by step
1. **Find your station letter.** It's on your cruise card or in the app, and an evacuation map is usually on the back of your cabin door.
2. **Watch the safety video** in the app or on your cabin TV. It covers the alarm, life jackets and how to reach your station.
3. **Walk to your assembly station** using the stairs. It may be an indoor lounge, a restaurant or an outdoor deck near the lifeboats.
4. **Get scanned.** A crew member checks in each person and may give a one-minute briefing.
5. **Carry on with your day.** You're done for the cruise.

Do it soon after boarding rather than in the last hour before sailing, when queues build at the stations. The [first hour aboard](/articles/what-to-do-first-hour-aboard-cruise/) guide fits it into the afternoon.

> **Watch out:** the app may need the ship's free wifi network to show the video. Connect to it as soon as you board, and keep cellular roaming off so your phone doesn't use the costly maritime network.

## The sound to remember
The general emergency alarm is seven or more short blasts followed by one long one. If you ever hear it for real, go to the same station with medication and a warm layer if they're within reach. What the other announcements mean is covered in [cruise ship emergency codes explained](/articles/cruise-ship-emergency-announcements-explained/).

## Families, mobility and special cases
- **Children** must be checked in too. If an alarm sounds while they're in the kids club, youth staff typically take them to the right station.
- **Mobility needs:** tell guest services at boarding. Crew can arrange help, and in a real emergency lifts aren't used (see [accessible cruising](/articles/accessible-cruising-mobility-ships/)).
- **Hearing loss:** ask for a visual or vibrating alert kit for your cabin.
- **Back-to-back cruises:** expect to repeat the drill for each new sailing.

## What if you skip it?
Your name stays on the incomplete list. Crew will page you, call your cabin and may come looking, sometimes delaying departure. Persistent refusal can mean being asked to leave the ship before it sails.
