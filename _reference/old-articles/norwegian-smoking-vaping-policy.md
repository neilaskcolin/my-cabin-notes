On Norwegian, you can smoke or vape only in marked outdoor areas and, on most ships, a designated corner of the casino. Cabins and balconies are off-limits without exception, and the cleaning fee for breaking that rule runs to several hundred dollars.

## Where can you smoke on a Norwegian ship?
- **Designated outdoor areas:** usually a section of an open deck, often near a pool bar, marked with signs and listed in the daily program
- **The casino:** a designated smoking section on most ships for guests who are playing, with tighter rules on newer ships
- **Cigar lounge:** some ships have a separate lounge for cigars; check your ship's deck plan

Ask at Guest Services on embarkation day if you can't find the signs. The [smoking and vaping rules on cruise ships](/articles/smoking-vaping-rules-cruise-ships/) page explains why lines keep shrinking these areas.

## Where you can't smoke on Norwegian
Your cabin and your balcony are smoke-free, as are restaurants, theatres, bars, corridors and children's areas. On Great Stirrup Cay, Norwegian's private island in the Bahamas, smoking is limited to marked spots.

## Vaping and e-cigarettes
Norwegian treats a vape exactly like a cigarette: you can only use it where smoking is allowed, and vaping on a balcony is fined the same way.

If you are flying to your ship, e-cigarettes and spare batteries must travel in your carry-on, not your checked bag, on Canadian and US flights. That matters for anyone flying from Calgary, Edmonton or Toronto to Seattle or Miami.

## What getting caught costs
Norwegian charges a cleaning fee, usually several hundred dollars, to your onboard account for smoking or vaping in a cabin or on a balcony. Repeat offenders can be disembarked at the next port at their own expense, which on an Alaska sailing could mean finding your own way home from Juneau.

> **Watch out:** cabin smoke detectors are sensitive, and stewards report smoke smells. Assuming a quick cigarette on the balcony will go unnoticed is the most expensive mistake on this page.

### Cannabis is a separate issue
> **Canadian tip:** cannabis is not allowed on Norwegian ships in any form, including edibles and vapes, even on sailings from Vancouver. Norwegian bans it fleet-wide, and Alaska sailings call at US ports such as Juneau and Ketchikan, where it is illegal under US federal law. [Why you can't take cannabis on a cruise](/articles/cruise-cannabis-canada-why-no/) explains the rules.

## Bringing tobacco home to Canada
Canada's personal exemption after 48 hours away includes 200 cigarettes or 50 cigars per adult. Cigarettes not stamped as Canadian duty paid can still attract a special duty at the border, so the ship's duty-free cartons are often less of a bargain than they look.

## Who this affects most on Norwegian
Norwegian has been reducing smoking space on its newer ships, so the experience depends heavily on which ship you are on. Older ships usually have a casino section and an outdoor deck area; the Prima class ships have smaller and fewer smoking areas. If smoking matters to you, check the policy for your specific ship before booking rather than assuming the fleet is uniform.

## How Norwegian compares
| Line | Smoking in the casino | Cabins and balconies |
|---|---|---|
| **Norwegian** | Designated section on most ships; tighter on newer ships | Banned |
| [Royal Caribbean](/articles/royal-caribbean-smoking-vaping-policy/) | Designated section on many ships | Banned |
| [Carnival](/articles/carnival-smoking-vaping-policy/) | Designated section on most ships | Banned |

All three lines ban smoking in cabins and on balconies, so the real difference is the casino and how much outdoor space each ship sets aside. Norwegian's newest ships are the most restrictive of the three, while its older ships look much like Royal Caribbean's and Carnival's.
