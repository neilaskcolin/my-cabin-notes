Book the ship first and the plane second. Cruise cabins for Alaska 2027 are already selling and the best ones go early, while cash fares on short domestic flights rarely reward booking the day schedules open. The exceptions are points bookings and one-way trips home from Anchorage, which are worth locking in sooner.

## Why the cruise comes first
Early cruise bookers get the widest choice of cabins, including the balconies and midship locations that matter most on a scenery-driven trip like Alaska, plus early-booking fares and promotional extras. On most standard fares the deposit is refundable until final payment, often about 75–90 days before sailing, so booking early mostly costs flexibility rather than money. Check your fare type, though, since some cheaper fares come with non-refundable deposits (see [cruise deposits and final payment](/articles/cruise-deposits-final-payment/)).

Booking flights before the cruise is confirmed is the classic mistake: you end up with non-refundable tickets for dates when your preferred cabin has sold out. The wider case for booking now is in [booking 2027 sailings now vs waiting](/articles/booking-2027-now-vs-waiting/).

## Your 2027 timeline, in order
1. **Now to the end of 2026: choose and book the sailing.** Most 2027 flights are already loaded, so set fare alerts for your route and dates the same day. If you are paying with points, search for award seats now.
2. **January to March 2027: watch wave season.** Cruise promotions peak, and a booked cruise can often be moved to a better offer before final payment. [Wave season explained](/articles/wave-season-explained-canada/) covers what to look for.
3. **Three to six months before sailing: buy cash flights.** For a June sailing, that is roughly December to March; for August, February to May. Fares on routes like Calgary–Vancouver tend to be at their best in this window.
4. **By final payment: decide on cruise line air.** If you want the line's protected flights, get them quoted before you pay in full, as the trade-offs in [cruise line air vs your own flights](/articles/cruise-line-air-vs-own-flights/) explain.
5. **Inside six to eight weeks: stop waiting.** Short-haul fares usually climb sharply, especially in summer.
6. **The day before sailing: be in Vancouver,** with a hotel booked near the terminal.

> **Canadian tip:** 2027 long weekends that push up flight prices include Victoria Day (Monday May 24), Canada Day (Thursday July 1), the August civic holiday (Monday August 2) and Labour Day (Monday September 6). Alberta and BC schools finish in late June, so July and August sailings and flights cost more.

## Common booking mistakes
- **Buying non-refundable flights for a cruise you might still cancel.** Match the flexibility of your flights to your cruise deposit.
- **Leaving one-way flights home from Anchorage too late.** Seats are fewer and the route usually connects through Vancouver or Seattle; [where one-way sailings end](/articles/anchorage-vs-whittier-vs-seward/) explains the logistics.
- **Forgetting the pre-night.** Book the flight for the day before sailing, never the same morning.
- **Not using the 24-hour window.** Air Canada and WestJet have generally allowed a free cancellation shortly after booking on most fares, so you can lock a good price and still correct mistakes. Confirm the rule for your ticket.
- **Never checking the price again after you book.** Cruise fares drop too, and you may be able to claim the difference before final payment (see [how to ask for cruise price protection](/articles/cruise-price-protection-asking-for-it/)).

## Package air vs DIY on timing
Air booked with the cruise carries delay protection: if a flight goes wrong, getting you to the ship becomes the line's problem. DIY air is often cheaper on the short Alberta–Vancouver hop, while package air can win in peak season or on longer one-way itineraries. The honest move is quoting both in the same week.
