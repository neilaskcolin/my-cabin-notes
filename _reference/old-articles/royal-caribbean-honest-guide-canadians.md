Royal Caribbean is right for you if you want a ship that works like a resort, with something happening from breakfast to midnight. It suits families, groups and active couples best. If your ideal cruise is a quiet library and a long dinner, you'll likely be happier on a smaller ship or a different line.

## What a Royal Caribbean cruise feels like
The line's signature is the Royal Promenade, an indoor street of bars, shops and parades running through the middle of most ships. Around it sit the things Royal Caribbean is known for: FlowRider surf simulators, rock-climbing walls, ice shows, and full productions of musicals in the main theatre.

The biggest ships add more. Oasis-class ships have Central Park, a planted open-air walkway, and an outdoor aqua theatre. Icon-class ships, introduced in 2024 and now the largest cruise ships afloat, add a full water park and neighbourhoods aimed at different groups, including Surfside for young families.

The mood is lively and casual. Pool decks are busy on sea days, the crowd skews younger than Holland America or Princess, and school holidays bring a lot of children.

## Which Royal Caribbean ship? The classes compared
The fleet varies more than most, so the ship matters as much as the brand.

| Class | Example ships | What it's like | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Icon | Icon, Star of the Seas | Largest; water park, neighbourhoods | Families with young kids |
| Oasis | Wonder, Utopia, Symphony | Central Park, Boardwalk, zip line | Families, groups, first-timers |
| Quantum | Ovation, Anthem, Odyssey | North Star capsule, indoor sports hall | Alaska from Seattle, mixed groups |
| Freedom and Voyager | Liberty, Navigator, Mariner | Royal Promenade, FlowRider on many | Value, short Caribbean trips |
| Radiance | Radiance, Serenade, Brilliance | Lots of glass, calmer | Alaska scenery, couples |
| Vision | Enchantment, Rhapsody | Smallest and oldest | Budget, shorter sailings |

Ship deployments change every year, so treat the "best for" column as a guide and check which ship actually sails your dates.

## Who Royal Caribbean suits, and who it doesn't
It suits families with children of about 3 to 15, multigenerational groups who want everyone occupied, friends travelling together, and first-timers who aren't sure what they'll enjoy. Kids' programming is free from age 3, with a paid nursery on many larger ships; the details are on the [Royal Caribbean kids club ages](/articles/royal-caribbean-kids-club-ages/) page.

It suits less well if you dislike crowds, want refined dining every night, or prefer port-intensive itineraries with long days ashore. Celebrity, Royal Caribbean's sister line, is the usual step up in calm and food. Couples who want to avoid the bustle should read [quiet adult-focused ships without waterslides](/articles/quiet-ships-without-waterslides/).

> **Good to know:** Royal Caribbean's private island in the Bahamas, Perfect Day at CocoCay, is included on many short Bahamas and Caribbean sailings. Beaches and pools are free; the Thrill Waterpark and the adults-only Hideaway Beach cost extra.

## Royal Caribbean from Canada: the practical side
Royal Caribbean sells fares in Canadian dollars to Canadian residents, which removes exchange-rate guesswork on the fare itself. Onboard spending still posts in US dollars, so a no-foreign-transaction-fee card helps.

For the Caribbean, most sailings leave Miami, Fort Lauderdale, Port Canaveral or Tampa, and winter flights to Florida are frequent from Toronto and Montreal, with seasonal service from western cities. For Alaska, the line leans on Seattle round trips, with some Vancouver sailings in certain seasons. Canada and New England cruises run in the fall from the US northeast. > **Canadian tip:** Royal Caribbean's Crown & Anchor Society loyalty program and Celebrity's program have offered status matching. If you hold status with one, ask about it before booking the other; [loyalty match programs](/articles/loyalty-match-programs-cruise-lines/) explains how these work.

### How to choose the right Royal Caribbean sailing
1. Pick the ship class first, using the table above and the ages in your group.
2. Check how many sea days the itinerary has. More sea days suit the big ships; port-heavy trips suit smaller ones.
3. Avoid March break and summer if you want fewer children on board.
4. Price the extras: [gratuities](/articles/royal-caribbean-gratuities-per-day/), drinks and wifi add a lot on this line.
5. Compare against the [best cruise lines for family reunions](/articles/best-cruise-lines-family-reunions/) if you are planning a group trip.

## Finding the right Royal Caribbean ship for your trip
Because the fleet ranges from 1990s Vision-class ships to the Icon class, two Royal Caribbean cruises can feel like different holidays. Match the ship, not just the brand, to the people travelling, and the line is easy to enjoy.
