The health questionnaire takes about a minute and asks one basic thing: are you carrying something contagious onto a ship with thousands of people? Answer it honestly. A yes leads to a conversation with the medical team, and most of those end with you boarding.

## Why do cruise lines ask health questions?
A ship is a closed community with shared dining, lifts and handrails. A stomach bug such as norovirus can spread fast, and one sick passenger on day one can mean hundreds by day four. Ships sailing from US ports also report gastrointestinal illness to US public health authorities, so lines take screening seriously. The questionnaire is the cheapest way to catch it before it boards. The broader picture is in [how safe cruise ships really are](/articles/whats-safe-cruise-ships-honest-answer/).

## What the questionnaire asks
Most versions are a short list of yes-or-no questions:
- Vomiting or diarrhea in the past few days
- Fever, chills or flu-like symptoms
- Close contact with someone who has those symptoms
- Sometimes, other questions linked to current public health advice

It does not ask about your diabetes, heart condition or medications. Those don't stop you boarding, though they matter for [travel insurance and the ship's clinic](/articles/cruise-medical-care-costs/).

## The forms that are often confused

| Form | What it covers | When |
|---|---|---|
| Health questionnaire | Contagious symptoms right now | Online check-in, sometimes again at the terminal |
| Special assistance form | Wheelchairs, scooters, hearing or vision needs | Weeks before sailing |
| Medical equipment notice | CPAP, oxygen, dialysis, sharps | Weeks before sailing |
| Pregnancy declaration | Weeks pregnant at end of cruise, doctor's letter | At booking or check-in |

For example, CPAP users should read [distilled water for a CPAP on board](/articles/cpap-distilled-water-cruise-ships/), and expectant parents should check [cruising while pregnant, rules by line](/articles/cruising-while-pregnant-rules-by-line/).

## What happens if you tick yes
A crew member will usually send you to a medical team at the terminal. They'll ask when symptoms started and how you feel now. Mild or resolved symptoms often mean you board, sometimes with a request to stay in your cabin until you're clear. Active vomiting or a high fever can mean being refused boarding for that sailing.

> **Watch out:** cruise lines generally don't refund a passenger turned away for illness. Trip cancellation insurance covering sudden illness is what gets your money back, so check your policy before you travel. If you feel unwell in the days before, read [what to do if you're sick the day before a cruise](/articles/what-if-you-get-sick-day-before-cruise/).

### Why honesty matters
Giving false answers breaches the terms you agree to at check-in, and symptoms that show up on board usually lead to cabin isolation anyway. You'd miss days of the cruise either way, and expose others.

## Understanding this routine but important step
Fill the questionnaire in honestly during [online check-in](/articles/cruise-check-in-online-documents/), keep your insurance details handy, and if you're unwell, call your insurer before you head to the port. It's a small step that protects everyone on board, including you.
