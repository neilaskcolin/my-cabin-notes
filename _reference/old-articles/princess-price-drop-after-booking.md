Princess generally lets you move to a lower advertised fare before final payment, as long as it is for the same ship, sailing and cabin category. You keep your cabin and the balance comes down. Once final payment has gone through, the chance of money back is essentially gone.

## Does Princess honour price drops before final payment?
Generally, yes. If the fare for your cabin category drops and the new price is publicly available, you can ask for your booking to be repriced. The cabin number and booking reference stay the same; only the amount owed changes.

The catch is that a reprice moves you onto the new fare, with its own terms. If your original booking came with onboard credit, a reduced deposit or a Princess Plus promotion, the new fare might not. Policies change, so confirm the current terms with Princess or whoever holds your booking before relying on any of this.

## How to ask Princess for a lower fare
1. Note your ship, sailing date, cabin category, fare type and final payment date.
2. Check the current price for the same category on Princess's Canadian site, in Canadian dollars, and make sure you are comparing the same fare type with the same inclusions.
3. Ask whoever holds your booking to reprice it. If you booked direct, call Princess.
4. Before agreeing, compare what you gain with any perks you lose.
5. Get a revised confirmation and check the new balance and that your cabin number has not changed.

## What happens after final payment?
Cash refunds stop. What remains is occasional goodwill, such as an upgrade at the price difference, or very rarely some onboard credit. One polite ask is reasonable; more than that rarely helps. The general rules are in [cruise price drop after final payment](/articles/price-drop-after-booking-cruise/).

## The Alaska timing that matters
Alaska demand shapes Princess pricing more than anything else. The line sells a lot of its summer early, and July and August balconies on Vancouver sailings tend to climb rather than fall.

| When you are looking | What Princess Alaska fares tend to do |
|---|---|
| Season release, 18–24 months out | Solid early fares and the best cabin choice |
| Wave season, January to March | The richest promotions on the summer ahead |
| Spring before sailing | Discounts on May and September shoulder dates |
| Final months before sailing | Peak dates rarely drop; deals mostly on shoulder dates and inside cabins |

The strategy in [when do Alaska cruise prices drop](/articles/when-do-alaska-cruise-prices-drop/) applies directly: book early on a refundable fare, then reprice into a later sale if one appears. [Wave season explained](/articles/wave-season-explained-canada/) covers what the January to March promotions usually look like.

## Princess Plus and Premier: compare the whole package
Princess sells fares with and without its bundles, which add drinks, MedallionNet wifi and crew appreciation. A lower standard fare may not beat a slightly higher fare that includes Princess Plus, if you would otherwise buy the package. Total both versions before repricing, as covered in [cruise line sales decoded](/articles/cruise-line-sales-decoded/).

## Repricing a cruisetour
Cruisetours, which combine the cruise with land nights at Princess's Denali and Fairbanks lodges, are priced as packages. Repricing one involves the whole package, not just the cruise. Ask specifically, because a sale on the cruise-only fare does not always carry over.

## For Canadian bookers
Princess quotes Canadian residents in CAD. If you spot a "lower" price in US dollars on a US site, convert it before asking for a change, and remember that bookings are normally repriced in the currency you booked in.
