A Tahiti cruise is one of the most expensive cruise trips a Canadian can take, but the total depends heavily on how you do it. Sailing from Los Angeles on a big ship can cost roughly half as much as flying to Papeete for a week on a small one.

## Two ways to cruise French Polynesia
| | Long voyage from the US West Coast | Small-ship cruise from Papeete |
|---|---|---|
| Typical ships | Mainstream lines, mid-size to large ships | Small premium and luxury ships based in Tahiti |
| Length | Usually two weeks or more, round trip | Often 7–10 nights, round trip |
| Time in the islands | A few port days among many sea days | Every day in the islands |
| Fare per person | Inside from roughly US$1,200–2,000 on promotion | Commonly US$3,000–6,000+ for a week |
| What's included | Standard mainstream inclusions | Often drinks, and sometimes gratuities |
| Flights from Canada | Return to Los Angeles or San Francisco | Return to Papeete via a US hub |
| Rough total per couple | C$6,000–11,500 | C$12,500–25,000 |

These are broad ranges at recent prices, and fares in this niche vary a lot by ship, season and promotion. Treat them as a starting point and price your actual sailing.

## The long voyage from California
Some mainstream lines run round trips from Los Angeles or San Francisco that sail to Tahiti, Moorea and Bora Bora, often with stops in Hawaii or the Marquesas along the way. Occasionally a fall repositioning from Vancouver heads to Australia through the South Pacific.

For a couple in an inside cabin, a realistic budget looks like this:
- **Fare with taxes:** about C$3,300–5,500
- **Flights to California:** C$600–1,600 for two, cheapest from Vancouver and Calgary
- **Hotel the night before:** C$250–400
- **Gratuities:** about C$700–800 for 16 nights
- **Drinks:** C$500–1,000 over two-plus weeks
- **Excursions:** C$600–1,500, since lagoon tours in Bora Bora are not cheap
- **Travel insurance:** C$300–800

The catch is time: you spend more days crossing the Pacific than in Polynesia. If you love sea days, it is excellent value. Our guide to [repositioning cruises](/articles/repositioning-cruises-explained/) explains similar long one-way voyages.

## The small-ship week from Papeete
This is the classic Tahiti cruise: small ships that stay in French Polynesia, sailing between islands such as Huahine, Raiatea, Taha'a, Bora Bora and Moorea, often anchoring in lagoons where big ships cannot go.

### Flights to Papeete
Return flights from Canada typically cost C$1,400–2,500 per person at recent prices, via Los Angeles or San Francisco. The Pacific leg is often overnight, so plan at least one night in Papeete before sailing.

> **Watch out:** with only a few flights a day to Papeete, a missed connection can mean a missed ship. Book a night or two in Tahiti before embarkation and buy insurance with missed-connection cover, as explained in [travel insurance for Canadian cruisers](/articles/travel-insurance-cruise-canadians/).

### What is often included
Some small-ship fares include drinks, gratuities and water sports from a platform at the back of the ship, which narrows the gap with a mainstream fare once you add up what you would otherwise pay. Check what your fare covers before comparing.

## Extending with a resort stay
Many Canadians who make the long trip add a few nights in an overwater bungalow on Bora Bora or Moorea. That can easily cost more per night than the cruise, so budget it separately. Inter-island flights add more.

> **Good to know:** French Polynesia uses the CFP franc, and prices ashore are high because almost everything is imported. A no-foreign-transaction-fee credit card helps, and lunch in Bora Bora can cost more than you expect.

## Entry and timing
Canadians currently do not need a visa for a short tourist stay in French Polynesia, but check Government of Canada travel advice before booking, and make sure your passport is valid well beyond your return date. Your provincial health plan will cover very little this far from home, so travel medical insurance is essential. Tahiti is in the same time zone as Hawaii, so jet lag from western Canada is modest.

The drier months from May to October are the most popular. ## Planning the trip properly
With long flights, limited connections and a possible resort extension, price every piece together before committing. Decide first between weeks at sea from California and a week in the islands, since that choice sets the budget more than anything else.
