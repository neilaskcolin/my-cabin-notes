There is no official resale market for cruise cabins, and lines tie each booking to named passengers for manifests, border checks and security. That sounds bleak, but in practice most travellers who can't go still recover a good share of their money through the options below.

## Can you sell a cruise you can't use?
Not in the way you would sell a concert ticket. Mainstream lines do not run resale platforms, and the person sailing must be the person on the booking.

What many lines do allow is a guest name change, sometimes called a substitution. That can let a friend or family member take your place, and you settle up with them privately. Selling to a stranger through that route is possible in principle, but it carries real risk for both sides.

## Your options, and what each typically returns

| Option | When it usually works | What you typically get back |
|---|---|---|
| Name change | Line permits it, before its cutoff; fee may apply | Booking value passes to the new guest; you settle privately |
| Move to a later sailing | Before final payment on many fares | Value carried over; fare difference applies |
| Cancel, refundable fare | Before final payment | Full refund, less any fees |
| Cancel, non-refundable deposit fare | Before final payment | Deposit kept, sometimes returned as cruise credit |
| Cancel after final payment | Sliding penalty scale | Partial refund, shrinking to little or nothing near sailing |
| Insurance claim | Covered reason such as illness | Non-refundable costs, up to policy limits |
| Line cancels or changes the cruise | Line's decision | Refund or credit, sometimes with a bonus |

The details of refundable and non-refundable fares are in [cruise fare classes explained](/articles/cruise-fare-classes-refundable/). If the line itself cancels, see [what happens when the cruise line cancels](/articles/what-happens-cruise-cancelled-by-line/).

## How name changes work
Policies differ a lot between lines, so ask before assuming. Common rules include:

- A deadline, often tied to final payment, after which a name change may be treated as a cancellation.
- A per-person fee, or a requirement to reprice the cabin at today's fare.
- On some lines, at least one original guest has to remain on the booking.
- The new guest's name and details must match their passport exactly.
- Perks tied to the original guest, such as loyalty benefits, senior or resident rates, or casino offers, may not carry over.

Group bookings are often more forgiving until the group's names deadline, as explained in [what happens if someone drops out of a group cruise](/articles/what-if-someone-drops-out-group-cruise/).

> **Watch out:** cruise bookings offered for sale on classified sites and social media attract scams. Don't exchange money with someone you don't know until the line or agent has confirmed in writing that the new name is on the booking.

## If only one of two guests can't go
The other person can usually still sail, but cabin fares assume two guests. Dropping to one can trigger a single supplement, so the remaining guest may pay noticeably more per person. Get a revised quote first, and consider swapping in a friend instead (see [the single supplement explained](/articles/single-supplement-how-to-dodge/)).

## Why insurance is the real safety net
Because resale is not an option, cancellation cover does the heavy lifting. Buy it at deposit, not at final payment, so a problem that appears in between is covered. Check whether your policy covers the reason you can't travel, such as your own illness or a family emergency. Cover through a Canadian credit card may cap the amount per person, so compare it against your non-refundable total.

If your reason may not be covered, [cancel-for-any-reason insurance](/articles/cancel-for-any-reason-cruise-insurance-explained/) is worth knowing about for future trips. The basics are in [cruise insurance in Canada](/articles/travel-insurance-cruise-canadians/).

## What to do first if plans change
1. Find your fare type, final payment date and cancellation schedule.
2. If insured, call the insurer before cancelling and ask what documents they need.
3. Ask the line or your agent about a name change or a move to a later date.
4. Only then cancel, choosing refund or credit if both are offered.

If you end up with credit, [future cruise credits explained](/articles/future-cruise-credit-explained/) covers how to use it before it expires.

## The honest bottom line
You can't sell a cruise like a ticket, but acting early keeps most doors open. The week you know you can't travel is the week to make calls, while the fare is still flexible and the deadlines are still ahead of you.
