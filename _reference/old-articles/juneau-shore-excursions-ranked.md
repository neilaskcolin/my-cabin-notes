If you can afford only one paid outing in Juneau, make it whale watching; if you want the most for the least, ride a shuttle to Mendenhall Glacier and walk the trails yourself. Everything else, from the tram to the helicopters, depends on weather and budget.

## Why Juneau is the excursion port

Alaska's capital has no road connection to the rest of North America, but it has more within an hour of the dock than any other cruise stop: a drive-up glacier, a whale-rich bay, an alpine tram and an icefield reachable by helicopter. It is also busy, and the city now limits daily cruise passenger numbers under an agreement with the lines. Plan ahead rather than deciding on the pier.

### 1. Mendenhall Glacier, on your own

The glacier sits about 20 kilometres from downtown, across a lake from the visitor centre. Shuttles run from the cruise docks, and once there you can walk the easy Nugget Falls trail to a waterfall beside the ice. It costs a fraction of a guided tour and gives you a real glacier up close. For a family of four, the saving can cover one whale-watching ticket.

### 2. Whale watching from Auke Bay

In summer, humpbacks feed in the waters around Juneau so reliably that many operators promise a refund or partial credit if you don't see one. You might see bubble-net feeding, where whales work together to trap fish, and orcas are an occasional bonus. This is the most recommended excursion in the port.

### 3. The combo tour

Packaging Mendenhall and whales into one five-to-six-hour trip is Juneau's signature product, and it usually costs less than booking both through the ship separately. For a first visit, it is the default right answer.

### 4. Mount Roberts Tramway

The tram rises from beside the cruise docks to trails, a nature centre and views over the channel. Check the mountain from your balcony on the morning: if the summit is in cloud, skip it.

### 5 and 6. The helicopter splurges

A helicopter flight to land on the Juneau Icefield is spectacular, and the dog-sled version adds a ride with a summer training team. Both are weather-dependent; flights get cancelled in low cloud, usually with a refund. Book them as your trip's one treat or skip them without guilt.

> **Quick math:** a couple doing the combo tour through the ship might spend around US$500, roughly C$690. Doing Mendenhall by shuttle and booking whales independently could come closer to US$400, roughly C$550, saving enough for dinner ashore. The trade-off is that the ship won't wait if an independent tour runs late; see [ship excursions vs independent](/articles/cruise-excursion-through-ship-vs-independent/).

## Free and cheap things in town

Downtown is compact and walkable from the docks. Browse South Franklin Street, visit the Alaska State Capitol, step into the Red Dog Saloon, or hike the start of the Perseverance Trail for forest and old mining ruins. A salmon dinner in town is a good way to finish the day.

> **Watch out:** peak-week whale and helicopter slots sell out well before sailing. Juneau is the port to book ahead, not at the pier, especially in July and early August.

## How Juneau compares with the other ports

Juneau is where most people spend their excursion money, which makes the other ports good places to save. ## Paying for Juneau

Excursions are charged in US dollars, so pay with a card that has no foreign-transaction fee. If your booking came with onboard credit, Juneau is where it does its best work: US$200–300 of credit can cover much of a ship-booked whale trip for two. That is worth asking about before you pay the brochure price.
