Somebody always ends up chasing deposits, answering "which deck is quietest?" fifteen times and keeping the spreadsheet going. Cruise lines know this person exists, and group contracts reward them. The catch is that most of the reward only appears when the trip is booked as a proper group.

## The headline reward: the tour conductor credit
The main prize is the **tour conductor credit**: typically one free berth for every 16 paying guests, which most groups reach at about 8 cabins. It covers one person's cruise fare. It doesn't cover the whole cabin, taxes and port fees, gratuities or flights.

Bigger groups earn more: around 16 cabins usually brings a second credit, and large groups have more room to ask for extras.

> **Example:** nine cabins book a 7-night Alaska sailing at US$1,100 per person, so the organiser earns one credit worth about US$1,100 (roughly C$1,500). Applied to their own cabin, their partner still pays a full fare, and both pay taxes and gratuities. Spread across the group instead, it's about US$120 off each cabin.

## What else groups can get
Amenities vary widely by line, ship and group size. Treat this as a list of things to ask about, not a promise.

| Perk | How often it's offered | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Group rate held for the block | Common | Protects against rising prices; compare with public sales |
| Onboard credit per cabin | Sometimes | Amounts vary; usually can't be cashed out |
| Private cocktail party | Often, for larger groups | Times and spaces are limited |
| Reserved dining tables | On request | Ask early, especially for set dining times |
| Private meeting or event space | On request | Subject to availability |
| Welcome gift or group photo | Sometimes | Depends on line and contract |

How onboard credit works, and why it can't usually be withdrawn as cash, is covered in [onboard credit explained](/articles/onboard-credit-explained/).

## What perks don't cover
> **Watch out:** a "free cruise" for the organiser still comes with bills. Taxes and port fees, gratuities, flights, drinks, excursions and the travelling companion's fare are all extra. On a 7-night sailing, those can add up to well over C$1,000 for a couple.

## Ways to use the reward
- **Keep it.** The usual choice, and fair pay for months of admin. Just tell the group.
- **Split it between co-organisers.** Two people sharing the work can share the credit.
- **Spread it across every cabin** as a small discount for everyone.
- **Fund a shared event**, such as a private dinner or a group excursion.
- **Raffle it** at a fundraiser or club event, then donate the proceeds.

## How to make sure you actually get it
### Book as a group from day one
Perks attach to a group block. Ten families booking separately, even on the same sailing, usually earn nothing. Bookings can sometimes be moved into a group later, but don't count on it.

### Get the amenities in writing
Ask what the group contract includes, and keep a copy. If it isn't written down, it may not show up on board.

### Protect the count until final payment
Credits are calculated on paid guests. If cabins cancel before final payment and the group drops below the threshold, the credit can disappear. [Group cruise deadlines](/articles/group-cruise-deadlines-deposits/) shows how to keep the block intact.
