Packing cubes earn their place on a cruise because cabins are small and you unpack once. A cube system lets you move from suitcase to drawer in minutes and keeps the room livable all week.

## How much storage does a cruise cabin have?
Less floor space than a hotel room, but more storage than it first looks. A typical inside or balcony cabin runs roughly 150–200 sq ft, with a wardrobe, a set of drawers, shelves at the desk or vanity, and space under the bed. Sizes and layouts differ by category, as explained in [cruise cabins explained](/articles/cruise-cabins-explained-inside-oceanview-balcony-suite/).

Check every cupboard before deciding you're short of space. Drawers often hide behind mirrored doors, and there is usually a shelf above the wardrobe rail.

## A simple cube plan
Use one colour per person, then one cube per category. Here is how a couple might pack for a 7-night Alaska sailing from Vancouver (the layers themselves are covered in [packing for Alaska in May vs September](/articles/alaska-packing-may-vs-september/)):

| Cube | What goes in | Where it lives on board |
|---|---|---|
| Large cube | Tops and fleece layers | One drawer each |
| Medium cube | Trousers, jeans, leggings | Wardrobe shelf |
| Small cube | Underwear and socks | Top drawer |
| Slim cube | Swimwear and gym kit | Bottom drawer |
| Pouch | Chargers, adapters, lanyard | Desk drawer |
| Laundry bag | Dirty clothes | Wardrobe floor |

Hang dressier outfits on hangers, rather than in cubes, so they don't crease. A few slim hangers of your own help, since wardrobe hangers are sometimes in short supply.

> **Example:** on a Caribbean week from Florida, the same couple could drop the fleece cube and add a beach cube with a cover-up, water shoes and a dry bag. Fewer bulky layers means one checked bag between two may be enough.

## Small extras that make a cabin work
- **Magnetic hooks.** Cabin walls on many ships are metal, so hooks hold lanyards, hats and the daily program. Bring a couple and test them gently.
- **An over-the-door organizer.** Clear pockets on the bathroom door hold sunscreen, toiletries and small items off the tiny counter.
- **A hanging toiletry bag.** Bathroom shelves are narrow, and a bag on the hook keeps things from sliding.
- **A collapsible day bag.** For port days and the walk to the pool.
- **A USB charging hub.** Outlets are few, but power bars with surge protection are usually confiscated, as explained in [extension cord rules on cruise ships](/articles/extension-cord-cruise-ship-rules/).

## Compression cubes and airline limits
Compression cubes squeeze bulky layers to about two-thirds of their size, which is handy for Alaska fleeces and rain jackets. They don't reduce weight, though, and a tightly packed bag can quickly pass your airline's 23 kg limit. Weigh bags at home, and read [luggage rules when flying to a cruise](/articles/luggage-rules-flying-to-cruise/).

## Embarkation day and the last night
Checked bags may not reach your cabin until early evening. Keep one cube with a change of clothes and a swimsuit in your carry-on, along with passports and medication.

On the last night, most lines collect bags from your corridor around bedtime. Cubes make this easy: zip them up, load them in, and keep tomorrow's clothes and toiletries in your carry-on. [Disembarkation morning](/articles/disembarkation-morning-getting-off-ship/) covers what happens next.

## Pack less to begin with
Cubes organize; they don't shrink a week's wardrobe. Plan outfits around a few colours, use the ship's laundry once if needed, and check [what not to pack for a cruise](/articles/what-not-to-pack-for-a-cruise/) before you start.
