There is no magic hour. Cruise lines change fares when cabins sell or promotions launch, and that can happen at any time of day, so booking at 2 a.m. will not reliably save you a cent.

## Where the "best time to book" idea comes from
It is borrowed from airline folklore about booking on a Tuesday afternoon, which itself has been largely debunked. Cruise pricing is run by revenue-management systems that respond to how quickly a sailing sells. Prices move over weeks and months, not hours, and nobody has shown a repeatable pattern by time of day.

The same goes for clearing cookies or browsing in a private window. It does no harm, but there is no good evidence it changes the fare a cruise line shows you.

## When timing within the day does matter
There are a few moments where the clock genuinely counts.

### Sale launches and new itineraries
When a line opens a new season or launches a big sale, it usually announces when. The best cabins on popular sailings, such as midship balconies for summer Alaska or connecting cabins over March Break, can go in the first hours. Being online when bookings open is about choice, not price.

### Promotions that end at midnight
Sales often expire at a stated time, typically in US Eastern time. If you are in Calgary or Vancouver, a "midnight" deadline may land at 10 p.m. or 9 p.m. your time. Check the time zone in the fine print.

> **Good to know:** many lines and travel agents can place a short courtesy hold on a cabin, often for a day or a few days, with no deposit. That gives you time to check flights and talk it over without losing the cabin or the promotion.

## What to do instead of chasing the clock
1. **Pick two or three target sailings** and note the full price, including taxes, fees and any extras, so you have a baseline.
2. **Set up price alerts** so changes come to you. The [cruise price alerts guide](/articles/how-to-set-cruise-price-alerts/) walks through the options.
3. **Book a refundable fare** once the price and cabin feel right. See [refundable vs non-refundable fares](/articles/cruise-fare-classes-refundable/) for the trade-off.
4. **Keep watching until final payment.** If the fare drops, many lines will adjust it, as explained in [price drops after booking](/articles/price-drop-after-booking-cruise/).
5. **Check the currency.** Some line websites let you price in CAD or USD. Compare the two using your card's exchange rate before paying.

## The timing that really moves prices
Season, demand and how far ahead you book make the real difference. Wave season in January to March brings the year's biggest promotional push, and shoulder months such as May and September are usually cheaper than peak summer. [Wave season explained for Canadians](/articles/wave-season-explained-canada/) and [booking 2027 now vs waiting](/articles/booking-2027-now-vs-waiting/) cover those bigger decisions.

## The short version
Book when the cabin, price and promotion suit you, not when the clock says so. Being prepared with a baseline and a refundable fare beats any booking-hour trick.
