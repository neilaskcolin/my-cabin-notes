Almost every Alaska cruise passes whales, but some sailings put the odds firmly on your side. The three things that matter most are the month you sail, the ports on your itinerary, and how much time you spend on deck with binoculars.

## Which months are best for whales in Alaska?
Most humpbacks that feed in Southeast Alaska spend the winter in Hawaii and return in spring. Numbers build through May and peak in summer, when cooperative bubble-net feeding is most likely in places like Icy Strait and Chatham Strait.

| Month | Humpbacks | Other whales and wildlife | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| May | Building numbers | Gray whales migrating along the outer coast early in the season | Lower fares, fewer crowds |
| June | Good and rising | Orcas possible, sea otters, Steller sea lions | Long daylight for evening watching |
| July | Peak | Bubble-net feeding most likely | Alberta and BC schools are out, so busiest and priciest |
| August | Peak | Strong feeding activity | Still busy until late month |
| September | Still good | Whales feeding hard before migration | Shorter days, more rain, better prices |

> **Canadian tip:** if your family is tied to school holidays, late June and the last two weeks of August often balance whale activity against peak July pricing. Our guide to the [best month to cruise Alaska from Vancouver](/articles/best-month-cruise-alaska-vancouver/) compares the trade-offs.

## Which itineraries deliver the most whales?
Port choice matters more than ship choice. Look for these when comparing sailings:

### Juneau
Whale-watching boats leave from Auke Bay, about 20–25 minutes from the downtown docks, into waters humpbacks use all summer. Trips are frequent and reliable enough that some operators offer a refund or credit if no whales are seen. A long port day gives you time for a whale trip plus the Mendenhall Glacier. See [Juneau shore excursions ranked by value](/articles/juneau-shore-excursions-ranked/).

### Icy Strait Point
The waters off Hoonah are some of the most productive in the region, and whales can sometimes be seen from shore. Whale trips here are short hops from the dock.

### Glacier Bay and scenic cruising days
Glacier Bay National Park has designated whale waters where ships must slow down, and humpbacks are often seen near the entrance. Park rangers usually board and call out wildlife. Sitka, when included, adds sea otters and a good chance of humpbacks in Sitka Sound.

Ketchikan and Skagway are fine ports but rarely the best for whales, so a Ketchikan-Skagway-Juneau sailing should save its whale trip for Juneau.

## How to spot whales from the ship
- Bring binoculars, ideally 8x42 or 10x42, one pair per person.
- Watch for a blow first: a puff of mist, then a dark back, then perhaps a tail.
- Early morning and long summer evenings are productive and the decks are quieter.
- Midship on a low open deck gives a steadier view than the top deck.
- Listen for bridge or naturalist announcements, and keep the app or TV channel on.
- The first evening out of Vancouver can bring humpbacks or orcas in BC waters, so don't skip sail-away.

> **Good to know:** US rules require boats to stay at least 100 yards from humpbacks in Alaska waters. Excursions won't chase whales to touching distance, but curious whales sometimes come close on their own.

## Ship deck or a small-boat excursion?
The ship gives you whales in passing, often at a distance. A small boat gives you an hour or more on active feeding grounds, a naturalist, and often a hydrophone so you can hear the whales. If whales are a main reason you're going, book one dedicated trip.

> **Quick math:** two seats on a Juneau whale trip at US$180 each cost about US$360, roughly C$490–505. Onboard credit from your booking can often be applied if you book through the ship.

Peak-week departures sell out, so book a few months ahead. ## Picking your whale sailing
The short version: mid-June to August, a Juneau call with at least six hours in port, Icy Strait Point or Glacier Bay if you can get them, and a cabin or deck spot you'll actually use at dawn. Choosing your side of the ship matters less than you'd think, as our guide to [which side of the ship is best in Alaska](/articles/which-side-of-ship-alaska/) explains.
