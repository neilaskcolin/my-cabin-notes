Alaska is one of the most accessible big itineraries for wheelchair users. Its headline moments, from Glacier Bay to whales off the bow, happen from the deck of the ship. The work is in three choices: the cabin, the ports and the excursions.

## How to book an accessible Alaska cruise
Do these in order. The first step is the one most people leave too late.

1. **Hold the accessible cabin first.** Each ship has only a small share of fully accessible cabins, spread across inside, balcony and suite categories. Book it before flights or hotels.
2. **Get the specifications in writing.** Ask the line's accessibility team to confirm door widths, shower type (roll-in or tub with grab bars), bed height and whether the balcony has a ramped threshold.
3. **Check every port's berth for your sailing.** Docked calls mean a gangway to the pier. Tender calls mean transferring to a small boat, which many lines don't allow for powered chairs or scooters.
4. **File your needs with the line.** Most lines have a special assistance form. List your equipment's dimensions and weight, and whether you need help at embarkation.
5. **Book adapted excursions early.** Lift-equipped buses and accessible boat seats are limited.
6. **Arrange flights and Vancouver transfers last,** once the cabin is secure.

> **Canadian tip:** under Canadian accessible transportation rules, large Canadian airlines carry mobility aids at no charge, and on domestic routes you may qualify for a free seat for a required attendant. Ask Air Canada or WestJet when booking your flight to Vancouver.

## Which Alaska ports work best on wheels?
Berthing is decided sailing by sailing, so treat this as a guide and confirm with the line.

| Port | Usually docks or tenders | Near the pier | Accessible highlight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ketchikan | Docks; occasional tendering on very busy days | Flat waterfront, hills behind town | Creek Street area and waterfront shops |
| Juneau | Docks downtown | Flat downtown core | Mendenhall Glacier visitor centre and paved Photo Point trail |
| Skagway | Docks, a short distance from town | Flat Broadway, some boardwalk sections | White Pass railway, with accessible cars on request |
| Icy Strait Point | Now usually docks | Paths can be uneven in places | Cannery area and cultural performances |
| Sitka | Often docks at a terminal outside town, or tenders | Shuttle bus into town | Waterfront and historic sites |
| Glacier Bay, Hubbard, Tracy Arm or Endicott Arm | Scenic cruising, no port | From the ship | All of it, from your balcony or deck |

> **Watch out:** tides in Southeast Alaska can exceed five metres, so gangway angles change through the day. A ramp that is gentle at high tide can be steep at low tide. Ask the crew about the best time to go ashore, and accept help on the ramp.

## Excursions that work well
Accessible options in Alaska are better than many people expect. Some whale-watching operators have boats with step-free boarding and accessible washrooms. The Mendenhall visitor centre area is paved and step-free to the main viewpoint. The White Pass & Yukon Route railway can usually accommodate wheelchair users with notice. Book through the ship or an operator who confirms the adapted equipment in writing, rather than one who says "it should be fine".

If you are considering adding Denali by rail, the land portion needs its own checks for lodges and coaches. [Alaska cruisetours explained](/articles/alaska-cruisetours-denali-rail/) covers how those packages work.

## At the Vancouver end
Canada Place is a modern terminal with elevators and step-free routes, and assistance through check-in can be arranged in advance. From YVR, the Canada Line SkyTrain to Waterfront station is step-free, followed by a short, level walk to the terminal. Request boarding assistance when you book, not on the morning. The [Canada Place embarkation walkthrough](/articles/canada-place-embarkation-walkthrough/) explains each stage of the terminal.

Your provincial health plan covers very little in Alaska, so buy travel medical insurance that covers your pre-existing conditions and your equipment.

## Is Alaska the right accessible cruise for you?
For most wheelchair users, yes, as long as the cabin and berthing are secured early. If you want broader guidance on ship design, scooter rentals and cabin types across all destinations, read [accessible cruising: mobility-friendly ships and cabins](/articles/accessible-cruising-mobility-ships/). A licensed cruise consultant who regularly books accessible cabins can also hold specific cabin numbers and check berths for you.
