faq1q: Is it cheaper to book shore excursions independently?
faq1a: Usually. Independent operators often charge around 20–40% less than the ship for a similar tour, and in Alaska ports the gap is more often 15–30%. Compare like for like, including transport and park fees.
faq2q: What happens if an independent tour makes you miss the ship?
faq2a: The ship has no obligation to wait. You'd pay your own way to the next port, plus any hotel, and the port agent holds your passport details. Travel insurance may help with some costs, depending on the policy.
faq3q: Can I cancel a ship excursion if I change my mind?
faq3a: Most lines let you cancel without penalty up to a deadline before the port day, and refund automatically if the ship misses the port. Independent operators set their own terms, so check the weather and missed-port policy before paying.

It comes down to timing and risk. Book through the ship when the tour runs close to all-aboard, the port is unfamiliar or the logistics are complicated. Book independently when there's a generous buffer, the operator has a strong track record and the savings are real.

## What's the difference?

| | Ship excursion | Independent tour |
|---|---|---|
| Price | Higher | Often 15–40% lower |
| If the tour runs late | Ship waits or gets you to the next port | You pay to catch up |
| Group size | Often large coaches or boats | Varies; often smaller |
| Payment | Charged to your onboard account in USD | Paid to the operator, usually by card |
| If the ship misses the port | Automatic refund | Depends on the operator |
| Meeting point | At or near the pier | At the pier or a short walk away |
| Choice | The ship's partner operators | Wider range, including niche tours |

## What the ship's guarantee actually buys
Ship-booked tours are scheduled around the ship's times, and the line takes responsibility if a delay is its partner's fault. If a bus breaks down or a boat returns late, the ship normally waits, or the line arranges and pays for you to rejoin at the next port. That promise is most of what the higher price pays for. [What happens if you miss the ship at a port](/articles/what-happens-miss-ship-port/) explains the alternative.

Some independent booking sites offer their own "back to the ship" guarantee. Read exactly what it covers before relying on it.

## How to decide, port by port
1. **Check the time in port.** If the tour ends within 90 minutes of all-aboard, lean towards the ship.
2. **Check whether it's a tender port.** Tender queues can add an hour each way, which eats into an independent buffer. [Cruise ship tenders explained](/articles/cruise-ship-tenders-explained/) covers how tendering works.
3. **Look at the distance.** A tour that drives two hours inland carries more traffic risk than a walk-up boat trip from the pier.
4. **Compare like for like.** Check group size, what's included, and park or entry fees.
5. **Read recent reviews for punctuality,** not just for scenery. Timing is the real risk.
6. **Check your insurance.** Some Canadian travel policies include missed-departure or "catch-up" cover. Many don't, and your provincial health plan won't help with any of it.

> **Example:** in Juneau, whale-watching tours usually include a shuttle from the cruise docks to the boat harbour and typically run three to four hours. With a full day in port and a reputable operator, many cruisers book independently and save a meaningful amount per person. A helicopter-and-glacier tour with weather delays and tight timing is a different story, and the ship's version may be worth the premium.

## Where independent booking works well
Ports where tours start at the pier and the town is compact suit independent booking best. Ketchikan, Juneau and Skagway in Alaska, and well-trodden Caribbean stops like Cozumel and St. Maarten, have plenty of established operators. Alaska's options are compared in detail in [Alaska independent vs package shore tour costs](/articles/alaska-independent-vs-package-shore-tour-cost/).

### Where the ship usually makes sense
- Long transfers, such as Mediterranean ports where the famous sight is an hour or two away.
- Tender ports with tight all-aboard times.
- Remote or unfamiliar destinations with few reviewed operators.
- Travellers with mobility needs, where the ship can confirm accessible transport.

## Mix and match
Most experienced cruisers do both on one trip: the ship for the tight or complex day, independent for the easy ones, and a free walking day somewhere like Ketchikan (see the [free Ketchikan walking plan](/articles/ketchikan-free-walking-plan/)). Deciding [how many excursions to book per cruise](/articles/how-many-excursions-book-per-cruise/) first keeps the budget in check.

## Make the call port by port
Look at each port's time ashore, distance and tender situation, then decide. Where a missed ship would cost far more than the saving, pay for the guarantee. Where the buffer is generous and the operator is solid, keep the difference.
