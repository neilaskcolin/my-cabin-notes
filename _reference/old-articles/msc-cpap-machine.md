Yes, you can bring a CPAP on MSC Cruises, and plenty of guests do. The things that trip people up on MSC are specific to a European line: socket types, a distilled water supply that varies from ship to ship, and a special needs request that has to reach the right market's team.

## Before you sail: the CPAP checklist for MSC
1. **Note the CPAP on your booking.** Ask whoever holds your reservation to add it through MSC's special needs process, or complete the form yourself. For Canadians, that is the North American channel you booked through, not a European MSC site.
2. **Request an approved extension cord** at the same time. Outlets in some MSC cabins sit at the desk, a long way from the bed.
3. **Check your power supply label.** Most modern CPAP power bricks say "100–240V", which means a simple plug adapter is all you need, not a voltage converter.
4. **Pack a plug adapter.** Many MSC ships were designed for European itineraries, and cabins can have European two-pin sockets alongside, or instead of, North American ones. A few dollars removes the guesswork.
5. **Sort your water.** Decide now whether you are bringing distilled water or buying it near the port.
6. **Check your travel insurance** covers medical equipment, since provincial health plans won't help you at sea.

## Does MSC provide distilled water?
Reports from MSC guests are mixed. Some who listed distilled water on the special needs form received a bottle or cups of it nightly from their stateroom attendant. Others, including guests sailing from US ports, were told MSC does not supply it.

Because MSC's terms restrict bringing drinks aboard, mention on your request that the water is for a medical device, and keep it in sealed, labelled bottles. Many CPAP users run the machine without the humidifier for a week instead; ask your clinic whether that suits you. The wider options are in [CPAP distilled water on cruise ships](/articles/cpap-distilled-water-cruise-ships/).

> **Good to know:** if you are sailing MSC from Seattle to Alaska, you can buy distilled water at any pharmacy or grocery store in Seattle the day before. From Miami or Port Canaveral, a pharmacy stop between the airport and your hotel does the same job.

## The extension cord rule
Like most big lines, MSC bans guests' own extension cords and surge-protected power bars because of fire risk; Carnival is the main exception. The approved medical cord is the workaround. [Extension cord rules on cruise ships](/articles/extension-cord-cruise-ship-rules/) explains what security will confiscate.

## What goes in your carry-on
Keep the CPAP with you from your home airport to your cabin. Bags handed over at the MSC terminal can take until evening to arrive.
- The machine, mask, tubing and power supply
- Plug adapter and a spare filter
- A copy of your prescription or a clinic letter
- Distilled water, if you are bringing it from home and driving to port

Canadian airlines generally treat a CPAP as a medical device that doesn't count toward your carry-on limit, but confirm with your airline before you fly.

## How MSC compares
| Line | Desk to contact | Distilled water |
|---|---|---|
| **MSC** | Special-needs desk for your market | Varies by ship and market |
| [Carnival](/articles/carnival-cpap-machine/) | Special Needs team | Paid; pre-order, buy on board or bring your own |
| [Norwegian](/articles/norwegian-cpap-machine/) | Access Desk | On request, delivered to stateroom |

All three welcome CPAP machines. MSC and Norwegian supply an approved extension cord, while Carnival lets you bring your own plain cord. Water differs too: on MSC, plan to bring your own; Norwegian delivers it on request, and Carnival sells it or lets you carry your own. ## Getting it noted on the booking properly
