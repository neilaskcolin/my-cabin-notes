Disney Cruise Line is the most expensive of the big family cruise lines, and the premium is real. For families with children of about 3 to 12 who love the characters, it usually earns its price. For couples and older teens, it is a harder sell.

## What the Disney premium actually buys
Most of the difference is in things you cannot get on another line:

- **Character time and themed shows.** Character greetings run all week, and the main-stage shows are original Disney, Pixar and Marvel productions.
- **Rotational dining.** You rotate through three themed main restaurants, and your serving team moves with you, so the same servers learn your children's names and allergies.
- **Included kids clubs.** The Oceaneer Club takes children from 3 to 12, usually until late evening, with Edge and Vibe for tweens and teens. A paid nursery takes babies from 6 months.
- **Private islands.** Castaway Cay and the newer Lookout Cay at Lighthouse Point, both in the Bahamas, are built around families.
- **Small inclusions that add up.** Soft drinks at the beverage stations and with meals are included, and many staterooms have a split bathroom that makes a family of four work.

What you will not find is a casino. Adults get their own pool, lounges and the adult-only Palo restaurant, but the ship is designed around children first.

## The Disney fleet at a glance
| Ships | What they are like | Where Canadians meet them |
|---|---|---|
| Disney Magic, Disney Wonder | Oldest and smallest, classic ocean-liner styling | Alaska from Vancouver in summer 2026; other regions in other seasons |
| Disney Dream, Disney Fantasy | Larger, with more pool space and adult dining | Mainly Bahamas and Caribbean from US ports |
| Disney Wish, Treasure, Destiny | Newest Wish class, most themed venues | Mainly Florida, for the Bahamas and Caribbean |
| Disney Adventure | Very large, based in Singapore | Not practical for most Canadians |

Itineraries change from season to season, so check where your ship sails in the year you want to go.

## Being honest about the price gap
Disney fares often run well above a comparable Royal Caribbean or Carnival sailing, and the gap grows in school holidays, when Disney is busiest. Disney also discounts less: its fares tend to rise as a sailing fills, as the [Disney price drop](/articles/disney-price-drop-after-booking/) page explains.

The fare is only part of the bill for most Canadian families, because they have to fly to the port.

> **Quick math:** a family of four from Toronto on a 4-night Bahamas sailing from Port Canaveral might add return flights to Orlando (often C$1,600–2,800 for four), a hotel the night before (roughly US$150–250), gratuities at US$16 per person per night (US$256, roughly C$345–360) and transfers. That can be C$2,500 or more on top of the fare.

## Who gets the most value
- Families with children of roughly 3 to 12, especially first-time cruisers.
- Grandparents taking grandchildren, who value the safe, well-run kids clubs.
- Families on an Alaska trip from Vancouver, who skip the flight to Florida and still get the Disney experience.

## Who should think carefully
- Couples without children, who pay the premium for clubs and characters they will not use.
- Families with teens who have outgrown the characters. Vibe is good, but a teen may prefer the thrill rides on a big Royal Caribbean ship.
- Budget-focused families, who could put the difference towards a longer sailing or more excursions.
- Anyone wanting a casino or late-night party atmosphere.

## The Canadian logistics
For most of the year, Canadians fly to Florida. Orlando is the airport for Port Canaveral, and Fort Lauderdale's airport is close to its cruise port; see [Toronto to Fort Lauderdale cruise flights](/articles/toronto-fort-lauderdale-cruise-flights/) and, from Quebec, [flying from Montreal to a US cruise port](/articles/montreal-caribbean-cruise-us-port/). Flying in the day before is strongly advised, as [whether to fly the day before your cruise](/articles/fly-day-before-cruise/) explains.

Alaska from Vancouver is the exception. A family from Calgary or Edmonton can fly or drive to Vancouver, board at Canada Place and cruise the Inside Passage with Disney without crossing a border to board. The [Canada Place embarkation walkthrough](/articles/canada-place-embarkation-walkthrough/) covers that terminal.

> **Canadian tip:** March break and summer sailings sell out early and cost the most. If your children's school calendar allows, a late-August Alaska sailing or a November Bahamas cruise is usually easier to book and kinder on the budget.

Bring a valid passport for every family member, including babies. It is the simplest document for flying to a US port and for any unplanned flight home.

## Deciding if the Disney premium fits your family
Price the whole trip in Canadian dollars, including flights, a hotel night and gratuities, then compare it with a mainstream line on similar dates. If your children are at the age where meeting the characters is the highlight of their year, the gap is usually worth it.
