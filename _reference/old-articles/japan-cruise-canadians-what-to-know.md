A Japan cruise packs temples, gardens, castles and big-city energy into one trip with a single unpack. For Canadians, the big decisions are timing, flights across the Pacific and a few entry rules that catch people out.

## What a Japan cruise looks like
Most itineraries are 7–14 nights, starting and ending in Yokohama, Tokyo or Kobe. Some circle the whole country, while others focus on one region and add a stop in South Korea or Taiwan. Princess has long based a ship in Japan, and several other lines now run Japan seasons too.

Typical ports include:

- **Yokohama or Tokyo:** about 30–45 minutes by train from central Tokyo.
- **Shimizu:** the classic port for Mount Fuji views, on a clear day.
- **Kobe and Osaka:** gateways to Kyoto, about an hour by train.
- **Hiroshima:** the Peace Memorial Park and nearby Miyajima island.
- **Nagasaki and Kagoshima:** history, gardens and the smoking Sakurajima volcano.
- **Kanazawa, Aomori, Hakodate and Otaru:** quieter northern and western stops.

## When to go
| Season | What to expect |
|---|---|
| Late March to early April | Cherry blossoms in Tokyo and Kyoto, later further north. Highest demand and prices |
| Late April to early May | Golden Week, a run of Japanese holidays. Busy trains and sites |
| June to mid-July | Rainy season across much of the country |
| July to August | Hot and humid |
| August to September | Peak typhoon season, which can change itineraries |
| October to November | Autumn colour, peaking around mid to late November in Kyoto |

Blossom timing shifts each year with the weather, so a late-March sailing is a good bet but not a guarantee.

## Getting there from Canada
Vancouver to Tokyo takes roughly 10 hours, and Toronto about 13. Air Canada flies nonstop from Vancouver, Toronto and Montreal, Japanese carriers fly from Vancouver, and Calgary has had seasonal nonstops. Crossing the date line means you land the next calendar day, so arrive two days early to beat jet lag and protect against delays. [Ship's time and jet lag explained](/articles/cruise-cabin-jet-lag-time-zones/) has more on adjusting.

> **Canadian tip:** Canadians don't need a visa for tourist visits of up to 90 days. Register on Visit Japan Web before flying to speed up airport arrival, and expect a face-to-face immigration check for all passengers at the ship's first Japanese port, which can take a while.

## Rules and habits worth knowing
> **Watch out:** Japan prohibits some medicines that are routine in Canada, including stimulant ADHD medications such as amphetamines and some pseudoephedrine cold and allergy products. Larger supplies of certain prescriptions need an import certificate. Check Japan's Ministry of Health guidance and talk to your doctor months before you travel.

### Money
Cards are widely accepted in cities, but small temples, shops and rural buses may want cash. Convenience store ATMs generally accept Canadian cards. A rechargeable transit card makes trains easy. Tipping isn't customary ashore, though ship gratuities still apply on board.

### Etiquette
Shoes come off at many temples and traditional inns, phone calls on trains are frowned upon, and public bins are scarce, so carry a small bag for your rubbish. Excursions usually brief you on these, and travellers going it alone soon pick them up.

## Ship excursions or on your own?
Tokyo, Yokohama, Kobe and Nagasaki are easy to explore independently. Kyoto from Kobe, Mount Fuji from Shimizu and Hiroshima's island trips are longer days where a ship tour removes the worry about getting back on time. [Shore excursions through the ship vs independent](/articles/cruise-excursion-through-ship-vs-independent/) explains the trade-off. On ports with long transfers, an all-aboard time missed by a few minutes is still missed.

## What it costs
Japan cruises often cost more than a Caribbean week once you add long-haul flights and pre-cruise hotel nights in Tokyo.
