You don't need an adults-only ship to cruise like a grown-up. Most family-friendly mega-ships hide quiet, adults-only spaces, and some stay serene even in July. The key is knowing what your ship has before you book.

## What adults-only areas do the big lines have?
Names and age limits vary by ship, so treat this as a starting point and confirm for your sailing.

| Line | Free adults-only area | Paid or restricted option |
|---|---|---|
| Royal Caribbean | Solarium, generally 16+ | Suite-only areas on some ships |
| Carnival | Serenity Adult-Only Retreat, 21+ | Spa thermal suite passes |
| Princess | Adults-only pool areas on many ships | The Sanctuary retreat, paid |
| Norwegian | Spice H2O on some ships | Vibe Beach Club, paid and limited |
| Celebrity | Solarium | The Retreat, for suite guests |
| Disney | Quiet Cove pool, 18+ | Palo and Remy adult restaurants, 18+ |
| MSC | Adults-only areas vary by ship | Top 18 solarium, paid; Yacht Club suites |

Some lines skip the question entirely: Viking's ocean ships and Virgin Voyages are adults-only throughout. See [quiet adult-focused ships without waterslides](/articles/quiet-ships-without-waterslides/) for more options.

### Free sanctuaries vs paid retreats
Free areas usually offer loungers, a pool or hot tubs and often a bar or café. On some ships they stay calm all day; on others, they're small and fill by mid-morning. Paid retreats cap numbers, so they stay quiet, with padded loungers, cabanas and waiter service. They earn their price on itineraries with lots of sea days in peak season, but are easy to skip when the ship empties out in port every day.

> **Canadian tip:** March break, July, August and the Christmas holidays bring the most children aboard. If you can sail in May, September or early December, the entire ship feels like an adults-only area for much less money.

## How to find quiet on a family ship
1. **Study the deck plan before you book.** Find the solarium or adult retreat and check its size against the ship's passenger count.
2. **Pick a cabin near the quiet zones,** away from kids' clubs, the pool deck and elevator lobbies. Our guide to the [quietest cabins on a cruise ship](/articles/quietest-cabins-where-to-book/) shows where to look.
3. **Claim adult spaces early on sea days.** Loungers fill quickly after breakfast.
4. **Use port days to enjoy the ship.** If you stay aboard, pools and decks are at their emptiest.
5. **Book specialty restaurants.** Cover charges keep them mostly adult, and late seatings are quieter still.
6. **Try the spa's thermal suite.** Heated loungers, steam rooms and a strict adult policy make for a peaceful afternoon, and it's even better as a couple (see [couples spa experiences on cruise ships](/articles/couples-spa-experiences-cruise/)).
7. **Head to late-night venues.** Comedy clubs, piano bars and lounges skew adult after 9 or 10 p.m.

> **Watch out:** "adults-only" doesn't always mean quiet. Some adult pools have a DJ or a party bar, and spring break sailings can bring lively crowds of young adults. Read recent descriptions of your specific ship rather than relying on the line's name.

## Is a family ship right for you?
If you love a big ship's restaurants and shows but want calm spaces to retreat to, a mainstream ship with a strong adults-only area works well. If you want peace everywhere, all day, an adults-only line or a smaller ship is the better fit.
