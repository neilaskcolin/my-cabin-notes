The old idea that cruise room service is unlimited and always free is out of date. Breakfast in your cabin usually still costs nothing, but beyond that the rules differ by line and fare.

## What's free and what costs extra?
Here is the general pattern on mainstream lines at recent prices. Confirm the details for your ship, since lines adjust these often.

| What you order | Usually | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Continental breakfast | Free | Order the night before using the door card or app |
| Hot breakfast items | Free on some lines, charged on others | Eggs and bacon are the usual dividing line |
| All-day menu (sandwiches, pizza, salads) | Delivery fee or per-item prices on many lines; free on some | Fees are often around US$5–10 per order |
| Late-night snacks | As for the all-day menu | Popular after shows |
| Alcohol, pop, specialty coffee | Charged | A drink package may cover these |
| Celebration extras (cake, champagne, chocolate strawberries) | Charged | Order through the app or guest services |
| Suites and premium fares | Often fully included | Check your fare's inclusions |

Room service is one of several basics covered by your fare; the bigger list is in [what's included in a cruise fare](/articles/whats-included-cruise-fare/).

> **Quick math:** a couple ordering a late snack on three nights at about US$8 per order pays around US$24, plus any service charge. That's roughly C$35–40 for the week, often less than one round of cocktails.

## How ordering works
For breakfast, tick what you want on the card, choose a time window and hang it on your door before the deadline, usually late evening. Many ships now let you order in the app instead. For other meals, use the cabin phone or the app.

Charges go to your onboard account in US dollars, the same way as everything else (see [how cruise cards work on board](/articles/cruise-cards-how-you-pay-onboard/)).

> **Good to know:** delivery isn't hotel-fast. On embarkation day, around dinner and on early port mornings, waits of 30–45 minutes are common. For a breakfast before an early excursion, pick the earliest window, not the last one.

## When room service is worth it
- **Early port mornings.** Coffee and a pastry in the cabin beats the buffet scramble before a 7 am tour.
- **Scenic cruising.** Breakfast on your balcony as you sail into a glacier fjord is one of cruising's best free pleasures. Order the earliest slot on an Alaska glacier day (see [what happens on an Alaska glacier day](/articles/alaska-cruise-glacier-day/)).
- **Families with young kids.** Naps and early bedtimes make eating in sometimes easier than a dining room.
- **Seasick or unwell.** Crackers, toast and ginger ale delivered to your cabin.
- **A lazy sea-day start.** Especially on longer cruises.

## Who gets the best deal?
Suite guests often get free delivery from a fuller menu, and on some lines can order dishes from the main dining room. If you're weighing a suite, add room service to the list of perks you'd actually use, alongside priority boarding and lounge access. [How to pick your first cabin](/articles/pick-first-cruise-cabin/) covers whether the jump in category pays off.

On mainstream fares, the included options are generous enough that most people use room service only for breakfast. The [main dining room, buffet and specialty restaurants](/articles/main-dining-buffet-specialty-restaurants/) cover everything else at no extra cost, apart from the specialty venues.

## Check your fare before you assume
Room service fees are small, but they're also one of the rules that changes most often between lines and promotions. Look in your cruise line's app or fare details before sailing, and leave the trays outside your door when you're done so your steward can clear them.
