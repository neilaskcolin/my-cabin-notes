MSC does not allow you to bring alcohol on board to drink during your cruise, and that includes wine. It is the strictest of the big lines on this, so the real decision on MSC is not what to pack but how you will pay for drinks once you are aboard.

## Can you bring wine on MSC Cruises?
No. MSC's conditions of carriage say passengers may not bring drinks aboard for use during the voyage. That covers the bottle of wine most North American lines allow at embarkation. If a bottle turns up in your bag at the terminal in Miami or Port Canaveral, it is tagged, stored and returned at the end of the cruise.

The rule comes from MSC's European roots. On Mediterranean sailings the line has always sold wine as part of the onboard experience rather than letting guests bring their own, and it applies the same terms on its North American ships. Enforcement can feel uneven from sailing to sailing, but planning around a bottle slipping through is a poor bet.

### Soft drinks and water
MSC's terms are written about drinks in general, not just alcohol. Don't plan on carrying a case of soda or bottled water on board either. Baby formula and liquids you need for medical reasons, such as distilled water for a CPAP, are the usual exceptions; flag them in advance.

## What happens to bottles bought in port
Bottles you buy ashore in Cozumel, Nassau or Juneau, or in the ship's own duty-free shop, are held by the crew and handed back near the end of the cruise, usually on the last evening or at disembarkation. The same applies to bottles from Ocean Cay, MSC's private island in the Bahamas.

> **Canadian tip:** if you plan to bring bottles home, the CBSA personal exemption after 48 hours away is 1.5 litres of wine, or 1.14 litres of spirits, or 8.5 litres of beer, and you must meet the drinking age of the province you land in. Anything over that is taxed at the border, so the "bargain" bottle may not stay one.

## What you can do instead
Because nothing comes on in your suitcase, every drink is bought on board. Your realistic options are:
- **Pay as you go.** A cocktail commonly runs around US$12–15 at recent prices, plus an 18% service charge on USD-currency ships. Fine for one or two drinks a day.
- **Buy the Premium Extra package.** On North American sailings it is now MSC's only package that includes alcohol, after the Easy and Easy Plus packages were withdrawn. The [MSC drink package price](/articles/msc-drink-package-price/) page works through when it pays off.
- **Book MSC Yacht Club.** Suite guests in the ship-within-a-ship get premium drinks included, which removes the question entirely.
- **Order a bottle at dinner.** Sharing a bottle in the dining room often costs less per glass than ordering wine by the glass across the week.

> **Watch out:** packing a bottle "just in case" can cost you time in the security queue, not just the bottle. On a busy Saturday at PortMiami, the bag-check line is the last place you want to be.

## How MSC compares
| Line | Wine you can bring | Corkage (recent) |
|---|---|---|
| **MSC** | Generally none | Not applicable |
| [Carnival](/articles/carnival-alcohol-policy/) | 1 bottle per adult (21+) | ~$15 in bars and restaurants |
| [Norwegian](/articles/norwegian-alcohol-policy/) | No fixed limit | ~$15 per bottle, paid at boarding |

Many Canadians weighing MSC also look at Carnival and Norwegian for the same Florida sailings. MSC generally allows no wine at all, against one bottle per adult on Carnival and no fixed limit on Norwegian. If a couple would normally bring two bottles for a week, that gap is worth roughly US$60–100 in onboard wine. ## Sorting this before you pack
On MSC, the money question is settled at booking, not at the terminal. Decide whether you are a pay-as-you-go, Premium Extra or Yacht Club traveller, then leave the bottles at home and budget for drinks in your [cruise spending money](/articles/how-much-spending-money-cruise/).
