faq1q: Is Alaska or Norway easier to cruise from Canada?
faq1a: Alaska is much easier. Western Canadians can fly to Vancouver in an hour or two, or drive, and the time change is small, while Norway means a transatlantic flight, usually a connection and an eight- or nine-hour time difference from Western Canada.
faq2q: Is Norway's scenery actually different from Alaska's?
faq2a: Yes. Norway's fjords are narrower and steeper, with more waterfalls and farms and villages clinging to the slopes. Alaska is wilder and bigger in scale, with tidewater glaciers calving into the sea and far better odds of whales, bears and eagles.
faq3q: Can you see glaciers on a Norwegian fjords cruise?
faq3a: Yes, but differently. On typical fjords itineraries you see glaciers up in the mountains or on an excursion, such as the trip to Briksdal glacier from Olden, rather than calving ice faces from the ship as you do at Glacier Bay or Hubbard Glacier.

Start with Alaska unless you have a strong reason to choose Norway. Alaska is easier and cheaper to reach from Canada, and its glaciers and wildlife are a spectacular introduction to scenic cruising. Norway is a different kind of beautiful that's well worth the transatlantic flight for a second trip.

## How do Alaska and Norway compare?
| | Alaska from Vancouver | Norwegian fjords |
|---|---|---|
| Getting there | Short domestic flight, or drive | Transatlantic flight, often with a connection |
| Time difference | One hour behind Alberta, same as BC | Eight hours ahead of Alberta, six ahead of Ontario |
| Scenery | Huge wilderness, tidewater glaciers, forested islands | Narrow, steep fjords, waterfalls, cliffside farms |
| Wildlife | Humpbacks, bears, eagles, sea otters | Seabirds; whales mostly in the northern winter |
| Towns | Small frontier and Gold Rush towns | Historic cities such as Bergen and Ålesund |
| Daylight | Very long in June and July | Midnight sun on routes above the Arctic Circle |
| Season | May to September | About May to September |
| Rough all-in, 7 nights, inside cabin, per couple from Calgary | C$4,500–8,500 | C$6,000–10,500 |

The cost figures come from our detailed budgets for [an Alaska cruise from Calgary](/articles/calgary-alaska-cruise-cost/) and [a Norwegian fjords cruise from Canada](/articles/norway-cruise-cost-canada/). Both are at recent prices and vary widely by month and cabin.

## Why Alaska usually comes first
### Easy logistics
A couple from Calgary or Edmonton can fly to Vancouver in about 90 minutes, stay one night and board the next day. There is barely any jet lag, so you enjoy day one rather than recovering from it. Torontonians and Montrealers face a longer flight west, but still no ocean crossing.

### Wildlife and ice
Alaska delivers things Norway doesn't. Tidewater glaciers crack and calve into the sea at Glacier Bay and Hubbard Glacier, humpbacks are a near-daily sight in summer, and bald eagles are everywhere. If wildlife ranks high for either of you, start here. [Your first Alaska cruise](/articles/first-alaska-cruise-what-to-know/) covers the basics.

### A lower-risk test
If you're not yet sure scenic cruising suits you, with its cool weather, long hours on deck and fewer beach days, Alaska is the cheaper way to find out.

## When Norway makes sense first
- You're already planning time in London, Amsterdam or Copenhagen and can add a cruise.
- You've seen glaciers and bears on land in the Rockies or Yukon and want something different.
- History, architecture and walkable old towns matter more to you than wildlife.
- You want to see the midnight sun on a longer route to the North Cape.
- Either of you has Norwegian roots and a family connection to explore.

> **Good to know:** Norway is tightening emissions rules in its UNESCO-listed fjords, which may affect which ships visit the Geirangerfjord and Nærøyfjord. If those fjords are why you're going, confirm they are on your itinerary. Our guide to [Norwegian fjords cruises for Canadians](/articles/norwegian-fjords-cruise-canadians/) has more.

## What surprises people about each
In Alaska, many first-timers underestimate how much time they'll spend on deck and how cold it gets near the ice, even in July. Excursions in Juneau and Skagway are pricey, so budget for one or two good ones.

In Norway, the shock is often the cost ashore: lunch and a drink can be expensive by Canadian standards. The flip side is that many of the best experiences, such as walking Bergen's old wharf or the waterfall trail above Geiranger, cost nothing.

## Weather and packing
Pack much the same for both. Summer days in either region often sit somewhere around 10–20°C, with rain likely at some point: Ketchikan and Bergen are both known for wet weather. Bring a waterproof shell, a warm mid-layer, a toque and gloves for glacier or fjord mornings on deck, and comfortable walking shoes that cope with wet cobbles or trails.

> **Canadian tip:** a Canadian passport covers both without a visa. Alaska requires a passport for the US port calls. A Norway cruise starting in Southampton needs a UK Electronic Travel Authorisation, and Europe's ETIAS pre-travel authorisation has been scheduled to begin around late 2026, so check the rules before you fly.

## A sensible two-trip plan
Many couples end up doing both. A common pattern is Alaska first, in a balcony cabin in June, then Norway a year or two later on a 10- to 14-night sailing that reaches the Arctic Circle. Taking the longer Norway voyage also makes the transatlantic flight and jet lag feel more worthwhile.

## Choosing your first scenic cruise
If budget, simplicity or wildlife matters most, choose Alaska. If you're drawn to old European towns, waterfalls and the midnight sun, or you're already crossing the Atlantic, Norway can come first. Either way, a balcony earns its price on both routes.
