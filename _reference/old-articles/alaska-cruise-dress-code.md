Pack for the weather first and the dining room second. Alaska passengers spend their days in fleece and rain shells, and the evening dress code bends to match. Nobody expects a tuxedo on a ship that just spent the afternoon at a glacier.

## What do people wear during the day?
Function wins. On deck at a glacier, wind chill can feel close to freezing even in July, so the uniform is a base layer, a fleece or light down jacket, a waterproof shell, a toque and gloves. In port, it is the same minus a layer or two, with waterproof walking shoes.

Ports like Ketchikan and Juneau are working towns, not resorts. Jeans, hiking trousers and rain jackets are the norm ashore. For exactly what to pack by month, see [packing for May vs September](/articles/alaska-packing-may-vs-september/).

## What do people wear to dinner?
Most nights are smart casual, which on a mainstream line means no swimwear, tank tops, shorts or ball caps in the main dining room. Jeans with a decent shirt or sweater pass almost everywhere. The buffet and casual venues are more relaxed still.

Plenty of passengers come to dinner straight from the deck in a clean fleece. That is fine at the buffet, though in the dining room most people change into something a step up.

## How formal are formal nights in Alaska?
A 7-night sailing usually has one or two dressier evenings, depending on the line. In practice they are the gentlest formal nights in the fleet: collared shirts, sweaters, simple dresses, a scattering of jackets and ties, and the odd tuxedo worn by people who enjoy it.

Here is how the main lines label those nights:

| Line | What the dressy night is called | Realistic Alaska outfit |
|---|---|---|
| Princess | Formal night | Dress shirt and trousers, or a dress |
| Holland America | Dressy night | Collared shirt, optional blazer |
| Royal Caribbean | Formal night | Shirt and trousers; jackets common but optional |
| Celebrity | Evening Chic | Smart outfit; a blazer looks right |
| Norwegian | No formal nights; one optional "Dress Up or Not" night | Resort casual all week; smart casual in specialty restaurants |
| Carnival | Cruise Elegant | Shirt and trousers, or a dress |
| Disney | Optional semi-formal, plus Pirate Night | Smart outfit; pirate gear optional |
| MSC | Gala night | Smart outfit, often a little dressier |

Names and night counts change, so confirm on your line's current policy page.

> **Good to know:** skipping formal night is always allowed. The buffet keeps its normal casual code, and specialty restaurants are usually smart casual even on dressy nights.

## Premium, luxury and expedition ships
Premium lines nudge one notch dressier at dinner, and a blazer earns its suitcase space. Small luxury ships describe their style as elegant casual and mean it lightly. Expedition ships barely dress at all: fleece at dinner is normal.

## The packing translation
For a 7-night Alaska sailing, each person needs the layering system, two evening outfits that clear smart casual, and one step-up piece such as a blazer or a dress. That opens every door on the ship. Flying on a basic economy fare from Calgary or Toronto, that restraint also helps you avoid a checked-bag fee.
