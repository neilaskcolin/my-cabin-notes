Day one on a cruise is gentler than most first-timers expect. You will eat, explore, attend a short safety drill, watch the ship pull away, and have a relaxed dinner. Nobody expects you to know where anything is yet.

## What happens on embarkation afternoon?
Timings vary by line and port, but a typical boarding day runs roughly like this:

1. **Board around midday.** Head for the buffet or a casual venue for lunch, since cabins are often not ready until early afternoon.
2. **Find your cabin once it opens.** Your checked bags will turn up later, so don't wait around for them.
3. **Do the muster drill.** On most big lines this now means watching a safety video in the app or on your cabin TV, then checking in at your assigned assembly station. It is normally completed before the ship sails (see [what actually happens at the muster drill](/articles/cruise-ship-safety-drill-what-happens/)).
4. **Explore and book.** Walk the ship end to end and reserve anything that sells out.
5. **Sail away.** Get on deck for departure, usually in the late afternoon.
6. **Dinner and a show.** A casual first dinner, then whatever the evening programme offers.

> **Good to know:** pack a carry-on with medication, passports, a phone charger, a swimsuit and a layer for the deck. Checked bags can arrive as late as mid-evening.

### The things worth doing early
- Check that your cruise card or app account is linked to a payment card, so purchases go through (see [how cruise cards work on board](/articles/cruise-cards-how-you-pay-onboard/)).
- Book specialty restaurants, spa slots and headline shows if your line uses reservations.
- Confirm your dining time if you chose fixed seating.
- Put your phone in airplane mode or switch off data roaming before the ship leaves port. Canadian plans can charge steep rates on the ship's maritime network, so read [cruise wifi and phone roaming for Canadians](/articles/cruise-wifi-phone-roaming-canadians/) first.

## What is sail-away like?
It is one of cruising's simple pleasures: everyone gathers on the open decks, there is often music, and the ship slowly leaves the pier. Leaving Vancouver, you pass Stanley Park and slide under the Lions Gate Bridge, so a spot forward or on the port (left) side gives you the park view. Florida departures tend to be livelier, with a party by the pool. The [sail-away party guide](/articles/sail-away-party-what-is-it/) covers where to stand.

## The first evening
Dinner on night one is casual, whether in the main dining room or the buffet. Most ships put on a welcome show or live music in the lounges. Many people turn in early, because embarkation days often start with a dawn flight from Calgary, Edmonton or Winnipeg.

## Your first full day at sea
On a Caribbean sailing from Florida, the first full day is often a sea day. On an Alaska sailing from Vancouver, day two usually means scenic cruising through the Inside Passage. Either way, this is when the ship comes alive: trivia, talks, pool time, the spa, and often the first formal or dressy night.

### Settling in
- **Clocks may change.** Alaska is an hour behind Vancouver, and the ship will announce when it switches. [Ship's time explained](/articles/cruise-cabin-jet-lag-time-zones/) covers how this works.
- **Motion is normal.** If you feel queasy, eat a little, get fresh air and look at the horizon. [Seasickness on a cruise](/articles/seasickness-how-bad-really/) covers remedies worth packing.
- **Check your account.** Look at your onboard statement in the app to catch any charges that look wrong early.
- **Plan your first port.** Read the port guide in the daily programme and note the all-aboard time.

## Making the most of a relaxed first day
Beyond the muster drill, day one is yours to take at your own pace. Use it to get your bearings, lock in any bookings you still need, and enjoy the sail-away. For the hour-by-hour detail of boarding itself, see [what to do in your first hour aboard](/articles/what-to-do-first-hour-aboard-cruise/).
