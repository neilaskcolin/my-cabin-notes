Insurance is the dullest line in a cruise budget and the one that matters most when something goes wrong. Provincial health plans cover almost nothing at sea or abroad, and cruise fares become largely non-refundable well before you sail.

## Do you need cruise insurance in Canada?
Yes, for two reasons. **Medical:** a ship's medical centre bills privately, and an evacuation by helicopter or a hospital stay in a US port can run into tens or hundreds of thousands of dollars that your provincial plan won't touch, as covered in [cruise medical care costs](/articles/cruise-medical-care-costs/). **Cancellation:** after final payment, often around 90 days before sailing, an illness or family emergency can forfeit most of the trip.

Almost every Alaska port call is in the US, and most Caribbean cruises start there. Even a "Canadian" cruise from Vancouver spends most of its week outside provincial coverage.

## What a cruise insurance policy should cover

| Cover | What it pays for | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency medical | Ship's doctor, hospital ashore, evacuation, repatriation | $5 million or more; evacuation explicitly included |
| Trip cancellation | Prepaid costs if you cancel for a covered reason before departure | Covers the full trip cost, including flights and hotels |
| Trip interruption | Costs to rejoin the ship or fly home early | Includes catching up at the next port |
| Missed connection | Getting to the ship if a flight delay makes you miss sailing | Cruise-specific wording, not just "travel delay" |
| Cabin confinement and itinerary changes | Compensation if you're isolated on board or ports are skipped | Nice to have, not a reason to choose a policy |
| Baggage | Lost or delayed luggage | Usually low limits; home insurance may already cover |

## Cruise line plan vs a Canadian policy vs your credit card
Cruise lines sell their own protection plans, which are convenient and often generous on cancellation but typically weaker on medical cover. Some refund as future cruise credit rather than cash. Third-party Canadian policies usually carry far higher medical limits and can be sized to your whole trip. The detailed comparison is in [travel insurance vs cruise line protection plans](/articles/travel-insurance-vs-cruise-protection/).

Card coverage is real but conditional. Many cards cap the number of medical days, exclude travellers over 65 or 70, or cover cancellation only when the whole trip was charged to the card. A 15-day cap ends cover on day 15 of a 16-day cruise-and-hotel trip. Read the certificate, not the marketing.

## The five-clause check
Before buying any policy, find these five clauses:

1. **The medical limit**, and whether evacuation from a ship is named.
2. **The pre-existing condition stability period**, and whether you meet it for every condition.
3. **The maximum trip length**, counted from the day you leave home.
4. **The cruise-specific benefits:** missed connection, interruption, catching up at the next port.
5. **The exclusions list**, especially alcohol, risky activities and known events.

Ten minutes with these answers most of the questions people ask after something has gone wrong.

> **Watch out:** once a hurricane is named or a strike is announced, it becomes a known event, and a policy bought afterwards won't cover it. That's one more reason to buy at deposit time (see [what happens if a hurricane threatens your cruise](/articles/what-happens-hurricane-cruise-caribbean/)).

## How much does cruise insurance cost in Canada?
Cancellation cover is commonly priced at roughly 5–10% of the trip cost. Emergency medical varies mainly with age and health: modest for travellers under 50, rising steeply after 65 and with declared conditions. Get quotes rather than relying on ranges, since pricing varies sharply by insurer.

> **Example:** a couple in their 50s from Edmonton booking a C$6,000 Alaska cruise with flights and a Vancouver hotel would typically pay a few hundred dollars for a combined medical and cancellation package. A couple in their 70s with declared conditions could pay considerably more for the same trip, so quote early.

Age bands, medical questionnaires and stability clauses get their own guide: [cruise insurance for seniors in Canada](/articles/cruise-insurance-seniors-canada-pre-existing/). If you want the freedom to cancel for any reason, read [cancel for any reason cover explained](/articles/cancel-for-any-reason-cruise-insurance-explained/) first.

## If you need to claim on board
Call your insurer's emergency assistance line before agreeing to anything beyond basic treatment, if you possibly can. Many policies require pre-approval for hospital admission or evacuation. The ship will charge medical bills to your onboard account; keep every invoice, medical report and receipt for the claim.

## The timing rule
Buy at deposit time. Cancellation cover only applies to causes that arise after the policy starts, so a policy bought a week before sailing won't help if a condition worsened two months earlier. The government's own [travel insurance guidance](https://travel.gc.ca/travelling/publications/travel-insurance) makes the same point. If you fall ill just before sailing, [getting sick the day before a cruise](/articles/what-if-you-get-sick-day-before-cruise/) explains what to do.

## Fit the policy to the actual trip
Include the flights, the pre-cruise hotel and any cruisetour land nights in the trip cost and dates. A policy that covers the cruise but not the flight to Vancouver leaves a gap exactly where delays happen.

## Arranging insurance with the booking
The easiest time to get insurance right is the day you pay the deposit, when the dates and costs are fresh. A licensed cruise consultant can quote a Canadian policy sized to the whole trip alongside the cruise.
