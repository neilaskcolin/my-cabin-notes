The Inside Passage is the calm, sheltered route: round trips from Vancouver that thread between islands to Ketchikan, Juneau and Skagway and back. The Gulf of Alaska route is a one-way sailing that does most of the same Inside Passage, then crosses open water to the giant glaciers near Anchorage.

## What is the Inside Passage, exactly?

It is a network of channels and straits running up the coast of BC and Southeast Alaska, protected from the open Pacific by a chain of islands. On a typical 7-night round trip from Vancouver, you spend the first and last days cruising the BC coast and the middle of the week in Alaska's port towns, plus a glacier day in Glacier Bay or a fjord such as Endicott Arm.

## What does the Gulf of Alaska route add?

A one-way sailing between Vancouver and Whittier or Seward runs up the Inside Passage, calls at most of the same ports, then leaves the shelter of the islands to cross the Gulf of Alaska. The pay-off is Hubbard Glacier and College Fjord, plus the coastal St. Elias Mountains on a clear day, scenery the round trips can't reach.

Some travellers call it the "outside passage", which is a handy shorthand for the open-water leg.

## Inside Passage vs Gulf of Alaska, side by side

| | Inside Passage round trip | Gulf of Alaska one-way |
|---|---|---|
| Start and end | Vancouver to Vancouver | Vancouver to Whittier or Seward, or the reverse |
| Typical length | 7 nights | 7 nights, often with a land tour added |
| Sea conditions | Mostly sheltered, a few exposed hours | Same, plus around a day of open ocean |
| Glaciers | One glacier day, often Glacier Bay or Endicott Arm | Hubbard and College Fjord, sometimes Glacier Bay too |
| Scenery | Narrow channels, forest, close shorelines | Adds open-ocean horizons and huge icefields |
| Flights from Canada | Simple return to Vancouver | Open-jaw, flying home from Anchorage |
| Land tour | Not easily added | Natural fit for Denali |

## How the two routes feel on board

### The sheltered channels

On the Inside Passage, land is close on both sides for much of the trip. Forested slopes, small lighthouses, fishing boats and the odd whale blow go past the window. The water is usually calm, and many people barely feel the ship moving. A few stretches are more open, such as Queen Charlotte Sound off northern Vancouver Island, but they pass in hours.

### The open Gulf

The Gulf crossing feels like proper ocean: a wide horizon, more swell, seabirds following the ship and cooler air. Most crossings are comfortable, but it is the stretch of an Alaska cruise most likely to cause motion, as explained in [Alaska Gulf crossing seasickness](/articles/alaska-gulf-crossing-seasickness/). Then the mountains rise again, and the glaciers near the end of the route are some of the biggest on any itinerary.

> **Canadian tip:** for Albertans and Manitobans, the round trip is the easier trip by far: one return flight to Vancouver, no US connection, and you clear Canadian customs at Canada Place on the way home. The one-way usually means flying home from Anchorage via Seattle or Vancouver.

## Choosing in four questions

1. **Is Denali on your wish list?** If yes, choose the one-way and add a land tour. The order is covered in [Denali before or after the cruise](/articles/denali-add-on-before-after-cruise/).
2. **Does anyone get seasick?** If yes, the round trip keeps you in sheltered water almost the whole way. See [seasickness on the Inside Passage](/articles/seasickness-inside-passage/).
3. **Is this your first Alaska cruise?** The round trip covers the essentials with the least effort. On a second trip, the Gulf route shows you new ice.
4. **How much do flights matter?** Price both complete trips, including flights, before deciding. [Round-trip vs one-way](/articles/roundtrip-vs-oneway-alaska/) has a sample whole-trip budget.

## Quick chooser

- First Alaska cruise, flying from Calgary or Edmonton: Inside Passage round trip.
- Denali dreams or a second visit: Gulf one-way with a cruisetour.
- Seasickness worries: Inside Passage, with a midship cabin.
- Glacier fans: the one-way's Hubbard and College Fjord days are the season's ice jackpot.

## The total, not the fare

Fares for the two routes are closer than many people expect. Flights and any land tour are what move the total. Getting both priced as complete packages, with cruise, air and land together, is the only fair comparison.
