MSC charges US$17 per person per day on its North American sailings, or US$23 in the Yacht Club, at rates in force since May 2026. Unlike some lines, it charges children aged 2 and up the full amount, which makes a real difference to a family budget.

## What MSC charges on North American sailings
The daily "hotel service charge" applies to MSC's Caribbean, Bahamas, Alaska, US and Canada sailings. MSC raised it on May 11, 2026, from US$16 to US$17 in regular cabins and from US$20 to US$23 in the Yacht Club. Guests who had already prepaid at the old rate kept it.

| Party and sailing | Approx. USD | Roughly in CAD |
|---|---|---|
| Couple, 7 nights, regular cabin | $238 | C$320–335 |
| Couple, 7 nights, Yacht Club | $322 | C$435–450 |
| Family of four (kids 8 and 11), 7 nights | $476 | C$645–665 |
| Family of four, 4-night Bahamas to Ocean Cay | $272 | C$365–380 |

The charge posts to your onboard account each day in US dollars unless you prepay. Rates change, so confirm the figure for your sailing.

## Children and the MSC service charge
Older guides and forum posts often describe a reduced children's rate. On North American sailings that is no longer the case: everyone aged 2 and over pays the same daily amount, and only babies under 2 are free.

> **Example:** a Mississauga family of four with children aged 1, 6 and 9 on a 7-night MSC Grandiosa sailing from Port Canaveral pays for three people, not four: 3 × US$17 × 7 = US$357, or roughly C$480–500.

MSC often runs promotions where children sail free or cheaply. The fare saving is real, but the service charge is still added for each child aged 2 and over. [The catch with kids-sail-free deals](/articles/kids-sail-free-catch/) covers what else to check.

## European and other-market sailings
MSC grew up in Europe, and its Mediterranean and Northern Europe cruises follow that market's rules. The amount is often charged in euros, and for some markets, such as UK bookings, gratuities are already included in the fare. If you book a European MSC sailing as a Canadian, check the terms of the market you booked in rather than assuming the North American rate. [Mediterranean cruise costs for Canadians](/articles/mediterranean-cruise-cost-canada/) puts this in a full budget.

## How to prepay MSC gratuities
MSC prices Canadian bookings in Canadian dollars, and adding the service charge to that booking is the simplest way to lock both the rate and the exchange.
1. Count every guest aged 2 or over and multiply by nights and the daily rate for your cabin type.
2. Ask to add prepaid service charges to your reservation, at booking or before the pre-departure cut-off.
3. Check the new invoice total matches your calculation.
4. On board, confirm in the MSC app that your account shows the charge as prepaid, so it isn't posted again daily.

If you don't prepay, settle your account with a Canadian card that has no foreign-transaction fee. [Paying in CAD vs USD](/articles/paying-cad-vs-usd-cruise/) explains the trade-offs.

## Service charges on drinks and extras
On USD-currency ships, drinks bought one at a time carry an 18% service charge, or 15% on euro-currency ships. MSC drink packages bought in North America already include the service charge in the price. Terminal porters and excursion guides are tipped separately in cash, so pack a few small US bills.

## Should you tip extra on MSC?
It isn't expected. The daily charge is pooled across dining, housekeeping and behind-the-scenes crew, and MSC's international guests generally treat it as the tip. If a stateroom attendant or waiter stands out, a cash tip or a named note to Guest Services is appreciated. Guest Services can also adjust the charge for a genuine service problem. ## Getting the right MSC figure for your sailing
Because MSC's charge depends on the market, the cabin type and your children's ages, work out the exact amount for your booking and prepay it in Canadian dollars. It turns a daily US-dollar charge into one fixed number.
