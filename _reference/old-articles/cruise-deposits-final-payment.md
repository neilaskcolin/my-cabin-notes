Every cruise booking runs on two dates: the day you pay the deposit and the day the balance is due. Between them, on a standard fare, you can usually cancel for free. After final payment, penalties start, so that second date is the one to guard.

## How the booking timeline works
1. **Deposit day.** You pay a per-person deposit, often US$100–500 on mainstream lines and more for suites, holiday sailings and long voyages. It locks in your cabin, fare and any promotion.
2. **The flexible window.** Until final payment, a standard fare can usually be cancelled for a full deposit refund. You can also make extra payments whenever you like.
3. **Final payment.** Commonly 75–120 days before sailing on mainstream lines, longer for some longer voyages. The rest of the fare is due.
4. **The penalty period.** After final payment, cancelling costs a growing share of the fare, often reaching 100% in the final weeks.
5. **Sailing day.** Fully paid, documents done, onboard account ready.

> **Example:** for a 7-night Alaska sailing on July 17, 2027, a 90-day final payment rule means the balance is due around April 18, 2027. With a 120-day rule it is around March 19. Book now and you have five months of free flexibility before either date.

## Why the window in between matters
This is where you hold the cards. If the fare for your cabin drops, you can often ask to have it adjusted. If a better promotion comes along, you may be able to switch. If plans change, you can walk away on a refundable fare. [Price drops after booking](/articles/price-drop-after-booking-cruise/) explains how to ask.

### The trap: non-refundable deposits
Some fares are cheaper because the deposit is non-refundable. Cancel and you lose it, or get it back only as future cruise credit, sometimes minus a fee. That suits fixed plans and doesn't suit tentative ones. The trade-off is explained in [refundable vs non-refundable fares](/articles/cruise-fare-classes-refundable/).

### Booking inside the final payment window
If you book a sailing that is already within 75–120 days of departure, you usually pay in full at booking and penalties apply straight away.

## Canadian points to watch
If you book in US dollars, the deposit converts at today's rate and the balance at final payment, so the CAD cost can shift by a noticeable amount over a few months. [Paying in CAD vs USD](/articles/paying-cad-vs-usd-cruise/) shows how to manage that.

Buy travel insurance soon after the deposit. Many Canadian policies give their fullest cancellation benefits only if bought within a set number of days of your first payment, and final payment is exactly when that cover starts to matter.

## A simple payment routine
Choose a refundable fare, pay the deposit, buy insurance the same week, and put the final payment date in your calendar twice: once for the due date and once two weeks earlier. Keep an eye on the price until then. For group bookings, which follow their own deadlines, see [group cruise deadlines and deposits](/articles/group-cruise-deadlines-deposits/).
