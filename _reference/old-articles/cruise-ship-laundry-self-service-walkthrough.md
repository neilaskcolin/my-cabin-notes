If your ship has a self-service launderette, one mid-cruise wash lets you pack for half the trip. The process takes about two hours start to finish, costs less than a drink, and is easiest if you pick the right time.

## Which ships have self-service laundry?
This is the first thing to check, because many popular ships don't. The picture below is a general guide at recent check; confirm on your ship's deck plan.

| Line | Self-service launderettes? | If not, what's offered |
|---|---|---|
| Princess | Yes, on most ships, usually several decks | Also paid send-out service |
| Carnival | Yes, on most ships | Also paid send-out service |
| Disney | Yes, on every ship | Also paid send-out service |
| Holland America | On some ships | Paid send-out service |
| Royal Caribbean | Generally no | Paid per item or fill-a-bag deals |
| Celebrity | Generally no | Paid per item or fill-a-bag deals |
| Norwegian | Generally no | Paid per item or bag deals |
| MSC | Generally no | Paid per item |

If yours has none, [the cost of paid laundry on board](/articles/cruise-laundry-cost-alcohol-water/) explains the bag deals, which can still let you pack light.

## A step-by-step laundry run
1. **Find the launderette on the deck plan** on embarkation day. They're usually tucked along cabin corridors, sometimes one per deck (see [how to read a deck plan](/articles/reading-cruise-ship-deck-plans/)).
2. **Check how you pay.** Most machines take your cruise card; older setups use tokens from guest services.
3. **Sort before you go.** Bring one mesh or tote bag of darks and lights, plus a few pods in case detergent isn't dispensed.
4. **Load, tap and start.** Washes typically run 30–45 minutes.
5. **Set a phone timer and stay nearby.** A lounge or the corridor is fine; don't head to the pool deck.
6. **Move clothes to a dryer promptly.** Drying usually takes 45–60 minutes. Low heat protects synthetics and swimwear.
7. **Fold on the counter and leave the machine free** for the next person.

> **Good to know:** irons and steamers are banned in cabins on most ships, but launderettes that have them often include an ironing station. It's the only place on board you can press a formal-night shirt yourself.

## When to go
Sea days, especially the middle of the cruise, are when everyone has the same idea. The quietest times are early morning before 8am, late evening during the main shows, and port days when most passengers are ashore. If you're taking an excursion, a quick early-morning wash before breakfast works too.

### Launderette etiquette
- Don't leave a finished load in a machine for long; others may move it to a counter, which is generally accepted.
- Never take someone else's clothes out of a dryer that's still running.
- Wipe out lint filters after drying.

## Packing for a laundry day
Plan outfits for about four days on a 7-night cruise, or six or seven days on a longer one, then wash once. Quick-dry fabrics and packing cubes help (see [packing cubes and luggage organization](/articles/packing-cubes-luggage-organization-cruise/)). For fly-cruisers from Calgary, Winnipeg or Toronto, this is often what keeps a couple to one checked bag each, which matters given [airline luggage rules for cruises](/articles/luggage-rules-flying-to-cruise/).

## Confirming your specific ship's laundry setup
Look at your ship's deck plan before you pack. If there's a launderette, pack half and wash once. If there isn't, budget for a fill-a-bag deal on day four and pack the same way.
