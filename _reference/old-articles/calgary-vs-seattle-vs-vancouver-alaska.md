Vancouver is the better starting point for most people flying from Calgary or Edmonton. Seattle can still win on a particular ship or date, but its cheaper headline fares rarely survive once flights, hotel and transfers are priced in Canadian dollars.

## Vancouver vs Seattle, side by side
| Factor | Vancouver (Canada Place) | Seattle (Piers 66 and 91) |
|---|---|---|
| Flight from YYC | About 90 minutes, domestic, many daily | About 2 hours, transborder, fewer flights, usually pricier |
| US border | Preclearance in the cruise terminal | Preclearance at YYC before you fly |
| Airport to ship | Canada Line SkyTrain, about 26 minutes | Taxi, rideshare or line transfer, about 30–45 minutes |
| Pre-cruise hotel | Canadian dollars | US dollars |
| Route north | Inside Passage, sheltered water | Open Pacific west of Vancouver Island, often a sea day each way |
| Required foreign stop | None | Usually Victoria, often a brief evening call |
| One-way sailings to Whittier or Seward | Yes | Not on foreign-flagged ships |

## Why do Seattle cruises stop in Victoria?
The US Passenger Vessel Services Act stops foreign-flagged ships carrying passengers between two US ports without a foreign call. Since almost every big cruise ship is foreign-flagged, a Seattle round trip has to touch Canada, and Victoria is the handy stop. Calls are sometimes only a few evening hours, so it is rarely a highlight.

The same law explains why one-way Gulf of Alaska cruises start or end in Vancouver. A Seattle-to-Seward sailing would be two US ports with no foreign stop. If you want to add Denali or end in Anchorage, Vancouver is your port; [round-trip vs one-way Alaska](/articles/roundtrip-vs-oneway-alaska/) weighs that choice.

## The route difference nobody advertises
### From Vancouver
Ships head up the inside of Vancouver Island through narrow, island-dotted channels, with a few hours of more open water around Queen Charlotte Sound. The scenery starts on day one, and the water is usually calm (see [how calm the Inside Passage really is](/articles/seasickness-inside-passage/)).

### From Seattle
Ships exit through the Strait of Juan de Fuca and run up the outer coast of Vancouver Island in the open Pacific. Expect a sea day each way with more swell and less to look at. If you are prone to seasickness, that matters more than a small fare difference.

## Currency and the real cost
Onboard spending is in US dollars from either port, and many lines will quote Canadians a fare in Canadian dollars wherever the ship sails, so the fare itself may not differ much (see [paying in CAD vs USD](/articles/paying-cad-vs-usd-cruise/)). The real currency difference is everything before boarding: a Seattle hotel, meals and transfers are all in US dollars.

> **Quick math:** say a Seattle sailing is US$150 per person cheaper, or about C$410 per couple. Transborder flights can easily cost C$150–300 more for two, a downtown Seattle hotel in summer is often US$250 or more (roughly C$345+), and getting to the pier costs more than the SkyTrain. The saving shrinks to little or nothing.

## Coming home
After a Vancouver round trip, you clear Canadian customs at Canada Place and fly home domestically. From Seattle, you clear Canadian customs when you land at YYC. Either way, after more than 48 hours away each person can bring back up to C$800 of goods duty-free, so a week-long cruise qualifies from both ports.

## When Seattle genuinely wins
- The ship you want only sails from Seattle that season.
- Seattle has the dates you need, such as a week that fits Alberta school holidays, when Vancouver doesn't.
- A fare gap large enough to survive the full Canadian-dollar comparison.
- You are combining the cruise with a Washington or Pacific Northwest trip anyway.
- You are sailing with family who live in the US and want to meet at the ship.

A middle path some Albertans use is flying to Vancouver and taking the train or a coach to Seattle, about four hours with the border crossing. It saves on transborder airfare but adds a day.

## Price the same week both ways
Line up comparable sailings in the same week, then add flights, a hotel night, transfers and any parking for each, all converted to Canadian dollars. If Seattle still wins after that, take it.
