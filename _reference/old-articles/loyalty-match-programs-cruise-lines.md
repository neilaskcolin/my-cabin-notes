Yes, you can sometimes carry your cruise status to a new line, but only where a match program exists, and it is rarely a like-for-like swap. The receiving line decides what tier to give you, for how long, and what you must do to keep it.

## How cruise status matching works
A status match means a second line looks at the tier you hold elsewhere and grants you an equivalent, or near-equivalent, level in its own program. Your points do not move; recognition does. There are a few different versions, and it helps to know which one you are dealing with.

| Type of match | How it works | How long it lasts | Typical catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sister-brand reciprocity | Lines owned by the same company recognise each other's tiers, often on a published chart | Ongoing, as long as you hold the original tier | Tiers do not always map one-to-one |
| Competitor status match | A line invites elite members of rival programs to apply for a matched tier | Usually a trial period, often 12–24 months | You must sail within the window to keep it |
| Targeted offers | A line emails or advertises a one-off perk to members of a named rival program | One booking or one sailing | Easy to miss, rarely repeated |

Royal Caribbean and Celebrity are the best-known example of standing reciprocity between sister brands. Owning companies do not guarantee it, though: two lines under the same parent can still run completely separate programs. Competitor matches have tended to come from lines trying to grow market share, and MSC and Virgin Voyages are among those that have run them. Offers come and go, so confirm what is live before relying on it.

## What a matched tier usually gets you
Expect the practical perks of the tier you are given, not necessarily the one you held. Common matched benefits include:

- Priority check-in and boarding
- A members' welcome event or lounge access on board
- Discounts on drinks, wifi or specialty dining at higher tiers
- Occasional onboard credit or free laundry

Top-tier perks such as free cocktails every evening or suite-level lounges are often held back from matches, or require you to sail a set number of nights first.

> **Watch out:** many matches expire if you do not complete a sailing within a fixed period. Booking a cruise for two years out may not count, so check whether the sailing date or the booking date is what matters.

## How to apply for a cruise status match
1. Find the line's current match page, or ask the line directly whether an offer is running.
2. Take a screenshot or download a statement from your current program showing your name, member number and tier.
3. Submit it with your new line's loyalty number. Create a free account first if you do not have one.
4. Wait for confirmation in writing before you book anything that depends on the perks.
5. Note the expiry date and the sailing requirement in your calendar.

## Who gets real value from matching
Matching pays off most for cruisers with mid or high status on one line who want to try a different ship, itinerary or region. A Vancouver couple with years of status on a Caribbean-focused line, for example, might want an Alaska-heavy brand for a summer sailing without losing priority boarding at Canada Place.

It matters less if you are near the bottom of your current program, since entry tiers carry few perks. Our guide to [which cruise loyalty programs are worth chasing](/articles/cruise-loyalty-programs-worth-chasing/) explains how tiers build in the first place.

> **Canadian tip:** status perks like drink discounts are worth more when the onboard account is in US dollars. A 15% drink discount saves you roughly 15% of a bill that already costs you about 1.35–1.40 times as much in Canadian dollars.

## Matching versus promotions
A status match does not replace a good fare. Loyalty perks usually sit on top of whatever promotion you book, but some fares, such as casino or group rates, can limit them. Compare the full package, including any [onboard credit](/articles/onboard-credit-explained/) attached to the fare, and read [cruise line sales decoded](/articles/cruise-line-sales-decoded/) to judge whether the headline promotion is genuinely good.

## Before you switch lines
Ask three questions before you book: will the new line match my tier, what exactly does that tier include, and what do I need to do to keep it? If the answers are vague, treat the match as a bonus rather than the reason to switch, and choose the line whose ships and itineraries suit you best.
