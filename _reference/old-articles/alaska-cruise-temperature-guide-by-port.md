Alaska in summer is cool, not cold, until you stand on deck in front of a glacier. Port towns feel like a damp late-spring day back home, while the glacier viewing deck can feel like November. Here is what to expect at each stop.

## Typical highs and lows by port
These are rough long-term averages for daytime highs and overnight lows. Any single week can run several degrees warmer or colder, so treat them as a guide for packing, not a forecast.

| Port | May high | July high | September high | July low | Character |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vancouver | 17°C | 22°C | 19°C | 14°C | Mild; often dry in July |
| Seattle | 18°C | 25°C | 21°C | 14°C | Warmest start or finish |
| Victoria | 16°C | 20°C | 18°C | 11°C | Mild evening calls |
| Ketchikan | 13°C | 18°C | 14°C | 11°C | Very wet year-round |
| Juneau | 13°C | 18°C | 13°C | 10°C | Wet, often overcast |
| Skagway | 14°C | 19°C | 14°C | 10°C | Drier, sunnier, windy |
| Sitka | 12°C | 16°C | 14°C | 11°C | Cool, wet, ocean-facing |
| Icy Strait Point | 12°C | 17°C | 13°C | 10°C | Similar to Juneau |
| Seward and Whittier | 12°C | 17°C | 13°C | 10°C | Whittier is famously rainy |
| Anchorage | 13°C | 18°C | 13°C | 11°C | Mild, drier than the coast |
| Denali area | 14°C | 20°C | 11°C | 7°C | Bigger swings; snow possible any month at height |

The interior is the outlier. Denali and Fairbanks can reach the mid-to-high 20s on a summer afternoon and drop to near freezing at night, so a cruisetour needs a wider range of clothing. [Alaska cruisetours with Denali by rail](/articles/alaska-cruisetours-denali-rail/) covers that leg.

## What the numbers feel like
For Canadians, the easiest comparison is home:

- **July in Juneau or Ketchikan** feels roughly like late May in Vancouver or early June in Calgary, but damper and greyer.
- **May or September in the ports** feels like a cool April day in Toronto or Montreal: fine walking weather in a fleece and shell.
- **Rain changes everything.** At 13°C and drizzle, you will want the same layers you would wear on a wet fall day in Winnipeg or Edmonton.

## Why the glacier deck is so much colder
Three things stack up. The air near large ice fields is chilled, cold air often slides down off the glacier toward the water, and the ship's own speed adds a breeze across the bow. You may spend one to three hours outside at the face.

> **Quick math:** using the same wind chill scale Environment Canada uses, 5°C air with a 30 km/h wind feels close to 0°C. Ten degrees colder than the port forecast is a sensible planning rule for glacier mornings.

The [best spot on the ship for glacier viewing](/articles/best-spot-cruise-ship-glacier-viewing/) includes sheltered options if you feel the cold.

## Rain matters more than temperature
Ketchikan receives several metres of rain a year, among the most of any town in North America. Juneau and Sitka are also wet. Skagway, tucked behind mountains, gets a fraction of that. That is why a waterproof shell is the single most useful thing in your case, more than an extra sweater. For the month-by-month rain and daylight picture, see [Alaska cruise weather month by month](/articles/alaska-cruise-weather-by-month/).

## Practical takeaways
- Pack for 8–19°C plus rain as the baseline for every Southeast port.
- Add a toque, gloves and your warmest layer for glacier days.
- If your trip includes Denali, add a light warm-weather outfit and something for near-freezing nights.
- The full packing list, by month, is in [what to pack for May vs September](/articles/alaska-packing-may-vs-september/).

## Checking your actual week
About ten days before sailing, look at forecasts for each port in order, plus Vancouver or Seattle. If one glacier day looks clear and calm, plan to be outside for it. If the week looks wet, waterproof trousers will earn their space.
