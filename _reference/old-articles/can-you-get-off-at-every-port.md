Yes, you can get off at every port the ship actually calls at. Once local officials clear the ship, the gangway opens and the day is yours. You do not need a tour, a guide or permission, only your cruise card and the time you need to be back.

## How a port day works
1. **The ship arrives and is cleared** by local authorities. An announcement tells you when the gangway is open.
2. **You scan your cruise card** on the way off, so the ship knows you are ashore.
3. **You explore** on foot, by taxi, on a tour or not at all.
4. **You return before all-aboard time**, usually 30–60 minutes before departure. It is printed in the daily programme and the app.
5. **You scan back on** and go through a quick security check.

Carry the photo ID your line recommends for that country. Many experienced cruisers take their passport ashore, because it is what you need if you miss the ship and have to fly to the next port. [What happens if you miss the ship](/articles/what-happens-miss-ship-port/) explains why.

## Not every stop is the same

| Type of stop | Can you get off? | What to know |
|---|---|---|
| Docked port | Yes | Walk straight off down the gangway |
| Tender port | Yes, by small boat | Queues in the morning; can be cancelled in rough seas |
| Private island | Yes | Often docked, sometimes tendered |
| Scenic cruising day | No | Glacier Bay, Hubbard Glacier and fjords are viewed from the ship |
| Short evening call | Yes, briefly | Seattle round trips to Alaska stop in Victoria for a few hours to meet US shipping rules |

[Cruise ship tenders explained](/articles/cruise-ship-tenders-explained/) covers the tender process, and [what happens on a glacier day](/articles/alaska-cruise-glacier-day/) shows why scenic days are worth staying on deck for.

## The three wrinkles
### Tender ports
Ship excursion guests often get priority on early tenders, so independent explorers may wait. Powered scooters can be refused for safety, and rough seas can cancel the stop altogether.

### Ship's time
> **Watch out:** all-aboard runs on ship's time, which may differ from local time and from your phone, which can switch automatically to a local network. Set a watch to ship's time and check announcements. [Ship's time explained](/articles/cruise-cabin-jet-lag-time-zones/) covers how it works.

### Face-to-face immigration
A few countries require passengers to be inspected in person, either on board or in the terminal, before anyone goes ashore. That can mean an early-morning queue with passports. The daily programme warns you the night before.

For Alaska sailings from Vancouver, US border checks are normally done at Canada Place before you board, so you can walk off at your first Alaska port without another queue.

## Reasons you might not go ashore
- Weather cancels a tender port, or the captain changes the route.
- You are not admissible to that country. If anything in your history might affect entry to the US or another stop, sort it out before booking.
- You choose to stay on board, which is perfectly allowed. A port day with the ship half empty means quiet pools and an easier spa booking.

Balancing busy port days with relaxing ones is covered in [port days vs sea days](/articles/port-days-vs-sea-days-itinerary-balance/).
