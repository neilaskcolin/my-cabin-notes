A Baltic cruise is a week or two of elegant capitals, each a short sail from the next: Copenhagen, Stockholm, Helsinki, Tallinn and often Riga or Gdańsk. It suits couples who like history, design and walkable old towns more than beaches, and it is one of the easiest ways to see several Nordic and Baltic countries without changing hotels.

## Which ports does a Baltic cruise visit now?
Since 2022, mainstream lines no longer call at St. Petersburg, which used to be the highlight. Itineraries have been rebuilt around other capitals, and many now spend longer in each.

### The core capitals
- **Copenhagen:** a common start or end point, with Nyhavn's harbour and Tivoli Gardens close by.
- **Stockholm:** if the ship sails into the city, the passage through the archipelago is a highlight in itself. Some ships use Nynäshamn instead, about an hour away.
- **Helsinki:** compact and design-focused, with the Suomenlinna sea fortress a short ferry ride away.
- **Tallinn:** a medieval old town within walking distance of the cruise port, and often the favourite.

### Common extras
Riga's Art Nouveau quarter, Gdańsk's rebuilt old town (via Gdynia, about 30–40 minutes away), Visby on the island of Gotland, Oslo and Warnemünde. Warnemünde is the stop for Berlin, but it's roughly three hours each way, so a Berlin day is long.

## When is the best time to sail?
The season runs from about late May to early September. June brings the longest days, with daylight late into the evening in Stockholm and Helsinki. Midsummer, around June 20–24, is a major holiday in Sweden and Finland, and some shops and attractions close on Midsummer Eve. Summer days are often mild, around 15–22°C, with cooler evenings at sea.

> **Canadian tip:** July and August overlap with European school holidays, so popular sites are busiest then. Late May, early June and early September offer cooler weather, smaller crowds and often lower fares.

## How to plan a Baltic cruise from Canada
1. Check the current port list and confirm which capitals get a full day.
2. Choose your embarkation city based on flight connections from your home airport. Many routes connect through London, Amsterdam, Frankfurt or Reykjavík.
3. Book a pre-cruise night or two, especially for Copenhagen or Stockholm, both worth exploring. Read [planning an overnight layover before a cruise](/articles/overnight-layover-before-cruise/) for timing.
4. Bring a valid Canadian passport, and check entry rules for every country, including Europe's ETIAS pre-travel authorisation, scheduled to begin around late 2026, and the UK ETA if you start in Southampton.
5. Decide which ports you'll do independently and which justify a guided tour, such as Berlin.
6. Get a credit card with no foreign transaction fees, since you'll pay in euros, kronor and złoty.

## Is the Baltic right for you?
The Baltic suits second- or third-time European cruisers who have done the Mediterranean and want somewhere cooler, calmer and less crowded. If dramatic nature matters more than capitals, the [Norwegian fjords](/articles/norwegian-fjords-cruise-canadians/) are the better pick, and the [British Isles](/articles/british-isles-cruise-canadians/) offer another northern option with castles and coastlines.
