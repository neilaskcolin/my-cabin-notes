The best Alaska proposal spot is usually your own balcony during an evening of scenic cruising, when the light is soft and nobody else is watching. Glacier day is the most dramatic setting, but it is also the busiest day on board, so it needs more planning.

## Where on an Alaska cruise should you propose?

Each option trades drama against privacy. Match it to the person you are asking: some people would love an audience at the bow, and others would be mortified.

- **Your balcony during scenic cruising.** The most private choice. The Inside Passage channels stay close to forested shoreline for hours, and you control the timing completely.
- **Glacier day, from your balcony.** When the ship stops at the ice and slowly turns, each side gets its turn facing the glacier. Time the question for your side's turn and you get the full backdrop without the crowd (see [where to stand on glacier day](/articles/best-spot-cruise-ship-glacier-viewing/)).
- **Glacier day, on deck.** Spectacular but public. The forward rails fill an hour ahead, and it is cold enough that gloves and fumbling with a ring box do not mix.
- **A small private excursion.** A helicopter glacier landing from Juneau or a floatplane over Misty Fjords from Ketchikan puts you somewhere almost nobody else stands. Private tours for two cost more, but the moment is yours.
- **A quiet walk ashore.** Nugget Falls beside Mendenhall Glacier or a forest trail at Icy Strait Point costs little and avoids the ship entirely.

### The evening light advantage

In June and July, Southeast Alaska stays light until after 10 p.m., with a long golden glow before that. A late-evening balcony moment after dinner, when the decks have emptied, is one of the most underrated options on the whole itinerary.

> **Watch out:** Alaska weather changes by the hour, and fog can hide a glacier completely. Pick a first-choice moment and a backup on a different day, so a grey morning doesn't decide it for you.

## How to plan it, step by step

1. **Book a balcony.** It is the one cabin choice that makes almost every option above possible. The [best cabins for Alaska scenery](/articles/best-cabins-alaska-cruise-scenery/) explains which decks and positions work best.
2. **Check the itinerary.** Find which day is the glacier day and which ports have the excursion you want. Glacier Bay, Hubbard and Endicott Arm feel quite different (compared in [Glacier Bay vs Hubbard vs Endicott Arm](/articles/glacier-bay-vs-hubbard-vs-endicott/)).
3. **Book any private excursion early.** Small-group and private tours in peak season sell out well before sailing.
4. **Order extras a few weeks out.** Champagne, flowers or a cake delivered to the cabin, plus a photographer session, are commonly available. Ask for written confirmation.
5. **Plan the celebration.** A specialty restaurant dinner that evening gives you somewhere quiet to celebrate.

## Getting the ring there safely

Keep the ring in your carry-on and on your person, never in checked luggage, which porters handle and which can take hours to reach your cabin. It will go through X-ray screening at Canada Place, so don't panic if an officer asks to see the box.

> **Canadian tip:** take the ring to a CBSA office before you travel and ask to register it on an Identification of Articles for Temporary Exportation form (Y38). That record shows it left Canada with you, so there are no awkward questions on the way home. Check your home insurance covers jewellery while travelling too.

If you buy a ring in a port, it has to be declared on return. Juneau, Ketchikan and Skagway all have jewellery shops aimed at cruise passengers; compare prices at home first and read [duty-free limits for Canadians](/articles/cruise-shopping-onboard-duty-free/) before you buy.
