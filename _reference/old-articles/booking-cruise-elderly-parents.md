A cruise is one of the easiest holidays for older travellers: unpack once, meals nearby, help on every deck. Booking one well for your parents means checking the details brochures skip, from where the cabin sits to whether the ship can actually dock at each port.

## The checklist before you book
- **Cabin position:** midship, close to elevators, on a deck with cabins above and below.
- **Cabin type:** an accessible cabin if they use a wheelchair or scooter; otherwise check the shower has no high step.
- **Itinerary:** mostly docked ports and several sea days.
- **Insurance:** travel medical that covers their ages and conditions, bought at deposit.
- **Mobility help:** wheelchair assistance at the terminal and any equipment they need on board.
- **Medication:** enough for the trip plus spare days, carried on.
- **Companion:** someone travelling with them if they need any personal care.

## Choosing the right cabin
Midship cabins have the shortest walks and the least motion. Near the elevators is good; directly beside them can be noisy. A balcony earns its cost for older guests because it gives private fresh air without crossing a busy ship.

Accessible cabins have wider doors, roll-in showers and space to turn a wheelchair. Every ship has only a limited number, and they are often the first cabins to go. Standard bathrooms are compact, sometimes with a raised shower lip, so check photos or ask before booking. [Accessible cruising: mobility-friendly ships and cabins](/articles/accessible-cruising-mobility-ships/) compares the options.

## Picking an itinerary for limited mobility
At docked ports guests walk off down a gangway. At tender ports they step into a small boat from a moving platform, which crew may not allow for guests with limited mobility or powered scooters. [Cruise ship tenders explained](/articles/cruise-ship-tenders-explained/) shows what that involves.

| Itinerary | Ports | Pace | Fit for limited mobility |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alaska round trip from Vancouver | Mostly docked | Gentle, with scenic cruising from the ship | Very good; large tides can make gangways steep |
| Caribbean from Florida | Mostly docked, some tenders | Busy port days | Good, if you check each port |
| Mediterranean | Mixed, some tenders | Long, hilly, cobbled days ashore | Harder |
| Transatlantic or repositioning | Few ports | Many sea days | Very good for ship lovers |
| European river cruise | Docked in town centres | Short walks | Varies: some river ships have limited or no elevators |

## Medical care, medication and insurance
Ships have a medical centre with a doctor and nurses, set up to stabilize rather than treat serious illness. Visits are charged to the onboard account, and serious cases are taken ashore or evacuated. [Cruise ship medical care costs](/articles/cruise-medical-care-costs/) explains what to expect.

> **Canadian tip:** provincial health plans pay very little outside Canada, and nothing towards onboard medical bills or evacuation. Travel medical insurance is not optional for older parents, and it should be bought when the deposit is paid.

Declare every condition accurately on the medical questionnaire, and check the stability period for each one. A medication change in the months before sailing can affect cover. [Cruise insurance for seniors in Canada](/articles/cruise-insurance-seniors-canada-pre-existing/) covers age limits and pre-existing conditions.

Pack medication in original containers in a carry-on, with a few spare days' supply and a printed list of prescriptions and doses.

## Getting around the terminal and ship
Request wheelchair assistance for embarkation and disembarkation through the line's accessibility team when you book. Ship wheelchairs are generally kept for emergencies, so bring your own or rent a scooter or wheelchair from a company that delivers to the cabin.

> **Watch out:** crew do not help with personal care such as dressing, bathing or taking medication. If a parent needs that, someone who can provide it must travel with them, and some lines make this a condition of booking.

## Setting a comfortable pace
One gentle excursion beats three ambitious ones, and most ports offer tours marked with a low activity level. Early fixed-time dining at a shared table often becomes a highlight, and it is worth requesting at booking. ## Have it arranged, not improvised
Accessible cabin holds, docked-or-tender checks, dietary notes, terminal assistance and the right insurance all need sorting before sailing day. Get them on the booking early, and confirm each one in writing, so your parents can simply enjoy the trip.
