For most Celebrity cruises of 5 to 14 nights, final payment for North American residents falls 90 days before sailing. Shorter trips of 1–4 nights are due at 75 days, and voyages of 15 nights or more at 120 days, at recent terms. Before that date a refundable-deposit booking cancels free; after it, the penalty rises in steps to the full fare.

## When is Celebrity final payment due?
The date depends on cruise length and is printed on your confirmation. The schedule below is Celebrity's published one for North American residents, which covers Canadians. UK and Australian residents have different bands, so a schedule from an overseas forum may not apply.

| Cruise length | Free until | 25% penalty | 50% | 75% | 100% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–4 nights | 75 days out | not used | 74–61 days | 60–31 days | 30 days or less |
| 5–14 nights | 90 days out | 89–75 days | 74–61 days | 60–31 days | 30 days or less |
| 15+ nights | 120 days out | 119–75 days | 74–61 days | 60–31 days | 30 days or less |

Galapagos sailings on Celebrity Flora are stricter, with final payment around 150 days ahead. Celebrity can revise any of these, so check your own booking terms.

> **Example:** a couple from Edmonton books a 7-night Alaska sailing from Vancouver for C$5,000 and has to cancel 80 days before departure. That sits in the 25% band, so roughly C$1,250 of the fare is lost. At 45 days out the same cancellation costs about C$3,750, and inside 30 days the whole fare.

## Refundable vs non-refundable deposits
Celebrity sells most cabins two ways. A refundable-deposit fare costs a little more but returns the deposit in full if you cancel before final payment. A non-refundable deposit (NRD) fare is cheaper, keeps the deposit if you cancel, and charges a fee to change ship, date or names. See [cruise fare classes explained](/articles/cruise-fare-classes-refundable/) and [cruise deposits and final payment dates](/articles/cruise-deposits-final-payment/).

For a booking made a year or more out, the refundable fare is usually the safer buy. NRD makes more sense when you book close to the final payment date and are confident you will sail.

### All Included and Retreat bookings
On an All Included fare, the Classic drinks package and Basic wifi are part of the fare and cancel with it; Retreat fares work the same way. Specialty dining, excursions and other add-ons bought in your Celebrity account have their own terms, so cancel those separately.

## How to cancel a Celebrity cruise
1. Check your final payment date against the table above to see which band you are in.
2. Contact whoever holds the booking, Celebrity or your travel agent. The penalty is set by the day Celebrity receives notice, so don't wait.
3. Ask whether moving to a later sailing costs less than cancelling, especially on an NRD fare.
4. Get the refund amount and timing in writing, then open any insurance claim straight away.

## Cancelling one guest in the cabin
If one person drops out after final payment, the remaining guest is repriced at a single rate, which can approach the original two fares. Get that quote first; see [how to dodge the single supplement](/articles/single-supplement-how-to-dodge/).

## The Canadian angle: insurance and currency
Your provincial health plan does nothing for trip cancellation. That needs travel insurance with cancellation cover, ideally bought soon after the deposit, since many Canadian policies set a deadline for covering pre-existing conditions. Premium credit card cover can have a per-person limit below a cruise fare. [Cruise insurance in Canada](/articles/travel-insurance-cruise-canadians/) and [travel insurance vs cruise line protection](/articles/travel-insurance-vs-cruise-protection/) set out the options.

> **Canadian tip:** a Celebrity booking priced in Canadian dollars refunds in Canadian dollars, so a cancellation costs you no exchange loss. A booking paid in US dollars on a Canadian card can come back at a different rate, and the original foreign-transaction fee may not be returned.
