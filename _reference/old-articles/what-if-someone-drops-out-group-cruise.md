When someone pulls out of a group cruise, two things matter: when they cancel and what fare they booked. The group can usually keep the cabin by putting a new name on it. The organiser's job is to move quickly, check the deadlines and protect the block's cabin count.

## How much does the person who cancels get back?
This is the typical pattern on standard fares. Exact dates vary by line, sailing length and the group contract, so confirm for your booking.

| When they cancel | Typical refund | Effect on the group |
|---|---|---|
| Before final payment, refundable deposit | Deposit back, sometimes as cruise credit | Cabin leaves the block unless backfilled |
| Before final payment, non-refundable deposit fare | Deposit kept, sometimes as future cruise credit | Same |
| Soon after final payment | Partial refund; penalties rise as sailing nears | Harder to backfill |
| In the final weeks | Often little or nothing | Cabin usually lost |

Group contracts can have their own terms that differ from the line's public cancellation schedule. Ask your group contact rather than assuming. [Cruise deposits and final payment dates](/articles/cruise-deposits-final-payment/) explains the general rules.

## Can someone else take their place?
Usually, and it's often the cheapest fix. Many lines let you change the name on a cabin up to a cutoff before sailing, sometimes for a fee. The new guest needs to supply passport details by the group's names deadline. Some lines treat replacing every guest in a cabin as a cancellation and rebooking, which could cost the original fare, so check before promising anyone a straight swap.

## What about the person left in the cabin?
> **Quick math:** if one of two friends sharing a cabin drops out, the remaining guest may be repriced at a solo fare, commonly 150–200% of the per-person rate. On a US$900 per-person inside cabin, that's an extra US$450–900, or roughly C$620–1,250.

Options include finding a roommate within the group, moving to a cheaper cabin, or claiming under a travel insurance travelling-companion benefit if the reason for cancelling is covered.

## Does it cost the group its perks?
Only if the block falls below a threshold. Organiser perks are usually calculated on paid cabins at final payment. A block going from 9 cabins to 8 is usually fine; going from 8 to 7 can cost the free berth. That's why many organisers recruit a cabin or two above the target. The thresholds are explained in [the 8-cabin rule](/articles/group-cruise-8-cabin-rule/).

## What should the organiser do, in order?
1. **Get the cancellation in writing** and tell your group contact the same day.
2. **Check the fare type,** final payment date and name-change cutoff.
3. **Ask the person leaving** whether they know someone who wants the cabin.
4. **Offer it to your waitlist,** then the wider group chat.
5. **If you're near the threshold,** ask whether the line can hold the cabin for a few days while you look.
6. **Settle shared costs** under the rule you set at the start, such as refunding a prepaid group excursion only if the place is filled.
7. **Point the person leaving to their own insurer.** Their cancellation is their claim, not yours.

> **Watch out:** flights are separate. Canada's air passenger protection rules cover disruptions caused by the airline, not a passenger's own cancellation, so a non-refundable ticket may only return a credit, if anything.

## Insurance is each guest's safety net
Trip cancellation insurance reimburses non-refundable costs only for covered reasons, such as illness or a death in the family. Simply changing your mind usually needs [cancel-for-any-reason cover](/articles/cancel-for-any-reason-cruise-insurance-explained/). Each household should buy its own, as explained in [group cruise insurance](/articles/group-cruise-insurance-one-policy-everyone/), and [group cruise deadlines](/articles/group-cruise-deadlines-deposits/) shows when to set the insurance reminder.

## Write the dropout rule on day one
Put a short dropout policy in the first group message: who pays what, how shared costs are handled, and that everyone needs their own insurance. When someone does cancel, there's nothing awkward left to decide.
