Yes, you can bring a CPAP on Holland America, and you do not need permission. The line says no approval or advance notice is required for breathing devices. A short message to its Guest Accessibility Department still helps, because that is how the cord and water get sorted before you board.

## Do you need to tell Holland America about a CPAP?
Not officially, but it is worth doing. Contact Guest Accessibility once you have booked and ask for two things to be noted on your reservation: an approved extension cord and distilled water. Booked through an advisor? Ask them to add it.

> **Good to know:** Holland America says supplies for breathing devices are not available on board. Whatever your machine needs for the whole trip has to come with you.

## Distilled water: three ways to cover it
- **Pack it.** Holland America allows sealed distilled or purified water for a medical device in checked luggage, packed with the device. This is a specific exception to the line's ban on plastic water bottles.
- **Order it ahead.** The ship can usually supply distilled water on request. There may be a charge, and arranging it in advance tends to cost less than ordering through room service.
- **Bridge the first night.** Checked bags can take until evening to reach your cabin, so a small sealed bottle in your carry-on covers embarkation night.

The line-by-line picture is in [distilled water for a CPAP on a cruise ship](/articles/cpap-distilled-water-cruise-ships/).

## Extension cords and outlets
Cruise lines restrict guests' own power bars, especially surge-protected ones, for fire safety. The usual fix is an approved cord supplied by the ship, so ask for one rather than packing your own. [The extension cord rules](/articles/extension-cord-cruise-ship-rules/) explain why.

Outlet placement varies across the fleet. The Pinnacle-class ships, Koningsdam, Nieuw Statendam and Rotterdam, are the newest. Older ships such as Volendam and Zaandam date from around the turn of the century, and a bedside socket is far from guaranteed. Canadian plugs fit the North American sockets on board.

## Flying and packing your CPAP
Air Canada and WestJet generally let a CPAP travel as an extra medical item on top of your carry-on allowance, but confirm with your airline. [Luggage rules for flying to your cruise](/articles/luggage-rules-flying-to-cruise/) has the details for flights into Vancouver, Seattle or Florida.

What belongs in the CPAP bag:
- The machine, mask, tubing and power supply.
- Spare filters and a spare mask cushion for the full trip.
- A copy of your prescription, useful at airport screening.
- A sealed bottle of distilled water for the first night.

## Long voyages and cruisetours
Holland America runs some of the longest itineraries of the big lines: 14-night Alaska round trips, transatlantics and Grand Voyages of a month or more. Pack spare filters, a spare cushion and a backup tube, because parts are hard to find in most ports.

On an Alaska cruisetour with nights in Denali or the Yukon, luggage often travels separately between lodges. Keep the CPAP in your day bag on coach and rail days, as covered in [Alaska cruisetours explained](/articles/alaska-cruisetours-denali-rail/). If a bag does go missing on the ship side, see [lost or delayed luggage on a cruise](/articles/lost-delayed-luggage-cruise/).
