A short cruise can cost a club member about the same as a weekend away, with meals, a bed and entertainment included. Keep it affordable by choosing a 3–5 night shoulder-season sailing, making a shared inside cabin the default team rate, and collecting small refundable deposits before you commit to a block size.

## When should a club sail?
Match the trip to the end of your season and to the cheapest window on the water.

### Winter sports
Hockey, curling and ringette seasons wrap up around March and April. Late April and May is a sweet spot for the Caribbean: after the March break price peak and before hurricane season begins in June.

### Summer and fall sports
Soccer, baseball and ball hockey clubs finishing between August and October can sail Alaska from Vancouver until late September, which suits BC and Alberta clubs. Caribbean sailings in the fall are often cheap, but that is peak hurricane season, so insurance matters; see [what happens if a hurricane threatens your cruise](/articles/what-happens-hurricane-cruise-caribbean/).

## What does a club cruise cost per person?
A 4-night Bahamas or Western Caribbean sailing from Florida, per person at recent prices. Confirm for your sailing.

| Item | Budget: inside, four sharing | Standard: inside, two sharing | Upgrade: balcony, two sharing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cruise fare | US$250–400 | US$350–550 | US$550–900 |
| Taxes and port fees | US$100–150 | US$100–150 | US$100–150 |
| Gratuities, 4 nights | US$65–80 | US$65–80 | US$65–80 |
| Return flights, Toronto to Florida | C$350–700 | C$350–700 | C$350–700 |
| **Rough total** | **C$900–1,600** | **C$1,050–1,800** | **C$1,350–2,250** |

In practice, third and fourth guests in a cabin are often priced well below the first two, so a four-person cabin's average can come in under these figures. Clubs flying from Winnipeg, Calgary or Edmonton should budget more for flights, or look at Alaska from Vancouver instead.

## Funding the trip
Most clubs mix individual payment with one or two of these:
- **A club subsidy,** approved by the board as the season's celebration budget.
- **Sponsor-a-cabin** deals with local businesses that already back the team.
- **Fundraisers** such as bottle drives, 50/50 draws or raffles.
- **The organiser's free berth,** given to the coach, or raffled among members who have paid.

> **Canadian tip:** raffles and 50/50 draws in Canada need a licence from your provincial gaming regulator, or in Ontario often your municipality for smaller draws. If your club is a registered charity or registered amateur athletic association, check with your treasurer before using club money for a members' trip, as the rules on private benefit are strict.

## Setting it up, step by step
1. **Get board approval in principle,** with a subsidy amount if there is one and a written code of conduct.
2. **Survey members** on dates, budget and whether families are coming.
3. **Pick the sailing and open a group block** with low, refundable deposits. [How the 8-cabin rule works](/articles/group-cruise-8-cabin-rule/) explains the perks.
4. **Set a firm deposit deadline** to separate the keen from the maybes.
5. **Run a roommate list** so players can fill three- and four-person cabins.
6. **Have each member pay the line directly;** the club handles only its subsidy and fundraising money.
7. **Require travel medical insurance** for everyone, then lock names at final payment. See [group cruise deadlines](/articles/group-cruise-deadlines-deposits/).

## Youth players, families and non-drinkers
> **Watch out:** on sailings from US ports, most major lines set the drinking age at 21, and the cruise card enforces it. Players aged 19 and 20 who can drink at home can't on board, and under-21s usually need to share a cabin with someone 21 or older.

For junior teams travelling without parents, each child needs a valid passport and a signed consent letter from their parents, and the club needs enough adult chaperones to cover every cabin. Build the trip around beach days, snorkelling and ship activities rather than a bar crawl, and point non-drinkers to a soda or refreshment package if they want one. Families can use the [kids' clubs](/articles/kids-clubs-cruise-ships-ages-costs/) while adults join team events.

For team spirit, [matching shirts and branded items](/articles/group-cruise-matching-shirts-branded-items/) are popular on embarkation day, and a [private group excursion](/articles/group-cruise-shore-excursions-private/) can cost less per person than the ship's tours. Raising money for a cause on the trip instead? See [planning a charity fundraiser cruise](/articles/charity-fundraiser-cruise-group/).

## Bring the headcount and the dates
Start with your season's end date and a rough headcount. A shoulder-season sailing, shared cabins and an early deposit deadline do most of the work of keeping the trip affordable.
