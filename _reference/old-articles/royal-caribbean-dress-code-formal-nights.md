Royal Caribbean's dress code is lighter than it sounds. Most nights are casual or smart casual, a week-long cruise usually has two formal nights, and on those nights a collared shirt and trousers or a simple dress is enough to fit in.

## Royal Caribbean's three evening dress codes
The codes apply in the main dining room from dinner onwards. During the day it's resort wear everywhere, covered in [what to wear during the day on a cruise](/articles/cruise-ship-dress-code-daytime-wear/).

- **Casual:** what you'd wear to a decent restaurant at home. No swimwear, tank tops or bare feet in the dining room.
- **Smart casual:** a step up. Collared shirts, blouses, trousers, skirts or a sundress.
- **Formal:** the line suggests suits, tuxedos, cocktail dresses or gowns. In reality you'll see everything from black tie to a dress shirt and chinos, and nobody is turned away for lacking a jacket.

## How many formal nights on Royal Caribbean?
Two on a 7-night cruise, one on 3- to 5-night sailings, and sometimes more on longer voyages. The night's code is posted in the Cruise Compass in the Royal app, so check it each afternoon rather than guessing.

A week on an Oasis-class ship out of Fort Lauderdale might run like this. The pattern varies by sailing, so treat it as an example, not a schedule.

| Night | Typical code | What most people wear |
|---|---|---|
| 1, embarkation | Casual | Whatever you travelled in, tidied up |
| 2, sea day | Formal | Dress shirt and trousers, dresses, a few suits |
| 3 to 5, port days | Casual or smart casual | Polo or linen shirt, sundress, neat jeans |
| 6, sea day | Formal | The same step-up outfit as night 2 |
| 7, last night | Casual | Comfortable, since bags go out that evening |

## Where the dress code doesn't apply
The Windjammer buffet, the pizza and casual venues on the Royal Promenade and room service all ignore the code, and the food is the same any night. Specialty restaurants such as Chops Grille and Giovanni's ask for smart casual whatever the night. The trade-offs between venues are in [main dining room vs buffet vs specialty restaurants](/articles/main-dining-buffet-specialty-restaurants/).

Formal nights are also when the ship's photographers set up backdrops along the Promenade, so it's the night most people dress up a little.

## Packing for Royal Caribbean formal nights from Canada
One step-up outfit per person covers both formal nights, because nobody notices a repeat. Add two smart-casual combinations and your daywear.

> **Example:** a couple from Calgary on a 7-night Alaska sailing from Seattle packs one dark suit jacket that works with jeans or trousers, a wrap dress that layers under a cardigan, and one pair of dress shoes each. It all fits one checked bag, which matters on basic fares with bag fees.

Alaska evenings are cool, so layers matter more than fabric weight; see the [Alaska cruise dress code](/articles/alaska-cruise-dress-code/). Caribbean sailings in February call for light fabrics that don't crease in a suitcase. ## Kids and teens
Children are expected to be neat rather than formal. A collared shirt or a simple dress is plenty, and many families skip the dining room on formal night entirely.

## Confirming your sailing's formal nights
If you plan to book a specialty dinner, check which nights are formal first. Specialty restaurants stay smart casual, so a formal night is a handy time to book one if you'd rather not dress up, and a port day is better if you want the full formal night in the dining room.
