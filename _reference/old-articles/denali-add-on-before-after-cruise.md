For most people, and especially for Canadians flying from the Prairies or Ontario, doing Denali before the cruise is the better order. The active land days come first, the trip ends with a week of being looked after at sea, and you fly home from Vancouver instead of Alaska.

## How a cruisetour fits together

A Denali add-on always pairs with a one-way cruise between Vancouver and Whittier or Seward, the ports that serve Anchorage. The land segment connects to the Alaska end. So the order of your trip is set by the direction of your sailing:

- **Land first:** fly to Anchorage or Fairbanks, spend 3–7 nights inland, then board a southbound cruise that ends in Vancouver.
- **Sea first:** fly to Vancouver, sail northbound, then continue by rail and coach to Denali and fly home from Anchorage or Fairbanks.

The packages are explained in [Alaska cruisetours and Denali by rail](/articles/alaska-cruisetours-denali-rail/), and the cruise ends are compared in [Anchorage vs Whittier vs Seward](/articles/anchorage-vs-whittier-vs-seward/).

## Land first vs sea first, side by side

| Factor | Denali first (southbound cruise) | Cruise first (northbound cruise) |
|---|---|---|
| Energy | Busy days while you're fresh | Busy days at the end, when tired |
| Trip ending | A relaxing week at sea | Lodge changes and early starts |
| Flight home | Domestic flight from Vancouver | International flight from Alaska, often via Seattle or Vancouver |
| Luggage | Pack for land first, then settle into a cabin | Unpack once, then repack for lodges |
| Feel of the trip | Builds down to calm | Builds up to the wilderness |
| Good for | Most travellers, older travellers, families | People who want to ease in |

## The case for Denali first

The land segment is the hardest part of a cruisetour. Expect early bus departures, long days on the park road, a few hours on the train and a new lodge every night or two. Doing that first, with fresh legs and no jet lag, is easier on most bodies.

Then you board the ship, unpack once and let the scenery come to you. For Canadians, the last leg is a short flight home from Vancouver, with customs handled at the cruise terminal and no international connection to worry about at the end.

## The case for the cruise first

Starting on the ship is a gentler introduction, especially for people new to Alaska or to more rugged travel. Some like the build-up: glaciers and port towns first, then the vast interior as the finale. The cost is ending on effort, with a lodge checkout and a long flight home on the last day.

## What the order doesn't change

Your chance of seeing the mountain is the same either way. Denali makes its own weather, and the summit is often hidden even on fine days, so adding a night near the park helps more than picking an order.

> **Good to know:** a landslide at Pretty Rocks cut the Denali park road short at about the halfway point from 2021, and buses turned back at East Fork, around mile 43, through the 2026 season. A new bridge opened in September 2026, and the park expects full bus service to resume in 2027. Check what your tour includes before you book.

### A sample 11-night land-first trip from Edmonton

- **Day 1:** fly to Anchorage, usually connecting through Vancouver or Seattle.
- **Days 2–3:** rail north to the Denali area; park bus tour.
- **Day 4:** a second Denali day, or Talkeetna for a flightseeing trip.
- **Day 5:** rail and coach to Whittier or Seward and board the ship.
- **Days 5–11:** southbound cruise with a glacier day such as Hubbard or College Fjord, plus three ports.
- **Day 12:** disembark in Vancouver and fly home.

## Who should switch the order?

Choose cruise-first if your dates only work that way, if a northbound sailing is much cheaper, or if you'd like the cruise to be your warm-up. If anyone in your group has limited stamina or mobility, lean firmly towards land-first. ## Building the sequence that suits you

Cruise lines sell cruisetours in both directions, often on set departure dates. Decide the order before you book, since switching later usually means changing the whole package, and price the flights to and from Alaska alongside the tour.
