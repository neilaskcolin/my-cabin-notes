faq1q: What is the driest month for an Alaska cruise?
faq1a: May and June usually have the best odds of dry days in Southeast Alaska. Rainfall climbs through late summer, and September is reliably the wettest month of the cruise season.
faq2q: How much daylight do Alaska cruises get?
faq2a: Around the June solstice, Juneau gets roughly 18 hours of daylight, so scenic cruising after dinner is normal. By late September it is down to about 12 hours, and the Anchorage area at the north end of the route gets even longer days in June.

Alaska weather changes by the hour, but the monthly patterns are reliable enough to book and pack by. Late spring is drier and cooler, midsummer is warmest, and late summer gets wetter as the days shorten. Here is the season at a glance, using Juneau as a guide for the Inside Passage.

## Month-by-month at a glance
| Month | Typical highs | Rain odds | Daylight (mid-month, Juneau) | What stands out |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| May | 10–14°C | Lowest of the season | about 16–17 hours | Snowy peaks, full waterfalls, low fares |
| June | 12–17°C | Low | about 18 hours | Longest days, wildlife arriving |
| July | 15–19°C | Moderate, often brief showers | about 17–18 hours | Warmest, busiest, priciest |
| August | 14–18°C | Rising | about 15 hours | Salmon runs peak, bears at streams |
| September | 10–14°C | Highest of the season | about 13 hours | Fall colour, quiet decks, aurora chance |

These are long-term averages. Any given week can be sunny and warm or grey and drizzly, and the forecast for one port tells you little about the next.

## May: cool, clear and quiet
May has some of the season's best visibility. Snow sits low on the mountains, which makes glacier scenery especially striking, and meltwater waterfalls are at full flow. Decks are cold near the ice, sometimes below freezing with wind chill, so pack proper gloves and a toque. Fares are often among the lowest of the year.

## June: the long-day sweet spot
June balances warmth and dry odds better than any other month. With daylight from very early morning to late evening, you can watch scenery after dinner. Humpback whales are well established and wildlife is active. Late June is popular with retirees and couples, before Canadian schools finish.

## July: warmest and busiest
Port highs reach the high teens, with the odd warmer day into the low 20s. Showers are common but often short. This is peak family season across Canada, so fares and crowds both peak. For packing in warm months, a lighter mid-layer is often enough in port.

## August: salmon and bears
Early August looks a lot like July. The second half turns showery as North Pacific weather systems pick up. Salmon runs are at full strength, which draws bears to streams, so this is a strong month for a [bear-viewing excursion](/articles/bear-viewing-excursions-alaska/).

## September: wet, golden and cheap
Highs drop back to the low teens and the month brings the most rain of the season. In return you get fall colour, fewer passengers, softer prices, and dark evenings that occasionally produce the [northern lights](/articles/northern-lights-alaska-cruise/), though cloud often hides them.

> **Good to know:** weather in Vancouver or Seattle at the start and end of the trip is usually warmer and drier than in Alaska. Pack shorts for the port city if you like, but don't let a sunny Vancouver morning fool you about the glacier.

## Which month suits whom?
- **Best odds of dry weather:** May and June.
- **Warmest:** July and early August.
- **Families on Canadian school holidays:** July and August, with late June possible in some provinces.
- **Bears and salmon:** late July to early September.
- **Lowest fares and fewest people:** early May and September.
- **Aurora hopefuls:** late August and September, with modest expectations.

## Booking by the pattern, not the forecast
Pick the month whose pattern suits you, then pack the layering system regardless; rain can turn up in any week. Let price break the ties: the same cabin can cost noticeably less one or two weeks either side of peak.
