faq2a: Lines may bus you between stops, swap you onto a sister ship on the other side of the problem section, or change the itinerary. Low water is most likely in late summer and early fall, and high water after heavy spring rain, which is why good travel insurance matters on rivers.

Pick the Rhine if you want scenery: castles, vineyards and cathedral towns from your window. Pick the Danube if you want cities: Vienna, Bratislava and a famous night-time arrival in Budapest. Both are excellent first river cruises and both are easy to reach from Canada.

## Rhine or Danube: how they compare
| | Rhine | Danube |
|---|---|---|
| Classic 7-night route | Amsterdam to Basel, or reverse | Passau to Budapest, or Nuremberg to Budapest on longer trips |
| Highlights | Kinderdijk windmills, Cologne Cathedral, the Middle Rhine gorge, Strasbourg | Melk Abbey, Wachau Valley, Vienna, Bratislava, Budapest |
| Best for | Scenery from the deck | Cities, music and history |
| Currencies | Euro, Swiss franc in Basel | Euro, Hungarian forint in Budapest |
| Gateway airports | Amsterdam, Zurich or Basel | Munich, Nuremberg, Vienna or Budapest |
| Canadian flight options | Nonstops to Amsterdam from several Canadian cities | Usually one stop to Vienna or Budapest |

### The Rhine in brief
The UNESCO-listed Middle Rhine, between Rüdesheim and Koblenz, is the stretch people picture: a castle on nearly every bend and the Lorelei rock. Add Cologne's cathedral, Alsatian villages near Strasbourg and the Black Forest from Breisach, and it's the best river for watching from a lounge chair.

### The Danube in brief
The Danube stacks capitals. Vienna is a classic evening for concerts, Bratislava is compact and walkable, and the sail into Budapest with the parliament lit up is a highlight of European river cruising. The Wachau Valley, between Melk and Krems, gives you vineyards and apricot orchards between the cities.

## When to go
- **April to May:** fresh green banks, fewer crowds, cool evenings. Spring floods can occasionally disrupt sailings.
- **June to August:** long days and warm weather, but busier towns and the chance of heat.
- **September to October:** harvest season in the vineyards and pleasant walking weather, though late summer is when low water is most likely.
- **Late November to December:** Christmas-market sailings to Nuremberg, Vienna, Cologne and Strasbourg. Short, cold days and very popular, so book early.

> **Good to know:** river ships have to fit under bridges and through locks. In a dry spell they can run aground, and after heavy rain they may not clear the bridges. Lines usually respond with coach transfers or a ship swap, so the trip goes ahead, but in a different shape. Insurance with trip interruption cover is worth having.

## What river cruise fares include
River cruises bundle more than most ocean fares: often a guided walking tour in each port, wine and beer with lunch and dinner, wifi, and sometimes airport transfers. Some lines also include all drinks and gratuities. Compare what's included rather than headline fares, as explained in [what's actually included in a cruise fare](/articles/whats-included-cruise-fare/).

Cabins are compact. A "French balcony" is a sliding glass door with a railing, while a full step-out balcony costs more and sells out first. If you're weighing river against ocean, [river cruise vs ocean cruise for couples over 50](/articles/river-cruise-vs-ocean-cruise-over-50/) has the trade-offs.

## Getting there from Canada
Amsterdam, Frankfurt and Munich have nonstop flights from several Canadian cities, including Toronto, Montreal, Vancouver and Calgary, depending on season. Basel, Vienna and Budapest usually mean one connection. Since river cruises are one-way, book an open-jaw ticket: into the start city, home from the end city.

> **Example:** a Calgary couple on a Rhine sailing might fly nonstop to Amsterdam, spend two nights there before boarding, then sail to Basel and take the train to Zurich airport for the flight home.

Canadians can visit Schengen countries for up to 90 days in any 180 without a visa. Check whether the EU's new travel authorisation is required by your travel dates.

## Booking a river cruise, step by step
1. Choose the river first, then the line, since inclusions differ more than ships do.
2. Pick dates, keeping water levels and Christmas markets in mind.
3. Book a full balcony early if you want one; they often go a year ahead for peak dates.
4. Arrange open-jaw flights, ideally landing a day or two early.
5. Buy insurance at deposit time, with cancellation and interruption cover.
