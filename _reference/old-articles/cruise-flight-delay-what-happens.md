It depends on two things: how you booked the flight, and how late you will be. Ships sail on time, so if you miss the boarding cut-off on a flight you booked yourself, getting to the ship becomes your job and your bill. On air booked through the cruise line, it becomes theirs.

## Will the ship wait for you?
Almost never for individual passengers. Boarding typically closes 60–90 minutes before departure, and the schedule is tied to things the captain can't move: berth slots in the next port, compulsory coastal pilots, and tidal currents in the narrow channels north of Vancouver. A line may hold briefly when a large group on its own booked flights is late, but that is a decision about dozens of passengers, not two.

## A delay is unfolding: what to do, in order
1. **Tell the gate agent you are sailing today.** Ask for the earliest option, including partner airlines and flights to the same city through a different hub.
2. **Call the cruise line's travel emergency number.** It is on your boarding documents or in the app. Give your flight number and new arrival time. If you booked through an agency, call their after-hours line too.
3. **Call your insurer's assistance line before buying anything.** Many policies want approval before you spend money on replacement flights.
4. **Get the delay in writing.** Canadian airlines must tell you the reason for a delay; ask for it by email or screenshot it in the app.
5. **If you will miss sail-away, ask where to meet the ship.** The line can tell you the first port and alert its port agent there.
6. **Keep every receipt:** new flights, hotel, meals, taxis.

## What catching up actually involves
### On an Alaska sailing from Vancouver
The first port is often Ketchikan or Juneau, a day or two after leaving. Getting there usually means flying from Vancouver through Seattle on short notice, clearing US preclearance at YVR, possibly a hotel night, and a port agent getting you aboard. Budget well over C$1,000 per person, plus the cruise days you lose.

### On a Caribbean sailing from Florida
If the first day is at sea, you may have two days to reach a foreign port such as Cozumel or Nassau. You'll need your passport, and flights into smaller islands can be sparse. [How winter storms disrupt Caribbean flights](/articles/snow-delay-winter-flights-caribbean-cruise/) covers that season's extra risk.

## When the cruise line owns the problem
If your flights were booked as part of the cruise package, the line monitors them and steps in when they break down. Typically it will:

- rebook you on the next workable flight
- cover a hotel if you are stuck overnight
- fly you to the next port and arrange the transfer to the ship
- do all this without extra charge, provided you checked in for your original flight on time

This only applies to air sold by the line, not flights you booked separately on the same planes. The trade-offs are weighed in [cruise line air vs booking your own flights](/articles/cruise-line-air-vs-own-flights/).

> **Canadian tip:** under the Air Passenger Protection Regulations, a delay within the airline's control that isn't safety-related earns compensation from a large airline of C$400 for 3–6 hours, C$700 for 6–9 hours and C$1,000 for 9 hours or more, at the rates that have applied recently. Weather delays don't qualify. Claim within a year.

That compensation is a fixed amount and won't cover a missed cruise. For delays caused by strikes and wider airline problems, see [how airline disruptions affect embarkation](/articles/how-airline-disruptions-affect-cruise-embarkation/).

## What insurance pays
Missed-connection or trip interruption cover can reimburse catch-up flights, hotels and the unused part of the cruise. Expect conditions: written proof from the airline, a reasonable gap between your scheduled arrival and boarding, and caps that may be lower than a last-minute flight to Alaska. Read the clause before you travel, not at the gate.

## The fix that beats all of it
Nearly every version of this story starts with a same-day flight. Arriving the evening before costs one hotel night and turns a four-hour delay into an annoyance. The full risk math is in [flying the day before vs same day](/articles/fly-day-before-cruise/).
