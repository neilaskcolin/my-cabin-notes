If you want to actually cook, not just watch, choose your line carefully. Almost every ship has a chef demo on a sea day, but only a handful run a proper teaching kitchen with individual stations, and those classes sell out.

## Demo, galley tour or hands-on class?
These are three different experiences, and cruise marketing doesn't always make the difference clear.

### Cooking demonstrations
A chef cooks on a stage or in a show kitchen while you watch, sometimes with tastings. They're usually free and listed in the daily program. Good fun, but you won't touch a knife.

### Galley tours
A walk through the main kitchen to see how thousands of meals come together. Often free, sometimes paid when combined with a wider behind-the-scenes tour. Popular on sea days.

### Hands-on classes
You cook at your own or a shared station, guided by a chef, then eat what you made. This needs a purpose-built kitchen, so it's mostly limited to specific ships. Expect a fee and small groups.

## Which lines run real culinary programs?
Programs change, so treat this as a starting point and confirm on your actual ship and sailing.

| Line | What's on offer | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Oceania | Culinary Center on its larger ships, plus some chef-led food tours ashore | Hands-on classes at individual stations, paid |
| Regent Seven Seas | Culinary Arts Kitchen on its newer ships | Hands-on classes, paid |
| Silversea | S.A.L.T. Lab on newer ships, tied to the region you're sailing | Hands-on classes and demos |
| Holland America | Cooking demos in a show-kitchen setting on many ships | Mostly demos, some sailings add hands-on sessions |
| Big mainstream lines (Royal Caribbean, Carnival, Norwegian, MSC, Princess, Celebrity) | Demos, galley tours, multi-course chef's table dinners on many ships | Watch and taste, chef's tables paid |

Luxury lines often include more food experiences in the fare, which is part of what you pay for (see [luxury lines compared](/articles/luxury-lines-compared-extra-spend/)).

## What do cooking classes cost?
At recent prices, hands-on classes on premium lines are often around US$100 or more per person (roughly C$135+), and longer or market-tour classes cost more. Chef's table dinners on mainstream lines tend to run roughly US$100–150 per person. Demos are free.

> **Watch out:** classes on dedicated teaching kitchens can fill months ahead, especially Mediterranean sailings. If cooking is a priority, book through the cruise planner as soon as your booking window opens, the same day you'd book specialty dinners.

### Questions to ask before you book the cruise
Lines with a teaching kitchen don't always have one on every ship, so check these first:

- Does my specific ship have the hands-on kitchen, or only a demo stage?
- How many classes run on my sailing, and on which days?
- What does each class cost, and how many people does it take?
- Can a partner who doesn't want to cook come along to watch or taste?

## Cooking classes ashore
Some of the best food experiences happen in port. Lines sell excursions such as a Tuscan farmhouse class from Livorno, a market-and-cook day in Provence, or a Greek island taverna class. Independent cookery schools near the port are often cheaper but you'll have to manage timing yourself. Compare the options in [shore excursions through the ship vs independent](/articles/cruise-excursion-through-ship-vs-independent/).

## Who gets the most out of a culinary cruise?
- Couples who enjoy cooking together at home and want a sea-day activity with substance.
- Food-focused travellers choosing between a [Mediterranean itinerary for couples](/articles/mediterranean-cruise-best-itinerary-couples/) and other routes.
- Anyone pairing classes with wine tastings (see [wine and food-focused cruises for Canadians](/articles/wine-food-focused-cruises-canadians/)).

It suits casual cooks less if you'd rather spend sea days by the pool. If you have allergies or a special diet, tell the culinary team when booking; most classes can adapt, as explained in [dietary needs at sea](/articles/dietary-needs-cruise-ships/).

> **Example:** a Toronto couple on a 10-night Mediterranean sailing might book one hands-on pasta class on a sea day, one shore cooking excursion in Italy, and use the free demos for the rest. That gives them a food thread through the trip without spending every sea day in the kitchen.

## Planning a food-focused sailing
Decide whether you want to watch or cook. If watching is enough, any big ship will do. If you want your own station and apron, pick one of the lines with a teaching kitchen, check which ship has it, and book classes the day the planner opens.
