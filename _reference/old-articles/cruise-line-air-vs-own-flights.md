Cruise line air is the protected option; booking your own flights is the flexible one. The fare difference is often small, so the real question is how much a broken flight would hurt on your particular route, and whether you'd rather manage that risk yourself.

## What you are really comparing
| Feature | Cruise line air | Your own flights |
|---|---|---|
| Price | Sometimes higher, sometimes lower in peak season or on promotions | Often cheapest with early seat sales |
| If a flight is delayed or cancelled | The line rebooks you and gets you to the ship or next port | You rebook and pay; insurance may reimburse later |
| Choice of flights | Often assigned; picking your own may cost extra | Full choice |
| Seat selection | Sometimes limited until tickets are issued | Normal airline rules |
| Changes and cancellation | Often non-refundable once ticketed | Depends on your fare |
| Airline points | Usually earned, sometimes at lower rates | Full earning, and you can redeem points |
| Airport transfers | Often bundled or easy to add | Your own arrangements |

Most big lines run an air program. Royal Caribbean calls its version Air2Sea, Princess has EZair and Celebrity sells Flights by Celebrity, and Norwegian runs frequent "free air" promotions where you pay taxes and fees. Names, rules and which Canadian airports are offered change, so check what applies to your sailing.

## Where cruise line air earns its keep
### One-way Alaska sailings
A Vancouver-to-Whittier cruise means flying into Vancouver and home from Anchorage, usually with a connection. That return leg is longer and less frequent than a domestic hop, which is exactly where protection matters. [Where one-way Alaska sailings end](/articles/anchorage-vs-whittier-vs-seward/) explains the logistics.

### Winter flights to Florida
Toronto, Montreal, Winnipeg and Calgary all see winter storms, de-icing delays and cancelled connections. A January flight to a Caribbean sailing carries real risk (see [snow delay risk on winter flights](/articles/snow-delay-winter-flights-caribbean-cruise/)).

### Same-day arrivals and tight connections
Flying in a day early is always the better fix, but if you can't, line air means the missed ship isn't your bill. [What happens when your flight is delayed](/articles/cruise-flight-delay-what-happens/) walks through both outcomes.

## Where your own flights make sense
A nonstop from Calgary or Edmonton to Vancouver the day before sailing carries little risk; if it is delayed, you still have hours of buffer. Booking your own also suits points collectors, people loyal to one airline, and anyone who wants to extend the trip with a few days in Vancouver or Florida.

> **Example:** an Edmonton couple on a one-way sailing to Whittier gets a cruise line air quote that is C$150 more per person than booking themselves. Their homeward flight from Anchorage connects through Vancouver. If that connection fails, the DIY couple pays for the hotel and rebooking; with line air, it is the line's job. For C$300 total, many people would take the protection on that leg.

## Questions to ask before you buy line air
1. When will tickets be issued, and will I see my flight times before I pay?
2. Can I choose specific flights, and what does that cost?
3. If I cancel the cruise, is the air refundable or credited?
4. Does the protection cover my flight home as well as the outbound?
5. Will I earn airline points, and can I select seats?
6. Are airport transfers included, or extra (see [cruise ship transfers explained](/articles/cruise-ship-transfers-explained/))?
7. Is the price shown in Canadian or US dollars?

## The middle path many experienced cruisers use
Book your own flights for flexibility and points, arrive a day early, and buy insurance with solid missed-connection and trip interruption cover. That approximates the line's protection without the premium, as long as you read the policy's conditions carefully. Choosing a quieter travel day helps too; see [the best day of the week to fly for a cruise](/articles/best-day-week-fly-cruise-connection/).

## Deciding for your trip
Look at your route, the season and the price gap on your dates. Short domestic hop the day before: book it yourself. Long, connecting or winter itinerary: get the line's air quoted and weigh the difference.
