On MSC's North American sailings, the drink package question now has one answer for alcohol: Premium Extra, at roughly US$85–100 per person per day at recent prices, service charge included. That is roughly C$115–140 a day each, so it only pays off for people who drink steadily from lunch onward.

## What happened to Easy and Easy Plus?
MSC used to sell cheaper tiers. The Easy package was withdrawn on North American sailings in December 2024, and Easy Plus stopped being sold as an add-on in August 2025. Packages bought before those dates are honoured. Easy still appears in some other markets, including on European sailings, which is why older articles and forum posts quote lower prices.

## MSC drink packages on North American sailings
| Package | Recent price per person per day | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| Premium Extra | About $85 prepaid, $98 on board (7+ nights) | Spirits, cocktails, wine and champagne by the glass, beer, specialty coffee, juices and sodas, up to about $16 a drink |
| Alcohol-Free | About $28–35 | Sodas, mocktails, specialty coffee, juices, bottled water |
| Minors (under 21) | About $19–24 | Sodas, juices, water and similar |

Prices are in US dollars, include the 18% service charge on USD-currency ships, and vary by ship and sailing length. Confirm current figures for your sailing.

## The break-even for a couple
> **Quick math:** a couple from Edmonton on a 7-night Caribbean sailing from Miami pays about 2 × US$85 × 7 = US$1,190 for Premium Extra, or roughly C$1,610–1,665. A cocktail at around US$14 plus 18% is about US$16.50, so each person needs roughly six to seven drinks a day, counting coffees and bottled water at lower prices, to come out ahead.

A day of two cocktails, a beer, a specialty coffee and a bottle of water works out to roughly US$54 each with the service charge. That is well under the package price. The package wins when you add wine at dinner, a couple of pool-deck drinks and an evening in the bars.

### Who it suits
- Couples who will have a drink at lunch, wine with dinner and a nightcap, every day
- Travellers on a long sea-day-heavy cruise, such as a Grand Voyage from Europe
- Anyone who hates watching a running bar tab

### Who should skip it
- Light or occasional drinkers
- Guests spending long port days ashore, including a full day at Ocean Cay
- Anyone booking MSC Yacht Club, where Premium Extra is already included

> **Watch out:** the 15-drink daily cap resets at 6 a.m., and drinks over the price limit, such as top-shelf whisky or bottles of wine, are charged separately at menu price.

## Cheaper alternatives on MSC
MSC doesn't let you bring your own wine (see the [MSC alcohol policy](/articles/msc-alcohol-policy/)), so the cheaper routes are paying per drink, buying the Alcohol-Free package if your spend is mostly coffee and soda, or sharing bottles of wine at dinner. Prepaying is usually cheaper than buying on board, with occasional sales cutting the price further.

## How MSC compares
| Line | Package | Recent price per person per day | Catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MSC** | Premium Extra | About $85–100 USD incl. 18% | Only alcohol package on North American sailings; 15-drink daily cap |
| [Carnival](/articles/carnival-drink-package-price/) | Cheers! | $70–75 USD + 20% | 15 alcoholic drinks a day |
| [Norwegian](/articles/norwegian-drink-package-price/) | Unlimited Open Bar | $100–120 USD retail | Usually via Free at Sea (pay the 20% charge) |

With Easy Plus gone, MSC is no longer the cheap option on drinks in North America. Premium Extra now costs about the same as Carnival's Cheers! once Carnival's 20% charge is added, and less than Norwegian's retail price, though Norwegian guests often get theirs through a promotion. ## Getting the package for less
Ask whether your dates carry a drinks promotion or a pre-cruise sale before you buy at full price. For a couple who would buy Premium Extra anyway, a discount is worth hundreds of dollars.
