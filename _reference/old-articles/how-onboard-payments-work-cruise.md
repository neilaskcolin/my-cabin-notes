Nothing on a cruise ship takes cash at the till. Your cruise card, wristband or phone is your wallet, and behind it sits an onboard account that is settled at the end of the trip. Knowing how that account works heads off the two surprises first-timers hit: card holds and currency fees.

## Setting up your onboard account
1. **During online check-in**, add a credit card to your account in the line's app or website. You can also do it at the terminal or at guest services.
2. **Decide who pays for whom.** Everyone in a cabin can be linked to one card, or each adult can use their own. Couples who split costs often prefer separate cards.
3. **Set limits for children and teens** if your line allows it. Many lines let parents block or cap charging on a child's card.
4. **Prefer cash?** You can leave a cash deposit at guest services instead, usually a few hundred US dollars. Top it up as you spend, and collect any balance on the last day.

Your card is also your cabin key and ID for getting on and off the ship. Newer ships add wristbands, medallions or phone keys, as covered in [cruise cards and wearables](/articles/cruise-cards-how-you-pay-onboard/) and [using the cruise line app](/articles/using-cruise-line-app-before-during-trip/).

## Holds, charges and credits: what is what

| Item | What it is | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-authorization hold | A temporary block on your card when you board, and again as you spend | Reduces available credit; not a charge |
| Daily gratuity | An automatic per-person charge, unless prepaid | Shows up on your account every day |
| Purchases | Drinks, tours, spa, photos, shops | Added to your account as you go |
| Onboard credit | Money from the line or your booking | Comes off eligible purchases first |
| Final charge | Your total, minus credits, at the end | Posts to your card within a day or two |

> **Example:** a couple might see a hold of a couple of hundred US dollars at boarding, then further holds midweek as their bar tab grows. The final charge replaces them, but the holds can linger on their statement for several business days. Leave some room on your card's limit.

## Checking your account during the cruise
The app, and often your cabin TV, shows your running total. Check it every couple of days. Common errors include a drink charged to the wrong cabin, a cancelled tour still showing, or a specialty dinner billed twice. These are easy to fix while you are aboard and much harder after.

Some onboard credit is non-refundable and disappears if unused, so spend it before the last night. Ask guest services which of your credits are refundable.

## Paying in Canadian dollars
Most lines bill in US dollars. When you register a Canadian card, the line may offer to convert your bill into Canadian dollars at its own rate. Compare that rate with your card's. Paying in US dollars on a card with no foreign-transaction fee usually works out cheaper. The full reasoning is in [should Canadians pay in CAD or USD](/articles/paying-cad-vs-usd-cruise/).

> **Canadian tip:** several Canadian credit cards waive the usual 2.5% foreign-transaction fee. On a US$1,500 onboard bill, that is roughly C$50 saved.

## The last night
Your final statement arrives in the app or under your cabin door. Raise any disputes at guest services before you disembark. If you paid by cash deposit, collect any refund then too. After that, [disembarkation morning](/articles/disembarkation-morning-getting-off-ship/) follows its own routine.
