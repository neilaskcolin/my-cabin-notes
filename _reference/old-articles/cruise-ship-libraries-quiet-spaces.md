A big ship can carry thousands of people and still have places where all you hear is the engine and a page turning. You just have to know where they are and when to go.

## Where are the quiet spaces on a cruise ship?
Names differ, but most mainstream ships have some version of each of these:

- **Library or reading lounge:** comfortable chairs, a modest book collection, often jigsaw puzzles and board games
- **Card room:** tables for bridge, cribbage or a quiet coffee, usually with games to borrow
- **Adults-only pool or sun deck:** Royal Caribbean's Solarium, Carnival's Serenity and Disney's Quiet Cove are included in the fare
- **Paid retreats:** Princess's Sanctuary and Norwegian's Vibe Beach Club charge a pass, which keeps numbers down
- **Observation lounges:** forward-facing lounges high up, often near-empty in the daytime
- **Promenade decks:** on ships that have an outdoor wraparound deck, the loungers away from the doors are peaceful
- **The chapel:** on ships that have a wedding chapel, it's sometimes open for quiet reflection when not booked (see [religious services on a cruise](/articles/cruise-ship-religious-services-sunday/))

The [adults-only areas guide](/articles/adults-only-areas-cruise-ships/) explains the rules and age limits for each line's retreat.

## When to find them quietest

### Sea days
The pool deck and buffet are packed from late morning. The library, card room and indoor lounges are calmest mid-morning and mid-afternoon, before trivia and pre-dinner drinks start.

### Port days
The whole ship empties. Stay aboard for a morning or two on a port you've visited before and you'll have the pick of any lounger or armchair.

### Evenings
During the main show and the busy dinner hour, open decks and forward lounges clear out. It's the best time for a quiet drink or a sunset read.

> **Example:** a couple from Victoria on a 7-night Alaska sailing might spend the sea day morning with tea in the forward observation lounge as the ship enters a fjord, then take paperbacks to an adults-only deck in the afternoon while families head for the pool.

## Why libraries vary so much
Classic and premium ships, such as many Holland America and Princess ships, still give the library a proper room with sea views. On some newer, entertainment-led ships, it has shrunk to shelves in a lounge or been replaced by a café. If reading spaces matter, look at the ship's deck plan, not just the line's brochure (see [how to read a deck plan](/articles/reading-cruise-ship-deck-plans/)).

## Making a quiet day work
- **Bring your own reading.** Load an e-reader or download audiobooks before you sail; ship wifi is slow for big files.
- **Pack noise-cancelling earbuds** for loud pool decks and cabins near lifts.
- **Use the book swap.** Leave a finished paperback and take a new one.
- **Choose your cabin carefully.** A quiet cabin is your best retreat; see [where to book the quietest cabins](/articles/quietest-cabins-where-to-book/).

Glossary terms like "solarium" and "promenade" are explained in [lido, promenade and other ship terms](/articles/cruise-ship-terminology-lido-promenade-deck/).

## Choosing a ship known for good quiet spaces
If calm matters more than waterslides, the ship matters more than the line. Smaller and mid-size ships, adult-leaning lines and itineraries with many port days give you the most quiet. [Quiet ships without waterslides](/articles/quiet-ships-without-waterslides/) is a good place to start comparing.
