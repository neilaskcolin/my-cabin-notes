Disney calls its final payment deadline the paid-in-full date: about 90 days before sailing for cruises of up to 5 nights and 120 days for 6 nights or more. Before that date a standard stateroom can be cancelled for a full refund. After it, the penalties climb quickly.

## Disney's paid-in-full date by cruise length
The date depends only on the length of the cruise, and Disney has recently aligned concierge bookings to the same deadlines. A 3- or 4-night Bahamas sailing on the Disney Wish from Port Canaveral is due 90 days out. A 7-night Alaska sailing from Vancouver on the Disney Wonder or Disney Magic is due 120 days out.

Some special sailings have had their own terms over the years. Read the date printed on your confirmation rather than working it out yourself, and see [cruise deposits and final payment dates explained](/articles/cruise-deposits-final-payment/) for how this works across lines.

## The cancellation penalty schedule
At recent policy, penalties are charged per guest as follows.

| Days before sailing | 1–5 nights | 6+ nights |
|---|---|---|
| Before paid-in-full | Full refund (standard staterooms) | Full refund (standard staterooms) |
| 89–45 / 119–56 | Deposit lost | Deposit lost |
| 44–30 / 55–30 | 50% of the vacation price | 50% of the vacation price |
| 29–15 | 75% | 75% |
| 14 or fewer | 100% | 100% |

Confirm the current schedule in your booking terms before you rely on it.

### Concierge staterooms and suites
Concierge bookings follow the same 50%, 75% and 100% bands, but the deposit is forfeited from the day you book. If you are weighing a concierge stateroom on the Wish, Treasure or Destiny, decide on dates first. Changing your mind later costs real money.

> **Example:** a family from Edmonton books a 7-night Disney Wonder sailing leaving Vancouver on 12 July 2027. Paid-in-full falls around 14 March. Cancel in April and they lose the deposit. Cancel in early June and half the fare is gone; in mid-June, three-quarters. From 28 June, all of it.

## Why Disney families get caught out
Disney fares rarely fall, so most families book as soon as their sailing opens, often a year or more ahead. That makes the paid-in-full date easy to forget. The [Disney Cruise Line price drop](/articles/disney-price-drop-after-booking/) page explains why waiting rarely pays.

School holidays add pressure. A March break sailing on a 4-night Bahamas cruise is due in mid-December, right when the holiday bills arrive. A 7-night March break cruise is due in mid-November.

> **Watch out:** removing one guest from a stateroom after paid-in-full can reprice the fare for everyone else, because Disney prices by occupancy. Ask for the new total before you cancel one person.

## Before you pay the deposit, check
- The exact paid-in-full date on your confirmation, saved in your calendar the same day.
- Whether your category is concierge, which makes the deposit non-refundable.
- Whether your fare is in Canadian or US dollars, since a USD refund to a Canadian card can come back at a different exchange rate. See [paying in CAD vs USD for a cruise](/articles/paying-cad-vs-usd-cruise/).
- Who in your party has a medical condition, so your insurance covers it from day one.

## Insurance: the only way back after paid-in-full
Disney sells its own vacation protection plan. Canadian policies often give stronger medical cover, which matters because provincial health plans pay little or nothing at sea or in Florida. The trade-offs are in [travel insurance vs cruise line protection plans](/articles/travel-insurance-vs-cruise-protection/), and [cancel for any reason cover](/articles/cancel-for-any-reason-cruise-insurance-explained/) explains the option that pays out even without a covered reason.

Buy cover when you pay the deposit. Many Canadian policies only waive pre-existing condition limits if you buy within a set number of days of your first payment.
