Alaska excursions are where budgets double, but skipping them entirely means missing a lot. The trick is to spend on experiences your feet can't reach and walk everywhere else. Here is how that plays out port by port.

## What earns its price
A paid tour is worth it when it takes you somewhere the ship and the town cannot:
- **Whale watching:** boats go where the humpbacks are feeding, and you see blows, flukes and sometimes orcas up close.
- **Flightseeing or a glacier landing:** the only way to see icefields from above or stand on a glacier.
- **The White Pass railway:** the cliff-hugging gold-rush route out of Skagway is the main reason to be there.
- **Bear viewing in late summer:** a specialized trip, but there is no substitute. See [bear-viewing excursions in Alaska](/articles/bear-viewing-excursions-alaska/).

## Port by port: pay or walk?
### Ketchikan
Walk: Creek Street's boardwalk, the salmon ladder in season, and the Totem Heritage Center are all close to the docks. The [free Ketchikan walking plan](/articles/ketchikan-free-walking-plan/) strings them together. Pay: a Misty Fjords floatplane trip is the splurge here, scenic and expensive.

### Juneau
Pay: this is the best whale-watching port on most itineraries. Mendenhall Glacier is a must, but a shuttle bus gets you there for far less than a guided tour. Flightseeing over the Juneau Icefield is the big-ticket option. The full ranking is in [Juneau shore excursions ranked](/articles/juneau-shore-excursions-ranked/).

### Skagway
Pay: the railway. Walk: the preserved gold-rush main street, where the national park service offers ranger-led walks. A paid "town highlights" bus mostly drives you past things you can stroll to. Is the train worth it? See [the White Pass railway](/articles/skagway-white-pass-railway-worth-it/).

### Icy Strait Point
Pay: whale watching here can be excellent. The very long zipline is the other headliner. Walk: forest and shore trails, and the cannery museum near the dock. [What's at Icy Strait Point](/articles/icy-strait-point-whats-there/) explains the layout.

### Sitka
Walk or take a shuttle: Sitka National Historical Park's totem trail is free, and the raptor centre and bear rescue centre charge modest entry fees. This is the best port for a cheap, rewarding day.

### Glacier days
No excursion needed. The ship takes you to the ice, and a good spot on deck is free; see [the best spot for glacier viewing](/articles/best-spot-cruise-ship-glacier-viewing/).

## Budget tiers per couple
At recent prices, for a 7-night sailing, roughly:

1. **Lean, US$300–500 (roughly C$420–700):** one whale watch for two, plus walking and shuttles everywhere else.
2. **Balanced, US$700–1,100 (roughly C$970–1,530):** a whale watch, the railway, and a Mendenhall shuttle.
3. **Splurge, US$1,800 and up (roughly C$2,500+):** add flightseeing or a helicopter glacier trip, or a fly-out bear tour.

> **Example:** a couple from Toronto on a July sailing could book a Juneau whale watch and the White Pass railway, walk Ketchikan, and take the Mendenhall shuttle. That lands around US$850–950 for both, in the balanced tier, and covers the trip's signature moments.

## Ship tours or independent?
The same whale boats often sell both ways, with independent bookings usually cheaper. The ship's guarantee to wait for its own late tours matters on short port days, and much less on long ones. The numbers are worked through in [independent vs package shore tour costs](/articles/alaska-independent-vs-package-shore-tour-cost/).

## Can you skip excursions altogether?
Yes, and plenty of people do. Ketchikan, Skagway and Sitka are easy to explore on foot, Juneau's tramway and glacier shuttle cost far less than a tour, and the glacier day needs nothing at all. The one thing you will genuinely miss is a close look at whales, since sightings from a big ship's deck tend to be distant. If the budget only stretches to one paid tour, make it that. [How many excursions to book](/articles/how-many-excursions-book-per-cruise/) helps you pace the week.

## Common mistakes
- Booking a tour in every port and arriving at the glacier day exhausted.
- Paying for a bus tour of a town you could walk in an hour.
- Booking flightseeing on a cloudy morning with no refund option; weather cancellations are common, so check the policy.
- Waiting too long: popular tours sell out weeks ahead in July and August. [When to book cruise excursions](/articles/best-time-book-cruise-excursions/) covers timing.

## Spend where it counts
Pick one standout experience per person and one shared family or couple trip, then walk the rest. Charges post in US dollars, so a no-foreign-transaction-fee card saves around 2.5% on everything. Onboard credit from your booking can also cover excursions, so check whether your fare includes any before paying cash.
