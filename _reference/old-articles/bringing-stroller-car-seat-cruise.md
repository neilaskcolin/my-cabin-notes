Pack a small folding stroller and a baby carrier, and think twice about the car seat. That combination covers almost every situation on a cruise, from long ship corridors to tender boats and cobbled port streets, without filling your cabin.

## Strollers on board: what to expect
Every major line allows strollers. The constraint is space. Standard cabins are compact, and you cannot leave a stroller in the corridor, because hallways must stay clear for safety. A stroller that folds small enough to slide beside the bed or into the closet makes life much easier.

On the ship, a stroller is most useful for naps on the go and long walks to the buffet. In port it earns its place on flat, paved towns. It is less helpful on stairs, crowded elevators at peak times and uneven trails.

### Tender ports
At tender ports, you will usually need to fold the stroller and carry your child onto the boat. [Cruise ship tenders explained](/articles/cruise-ship-tenders-explained/) shows what boarding involves.

## Do you need a car seat?
Not on the ship, and rarely on excursions. Here is how to decide:

- **Driving to the port:** if you are driving from Calgary or Edmonton to Vancouver, or from Ontario to a US port, you have the car seat anyway. Leave it in the car.
- **Flying, then a shuttle or taxi:** many taxis and port shuttles do not supply car seats, and rules vary by place. Ask your transfer company in advance.
- **Rental car before or after the cruise:** bring your own or rent one with the car.
- **Ship excursions:** coaches and boats do not normally use car seats, and some tours have minimum ages for young children.

> **Canadian tip:** Air Canada and WestJet generally check a stroller and a car seat for each child free, outside your baggage allowance. Strollers can often be checked at the gate, so you have them right up to boarding. Confirm the rules for your fare, and see [luggage rules when flying to your cruise](/articles/luggage-rules-flying-to-cruise/).

## Packing order for baby gear
1. **Check your line's minimum age.** Many lines require infants to be at least six months old, and some longer itineraries require 12 months. [Cruising with a baby or toddler](/articles/cruising-with-baby-toddler/) covers the rules.
2. **Choose a compact stroller** with a one-hand fold and a sunshade.
3. **Add a soft-structured carrier** for tenders, stairs and busy ports.
4. **Decide on the car seat** based on your transfers, not the ship.
5. **Ask the line about a crib** when you book. Many lines provide a travel cot on request, so you do not need to bring one.
6. **Request a larger cabin layout if needed.** Families with lots of gear often find [connecting cabins](/articles/connecting-cabins-one-big-cabin-families/) worth the cost.

## Gear you can usually leave at home
- A full-size travel system stroller.
- A high chair, since dining rooms and buffets usually have them.
- Large toy bins: cabin space is better used for clothes and diapers.
- Extension cords or power strips for bottle warmers, which most lines hold at security.

Some lines offer nursery or late-night group care for younger children, covered in [cruise babysitting options](/articles/cruise-ship-babysitting-in-cabin-service/), which can change how much you need to carry around the ship.

## Planning baby gear for your cruise
Pack for the ship you are sailing, not your home routine. A compact stroller, a carrier and a quick check of your transfers will cover most families. Confirm crib availability and any excursion age limits before you sail, and your cabin will stay livable all week.
