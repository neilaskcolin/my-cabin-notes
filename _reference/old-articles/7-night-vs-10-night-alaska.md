For most first-timers, the 7-night cruise is the right call: it covers the classic Inside Passage ports and a glacier without eating two weeks of vacation. The 10-night format suits people with flexible calendars who want Sitka, a second glacier, or simply more time on deck. The catch is that "longer" does not always mean "more Alaska".

## What does a 7-night Alaska cruise include?
The classic week runs round trip from Vancouver or Seattle. Expect three or four ports, usually Ketchikan, Juneau and Skagway, sometimes Icy Strait Point, plus one scenic glacier day in Glacier Bay, Tracy Arm, Endicott Arm or at Hubbard Glacier. Seattle round trips also stop in Victoria, often for a short evening call, because US rules require a foreign port.

Seven-night one-way sailings between Vancouver and Seward or Whittier are the other common shape. They swap the return leg for the Gulf of Alaska and usually add Hubbard Glacier or College Fjord. See [round-trip vs one-way Alaska](/articles/roundtrip-vs-oneway-alaska/) for that choice.

## What do 10 or more nights add?
It depends heavily on where the ship starts.

### From Vancouver or Seattle
Extra nights here are mostly extra Alaska. Typical additions are Sitka, with its Russian-era history and totem park, Haines, Icy Strait Point, or a second glacier so you see both a fjord glacier and a big tidewater face. Port days are less often back to back, which leaves real time to watch the coastline.

### From San Francisco or Los Angeles
These 10- to 12-night round trips include several days at sea along the US coast before you reach the Inside Passage. They suit people who love sea days or live near those ports. For a Canadian flying in, a 10-night sailing from California may have barely more time in Alaska than a 7-night from Vancouver.

> **Watch out:** compare port lists and sea days, not just the number of nights. Count how many days the ship is actually in Alaskan waters.

## The money: per night vs per trip
Here is how the two formats tend to compare, at recent prices and per person in an inside cabin. Confirm current fares for your dates.

| | 7-night (Vancouver, July) | 10-night (Vancouver or Seattle, July) |
|---|---|---|
| Typical fare | US$900–1,400 (roughly C$1,250–1,950) | US$1,200–1,900 (roughly C$1,650–2,650) |
| Fare per night | about US$130–200 | about US$120–190 |
| Gratuities at US$18–25/day | US$125–175 | US$180–250 |
| Vacation days used | about 5–6 | about 8–9 |
| Alaska ports | 3–4 | 4–6 |

> **Quick math:** the 10-night fare often works out cheaper per night, but onboard costs scale with days. If you spend about US$60 a day each on drinks, wifi and extras, three more nights adds roughly US$180 per person (about C$250) before excursions in the extra ports.

Peak weeks in July and August, when Canadian schools are out, cost more in both formats. May and September sailings bring both down. The [Alaska shoulder season pros and cons](/articles/alaska-shoulder-season-pros-cons/) page covers what you trade for the lower price.

## Who should pick which?
**Pick 7 nights if:**
- It is your first Alaska cruise and you want the highlights.
- You are travelling with school-age kids in July or August and need to fit one week.
- Vacation days are scarce, or you want to add a few days in Vancouver or the Rockies.

**Pick 10 or more nights if:**
- You are retired, semi-retired or between jobs, and time is cheaper than money.
- You want Sitka or a second glacier on the same trip.
- You found the 7-night pace rushed last time, with early port mornings every day.

## The middle options
Nine-night sailings exist on some lines and can sneak in Sitka without the full commitment. Another route to "more Alaska" is a 7-night one-way cruise plus a land tour. [Alaska cruisetours with Denali by rail](/articles/alaska-cruisetours-denali-rail/) explains how those add 3 to 7 nights inland. For the truly keen, a northbound one-way followed by the same ship's southbound sailing makes a 14-night trip, often with a different port mix on each leg; [how back-to-back cruises are booked](/articles/back-to-back-cruises-how-booking-works/) covers the mechanics.

## How to compare two real sailings
Pick two or three specific departures and lay them side by side: ports, glacier, sea days, total fare including taxes, and your likely onboard spend. A first-time traveller from Edmonton or Winnipeg usually finds the 7-night wins on the vacation calendar. A repeat visitor often finds the 10-night is the better cruise.
