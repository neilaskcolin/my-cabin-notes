A Norwegian fjords cruise runs from roughly May to September, usually from Southampton or a northern European port, and lasts 7 to 14 nights. For Canadians who have already done Alaska, it is the natural next trip: the same glacier-carved drama, but greener, steeper and lined with villages.

> **Good to know:** despite the name, Norwegian Cruise Line is a Miami-based company, and its ships spend most of the year in the Caribbean, Alaska and Europe. It does schedule some northern Europe sailings in summer, but fjord cruises are offered by many lines, so compare ships and ports rather than going by the name.

## What a fjords cruise actually delivers
Ships sail waterways narrower than anything in Alaska's Inside Passage. In the Geirangerfjord and Nærøyfjord, rock walls rise straight from still water, waterfalls pour off the cliffs and farms cling to ledges hundreds of metres up. The water stays sheltered for most of the route, which is good news for anyone who worries about seasickness.

Ports are compact and walkable: Flåm, Olden, Geiranger and Ålesund in the fjords, with Bergen and Stavanger as the city stops. Bergen is one of the wettest cities in Europe, so pack a rain shell whatever the forecast.

## When is the best time to cruise the Norwegian fjords?
| Month | Daylight | What it's like | Prices |
|---|---|---|---|
| May | Long and growing | Snow on the peaks, cooler, quieter | Shoulder, often lower |
| June | Longest, near 19 hours in Bergen | Waterfalls at their fullest | High |
| July | Very long | Warmest, busiest, European school holidays | Peak |
| August | Long but shortening | Warm, still busy | High |
| September | Noticeably shorter | Cooler, wetter, fewer crowds | Shoulder, often lower |

Couples not tied to the Canadian school calendar do well in June or early September. Families locked into July and August pay peak prices and share the ports with the most ships.

## Routes: classic loops and the midnight sun
Classic 7-night loops cover the southern fjords and Bergen. Longer 10 to 14 night sailings push north past the Arctic Circle to Tromsø and the North Cape, where the sun doesn't set from roughly late May to late July. Some combine Norway with Iceland or the British Isles; see [Iceland and Greenland cruises](/articles/iceland-greenland-cruises-canadians/) and [British Isles cruises](/articles/british-isles-cruise-canadians/).

Norway is tightening emissions rules in its UNESCO-listed fjords, which may change which ships can enter the Geirangerfjord and Nærøyfjord in coming seasons. If those two are why you are going, confirm they are on your itinerary.

## Getting there from Canada
### Flights
Toronto, Montreal, Calgary and Vancouver all have nonstop flights to London, with rail or coach transfers to Southampton. For continental departures, connect through Amsterdam or Copenhagen. Norway is six hours ahead of Toronto and nine ahead of Vancouver, so arrive at least a day early; two is better from the west.

### Documents
A Canadian passport valid for your whole trip covers Norway and the EU without a visa. Southampton departures need a UK ETA, applied for online before you fly. [Flying the day before your cruise](/articles/fly-day-before-cruise/) explains why the buffer night matters even more with an ocean to cross.

> **Canadian tip:** when flights, transfers and the cruise are booked as one package, the line will usually help get you to the ship, or to the next port, if a connection in Europe goes wrong. That is worth a lot on a transatlantic trip.

## Shore days and what they cost
Excursions are gentler than Alaska's: the Flåm Railway, fjord kayaking, stave churches and glacier viewpoints near Olden. The Pulpit Rock hike from Stavanger is a real half-day walk, not a stroll. Norway is expensive, so ship tours and independent options are worth comparing port by port.

- Card payments are accepted almost everywhere, so you need little Norwegian kroner cash.
- A beer or lunch ashore costs noticeably more than at home.
- Use a card with no foreign-transaction fee to avoid 2.5% on top.

## Building the trip properly
Pick your month first, then the ship: mid-size ships fit the narrow fjords and smaller ports well. June cabins sell early, so book about a year out, and price flights from your home airport to both UK and continental departures before choosing a sailing.
