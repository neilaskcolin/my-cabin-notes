faq1q: Do ships turn around at Alaska glaciers?
faq1a: Yes. At Glacier Bay, Hubbard Glacier and the fjords such as Tracy Arm and Endicott Arm, ships hold position or turn slowly so each side gets extended time facing the ice. No balcony misses the main event.
faq2q: Is a balcony worth it on an Alaska cruise?
faq2a: Alaska is the strongest case for a balcony of any mainstream itinerary, because the scenery runs for hours at a time and you can step out in a jacket whenever something appears. If you'll splurge once, this is the trip.
faq3q: Which side of the ship gets the sunset in Alaska?
faq3a: Northbound, the sun sets on the port (left) side, since that faces west. Southbound, it's the starboard side. In June, sunset comes late in the evening, so a west-facing balcony gets long golden light.

Pick starboard (right) for a one-way northbound sailing and port (left) for a southbound one, if you want the mainland coast on your side. On a round trip from Vancouver or Seattle it evens out, and at the glaciers the ship turns for everyone.

## Port or starboard: the cheat sheet
| Your sailing | Mainland coast side | Sunset side | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Round trip from Vancouver or Seattle | Both, over the week | Both | Choose deck and position, not side |
| One-way northbound, Vancouver to Seward or Whittier | Starboard | Port | Slight edge to starboard |
| One-way southbound, Seward or Whittier to Vancouver | Port | Starboard | Slight edge to port |
| Sail-away from Canada Place | Stanley Park on port as you leave | Depends on time | Go up on deck either way |

### Why the rule is weaker than people think
In much of the Inside Passage, the ship threads between islands, so there is forest and mountain on both sides for hours. The "land side" rule matters most on open stretches, such as the Gulf of Alaska on one-way sailings. Timing matters too: some of the most scenic channels are passed in the middle of the night, in which case the side doesn't matter at all.

## What happens on glacier day
The marquee stops are where the side debate falls apart.

- **Glacier Bay:** the ship usually spends a long stretch near the tidewater glaciers at the top of the bay, turning slowly so each side gets its turn. Our guide to the [best cabins for Glacier Bay viewing day](/articles/best-cabins-glacier-bay-viewing-day/) goes deeper.
- **Hubbard Glacier:** a vast ice face on Gulf of Alaska routes, where the ship typically holds and rotates.
- **Tracy Arm and Endicott Arm:** narrow fjords where the ship sails in and back out the same way, so each wall passes each side once, often close.

[Glacier Bay vs Hubbard vs Endicott](/articles/glacier-bay-vs-hubbard-vs-endicott/) explains how the experience differs at each.

> **Example:** a couple from Edmonton on a round trip from Vancouver book a port-side balcony. Northbound, they get the island side and the sunsets; southbound, they get the mainland. At Glacier Bay, the ship's turn gives them a long, direct view of the ice from their own chairs.

## What matters more than side
- **Midship** cabins move least in rough water, and that matters more on Gulf of Alaska routes.
- **Higher decks** see over the foreground, while lower decks feel closer to the water and wildlife.
- **Aft-facing balconies** give a wide, uninterrupted view of everything you've just passed, including glaciers as you leave them.
- **Obstructed views:** check the deck plan for lifeboats or structures in front of balcony cabins on lower decks.
- **The open decks** are where many people end up on glacier day anyway, especially forward.

## How to book the right cabin
Decide on round trip or one-way first, since that settles whether side matters at all. [Northbound vs southbound Alaska cruises](/articles/alaska-cruise-northern-vs-southbound-embarkation/) covers the direction choice. Then rank midship, deck height and aft options above side. Desirable Alaska cabins sell early, so book a specific cabin number rather than a guarantee if position matters to you.

## The short answer
On a one-way sailing, starboard northbound and port southbound give you a modest edge. On a round trip, don't pay extra for a side. Put your money into a balcony, a good deck and a midship spot, and the glaciers will come to you.
