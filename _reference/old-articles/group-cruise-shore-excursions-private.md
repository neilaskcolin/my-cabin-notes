A ship's shared excursion puts your group on a bus with dozens of strangers. Book your own private tour and you get the same sights at your own pace, often for the same price per person or less once there are eight or more of you.

## Why private tours get cheaper as the group grows
Private tours are usually priced per vehicle, boat or guide, not per head. Split that flat price across a group and the cost per person drops with every extra person, up to the vehicle's capacity.

> **Quick math:** an illustrative private van and guide quoted at US$900 for four hours works out to US$90 each for a group of 10, roughly C$125. A comparable shared ship tour might be US$120–180 per person. With 12 people, the private van drops to US$75 each.

The bigger gain is often the experience itself. Skip the stop nobody cares about, linger where everyone's happy, and ask the guide questions that suit your group, from toddlers to grandparents.

## Ship's group desk or local operator?
| Factor | Private tour through the ship | Independent local operator |
|---|---|---|
| Price | Higher, with ship pricing | Often lower |
| If the tour runs late | The ship waits, or gets you to the next port | Your responsibility |
| If the port is cancelled | Refunded automatically | Depends on the operator's terms |
| Flexibility | Set menu of options | Fully tailored |
| Payment | Charged to your onboard account in US dollars | Deposit to the operator, often in US dollars |

Use the ship for tight port calls, tender ports and anything that runs close to all-aboard time. Use a local operator when you have a long day in port and the tour stays near the pier. The trade-offs are covered in more depth in [shore excursions through the ship vs independently](/articles/cruise-excursion-through-ship-vs-independent/).

## Good ports for private group tours
### Alaska from Vancouver
- **Juneau:** a private whale-watching boat for 6–12 people is one of the best group upgrades in Alaska. Compare options in [Juneau shore excursions ranked](/articles/juneau-shore-excursions-ranked/).
- **Skagway:** a private van over the White Pass into the Yukon gives you stops on your own schedule. You cross back into Canada, so everyone needs their passport. See [Skagway and the White Pass railway](/articles/skagway-white-pass-railway-worth-it/) for the train alternative.
- **Ketchikan:** small-group fishing charters and totem park tours work well for groups.

### Caribbean
- **Cozumel and Roatán:** a private catamaran or snorkel boat for the whole group.
- **Nassau and other Bahamas ports:** a private beach-day transfer or boat trip.

Costs for Alaska are compared in [Alaska independent vs package shore tour costs](/articles/alaska-independent-vs-package-shore-tour-cost/).

## How to book a private group excursion
1. **Poll the group** on interest and budget before you get quotes. Not everyone will want the same day.
2. **Get two or three quotes** for the same port and duration, and confirm the vehicle or boat holds your exact headcount.
3. **Check credentials**: recent reviews, licensing where it applies, insurance, and whether they've worked with cruise groups before.
4. **Confirm pickup at the pier** and a return time at least an hour before all-aboard.
5. **Read the cancellation terms**, especially for tender ports and weather. Ask what happens if the ship skips the port.
6. **Collect money from the group** before paying the deposit, and settle the balance as agreed.
7. **Share the meeting point and the guide's phone number** with everyone, and budget a tip of about 15–20 percent for the guide.

Ask about accessible vehicles early if anyone uses a wheelchair or scooter, because they're limited in most ports. [Alaska accessible excursions port by port](/articles/alaska-cruise-accessible-excursions-port-by-port/) helps with planning.

## Arranging the group's own excursion
Compare the ship's group rate with one or two well-reviewed local operators for each port, and choose based on how much time the ship gives you. If a tour does run late, [what happens if you miss the ship at a port](/articles/what-happens-miss-ship-port/) explains the fallback.
