Bring your passport. That's the short answer for Canadians, whatever the itinerary and whatever other cards are in your wallet. The longer answer explains why the exceptions you may have read about mostly don't apply to you.

## The rule for Canadians: a passport, every time
Almost every cruise a Canadian takes either starts in Canada and enters US waters, or starts in the US. Either way, you deal with US border officials and the cruise line's own document rules, and both are simplest with a passport.

| Your cruise | What to bring | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Alaska round trip from Vancouver | Passport | You clear US preclearance at Canada Place before boarding |
| Alaska from Seattle | Passport | Cruise lines ask non-US citizens for a passport, even if NEXUS gets you across the border |
| Caribbean from Florida | Passport | Needed to board as a non-US citizen, and to fly home from a foreign port in an emergency |
| New England and Canada from Quebec City or Montreal | Passport | US ports on the route |
| Europe, Asia, South Pacific | Passport, plus any visas | Check each country's entry rules |

Alaska specifics are in [passports for an Alaska cruise from Vancouver](/articles/passport-alaska-cruise-vancouver/). Canadian citizens don't need a visa or ESTA for US cruises, as explained in [visas and ESTA for Canadians at US ports](/articles/canadians-visa-esta-cruise-us-port/).

## What about closed-loop cruises?
A closed-loop cruise starts and ends at the same US port, for example a 7-night round trip from Fort Lauderdale. US citizens can sail these with a birth certificate and government photo ID. That exception is written for US citizens. Major lines, including Norwegian, state that all non-US citizen guests must carry a valid passport. You will also need it if you fly into Florida.

## Why NEXUS and enhanced licences aren't enough
NEXUS cards, and the enhanced driver's licences some provinces have issued, are accepted by US officers when you cross by land and sea. That's useful if you drive to Seattle. But the cruise line decides who boards, and lines ask Canadians for passports. A passport is also the only document that gets you on a flight home if you have to leave the ship in, say, Mexico.

> **Watch out:** the name on your booking must match your passport exactly. A nickname, a missing middle name or a married name that hasn't been updated can cause problems at check-in. Fix it with the line well before sailing.

## Children, permanent residents and other cases
### Children
Every child needs their own passport, including babies. If a child travels with only one parent, or with grandparents, the Government of Canada recommends a signed consent letter from any parent not travelling. Lines may also ask for one.

### Permanent residents
Permanent residents need the passport of their country of citizenship plus their PR card. Depending on nationality, a US visa or ESTA may also be needed. Check before booking.

## How to check you're ready
1. Look at every passport's expiry date the day you book.
2. Renew any that expire within about six months of your return date.
3. Enter passport details exactly in online check-in (see [cruise check-in and documents](/articles/cruise-check-in-online-documents/)).
4. Pack passports in your carry-on, never in checked luggage.
5. Keep a photo of each passport's photo page on your phone.

## If your passport won't be ready in time
Standard processing is roughly 10 business days in person and about 20 by mail, but times vary in busy seasons. With proof of travel, passport offices offer faster urgent and express services for an extra fee. Check current options on [the passport office site](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/canadian-passports.html) and contact your cruise line about its document deadline.

> **Canadian tip:** renew in autumn if you can. Demand rises in late winter and spring as Canadians plan March break and summer trips, which is when processing times tend to stretch.

## Coming home
On a Vancouver round trip, you clear CBSA on return, and your passport speeds that up. Your duty-free allowance depends on how long you've been away; the details are in [shopping on board and duty-free](/articles/cruise-shopping-onboard-duty-free/).
