All three Caribbean routes deliver warm water and easy cruising. The real differences are what you do ashore, how many days you spend at sea, and how far you have to fly to reach the ship.

## Eastern, western and southern Caribbean at a glance

| | Eastern | Western | Southern |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical ports | St. Thomas, St. Maarten, San Juan, Tortola, a private island | Cozumel, Costa Maya, Roatán, Belize, Grand Cayman, Jamaica | Aruba, Curaçao, Bonaire, Barbados, St. Lucia, Antigua |
| Best for | Beaches, duty-free shopping, colonial old towns | Snorkelling, diving, Mayan ruins, adventure tours | Less-visited islands, Dutch and French character |
| Sea days on 7 nights | Often 3 | Often 2 | Few if starting in San Juan or Barbados; many from Florida |
| Usual start | Fort Lauderdale, Miami, Port Canaveral | Miami, Fort Lauderdale, Tampa, Galveston | San Juan, Barbados, or Florida on 10–14 nights |
| First-cruise fit | Very good | Very good | Better as a second trip |

## The eastern Caribbean: beaches and shopping
Eastern islands sit farther from Florida, so 7-night sailings usually spend more time crossing open water and fewer days in port. That suits people who like sea days for the pools, spa and shows.

### What you do ashore
St. Thomas is known for beaches such as Magens Bay and for duty-free shopping in Charlotte Amalie. St. Maarten offers two cultures on one island, Dutch and French. Old San Juan, with its forts and painted streets, is easy to walk from the pier. Many itineraries also include a line's private island in the Bahamas (see [private island stops explained](/articles/private-island-cruise-stops-explained/)).

> **Good to know:** duty-free prices in port don't change your allowance at home. Canadians returning after more than 48 hours can bring back up to C$800 of goods duty-free, with separate limits on alcohol and tobacco. [Is cruise duty-free actually cheaper](/articles/cruise-shopping-onboard-duty-free/) has the details.

## The western Caribbean: reefs and ruins
Western ports are closer together and closer to Florida, so itineraries often fit four port days into a week. The Mesoamerican Barrier Reef runs along Mexico, Belize and Honduras, which is why snorkelling and diving are the headline here.

### What you do ashore
Cozumel is famous for clear-water reef snorkelling. Costa Maya and Belize offer Mayan sites inland, and Roatán has easy beach snorkelling. Grand Cayman is home to Stingray City, and Jamaica brings waterfalls and river tours. Grand Cayman and Belize City are usually tender ports, where small boats ferry you ashore, so allow extra time (see [cruise ship tenders explained](/articles/cruise-ship-tenders-explained/)).

## The southern Caribbean: the far islands
Southern itineraries reach islands most Florida loops can't, including the Dutch ABC islands and the eastern arc from Barbados to Grenada. Some 7-night trips start in San Juan or Barbados, which means more port days but a longer flight from Canada. From Florida they usually stretch to 10–14 nights with several sea days each way.

These routes suit repeat cruisers and sea-day lovers. The wider warm-winter options are in [Cuba and southern Caribbean itineraries](/articles/cuba-southern-caribbean-warm-winter/).

> **Canadian tip:** flights decide a lot. Florida ports are a nonstop hop from Toronto and Montreal, and western routes from Tampa or Galveston can suit some connections better. Barbados is a direct flight from Toronto and from some other cities in winter, which can make a southern round trip simpler than it looks. See [Toronto to Miami for a Caribbean cruise](/articles/toronto-miami-caribbean-cruise/) or [Montreal's easiest US ports](/articles/montreal-caribbean-cruise-us-port/).

## Which is best for a first cruise?
Either classic loop works well. Choose western if you want more port days and active excursions, and eastern if you picture beaches, shopping and a sea day or two to enjoy the ship. Pick southern only if you have more time or have cruised before.

### Timing around Canadian holidays
March break and the Christmas holidays push Caribbean prices up across all three routes. Hurricane season runs from June to November, peaking from August to October, and the line may swap ports to avoid storms (see [what happens if a hurricane threatens your cruise](/articles/what-happens-hurricane-cruise-caribbean/)). For a balanced week, [port days vs sea days](/articles/port-days-vs-sea-days-itinerary-balance/) helps you decide how many sea days you actually want.

Whatever the route, bring passports for everyone and work through the [first cruise checklist for Canadians](/articles/first-cruise-checklist-canadians/).
