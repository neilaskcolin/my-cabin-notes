Big ships give you Alaska's greatest hits, a floating resort and the best prices, and most leave from Canada Place. Small ships give you a wildlife-first expedition with kayaks, naturalists and coves big ships can't enter, at several times the cost. The right choice depends on whether the ship is part of the holiday or simply the way in.

## The three sizes of Alaska ship

| | Big ship | Mid-size premium or luxury | Small expedition ship |
|---|---|---|---|
| Passengers | About 2,000–4,000+ | Several hundred to about 2,000 | Roughly 20–250 |
| Typical fare | About US$150–350 per person per night | In between | About US$500–1,000+ per person per night |
| Leaves from | Mostly Vancouver or Seattle | Vancouver, Seattle or one-way to Seward | Mostly Juneau, Sitka, Ketchikan or Seattle |
| Ports | The big three towns plus a glacier day | Similar, sometimes Sitka or Victoria | Small villages, wilderness landings |
| On board | Pools, shows, many restaurants, kids clubs | Quieter, more enrichment, adult-leaning | Lounge, dining room, naturalists |
| Included | Meals and entertainment | Often more, such as drinks or wifi | Often excursions, kayaks and guides |
| Best for | Families, first-timers, value | Couples who want calm | Wildlife lovers, active travellers |

## The big-ship version

Mainstream ships from Vancouver run the classic Inside Passage loop with every comfort attached: several restaurants, shows, pools and kids clubs, at fares that competition keeps sharp. Glacier days are handled well, with rangers aboard in Glacier Bay and slow turns at the ice. For families or mixed groups, the ship gives everyone something to do.

The trade-offs are crowds in port and a fixed schedule. When several big ships are in Juneau on the same day, the town is busy, and the ship won't change course to follow a pod of whales.

## The small-ship version

Small ships slide into narrow fjords, change plans when whales appear, put kayaks and inflatable skiffs in the water from the stern, and carry naturalists who know the wildlife. The schedule bends around nature rather than shopping. Expect more time outdoors, fewer people at every stop and a casual dress code.

What you give up is entertainment, choice of restaurants and, often, a balcony. Most small ships don't start in Vancouver, so add a flight to Juneau, Sitka or Seattle. The lines are compared in [best small-ship lines for wildlife and nature](/articles/best-small-ship-lines-wildlife-nature/).

### The honest middle

Premium and luxury mid-size ships split the difference: fewer passengers, quieter decks, strong lectures and better food, at fares between the two. For couples who find big ships too busy and expedition prices hard to justify, this tier is often the answer. See [luxury lines compared on extra spend](/articles/luxury-lines-compared-extra-spend/) and [small-ship lines Canadians overlook](/articles/quiet-luxury-small-ship-lines-overlooked/).

## What the price gap really looks like

> **Quick math:** a big-ship balcony at US$1,600 per person for 7 nights, plus about US$600 of excursions and US$300 of gratuities and drinks, comes to roughly US$2,500 per person, or about C$3,450. A small ship at US$700 a night is about US$4,900, or roughly C$6,750, often with excursions included. The small ship still costs nearly double, but the gap is smaller than the headline fares suggest.

> **Canadian tip:** both options are usually priced in US dollars. Use a credit card with no foreign-transaction fee for deposits and onboard spending, and check that your travel insurance covers remote areas, since a small ship may be hours from a hospital.

## Choosing in five questions

1. **Is the ship part of the holiday?** If you want shows, pools and choice, go big or mid-size.
2. **Are wildlife and time outdoors the point?** If yes, a small ship is the stronger choice.
3. **Are children coming?** Big ships have kids clubs; most expedition ships don't, and some set minimum ages. See [Alaska with kids: what ages work](/articles/alaska-cruise-with-kids-ages/).
4. **How much travel do you want?** Big ships from Vancouver are a single flight from most of Canada; small ships usually add a connection.
5. **Do you get seasick?** Big ships are steadier, though small ships stay in sheltered water most of the time.
