This is a checklist you can print or screenshot and tick off. It starts with the items that live in your carry-on, then the core wardrobe, then the add-ons that change with your route.

## What goes in your carry-on on embarkation day?
Checked bags are taken at the terminal and can reach your cabin as late as mid-evening. Everything you can't be without for the first afternoon goes in your hand luggage.

- Passports for everyone, plus a photo of each photo page stored separately
- Boarding pass and luggage tags from [online check-in](/articles/cruise-check-in-online-documents/)
- Travel insurance policy number and the 24-hour assistance line
- All medication for the trip plus a few days' extra, in original containers
- Phone, charger, power bank and any glasses or contact lenses
- Swimsuit, sunscreen and a light layer for the deck at sail-away
- The credit card you'll register for your onboard account

> **Good to know:** a passport is the safest document for Canadians on almost every cruise. NEXUS and other options are explained in [passports, NEXUS and ID for cruising](/articles/passports-nexus-id-cruise-documents/).

## The core wardrobe for any cruise
For a 7-night sailing, most people manage with one checked bag each by following a simple rule: daywear for half the trip, plus a way to do laundry (see [self-service laundry on board](/articles/cruise-ship-laundry-self-service-walkthrough/)).

### Daytime
- Four or five casual tops and two or three pairs of shorts, trousers or skirts
- A cover-up or shirt to throw over swimwear for indoor venues
- Comfortable walking shoes that are broken in, plus sandals
- A light sweater for air-conditioned theatres and dining rooms

### Evenings
- Smart-casual outfits for most nights: slacks or a nice skirt, collared shirt or blouse
- One dressier outfit for formal or "evening chic" nights (see [what people wear on formal nights](/articles/formal-nights-what-to-wear/))
- Shoes that aren't flip-flops, since most dining rooms ask for them at dinner

The full daytime rules by venue are in [what to wear on a cruise during the day](/articles/cruise-ship-dress-code-daytime-wear/).

## Adjusting the list for your route

| Add this | Caribbean or Mexico | Alaska | Europe |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rain layer | Light poncho | Waterproof jacket with hood | Packable rain jacket |
| Warmth | One light sweater | Fleece, toque and light gloves | Sweater for evenings |
| Footwear | Water shoes for beaches | Waterproof walking shoes | Shoes for cobbles and hills |
| Sun | Reef-friendly sunscreen, hat | Sunglasses, sunscreen for glare | Hat, sunscreen |
| Extras | Snorkel mask if you own one | Binoculars | Scarf to cover shoulders in churches |

For Alaska specifics by month, see [what to pack for Alaska in May vs September](/articles/alaska-packing-may-vs-september/). For Europe, check the outlet type: most ships serving North America have North American sockets, often alongside European ones, but bring one adapter for hotels ashore.

## Money, cards and small cash
- A credit card with no foreign transaction fee, since onboard charges post in US dollars (see [paying in CAD vs USD](/articles/paying-cad-vs-usd-cruise/))
- About US$50–100 in small bills for tips ashore, taxis and market stalls
- A debit card as backup, with your bank told about your travel dates

## Health and comfort
- Seasickness tablets or bands, started before you need them
- A small first aid kit: plasters, blister pads, pain relievers, antacids
- Hand sanitizer, lip balm and after-sun

## What to leave at home
Power bars with surge protectors, clothes irons and steamers, and candles are banned on most ships. Read [what you can plug in on a cruise](/articles/extension-cord-cruise-ship-rules/) before packing chargers. Cannabis is also out, even though it's legal at home: [why you can't bring cannabis on a cruise](/articles/cruise-cannabis-canada-why-no/) explains the rules. The broader list is in [what not to pack for a cruise](/articles/what-not-to-pack-for-a-cruise/).

> **Canadian tip:** flying south in January, wear a packable jacket to the airport rather than packing a parka. On the way home, it goes back on in the jet bridge.

## Adjusting this list for your specific trip
Check your line's dress code, your ship's outlet types and your itinerary's weather a week before you sail. Then pack once, lay it out, and take out a third of it. You'll still have enough.
