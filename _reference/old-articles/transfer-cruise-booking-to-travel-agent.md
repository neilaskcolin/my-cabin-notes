Yes. If you booked directly with a cruise line and later want an agent to manage it, most lines allow a transfer as long as you are within their window and before final payment. Nothing about your cruise changes except who looks after it.

## What a transfer actually is
A transfer moves the servicing of your booking from the line to a travel agency. Your ship, sailing, cabin, fare, deposit and promotions all stay the same. The line pays the agency its normal commission, which is why most lines permit transfers and why agents are happy to take them on.

| What stays the same | What changes |
|---|---|
| Ship, date and cabin number | Who you contact about the booking |
| Fare and any promotion applied | Who handles price drops and changes for you |
| Deposit paid and final payment date | Possible extras, such as onboard credit, added by the agency |
| Cancellation terms | Documents may come through the agency |
| Loyalty points and status | Nothing else |

## How long you have
Most major lines allow transfers within 30 days of the original booking date, and some extend that to 60. All require it to happen before final payment, and a booking already paid in full usually cannot move. Final payment typically falls about three months before sailing, but your deposit date starts the transfer clock, so the window can close long before then. [Cruise deposits and final payment](/articles/cruise-deposits-final-payment/) explains those dates.

## How to transfer, step by step
1. Find your booking number, ship, sailing date and the date you booked.
2. Contact a travel agent and ask whether your line's transfer window is still open.
3. Ask which extras, if any, they can add to your booking before you agree.
4. Sign the line's transfer authorization, which the agent usually sends you.
5. Wait for the agent to confirm the line has accepted it, then check the booking shows the agency.

The whole process usually takes a few days. Keep paying your balance on the original schedule while it goes through.

> **Canadian tip:** check that the agent is properly registered for your province. Ontario agents must be registered with TICO, Quebec agents licensed by the Office de la protection du consommateur, and BC agents licensed by Consumer Protection BC. Those regimes come with consumer protections if an agency fails.

## Why people transfer
The main reason is the extras. A consultant can often attach onboard credit, prepaid gratuities or other perks the line was not giving you, as described in [what Expedia Extras are](/articles/what-are-expedia-extras/). The other is having a person who watches for [price drops after booking](/articles/price-drop-after-booking-cruise/) and handles problems such as schedule changes or cancellations. Whether that adds up to real savings is covered in [is it cheaper to book a cruise through a travel agent](/articles/cheaper-book-cruise-travel-agent/).

## What usually can't be transferred
- Bookings past the transfer window or past final payment
- Bookings made through another agency, which need that agency's release
- Some casino-offer and group bookings, which have their own rules, as explained in [casino offers and free cruises](/articles/casino-offers-free-cruises-explained/)

If you are outside the window, nothing is lost: the agent can still help with your next cruise.

> **Watch out:** don't cancel and rebook to move a booking to an agent. A new booking can come at a higher fare, lose your promotion or trigger cancellation penalties. A transfer avoids all of that.

## Doing it in five minutes
Send the agent your booking number, ship and sailing date. They will confirm the line's window, send the authorization form, and tell you which extras apply before you sign. If the window has closed, there is no cost to asking.
