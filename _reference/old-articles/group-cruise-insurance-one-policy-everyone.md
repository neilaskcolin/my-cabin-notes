On a group cruise it is tempting to look for one tidy policy for everybody. In practice, insurance is personal: what suits a healthy couple in their thirties can leave a 72-year-old with a heart condition uncovered. The smooth approach is one rule for the group and one policy per household.

## Can one insurance policy cover a whole group?
Sometimes, within limits. A couples or family plan usually covers you, your spouse and dependent children, and some include grandchildren travelling with you. Adult children typically need their own policy once they are past the plan's age limit.

Some Canadian insurers also sell plans for organised groups. Even then, eligibility for medical cover, pre-existing condition terms and premiums are usually worked out person by person. A single policy does not remove those differences, it just bundles them.

## Why one policy per household usually works better

| What differs between travellers | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Age | Medical premiums and eligibility change with age, and some credit card medical benefits shrink or stop above a set age |
| Pre-existing conditions | Stability periods and medical questionnaires apply individually |
| Existing coverage | Some people already have annual plans, workplace benefits or card coverage |
| Amount at risk | A suite and an inside cabin need very different cancellation limits |
| Departure city | Flights from Calgary and Toronto bring different delay and connection risks |

Seniors in particular should read [cruise insurance for seniors and pre-existing conditions](/articles/cruise-insurance-seniors-canada-pre-existing/) before buying.

> **Example:** a group of twelve friends from Winnipeg books six cabins. Two retirees need cover that handles a heart condition, one couple already has generous card coverage, and a younger pair wants cancel-for-any-reason protection. No single blanket policy would suit all four households, but each can buy exactly what it needs in an afternoon.

## What a group organiser should do
1. **Raise insurance at the deposit stage.** Cancellation cover bought at deposit protects against problems that crop up in the months before sailing.
2. **Share a short checklist.** Emergency medical with a high limit and evacuation cover, trip cancellation and interruption up to each person's non-refundable cost, and no exclusion for cruising.
3. **Set a deadline before final payment.** The group's payment timeline is covered in [group cruise deadlines, deposits and final payment](/articles/group-cruise-deadlines-deposits/).
4. **Collect only what you need.** Ask each cabin for its insurer's name and 24-hour assistance number, not medical details.
5. **Carry an emergency list.** Keep those numbers on paper and on your phone for the trip.
6. **Explain what dropping out costs.** A cabinmate left alone may face a single supplement, as explained in [what happens if someone drops out](/articles/what-if-someone-drops-out-group-cruise/).

## The shared-cabin question
Two friends sharing a cabin often hold separate policies. Many policies treat a travelling companion's covered emergency as a reason you can cancel too, and some pay toward the extra cost if you sail alone. Check how each policy defines a travelling companion and make sure both people are covered for the same trip dates.

> **Canadian tip:** provincial health plans pay little or nothing for treatment on a ship or in a foreign port, and evacuation from a ship is where costs climb fastest. Every person in the group needs travel medical cover that names cruising, whatever they decide about cancellation cover. [Cruise ship medical care costs](/articles/cruise-medical-care-costs/) explains why.

## Line plans versus independent insurance
A cruise line's protection plan can be added to each booking in a click, which makes it popular with groups. It is mainly a cancellation product, though, and [travel insurance vs cruise line protection](/articles/travel-insurance-vs-cruise-protection/) sets out the differences. The full checklist is in [cruise insurance in Canada](/articles/travel-insurance-cruise-canadians/).

## One rule, many policies
Make insurance a group expectation with a clear date, then let each household choose what fits. For bigger events, add it to your planning timeline alongside cabins and dining, as in [planning a family reunion cruise](/articles/family-reunion-cruise-planning/).
