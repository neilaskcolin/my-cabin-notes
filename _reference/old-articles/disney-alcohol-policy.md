Each guest aged 21 or over can bring one unopened bottle of wine or champagne (up to 750 ml) or six beers of 12 oz or less when boarding a Disney ship. That allowance applies once, at embarkation, and spirits are not allowed at all.

## Disney's alcohol allowance after the June 2026 change
Disney tightened its rules in early June 2026, ship by ship. Blog posts and forum threads from before then describe the old, more generous allowance.

| | Before June 2026 | From June 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Wine or champagne | 2 bottles per adult at embarkation | 1 bottle (750 ml max) per adult |
| Beer | Allowed in place of wine | 6 beers (12 oz max) in place of wine |
| Re-stocking at ports | Allowed at each port | Not allowed; port purchases are held |
| Corkage in dining venues | About US$29 | About US$20 |
| Spirits and liqueurs | Not allowed | Not allowed |

The age limit is 21 on every ship except the Singapore-based Disney Adventure, where it is 18. Confirm the current wording on Disney's prohibited items page for your sail date.

> **Watch out:** Canada's drinking age does not apply on board. A 19-year-old from Vancouver or an 18-year-old from Calgary cannot bring wine or beer on a Disney ship, even on an Alaska sailing from Canada Place.

## What happens to bottles bought in port or on board
Alcohol bought at Castaway Cay, Lookout Cay, Nassau or an Alaska port is taken at the gangway, stored, and handed back near the end of the cruise, as are bottles from the ship's shops. Treat any souvenir bottle as something to take home.

## Corkage: where your bottle costs extra
Opening your own wine in your stateroom or on your verandah is free. Taking it to a rotational dining room costs about US$20 per bottle at recent policy. Because your serving team follows you from restaurant to restaurant all week, ask on night one whether they can hold an unfinished bottle for the next dinner.

## Getting your allowance on board without a hitch
Most Canadians fly to Florida or Vancouver, so the bottle has to survive a flight before it reaches the terminal.

1. Pack the wine or beer in your checked bag for the flight, since airport security limits liquids in hand luggage.
2. At the hotel the night before, move it into the bag you will carry on to the ship. Disney requires alcohol to be in carry-on, not in luggage handed to porters.
3. Keep it unopened and in its original packaging until you are on board.
4. Expect it to be checked at terminal security.

Flying in the day before is the safer plan anyway, as [whether to fly the day before your cruise](/articles/fly-day-before-cruise/) explains.

## Does it still make sense to bring your own?
For a couple, one bottle each covers two dinners: a small saving rather than a strategy. The [Disney Cruise Line drink package price](/articles/disney-drink-package-price/) page covers the multi-bottle wine packages and beer bundles Disney sells instead.

> **Good to know:** On an Alaska round trip from Vancouver, bottles you collect in Ketchikan or Juneau come back with you through CBSA. After more than 48 hours away, the personal exemption covers 1.5 litres of wine or 24 standard cans of beer, among other options. Larger amounts attract duty.

## How Disney compares
| Line | Wine you can bring | Corkage (recent) |
|---|---|---|
| **Disney** | 1 bottle or 6 beers per adult, embarkation only | ~$20 in dining venues |
| [Royal Caribbean](/articles/royal-caribbean-alcohol-policy/) | 2 bottles per stateroom | ~$15 in bars and restaurants |
| [Carnival](/articles/carnival-alcohol-policy/) | 1 bottle per adult (21+) | ~$15 in bars and restaurants |

Disney used to have the most generous allowance among the big lines. Now a Disney couple brings about what a Royal Caribbean couple does, though letting beer replace the wine is still unusual.
