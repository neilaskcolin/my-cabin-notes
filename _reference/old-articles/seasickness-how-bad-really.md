Seasickness is the worry that keeps more Canadians off cruise ships than almost anything else. The honest answer is reassuring: on today's big ships, most people either feel nothing or adjust within a day, and the few who struggle can tilt the odds heavily in their favour before booking.

## Why big ships move so little
Modern cruise ships are huge, often around 300 metres long and well over 100,000 tonnes. That mass alone damps small waves. Stabilizer fins below the waterline counter side-to-side roll, which is the motion that bothers most people.

What stabilizers can't remove is pitch, the slow front-to-back rise and fall when the ship heads into a swell. That's why a cabin at the very front feels livelier than one midship. On calm days, motion on a big ship feels more like a slow train than a small boat.

## How rough is each route?
Season and weather matter most, but some routes are reliably calmer than others.

| Route | Usual motion | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Alaska Inside Passage round trip | Very low | A few hours of open water, often overnight |
| Caribbean, winter and spring | Low | Trade winds can raise a moderate swell some days |
| Mexican Riviera | Low to moderate | Open Pacific along the coast |
| Mediterranean, summer | Low to moderate | Occasional strong winds |
| Gulf of Alaska, one-way Alaska | Moderate | Roughly a day of open ocean |
| Hawaii from Vancouver | Moderate | Several days of open Pacific each way |
| Transatlantic, spring and fall | Moderate to high | The longest open-ocean exposure |
| Drake Passage to Antarctica | High | Famously rough on smaller expedition ships |

For Alaska route detail, see [seasickness on the Inside Passage](/articles/seasickness-inside-passage/) and [the Gulf of Alaska crossing](/articles/alaska-gulf-crossing-seasickness/).

## Who feels it most?
Sensitivity is personal and hard to predict, but some patterns are common:

- People who get carsick or queasy reading in a car.
- Anyone prone to migraines.
- Children in the primary school years, more than toddlers or adults (see [seasickness in kids](/articles/seasickness-in-kids-what-to-do/)).
- People who are tired, hungover or dehydrated.

If none of those describe you, odds are good you'll be fine on a calm itinerary.

## Which remedies work?

| Remedy | How it's used | Things to know |
|---|---|---|
| Dimenhydrinate (Gravol) | Tablet before motion, repeat as directed | Sold at every Canadian pharmacy; can cause drowsiness |
| Meclizine | Once-daily tablet | Less drowsy for many; can be hard to find in Canada, so ask your pharmacist |
| Scopolamine patch | Behind the ear, lasts several days | Prescription only; side effects include dry mouth and blurred vision |
| Ginger | Capsules, chews or ginger ale | Mild, useful for breakthrough queasiness |
| Acupressure bands | Wristbands worn throughout | Cheap and drug-free; results vary |

> **Good to know:** remedies work best taken before rough water. Check the daily program or ask guest services when the ship expects open-sea conditions, and take a dose at bedtime the night before.

Ask your doctor or pharmacist before combining any of these with other medication, and avoid alcohol with drowsy tablets. Guest services sometimes hands out basic tablets, but bring your own.

### If you feel ill on board
Go to a low, midship deck, get fresh air and look at the horizon. Put away screens and books. Eat a little plain food, like crackers or a green apple, rather than nothing. If you're vomiting repeatedly or can't keep fluids down, the medical centre can give stronger treatment, billed privately, which is one more reason to carry insurance (see [cruise medical care costs](/articles/cruise-medical-care-costs/)).

## Choices at booking that help
Pick a calm route and season for your first cruise. Book a specific midship cabin on a low or middle deck rather than a guarantee fare, and favour a window or balcony. [How to pick your first cabin](/articles/pick-first-cruise-cabin/) explains how to find one on the deck plan.

## The reassuring part
Most people who feel queasy on day one are fine by day two, as their balance system adapts. Pack the remedies anyway. With a calm route and a midship cabin, there's a good chance you'll never open them.
