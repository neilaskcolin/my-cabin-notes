Princess final payment usually falls about 90 days before sailing on cruises of six nights or more, and about 75 days before shorter ones. Until then a standard-fare deposit is fully refundable; after it, the amount you lose climbs in steps until it reaches the whole fare in the final weeks.

## When is Princess final payment due?
| Booking type | Final payment, recent policy |
|---|---|
| Cruises under 6 nights | About 75 days before sailing |
| Cruises of 6 nights or more, including 7-night Alaska and Caribbean | About 90 days before sailing |
| Cruisetours (cruise plus Denali or Yukon land stay) | Follows the longer-sailing schedule |
| World cruises and some holiday sailings | Earlier; check your confirmation |

These are the usual patterns, not a contract. The date on your Princess confirmation governs, and it is worth putting in your calendar the day you book. The general mechanics are in [cruise deposits and final payment dates explained](/articles/cruise-deposits-final-payment/).

For Canadians the timing has a practical edge. A mid-July Alaska sailing from Vancouver reaches final payment in mid-April, right on top of tax season. A March break Caribbean cruise hits it in mid-December, alongside holiday spending.

## Refundable or non-refundable deposit?
On a standard Princess fare, the deposit comes back in full if you cancel before final payment. During some promotions Princess offers a reduced deposit that is non-refundable, which suits travellers who are certain of their dates and want to put less down. The booking page states which one you have, and [cruise fare classes explained](/articles/cruise-fare-classes-refundable/) covers how to choose.

Adding Princess Plus or Premier does not change this. The fare type underneath decides what is refundable.

## What happens after final payment?
Penalties run in bands that depend on cruise length. At recent policy, the first window after the deadline costs a portion of the fare, and the share rises in steps to 100% in the last couple of weeks. On a cruisetour, the land portion, including Princess's Denali and Fairbanks lodge nights, follows the same schedule as the cruise. Government taxes and fees are normally returned even when the fare is forfeited, but check your contract.

## Why the refundable choice matters more for Alaska
Princess sells Alaska a long way ahead, and cruisetours with Denali lodge nights fill early because the lodges have a fixed number of rooms. Booking a July balcony 12 to 18 months out on a refundable deposit costs almost nothing in flexibility: you hold the cabin, can reprice into wave-season sales as covered in [Princess Cruises price drop after booking](/articles/princess-price-drop-after-booking/), and can still walk away free until about 90 days out.

> **Example:** a family from Edmonton books a 7-night Vancouver round trip for July 2027 in autumn 2026. Until roughly mid-April 2027 they can cancel for a full refund or move to a cheaper fare. After that date, cancelling costs money, and insurance is what protects them.

## How to cancel a Princess booking
1. Check your fare type and the final payment date on your confirmation.
2. If you are cancelling for illness or another covered reason, contact your insurer first. Most Canadian policies want prompt notice and a doctor's note.
3. Cancel in writing through whoever holds the booking, Princess directly or your agency, and keep the cancellation number.
4. Confirm the refund amount and that it goes back to the original card, in the currency you paid.

## Cancelling one guest, and insurance
A partial cancellation after final payment can leave the remaining guest paying a single supplement, so ask for revised pricing before you confirm. See [single supplements and how to dodge them](/articles/single-supplement-how-to-dodge/).

Trip-cancellation insurance is the real safety net after final payment. The options are compared in [cruise insurance in Canada](/articles/travel-insurance-cruise-canadians/), and [cancel-for-any-reason cover](/articles/cancel-for-any-reason-cruise-insurance-explained/) is worth a look if your plans are less certain.
