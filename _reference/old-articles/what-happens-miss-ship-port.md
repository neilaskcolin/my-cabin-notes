The ship won't wait for individual passengers. Its schedule is tied to pilots, tides, berth bookings and the next port's arrival time, so once all-aboard passes and the gangway is pulled, you're on your own unless you were on a ship-sold tour.

## How all-aboard works
All-aboard is usually 30–60 minutes before departure and is printed in the daily program and the app. At tender ports, the last tender back can leave even earlier. When you return, your cruise card is scanned, so the crew know exactly who is missing. They'll call names over the speakers and may try your phone, but they won't hold the ship for long.

If you're not back, the crew contact the port agent, the local company that handles the ship's business in that port. If your passport is on board, it may be left with the agent, along with essential medication.

## The excursion rule that protects you
This is the most practical rule in cruising. If a tour you booked through the cruise line runs late, the ship usually waits, or the line arranges and pays for your travel to the next port. With an independent tour, the ship owes you nothing.

Independent tours are often excellent and cheaper. Some operators even offer a written promise to get you to the next port if they make you late, so read exactly what it covers. As a rule of thumb, book long or tightly timed tours through the ship and keep independent plans for relaxed port days (see [ship excursions vs booking independently](/articles/cruise-excursion-through-ship-vs-independent/)).

## What to do if you're left behind
1. **Call the port agent.** Their name and number are usually in the daily program for each port. They'll tell you the next realistic port to rejoin.
2. **Call your insurer's assistance line**, and ask what's covered before paying for anything.
3. **Book transport to the next port.** Flights, ferries or a taxi, depending on where you are.
4. **Clear local immigration if needed.** Cruise passengers are processed differently from arriving travellers, so the agent may need to help you get properly recorded.
5. **Contact the Canadian embassy or consulate** if you don't have your passport. They can issue emergency travel documents.
6. **Rejoin the ship**, and keep every receipt.

### Alaska and the Caribbean are very different places to be stranded
In Alaska, Juneau and Ketchikan aren't connected to the road network, so catching up means a commuter flight or the state ferry. In the Caribbean, missing the ship in a small island port can mean flying via Miami to reach the next stop, and the next stop may be in another country. Missing the final port before home usually just means flying home.

> **Example:** a Calgary couple who lose track of time in Juneau and miss the ship would typically need a flight or ferry to Skagway and a hotel night. That can easily run to several hundred US dollars each, with no insurance payout if the only cause was lateness.

## Will insurance pay?
Only if the reason is covered. Policies often cover catching up when the cause is a medical emergency, an accident or a delayed public carrier. Losing track of time, a long lunch or a slow independent tour usually isn't covered. The details are in [cruise insurance for Canadians](/articles/travel-insurance-cruise-canadians/).

## How to make sure it never happens
- **Use ship's time.** Your phone may switch to a local time zone that differs from the ship's clock. Alaska is an hour behind Vancouver, and some Caribbean islands differ from Florida (see [ship's time and time zones](/articles/cruise-cabin-jet-lag-time-zones/)).
- **Build a buffer.** Aim to be back an hour before all-aboard if you've organized your own day.
- **Photograph the port agent's details** in the daily program each morning.
- **Carry your passport or a copy and ID**, plus a credit card that isn't locked in the safe.
- **Check tender times.** Queues for the last tender build quickly ([cruise ship tenders explained](/articles/cruise-ship-tenders-explained/)).

## Missing the ship at the start
Missing embarkation because of a delayed flight follows similar rules, with one twist: if you booked the cruise line's air package, the line usually has to get you to the ship. [What happens if your flight is delayed](/articles/cruise-flight-delay-what-happens/) explains the options.
