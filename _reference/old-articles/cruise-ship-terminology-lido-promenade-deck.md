Daily programmes and deck plans assume you know these names. Here's what the common venue and deck terms mean, plus the places where lines use the same word for different things. For port, starboard, aft and the other basics, start with [cruise terminology decoded](/articles/cruise-terminology-decoded/).

## Open decks

### Lido deck
The main pool deck: pools, hot tubs, sun loungers, outdoor bars, and usually the casual buffet and grill. The name comes from the Lido beach near Venice. On sea days it's the busiest place on the ship.

### Promenade deck
Traditionally a lower outdoor deck where you can walk along the side of the ship, sometimes all the way round. Holland America ships are known for wraparound teak promenades with deck chairs. On ships without one, the jogging track on a top deck does the same job.

### Sun deck and sports deck
The highest open decks, usually with loungers, a jogging track, sports courts or mini golf. Windier than the lido, quieter too.

### Solarium
A glass-roofed pool area, often adults-only. It's warm on chilly days, which makes it popular on Alaska and northern Europe sailings. The free and paid adults-only options are compared in [adults-only areas on mainstream ships](/articles/adults-only-areas-cruise-ships/).

## Indoor venues
- **Atrium:** the central multi-storey lobby with guest services, glass lifts and often live music. Princess calls its version the Piazza.
- **Royal Promenade:** Royal Caribbean's indoor shopping and bar street, not an outdoor deck.
- **Main dining room:** the included sit-down restaurant, sometimes split across two decks.
- **Specialty restaurants:** paid dining venues such as steakhouses (see [main dining, buffet and specialty restaurants](/articles/main-dining-buffet-specialty-restaurants/)).
- **Observation lounge:** a forward-facing lounge high up, great for scenic cruising.

## Practical terms you'll hear
- **Muster station:** your assigned emergency assembly point, explained in [what happens at the muster drill](/articles/cruise-ship-safety-drill-what-happens/).
- **Gangway:** the walkway between the ship and the pier.
- **Tender:** a small boat that ferries you ashore when the ship anchors; see [cruise tenders explained](/articles/cruise-ship-tenders-explained/).
- **Midship:** the middle section of the ship, where motion is gentlest.
- **All aboard:** the time you must be back on the ship in port, usually 30–60 minutes before sailing.
- **Guest services:** the front desk, usually in the atrium.

> **Good to know:** many ships skip deck 13 for superstition, so the deck above 12 may be 14. Locations are written as deck plus position, like "Deck 5, forward", and lift lobbies show which venues are on each level.

## Getting comfortable with ship vocabulary quickly
Learn lido, atrium and muster station before you board and the daily programme makes sense straight away. The rest you'll pick up by the second sea day.
