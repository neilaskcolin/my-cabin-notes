You can cruise while pregnant on every major line, provided you will be under 24 weeks for the whole trip. The cut-off is counted to the last day of the cruise, which is the detail that trips people up.

## The 24-week rule, line by line
The mainstream lines Canadians book most often all use the same limit. Policies can change, so confirm the wording on your line's site before you pay a deposit.

> **Quick math:** take your weeks at boarding and add the length of the cruise. At 21 weeks on a 7-night sailing you finish at 22, which is fine. At 23 weeks you would finish at 24, so you cannot sail. On a 14-night cruise, the latest you can board is around 21 weeks.

## Why lines are so strict
A ship's medical centre can handle a lot, but it is not set up for premature labour or serious pregnancy complications. At sea, reaching a hospital can take a day or more, and a medical evacuation is risky and expensive. The limit is about the worst case, not about how you feel. The [cruise medical care costs](/articles/cruise-medical-care-costs/) page explains how onboard treatment is billed.

## The doctor's letter
Most lines ask for a note from your doctor or midwife, dated shortly before you sail. It should include:
- Your estimated due date
- Confirmation that you and the baby are in good health and fit to travel
- A statement that the pregnancy is not considered high risk

Carry it in your hand luggage with your passport. If you are turned away at the terminal for being over the limit, the line will usually treat it as your cancellation, with no refund.

## Insurance: the gap Canadians miss
Your provincial health plan pays very little outside Canada and nothing useful at sea. Travel medical insurance is essential, but pregnancy is one of its trickiest areas. Many Canadian policies stop covering pregnancy-related claims several weeks before the due date, exclude normal delivery, and do not cover a newborn born abroad.

> **Canadian tip:** ask your insurer three questions in writing: up to which week are complications covered, is a high-risk pregnancy excluded, and is trip cancellation covered if your doctor advises against travel. More on choosing a policy is in [travel insurance for Canadian cruisers](/articles/travel-insurance-cruise-canadians/).

## Picking a comfortable babymoon cruise
Weeks 14 to 22 tend to be the sweet spot: past early nausea, under the cut-off, and comfortable enough to walk a port.

- **Alaska from Vancouver** means a short flight or none at all for western Canadians, cool weather and calm Inside Passage water.
- **Caribbean and Mexico** sailings are warm and easy, but the Public Health Agency of Canada has advised pregnant travellers to avoid areas with Zika risk. Check current advice for each port.
- **Shorter sailings** keep you closer to home and make the week count simpler.
- **A midship cabin on a lower deck** feels the least motion. Ask your doctor before taking any seasickness medication.
