Leave the extension cord and power bar at home. They are among the most commonly confiscated items at cruise terminals, and the ban is close to universal; Carnival, which allows plain cords and power strips without surge protection, is the main exception. If you need power at the bedside for a medical device, the other lines will lend you an approved cord.

## What happens if you pack one
Security scans checked and carry-on bags. Anything banned is pulled and held, then returned on the last night or at disembarkation. You are not in trouble, but you go without it all week, and a flagged checked bag can reach your cabin hours late.

## Why the rule is so strict
Surge protectors can fail dangerously on a ship's electrical system, and cords add fire load in a small steel cabin. Lines treat this as a fleet-wide fire rule, like irons and candles, rather than a judgement call by the security officer.

## Allowed vs not allowed

| Usually allowed | Usually confiscated |
|---|---|
| USB charging cube, no surge protection | Extension cords of any length |
| Phone and laptop chargers | Power bars and surge protectors |
| Hair straighteners and curling irons | Clothes irons and steamers |
| Simple plug adapter, no surge protection | Multi-outlet cubes with surge protection |
| Battery packs in carry-on | Candles, hot plates, coffee makers |

Lines differ at the edges. Carnival allows plain extension cords and power strips as long as they have no surge protector, and rules on non-surge outlet splitters vary, so check your line's prohibited-items list. Drones have their own rules in [cruise ship drone rules](/articles/cruise-ship-drone-photography-rules/).

> **Good to know:** a four- or five-port USB cube with a USB-C port charges two phones, a watch and a tablet from one socket. It is the gadget that solves the outlet shortage for most couples.

## The medical exception
CPAP machines, oxygen concentrators and similar devices need power beside the bed, and cabin outlets are often over at the desk. Every major line that bans cords will supply an approved medical extension cord, and on Carnival you can bring a plain one of your own.

1. Tell the line's accessibility team about the device when you book.
2. Ask them to note that you need a bedside extension cord.
3. Confirm a week or two before sailing.
4. On boarding day, check the cord is in your cabin, and call guest services if not.

The full process, including distilled water, is in [bringing a CPAP machine on a cruise](/articles/bringing-cpap-machine-cruise-ship/).

## How many outlets will your cabin have?
Older ships may have just one or two outlets, often by the desk. Newer ships usually have several, plus USB ports at the bedside. If it matters, look at the ship's age and cabin details, as covered in [reading cruise ship deck plans](/articles/reading-cruise-ship-deck-plans/).

> **Canadian tip:** Canadian plugs are the same as US ones, so on most ships sailing from Vancouver, Seattle or Florida your chargers plug straight in. You only need an adapter for European sockets.
