You can see the best of Ketchikan on foot, for free, in under three hours. Creek Street, the salmon ladder, totem poles and a busy harbour full of floatplanes are all within a short walk of the downtown cruise docks.

## Before you set off

Ketchikan is usually the first or last Alaska port on a Vancouver sailing, and calls are often short. Check your all-aboard time, pick up a free town map at the visitor centre on the dock, and pack a rain jacket. Ketchikan gets several metres of rain a year, and it will probably drizzle at some point.

> **Good to know:** if your ship docks at Ward Cove instead of downtown, allow time for the shuttle bus each way. The loop below still works; just start it from where the shuttle drops you.

## The walking route, stop by stop

Allow 2.5–3 hours at an easy pace, with time for photos.

1. **The waterfront (start).** Step off the ship and look up: floatplanes take off from the channel, fishing boats unload, and bald eagles perch on light poles. The "Salmon Capital of the World" arch sits a block from the docks.
2. **Downtown totem poles (10 minutes).** Whale Park and the streets around it have carved poles with signs explaining their stories. They are free and easy to find.
3. **Creek Street (another 5 minutes).** The famous boardwalk on stilts over Ketchikan Creek was once the town's red-light district and is now galleries, gift shops and the best photos in town. Dolly's House, a small museum in a former brothel, charges a small fee if you want to step inside.
4. **Married Man's Trail (10 minutes).** A short forest boardwalk climbs from behind Creek Street, named for the men who used it to visit Creek Street unseen. It's five minutes of rainforest most visitors miss.
5. **The salmon ladder (10 minutes).** Follow the creek upstream to the fish ladder. From mid-July, salmon leap up the falls a few metres from the rail, with seals waiting below and eagles above.
6. **Totem Heritage Center (optional, 10 more minutes).** Original 19th-century poles brought from abandoned villages, displayed indoors. There's a small admission fee, and it's the best collection you can reach on foot.
7. **The loop back.** Walk back along the creek and through downtown to the docks, stopping for chowder or fish and chips.

## If it rains

It probably will. Ketchikan carries on regardless, and so should you. The boardwalks shine, the salmon don't care, and galleries and cafés along the route make good shelter breaks. This is what the rain jacket was packed for; [what to pack for May vs September](/articles/alaska-packing-may-vs-september/) covers the rest.

> **Canadian tip:** Ketchikan is often your first stop on US soil, so it's the first chance to use your carrier's US roaming plan. Switch roaming on only once you're ashore and on a land network, and back off before the ship sails, as explained in [cruise wifi and roaming for Canadians](/articles/cruise-wifi-phone-roaming-canadians/).

## Worth paying for, if you want one extra

- **Misty Fjords flightseeing.** A floatplane over granite cliffs and waterfalls, often with a water landing. Spectacular in clear weather, often cancelled in low cloud, and typically US$250 and up per person.
- **Totem Bight or Saxman.** The two big outdoor totem collections are a short drive out of town, north and south respectively. A shuttle or taxi gets you there if you don't want a tour.
- **The lumberjack show.** A paid, family-friendly show a short walk from the docks, popular with kids.

For more on the totem poles and the stories behind them, read [Tlingit culture and totem poles in Alaska ports](/articles/tlingit-culture-totem-poles-alaska-ports/).

## What to skip

Skip the town bus tours that drive the route you just walked. Skip the jewellery shops nearest the docks unless you're shopping on purpose. And don't try to cram a long excursion into a short call; missing the ship is far more expensive than any tour.

## Where Ketchikan fits in your budget

Because Ketchikan works so well on foot, it is the port to save money and put the excursion budget towards Juneau, where whales and the glacier are the big draws.
