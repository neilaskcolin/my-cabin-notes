For most people on a first cruise, a drink package costs more than paying for each drink. It only pays off if you would drink six or seven paid drinks a day without trying, and it only makes sense on itineraries with plenty of sea days.

## The break-even, worked out
A typical alcoholic package runs about US$70–110 per adult per day, plus an automatic service charge of around 18–20%. It is charged for every day of the sailing, including days you spend ashore.

> **Quick math:** a US$85 package plus 18% is about US$100 a day. If your average drink costs US$13 plus 18%, or about US$15.30, you need roughly 6.5 drinks a day to break even. Over 7 nights for two adults, the package costs about US$1,400, or roughly C$1,900–1,960.

Premium packages count specialty coffees, fresh juices, bottled water and mocktails, which helps. But dinner wine and two cocktails is only three drinks, nowhere near the target.

## Sea days vs port days
The itinerary matters as much as your habits. Here is one adult paying as they go on a 7-night Western Caribbean sailing from Fort Lauderdale with three sea days, at typical onboard prices:

| Day type | Typical order | Cost with 18% |
|---|---|---|
| Sea day | 2 lattes, 1 soda, 3 cocktails, 2 glasses of wine | about US$94 |
| Port day | 1 latte, 1 cocktail back on board, 1 glass of wine | about US$37 |
| Week (3 sea, 4 port) | | about US$430 |
| Package for the same week | | about US$700 |

> **Example:** that same person would need to drink like a sea day every single day just to come close. Alaska sailings tilt even further toward pay-as-you-go, because long excursion days in Juneau or Skagway leave little time at the bar.

## The rules that tip the maths
- **The all-adults rule:** most lines make every adult in the cabin buy the same package, so a light-drinking partner doubles your target.
- **One drink at a time:** package drinks are poured individually, for the card holder, on board only.
- **Daily caps:** some packages limit you to around 15 alcoholic drinks a day.
- **Price ceilings:** most packages cover drinks up to a set menu price, and you pay the difference on anything above it.

## Who comes out ahead
### Packages tend to win for
- Couples who both enjoy cocktails and would order five or more each anyway
- Sailings with four or more sea days, such as a Panama Canal or a transatlantic crossing
- Anyone who values a fixed cost over watching the tab, as long as you know that is what you are paying for

### Pay-as-you-go tends to win for
- Wine-with-dinner couples and port-heavy itineraries
- Anyone averaging under four drinks a day
- Coffee lovers who only need a non-alcoholic or coffee package, at a fraction of the price

Basic drinks such as drip coffee, tea, water and some juices are free on every line. The [guide to what drinks are included](/articles/cruise-drink-menu-what-included-basics/) shows what you get without paying.

Four lines bundle drinks into a fare or package rather than only selling them alone: Celebrity (All Included), Princess (Princess Plus), Holland America (Have It All) and Norwegian (Free at Sea, where you still pay the service charge). On North American sailings MSC now sells only one alcohol package, Premium Extra. Disney has no unlimited package, and since June 2026 its bring-your-own allowance is one bottle of wine or six beers per adult, as set out in [cruise line alcohol policies compared](/articles/cruise-line-alcohol-policies-compared/). For every line's rules side by side, see [cruise line policies compared](/articles/cruise-line-policies-compared/).

> **Canadian tip:** packages and bar tabs are charged in US dollars. Paying with a no-foreign-transaction-fee card saves about 2.5%, which is around C$45 on a US$1,400 package.

## The version that beats both
Fares that include drinks come up regularly, and when the booking carries the package, the debate disappears. Compare the total of each fare, not just the headline, and see [what "all-inclusive" really means on a cruise](/articles/what-does-all-inclusive-mean-cruise/) before you assume a drinks perk covers everything.
