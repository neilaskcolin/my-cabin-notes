Cabin thermostats don't run their own air conditioner. They fine-tune air supplied by the ship's central system, which is why a cabin can feel stubborn: you're adjusting within a range the ship has already set. Most "broken" thermostats turn out to be a closed vent, an open balcony door or a fault maintenance can fix in minutes.

## How cruise cabin thermostats work
Look for a dial or small digital panel near the cabin door, the desk or the bed. Controls vary:

- **Numbered dials or digital displays,** sometimes in Fahrenheit on ships built for the North American market. For reference, 20°C is 68°F, 22°C is 72°F and 24°C is 75°F.
- **"Cooler–warmer" scales** with no numbers at all, where small turns make a real difference.
- **Fan speed buttons** on some ships, separate from temperature.

Give any change 30 to 60 minutes to take effect.

### Why the cabin feels warmer than expected
Afternoon sun on a balcony cabin in the Caribbean can overwhelm the cooling, especially on the side facing west. Keep the curtains drawn during the day. Some ships, especially European-built ones, also need your cruise card in a slot by the door to keep power and cooling running.

> **Good to know:** many ships cut the air conditioning while the balcony door is open. Leaving it ajar for the sea breeze at night can leave you waking up in a warm, humid cabin.

### Why the cabin feels too cold
Alaska sailings can be the opposite problem: the system still pushes cool air while it's 10°C outside. Turn the dial to warm, partly close the ceiling vent if it has louvres, and ask your cabin attendant for an extra blanket. Space heaters are generally banned as a fire risk. ## What to do if it won't cool down or warm up
1. **Check the vent.** Make sure the ceiling vent isn't closed or blocked by luggage.
2. **Close the balcony door fully** and wait a few minutes.
3. **Set the thermostat to its coolest or warmest point** for an hour.
4. **Report it** to guest services or your cabin attendant if nothing changes. Ask for maintenance to check the airflow.
5. **Ask for a fan** as a stopgap. Some ships have a few to lend, and some lines allow small battery or USB fans in luggage, but many restrict corded appliances, so check before packing one.
6. **Ask about a move** if the fault can't be fixed and the ship has space.

## Sharing a cabin with different preferences
One person's perfect 20°C is another's icebox. Agree a setting early and add a light blanket for the cold sleeper. If temperature, noise or motion matter a lot to how you sleep, cabin location matters too; [how to pick your first cruise cabin](/articles/pick-first-cruise-cabin/) explains why midship cabins on middle decks tend to be the most comfortable.

## Report it the same day
Temperature problems are among the easiest fixes on a ship, but only if the crew knows. If the cabin is still uncomfortable after the checks above, raise it that day, and if it's part of a wider problem like noise, see [what to do about a noisy cabin](/articles/cruise-cabin-noise-complaints-what-to-do/).
