For most eastern Canadian couples, a Canada and New England fall cruise costs between C$3,500 and C$7,000 all-in for an inside cabin. It is the rare cruise where you can skip the airport on at least one leg, which is a big part of why it is good value.

## The fare and what drives it
At recent prices, inside cabins on a 7-night sailing run roughly US$700–1,100 per person, and balconies US$1,200–2,000 in the peak foliage weeks. Ten- and twelve-night itineraries that add Saguenay, Gaspé or Sydney cost more in total but often less per night.

Two things push fares up: the last week of September through mid-October, when colour peaks, and the Canadian Thanksgiving long weekend, when Canadian demand piles in. Early September and late October sailings are usually cheaper, with greener or barer trees.

## How you get there changes the budget
Most sailings are one-way between Quebec City or Montreal and Boston or New York, though round trips from Boston or New York to the Maritimes are also common. The cheapest plan depends on where you live.

| Starting from | Getting to the ship | Rough cost for two |
|---|---|---|
| Montreal or Quebec City | Taxi, transit or a short drive | Under C$100 |
| Ottawa | About 2 hours to Montreal, or Via Rail to Quebec City | C$100–300 |
| Toronto | Fly to Quebec City, Montreal or Boston, or a full day's drive or train | C$300–800 |
| Halifax or the Maritimes | Fly to Boston or Montreal, or join a sailing that calls at Halifax | C$400–900 |
| Calgary, Edmonton, Vancouver, Winnipeg | Fly to Montreal, Quebec City or Boston, often with a connection | C$900–2,000 |

> **Watch out:** on a one-way sailing your car ends up in the wrong country. If you drive to Quebec City and disembark in Boston, budget for a flight, bus or train home and long-term parking, or choose a round-trip sailing instead.

## A sample budget, per couple
Take an Ottawa couple on a 7-night sailing from Quebec City to Boston in early October, in an inside cabin:

- Fare with taxes: about C$2,200–3,000
- Via Rail to Quebec City and a hotel night before: C$400–650
- Flight home from Boston: C$300–700
- Gratuities: about C$300–350
- Excursions and lunches ashore: C$500–1,000
- Drinks on board: C$250–500
- Travel insurance: C$150–400

That totals roughly C$4,100–6,600. Sailing from Montreal on a round trip, or in early September, can bring it closer to C$3,500.

## Where the money goes ashore
Unlike Alaska, where excursions dominate the extras budget, this is a walking itinerary. Quebec City, Halifax, Bar Harbor, Charlottetown and Saint John all reward exploring on foot from the dock. Most couples book one or two paid trips, such as Peggy's Cove from Halifax at about US$60–90 or the Acadia National Park loop from Bar Harbor at about US$70–110, and walk the rest.

The costs that catch people out are a pre-night hotel in Quebec City during peak weeks, which can run C$250–400, and lobster lunches in every port. Budget around C$100–150 per couple per port day if you plan to eat ashore. The [Canada and New England fall cruise guide](/articles/new-england-canada-fall-cruises/) covers what each port is like.

> **Canadian tip:** you need a valid passport for sailings that visit US ports. When you come home after 48 hours or more away, each person can bring back up to C$800 in goods duty-free.

## When to book for the best price
This short season has a loyal repeat audience, so prime cabins go early.

1. Choose your dates and ports by the fall before you sail.
2. Book during wave season, January to March, when the best promotions appear (see [wave season explained](/articles/wave-season-explained-canada/)).
3. Pick a refundable fare so you can reprice if a better offer comes along.
4. Book your Quebec City hotel and train or flights as soon as the cruise is confirmed.
5. Check the fare again before final payment, usually around three months before sailing.

By early summer, balconies on late-September and early-October departures are often gone. If your dates are flexible, [cruising over Canadian long weekends](/articles/cruise-canadian-long-weekends-holidays/) explains when holiday demand pushes prices up.

## Is it good value?
For eastern Canadians, yes: the travel is short, the ports are walkable and the scenery is the draw. It suits couples, multigenerational groups and anyone who wants a first cruise without long flights. If you live in the West, compare it with an Alaska sailing from Vancouver, where your flights are shorter.
