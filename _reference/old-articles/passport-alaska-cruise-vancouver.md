Yes, in practice: bring a valid passport for every person in your group. An Alaska cruise from Vancouver visits the United States, and you formally enter the US at Canada Place before you board, so the paperwork rules of a US border apply.

## Why a Canadian sailing needs US paperwork

Canada Place hosts [US Customs and Border Protection preclearance](https://www.cbp.gov/travel/preclearance). You clear US entry in Vancouver, then step ashore in Ketchikan, Juneau or Skagway already admitted. It is usually quicker than airport queues, but it is a real border. Without acceptable documents you won't board, and a line that turns you away at check-in won't refund your fare.

## Which documents count?

US rules for Canadian citizens arriving by sea are broader than many people think. The catch is that your cruise line can be stricter, so check its travel documents page for your sailing.

| Document | Accepted under US rules for sea arrival? | The catch |
|---|---|---|
| Canadian passport | Yes | None; accepted by every line |
| NEXUS card | Yes, for Canadian citizens | Not every line lists it; useless if your card has expired |
| Birth certificate, age 15 and under | Yes, for children | Won't work if the child needs to fly home |
| Birth certificate, age 16 and over | No | Adults need a passport or NEXUS |
| Enhanced driver's licence | Accepted at some land and sea crossings | Many cruise lines don't accept it |
| PR card alone | No | Must be paired with a foreign passport |

> **Watch out:** if you miss the ship in a port or need to leave the cruise for medical reasons, you'll have to get home from Alaska. A passport handles any route, including a flight connecting through Seattle. That is why lines and border officials recommend one even when another document is technically allowed.

## The validity question

Check expiry dates the day you book, not the week you sail. US rules only require your passport to be valid for your stay, but some cruise lines ask for six months beyond the return date. Spring is the worst time to need a renewal, as queues grow just as Alaska season approaches.

If a renewal is needed, take these steps:

1. Check every passport in the group as soon as you choose a sailing.
2. Apply for renewals right away, allowing extra time between March and June.
3. Book with a refundable deposit if the renewal is still in progress.
4. Enter passport details in online check-in once the new passport arrives.

## Kids, grandkids and blended families

Each child needs their own document. If a child is travelling without both parents, for example with grandparents or one parent, carry a signed consent letter from the absent parent or parents. The Government of Canada recommends one, and it saves awkward questions at the border on the way out and back.

> **Canadian tip:** permanent residents of Canada are not covered by the rules for Canadian citizens. They need their own country's passport plus their PR card, and some nationalities also need a US visa or ESTA. Sort this out before booking, not at the terminal.

## The closed-loop exception, and why it doesn't apply

You may read that some cruises accept a birth certificate and photo ID for adults. That "closed-loop" rule is for US citizens on sailings that start and end at the same US port. A Vancouver departure doesn't qualify, and it doesn't cover Canadians.

## Coming home

At the end of the cruise you clear Canadian customs at Canada Place, where your passport is the quickest proof of citizenship. Keep receipts for anything bought in Alaska ready for your declaration, and check [duty-free limits for Canadians](/articles/cruise-shopping-onboard-duty-free/) before you shop. If you're unsure what your sailing requires, a licensed cruise consultant or the line itself can confirm before you pay in full.
