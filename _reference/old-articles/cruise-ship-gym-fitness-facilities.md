You can keep up a full workout routine at sea without paying a cent beyond your fare. The gym, pools and jogging track are free. The charges start where an instructor, a therapist or a heated stone lounger gets involved.

## What's free and what costs extra

| Facility or service | Usually included? | Typical cost at recent prices |
|---|---|---|
| Gym: cardio, weights, machines | Yes | Free |
| Jogging or walking track | Yes | Free |
| Main pools and hot tubs | Yes | Free |
| Sports courts, mini golf | Yes | Free |
| Basic stretch or abs class | Often | Free |
| Spin, yoga, Pilates, boot camp | Usually not | US$12–20 per class |
| Personal training session | No | Around US$100 or more |
| Thermal suite (sauna, steam, heated loungers) | No | Day or cruise-long pass |
| Adults-only paid retreat decks | No | Day or cruise-long pass |
| Spa treatments | No | Plus an automatic gratuity, often around 18–20% |

Prices are in US dollars, so add roughly 35–40% for the Canadian equivalent.

## Getting the most from the free options
- **Go early or on port days.** The gym is busiest from about 7 to 10am on sea days. On port days it's often near empty.
- **Use the track.** Most ships post how many laps make a mile or kilometre. On Alaska sailings, a morning lap with mountains alongside beats any treadmill.
- **Grab a window machine.** Many gyms face forward or overlook the sea; arrive early for those.
- **Bring compact gear.** Resistance bands, a skipping rope or a travel yoga mat let you train in the cabin or on a balcony.
- **Mind the motion.** Treadmills feel different on a rolling ship. Slow down and hold the rail on rough days.

## Where ships quietly charge
The spa runs free seminars on topics like posture, detox or metabolism. They're informative, but many end with a product or treatment pitch, so go in knowing that. Body composition scans and fitness assessments can also come with a sales conversation attached.

Some classes advertised as "fitness" in the daily programme carry a fee in small print. Check before you sign up, because the charge goes straight to your cruise card. If you plan to pay for several classes, ask whether a class pass is sold; it's often cheaper.

> **Quick math:** a thermal suite day pass at around US$50 on three sea days is US$150, roughly C$205–210. If a cruise-long pass costs less than that, and you'd use it on port evenings too, the pass wins. Spa passes are capped, so popular ships sell out early in the cruise.

## Adults-only and quiet spaces
Some lines include an adults-only solarium or sun deck in the fare, while others charge for a premium retreat with cabanas. ## Charging your fitness tracker
Cabins often have only a couple of outlets, and most lines ban power bars. The rules are in [what you can and can't plug in on a cruise](/articles/extension-cord-cruise-ship-rules/). A small USB charger with several ports usually solves it.

## Planning your fitness routine for the cruise
Look up your ship's gym hours and class schedule in the app on embarkation day, decide which paid classes are worth it, and set a spa budget before you walk past the reception desk. Everything else is free.
