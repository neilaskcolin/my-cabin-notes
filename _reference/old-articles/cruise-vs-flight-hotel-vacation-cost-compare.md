A cruise and a do-it-yourself trip can cost surprisingly similar amounts. What changes is what you get for the money: a cruise buys breadth and convenience, while an independent trip buys depth and freedom. The trick is comparing true totals rather than a cruise fare against a hotel rate.

## Is a cruise cheaper than flights and hotels?
For multi-stop trips, often yes. Moving between cities or islands is where independent trips get expensive, and a cruise folds that cost into the fare along with your room, most meals and evening entertainment.

For a single destination, usually no. If you only want one beach or one city, you pay for a ship's worth of facilities you might not use, plus the cost of getting to a port.

## What each price really includes
**A cruise fare typically covers:**
- Your cabin and transport between ports
- Main dining room, buffet and basic drinks such as water, coffee and juice at breakfast
- Most entertainment, pools and the gym

**On a cruise you usually pay extra for:**
- Gratuities, commonly around US$16–20 per person per day at recent rates
- Alcohol, specialty coffee and soft drinks
- Wifi, specialty restaurants and shore excursions
- Port taxes and fees, if not already in the quoted price

**A flight-and-hotel trip adds:**
- Every meal and drink
- Transport between cities, plus airport and station transfers
- Hotel taxes and, in many cities, a local tourist tax
- Tours and admissions, much the same as cruise excursions

The full list of cruise inclusions is in [what's actually included in a cruise fare](/articles/whats-included-cruise-fare/).

## A worked example: one week in the Mediterranean from Toronto
Here is a rough comparison for a couple, at recent summer prices, excluding the transatlantic flights, which cost about the same either way. The cruise is a 7-night round trip from Barcelona in an inside cabin. The independent trip is seven nights split between Barcelona, Florence and Rome in mid-range hotels.

| Per couple, CAD | 7-night cruise | Independent, 3 cities |
|---|---|---|
| Cruise fare or hotels | C$2,200–3,600 | C$1,600–2,700 |
| Taxes and port fees | C$400–700 | Included above |
| Pre-cruise hotel night | C$250–400 | Not needed |
| Travel between cities | Included | C$300–600 |
| Meals | Mostly included | C$900–1,400 |
| Gratuities | C$320–380 | Tips in restaurants, within meals |
| Drinks and coffee | C$300–600 | Within meals |
| Excursions, port transport, admissions | C$600–1,500 | C$400–900 |
| **Rough total** | **C$4,100–7,200** | **C$3,200–5,600** |

On these numbers the independent trip can come in a little cheaper, but it covers three cities. The cruise typically calls at five or six, such as Marseille, Florence via Livorno and Rome via Civitavecchia, without repacking once. Some of those ports sit an hour or more from the city, which is why port transport appears on the cruise side. The detail on Med budgets is in [Mediterranean cruise cost from Canada](/articles/mediterranean-cruise-cost-canada/).

> **Quick math:** divide each total by the number of nights and by the number of places you will actually see. A trip that costs C$700 a night but shows you six cities can be better value than one at C$600 a night that shows you three.

## Where each format usually wins
**A cruise usually wins for:**
- Alaska's Inside Passage. Juneau has no road connection to the rest of North America, and flights between small towns add up fast, so a cruise from Vancouver is the simple option (see [Alaska cruise cost from Vancouver](/articles/alaska-cruise-cost-vancouver-realistic-budget/)).
- Several Caribbean islands in one week, where inter-island flights are limited and pricey.
- Families who would otherwise need two hotel rooms and three restaurant bills a day.
- Anyone who hates packing and unpacking every two nights.

**An independent trip usually wins for:**
- One beach for a week. A Toronto or Montreal flight straight to a resort skips the Florida flight and hotel night a Caribbean cruise often needs. The [cruise vs all-inclusive comparison](/articles/cruise-vs-all-inclusive-resort-value/) covers that choice.
- Slow travel in one or two cities, with evenings in town rather than back on board.
- Travellers with strong hotel or airline points balances to burn.
- Anyone with dietary, timing or mobility needs better served by choosing every hotel and meal.

## The hidden costs people forget
- **On the cruise side:** a hotel night before sailing, drinks, wifi and the excursions that get you from port to city.
- **On the independent side:** luggage fees on short flights, taxis to stations, city tourist taxes and the time lost to transfers.
- **On both:** travel insurance, since your provincial health plan covers very little abroad.

## How to run your own comparison
Price the same dates both ways, list every cost line in the tables above, and convert everything to Canadian dollars at your card's real rate. If the totals are close, choose the format that suits how you like to travel. For a first-timer weighing the extras, [first cruise mistakes that cost money](/articles/first-cruise-mistakes-cost-money/) is a useful check.
