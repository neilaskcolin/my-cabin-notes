faq1q: Is onboard credit the same as a discount?
faq1a: Not quite. A discount lowers what you pay before you sail and the saving is yours however you use it, while onboard credit is spending money on your ship account that only works on board during that sailing and often expires unused at the end.
faq2q: Can unused onboard credit be refunded as cash?
faq2a: It depends on the type. Credit described as refundable can come back to your card at the end of the cruise, but most promotional and agency credit is non-refundable and simply disappears, so plan to spend it before the last night.
faq3q: Can onboard credit pay for gratuities?
faq3a: On most major lines, yes. The automatic daily service charges post to your onboard account, and credit is applied against that balance first. Confirm for your line, and remember this only works if you have not already prepaid gratuities.

When a cruise offer comes in two flavours, a cash discount or onboard credit (OBC), the equal-sounding numbers hide a real difference. The right pick comes down to what you would spend on board anyway, which currency each offer is in, and how the fare behaves if plans change.

## What each option actually gives you
A cash discount lowers the fare. You pay less at final payment, and the saving can go toward flights, a hotel in Vancouver or Fort Lauderdale, or nothing at all.

Onboard credit is money loaded onto your shipboard account, usually in US dollars. It pays for things charged on board, such as gratuities, drinks, specialty dining, spa treatments, wifi and ship-booked excursions. It normally cannot pay the fare, and on some lines it is excluded from the casino. How it is earned and stacked is covered in [onboard credit explained](/articles/onboard-credit-explained/).

## Why credit offers are often bigger
Lines can often afford to offer more in credit than in cash, because a dollar of credit spent at the bar or the spa costs them less than a dollar off the fare. It is common to see something like US$300 of credit offered against US$150 off. If you would spend the credit, the bigger number usually wins.

## Which is better for your trip?

| Your situation | Usually better | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Gratuities not prepaid, typical spender | Onboard credit | Daily tips alone can absorb it |
| Gratuities and a drinks package already prepaid | Cash discount | Little left on board to charge it against |
| Short 3–4 night sailing, light spender | Cash discount | Hard to use the full credit |
| Credit offered is clearly larger than the cash | Onboard credit | Bigger value if you spend it |
| Non-refundable fare, some doubt about travelling | Cash discount | Less money at risk if you cancel |
| Trip cost drives your insurance premium | Cash discount | A lower fare can trim the premium slightly |

> **Example:** a couple from Edmonton on a 7-night Alaska sailing is offered US$250 of onboard credit or C$250 off a fare booked in Canadian dollars. At about 1.37, the credit is worth roughly C$340. Automatic gratuities at around US$18 per person per day come to about US$252 for the week, so the credit covers their tips almost exactly. As long as they don't prepay gratuities, the credit is worth about C$90 more than the discount.

## The catches with onboard credit

### Use it or lose it
Most promotional credit is non-refundable. Whatever is left when you disembark is gone. Check your account balance in the line's app two nights before the end and spend the remainder on something useful, such as a spa treatment, a shop purchase or an extra dinner.

### Per person or per cabin
A "US$100 onboard credit" offer may mean per cabin or per person. That difference doubles or halves the value for a couple, so read the confirmation wording.

### Currency
Credit is almost always in US dollars, while a discount on a Canadian-dollar booking is in CAD. Convert before comparing, using roughly 1.35–1.40. [Should Canadians pay in CAD or USD](/articles/paying-cad-vs-usd-cruise/) covers the wider currency picture.

### Repricing
If the fare drops later and you ask for a reprice, a new promotion may carry different extras. Make sure a lower fare does not quietly remove credit worth more than the saving (see [cruise price protection](/articles/cruise-price-protection-asking-for-it/)).

## How to decide in three questions
- Would I spend at least this amount on board on things I would buy anyway?
- Are both offers in the same currency once converted?
- Is the credit refundable or non-refundable, and per person or per cabin?

If the answers are yes, yes and known, credit of equal or greater value is effectively cash. If you are unsure you will spend it, take the discount.

## The rule of thumb
Credit that matches your normal onboard spending is as good as money. Credit that forces you to buy things you didn't want is worth less than its face value. For more on how promotions get framed, see [cruise line sales decoded](/articles/cruise-line-sales-decoded/).
