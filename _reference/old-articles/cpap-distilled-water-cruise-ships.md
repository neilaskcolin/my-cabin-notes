Plan on the ship supplying most of it and bring the first night's water yourself. Every major line can usually provide distilled water for a CPAP humidifier, but you have to ask ahead, the price varies, and the request doesn't always reach the cabin on day one.

## How do you get distilled water on a cruise ship?
The process is much the same across the big lines:

1. **Register your CPAP after booking.** Use the line's accessibility or special needs form, or ask whoever made the booking to submit it. Mention distilled water and the medical extension cord in the same request.
2. **Ask what it costs.** Some lines supply it free, others charge a small amount per bottle or a set fee for the week.
3. **Pack a first-night supply.** Two one-litre bottles in your carry-on covers the first couple of nights.
4. **Confirm with your cabin attendant.** On boarding day, tell your attendant you use a CPAP and check the water has been arranged. If not, guest services can chase it.
5. **Top up if needed.** Most ships can find more with a day's notice.

The general machine rules, including when your own extension cord is and isn't allowed, are in [can you bring a CPAP machine on a cruise ship](/articles/bringing-cpap-machine-cruise-ship/) and [extension cord rules on cruise ships](/articles/extension-cord-cruise-ship-rules/).

## How much distilled water will you need?
A typical humidifier uses roughly 250–350 ml a night, depending on your settings and how dry the cabin air is. Ship air conditioning runs dry, so expect the upper end.

| Cruise length | Water needed (approx.) | What to carry on |
|---|---|---|
| 3–4 nights | 1–1.5 litres | All of it, if you like |
| 7 nights | 2–2.5 litres | 1–2 litres, rest from the ship |
| 10–14 nights | 3.5–5 litres | 1–2 litres, rest from the ship |

## Getting water to the port
Medical liquids are routinely allowed through cruise terminal security in reasonable quantities. Tell the screener it's for a CPAP.

Flying is different. Screening rules for medically necessary liquids over 100 ml leave the decision to the officer, so it's simpler to fly with an empty humidifier tank and buy water after you land.

> **Watch out:** never pack the machine with water in the humidifier chamber. It can leak into the device in transit. Empty and dry it before every flight.

### Where to buy it
Supermarkets and pharmacies near the Vancouver, Seattle, Fort Lauderdale and Miami cruise ports stock distilled water. A pre-cruise hotel night, which is wise anyway (see [why to fly in the day before](/articles/fly-day-before-cruise/)), gives you time for a quick shop. If you're driving to Canada Place, simply buy it at home and bring it along.

## If the water runs out
Bottled drinking water is a workable stand-in for a night or two; clean the chamber afterwards to clear mineral scale. Many machines can also run with the humidifier switched off, which some people find drying but tolerable for a night. Check your machine's manual or ask your sleep clinic before you travel.

## One request covers it all
Machine registration, the extension cord and the water are best handled as a single request to the line's accessibility desk soon after booking. If you also fill in a pre-cruise health form, the [cruise medical questionnaire guide](/articles/cruise-ship-medical-form-health-questionnaire/) explains what lines ask.
