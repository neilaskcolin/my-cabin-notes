A short cruise is one of the easiest ways to run a bachelorette or stag weekend: one booking, meals and entertainment included, and nobody has to drive. The things that make or break it are the length of the sailing, the age rules, cabin placement and an honest look at the bar bill.

## Which cruises work best for a bachelorette or stag?
- **3–4 night Bahamas sailings from Miami, Fort Lauderdale or Port Canaveral.** These are the classic choice for Toronto, Ottawa and Montreal groups, with a nonstop flight of about three hours and a long weekend at sea.
- **4–5 night Western Caribbean sailings.** These add a beach day in a port like Cozumel, worth it if the group wants a proper getaway rather than just a party weekend.
- **Short Pacific coastal sailings from Vancouver.** In spring and fall, ships repositioning to or from Alaska sail short one-way trips along the coast. These suit Western Canadian groups who'd rather skip a long flight, though you'll need to get home from the other end.
- **Adults-only ships.** Virgin Voyages sails from Miami with no kids on board, which suits groups who want a lively, grown-up atmosphere.

## The age rules that catch groups out
> **Watch out:** on sailings from North American ports, most major lines set the drinking age at 21, not Canada's 18 or 19. Most also require someone aged 21 or older in every cabin. If anyone in your group is under 21, check the line's rules before anyone pays a deposit.

Everyone will also need a valid passport for most itineraries, and the names on the booking must match those passports exactly.

## What does a cruise bachelorette cost per person?
Here is a sample 4-night Bahamas sailing from Toronto, two people to an inside cabin, at recent prices. Your figures will vary by line and dates.

| Item, per person | Rough cost |
|---|---|
| Cruise fare, inside cabin shared by two | US$300–600 (roughly C$410–830) |
| Taxes and port fees | US$100–150 (roughly C$140–210) |
| Gratuities, 4 nights | US$64–80 (roughly C$90–110) |
| Return flights Toronto to Florida | C$300–550 |
| Half a pre-cruise hotel room | C$80–150 |
| Drinks, paying as you go | US$150–350 (roughly C$200–480) |
| One beach day or excursion | C$70–200 |
| **Rough total** | **C$1,290–2,530** |

Booking three or four people into a cabin can lower the per-person fare, because third and fourth guests often pay less. Expect a squeeze in the bathroom on the big night.

## Cabins: close together, not crammed
Ask for cabins on the same corridor, or connecting cabins for the core group. Being able to wander between rooms for pre-drinks and outfit changes is half the fun. Book as a single group block rather than separate bookings, so the cabins are placed together and everyone works to the same payment dates.

## The drink package maths
A group that drinks steadily all day may clear the break-even point easily. A group planning one big night and moderate drinking otherwise usually does better paying as they go. Because most big lines make everyone over 21 in the same cabin buy the package if one person does, decide cabin by cabin. ## Planning the celebration on board
- **Door decorations:** cabin doors on most ships are metal, so magnetic decorations work. Check your line's rules before using tape or adhesive.
- **One headline event:** a private cocktail hour, a specialty dinner or a reserved table at a show gives the trip its centrepiece.
- **Optional extras:** keep the spa day, the pricier excursion and the specialty dinner optional, so nobody is priced out.
- **Downtime:** build in a pool day. Not everyone wants five straight days of group energy.
- **Outfits and sashes:** fine around the ship, but your line's dress code still applies in the main dining room on dressy nights.
