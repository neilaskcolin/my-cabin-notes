On Carnival, what happens after a price drop depends on the fare you chose. Early Saver bookings can claim a lower advertised fare until two business days before sailing, while standard fares can usually only be repriced before final payment.

## Early Saver: protection almost to the gangway
Early Saver is Carnival's discounted early-booking fare, sold until about 76 days before short cruises and 91 days before longer ones. If Carnival later advertises a lower fare for the same ship, sailing date, cabin category and number of guests, you can ask for the difference.

How you get it depends on timing:

- **Before final payment:** the booking is repriced, so the fare itself goes down. This is the best outcome.
- **After final payment:** the difference arrives as non-refundable onboard credit on your Sail & Sign account.
- **Deadline:** requests are accepted up to two business days before sailing, so a Saturday departure means getting it in by Wednesday or Thursday.

> **Example:** a couple from Winnipeg books a 7-night balcony on Carnival Celebration at US$1,200 each on Early Saver. Sixty days out, after final payment, the same category drops to US$1,050. They claim US$300 in onboard credit, roughly C$405–420, enough to cover their gratuities and then some.

The trade-off is the deposit. Early Saver deposits aren't refundable: cancel before final payment and you get a future cruise credit minus US$50 per person. Changing ship or date before final payment costs US$50 per guest. The full picture is in [Carnival cancellation policy](/articles/carnival-cancellation-policy-final-payment/).

## Standard fares: the final-payment rule
On Carnival's other fares, a lower advertised fare can usually be requested as a reprice before final payment. Check whether the new fare drops any perks you booked with, such as onboard credit or a reduced deposit, before you accept it. After the balance is paid, drops are generally not honoured, apart from occasional upgrade offers. How fare types differ across lines is in [cruise fare classes explained](/articles/cruise-fare-classes-refundable/).

## The fine print that trips people up
- The lower fare has to be bookable when you submit, so screenshot it with the date and time.
- Super Saver and fares where you can't choose your cabin can undercut Early Saver, but generally don't count for a claim.
- Casino offers, past-guest targeted deals and rates for specific groups are excluded.
- Drops in air, transfers or taxes don't qualify; only the cruise fare does.
- Credit issued after final payment has no cash value and is lost if unspent at the end of the cruise, as [onboard credit explained](/articles/onboard-credit-explained/) covers.

## How to claim a Carnival price drop
1. Note your exact ship, sailing date, cabin category and guest count from your confirmation.
2. Check Carnival's site regularly, or set alerts as in [how to set cruise price alerts](/articles/how-to-set-cruise-price-alerts/).
3. When the fare drops, screenshot the full price for the same category and guest count.
4. Submit Carnival's price protection request, or ask whoever booked you to do it.
5. Confirm the reprice or the credit amount in writing, then check it appears on your invoice or Sail & Sign account.

### When prices tend to move
Carnival's fares move often, with sales clustering around January to March wave season and Black Friday. [Wave season for Canadians](/articles/wave-season-explained-canada/) explains when deals tend to surface.

> **Canadian tip:** onboard credit is in US dollars. If your booking is in Canadian dollars, compare against the fare shown in the same currency, because an exchange-rate swing isn't a price drop and Carnival only matches its own advertised fares.

## Choosing the right Carnival fare in the first place
Early Saver gives you price protection but a non-refundable deposit; a standard fare gives you a refundable deposit but stops protecting you at final payment. Decide which matters more to you before booking, not after the price falls.
