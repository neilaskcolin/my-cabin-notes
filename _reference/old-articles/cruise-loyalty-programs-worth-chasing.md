Loyalty status is a nice bonus, not a reason to choose a cruise. Every big line rewards repeat guests, and the perks get genuinely good at the top. But for a Canadian who cruises once a year, those top tiers are usually a decade or more away, so pick the right trip first and let status build quietly.

## How cruise loyalty programs work
Each line runs its own program, and you're usually enrolled automatically after your first sailing:

- **Royal Caribbean:** Crown & Anchor Society, one point per night, two in a suite
- **Celebrity:** Captain's Club, points per night, more for higher cabin grades
- **Carnival:** Carnival Rewards, which replaced the VIFP Club in 2026 and earns points on spending rather than nights
- **Princess:** Captain's Circle
- **Holland America:** Mariner Society
- **Norwegian:** Latitudes Rewards
- **MSC:** MSC Voyagers Club
- **Disney:** Castaway Club, based on the number of sailings rather than nights

Most programs count cumulative nights or sailings, and once you reach a tier you keep it. That's kinder than airline programs, because a cruise from five years ago still counts.

## What do the tiers actually unlock?
Exact perks differ, but the pattern is similar across lines.

| Stage | Typical perks | Roughly how long for a one-cruise-a-year couple |
|---|---|---|
| Entry (after first cruise) | Past-guest offers, a welcome-back event, member pricing on some sailings | Right away |
| Middle tiers | Priority check-in, small onboard discounts, some free wifi time or laundry on certain lines | 4–6 years |
| Upper tiers | Priority boarding and tendering, lounge access, drink perks on some lines | 8–12 years |
| Top tiers | Free laundry, specialty dining, suite-style perks | Usually only for frequent or long-voyage cruisers |

> **Example:** in Royal Caribbean's Crown & Anchor Society at recent thresholds, Platinum takes 30 points, Emerald 55 and Diamond 80. A couple from Edmonton taking one 7-night Alaska cruise a year earns 7 points each annually, reaching Platinum in year five and Diamond in about year twelve. Book suites and it roughly halves.

## Should you stick to one line?
Not at first. Trying two or three lines across your first few cruises tells you far more than any loyalty perk. A family that loved Royal Caribbean's water slides may find Holland America's quieter style suits them better once the kids are grown.

### The Canadian twist
Canadians often cruise in two very different markets. Alaska from Vancouver is dominated by lines like Princess and Holland America, while Caribbean sailings from Florida lean towards Royal Caribbean, Norwegian and Carnival. If you split your trips, your status splits too. That's fine, as long as you don't choose a worse itinerary just to stay with one brand.

### Lines in the same family
Royal Caribbean and Celebrity share a parent company and recognize each other's status. Status-match offers between unrelated lines exist from time to time, explained in [loyalty and status match programs](/articles/loyalty-match-programs-cruise-lines/).

## When chasing status makes sense
- You cruise two or more times a year.
- You take long voyages, such as 14-night or longer itineraries, which build nights quickly (see [longer cruises for retirees](/articles/longer-cruises-retirees-14-nights/)).
- You're close to a tier with a perk you'd really use, like free drinks.
- You've already found the line you prefer.

## When it doesn't
- You cruise once every year or two.
- You'd pay more for the same itinerary on your status line than on a competitor.
- You're still working out which line suits you.

## Getting more from repeat cruising
Loyalty perks usually stack with promotional offers rather than replacing them, so a member discount or onboard credit can sit on top of a sale fare. Many lines also sell future-cruise deposits on board that add credit or perks to your next booking.

On credit cards, most cruise-line co-branded cards are aimed at US residents. Canadians usually get more from a flexible travel-rewards card, compared in [cruise line credit cards and rewards points](/articles/cruise-line-credit-card-rewards-points/).

## The bottom line
Let status accumulate in the background and spend your decision-making on the itinerary, ship and price. Once you know which line you'd book again, concentrating your cruises there starts to pay off.
