Celebrity suits you if you want a polished, food-focused cruise with a grown-up atmosphere and don't mind paying a bit more than mainstream fares. It is a poor fit if you want waterslides, wall-to-wall kids' activities or the lowest possible price.

## Where Celebrity sits in the market
Celebrity calls itself "modern luxury". In practice it is premium: a step up from its sister line Royal Caribbean, Norwegian or Carnival, and a step below luxury lines that include nearly everything. Expect design-led interiors, a serious art collection, well-regarded specialty restaurants and a guest mix that leans towards couples and travellers over 40.

Families do sail, mostly in summer and over Christmas, but the ships aren't built around them. Pools and lounges tend to be calmer than on the biggest mega-ships. If crowds are what put you off cruising, see [cruises for couples who don't like crowds](/articles/cruise-for-couples-who-dont-like-crowds/).

## Edge class or an older ship: which Celebrity are you booking?
The ship matters more on Celebrity than on many lines, because the fleet spans several generations.

| Class | Ships | What stands out |
|---|---|---|
| Edge | Edge, Apex, Beyond, Ascent, Xcel | Magic Carpet platform, Infinite Veranda cabins, Eden, Rooftop Garden, the newest tech |
| Solstice | Solstice, Equinox, Eclipse, Silhouette, Reflection | Real-grass Lawn Club, classic Celebrity layout, often good-value balconies |
| Millennium | Millennium, Infinity, Summit, Constellation | Smallest and oldest, refitted with Retreat suites, more intimate |

Edge-class ships usually cost more for a similar itinerary, and Solstice-class sailings are often where the value sits. In Alaska either works well, because the scenery is the main event.

## What's included, and what isn't
A cruise-only fare covers your cabin, the main dining room, the Oceanview Café buffet, entertainment, and basics like water, coffee and tea at the buffet. On Edge-class ships the main dining room is four complimentary restaurants rather than one. Celebrity also sells an All Included fare that adds the Classic drinks package and Basic wifi.

Daily gratuities were taken out of All Included in late 2023 and are now extra: US$19.50 per person per day in standard cabins since July 2026. Specialty restaurants, Premium wifi and excursions cost extra on any fare except where a suite includes them.

### Cabin tiers worth knowing
- **Concierge Class:** a balcony cabin with small upgrades such as priority boarding and extra in-cabin touches.
- **AquaClass:** spa-focused cabins with access to the Blu restaurant and the spa's relaxation areas.
- **The Retreat:** suites with a private lounge and sundeck, the Luminae restaurant and butler service, plus the Premium drinks package and Premium wifi at recent terms.

> **Quick math:** a couple on a 7-night cruise-only fare who want drinks and wifi would pay about US$85 per person per day for the Classic package plus 20% service, and around US$30 a day for one Premium wifi device. That is roughly US$1,640 for the week, or about C$2,200–2,300. Compare that with the All Included price difference for your sailing before deciding.

## Celebrity for Canadians: ports, flights and currency
### Alaska from Vancouver
For 2026, Celebrity's Alaska ships are Celebrity Edge, Solstice and Summit, sailing from Vancouver, Seattle and one-way from Seward. Its itineraries usually feature Hubbard Glacier or Endicott Arm rather than Glacier Bay, which matters if Glacier Bay is on your list; compare them in [Glacier Bay vs Hubbard vs Endicott Arm](/articles/glacier-bay-vs-hubbard-vs-endicott/). Albertans weighing ports can use [Calgary to Seattle vs Vancouver](/articles/calgary-vs-seattle-vs-vancouver-alaska/).

### Caribbean from Florida
Celebrity's Caribbean sailings centre on Fort Lauderdale and Miami, with Celebrity Beyond sailing the Caribbean year-round from those ports. Nonstop flights from Toronto and Montreal make the fly-cruise simple, as covered in [Toronto to Fort Lauderdale cruise flights](/articles/toronto-fort-lauderdale-cruise-flights/).

### Paying in Canadian dollars
Celebrity sells fares to Canadian residents in CAD, but everything on board posts in US dollars. Prepaying gratuities on your booking, and settling the onboard account with a no-foreign-transaction-fee card, keeps the exchange cost down.

> **Canadian tip:** March Break and Christmas sailings from Florida carry more children and higher fares. If you want Celebrity at its calmest, late January, early February and the Alaska shoulder months of May and September are usually quieter.

## Who Celebrity suits, and who should look elsewhere
Celebrity is a strong choice for:
- Couples and friends who rate food, wine and design highly.
- Travellers over 50 who want an elegant ship without luxury prices; see [best cruise lines for couples over 50](/articles/best-cruise-lines-couples-over-50/).
- First-timers who want a clear step up from mainstream lines.

Look elsewhere if:
- Your kids want waterslides and big family shows. Celebrity has a kids club but no waterslides.
- Price is the deciding factor, since mainstream lines are cheaper.
- You want drinks, gratuities and excursions all in; compare [luxury lines and their real extra spend](/articles/luxury-lines-compared-extra-spend/).
- Glacier Bay is a must on your Alaska trip.
