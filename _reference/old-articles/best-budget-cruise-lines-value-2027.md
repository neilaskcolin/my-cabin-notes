Carnival and MSC usually have the lowest fares among the big cruise lines, with Norwegian and Royal Caribbean's older ships close behind once promotions are counted. But the cheapest fare isn't always the best value. For Canadians, the total cost per day, plus the cost of getting to the ship, matters more than the headline price.

## Which mainstream lines offer the best value?
These are general patterns at recent prices. Fares and inclusions change often, so confirm for your sailing.

| Line | Why fares are low | What usually costs extra | Best value for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carnival | Large fleet, frequent short sailings, casual style | Drinks, specialty dining, wifi | Fun-focused couples, families, short getaways |
| MSC | Very competitive base fares, especially in the Caribbean | Drinks and wifi unless you choose a higher fare tier | Budget travellers who check the tier inclusions |
| Norwegian | Frequent bundles of drinks, wifi or dining | Higher base fare and daily service charge | Travellers who'd buy those extras anyway |
| Royal Caribbean, older ships | Lower fares than flagship ships, same brand | Drinks, wifi, some activities | Families wanting big-ship features for less |
| Holland America and Princess, shoulder season | Discounts on May and September Alaska | Drinks, wifi, gratuities | Couples wanting a calmer ship at a fair price |

For a closer look at each line, see the honest guides to [Carnival](/articles/carnival-honest-guide-canadians/), [MSC](/articles/msc-cruises-honest-guide-canadians/) and [Royal Caribbean](/articles/royal-caribbean-honest-guide-canadians/).

## Why the cheapest fare isn't always the best value
Two cruises with the same fare can end up hundreds of dollars apart. Gratuities run about US$16–20 per person per day on mainstream lines. A drink package can add US$60–100 or more per person per day. Wifi, specialty dining and excursions add up too.

### Compare the daily total, not the fare
Add the fare, taxes, gratuities and the extras you know you'll buy, then divide by the number of nights. A line with a higher fare that includes drinks or wifi can come out ahead of a cheaper fare with nothing included. [How cruise pricing works](/articles/how-cruise-pricing-works/) explains why the same cabin can sell at very different prices.

> **Watch out:** MSC's lowest fare tier is very basic. If you would add drinks, wifi or a better cabin location later, price the higher tier up front, as it can be cheaper than buying the pieces separately.

## The cost of getting to the ship
For Canadians, flights can cost as much as the cruise. A cheap Caribbean fare from Miami becomes less of a bargain once you add flights from Calgary and a hotel night.

That's why the best budget line for you may simply be the one sailing from somewhere easy to reach:
- **Western Canada:** Alaska sailings from Vancouver need no flight for BC residents and only a short one from Alberta.
- **Ontario and Quebec:** Florida sailings, or Bermuda and Canada-New England sailings from New York and Boston.
- **Long weekends:** short Bahamas sailings; see [Bahamas cruise costs from Canada](/articles/bahamas-cruise-cost-canada/).

> **Canadian tip:** some lines and agencies offer pricing in Canadian dollars, while others charge in US dollars. Compare both, including your card's foreign transaction fee, before you pay. [Paying in CAD vs USD](/articles/paying-cad-vs-usd-cruise/) runs through the comparison.

## How to find the best value on any line
1. **Sail in shoulder season.** May and September in Alaska, and early December or late April in the Caribbean, are often much cheaper than peak weeks.
2. **Look at older ships.** They often have lower fares, fewer crowds and the same food and service standards.
3. **Consider repositioning cruises.** These one-way sailings offer some of the lowest per-night prices; see [repositioning cruises explained](/articles/repositioning-cruises-explained/).
4. **Book during wave season.** January to March brings many of the year's best promotions; see [wave season explained](/articles/wave-season-explained-canada/).
5. **Choose an inside or guarantee cabin** if you'll mainly use the cabin to sleep.
6. **Avoid school holidays.** March break, July, August and Christmas push fares up.
7. **Watch for price drops** after booking, and ask whether you can reprice before final payment.

## Planning for 2027
Most lines already have 2027 sailings on sale. Booking early usually gets you the best choice of cabins, while waiting can sometimes bring promotions on slower-selling dates. [Booking 2027 now vs waiting](/articles/booking-2027-now-vs-waiting/) sets out the trade-offs.

## Who budget lines suit, and who they don't
Budget lines suit families, groups, first-timers and anyone happy with a lively ship. They suit people less well if they want quiet, fine dining or lots of included extras. If calm matters more than cost, look at [quiet adult-focused ships](/articles/quiet-ships-without-waterslides/) instead.

## The value checklist
Before booking, ask what is included, what gratuities will cost, whether the fare is refundable, and how much it will cost to reach the port. Comparing those four things across two or three lines is the surest way to find real value.
