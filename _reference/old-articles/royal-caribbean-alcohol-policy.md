You can bring two 750 ml bottles of wine or champagne per stateroom onto a Royal Caribbean ship, carried on at embarkation, and nothing stronger. Drink them in your cabin for free or pay a corkage fee of about US$15 per bottle in a restaurant or bar.

## What alcohol can you bring on Royal Caribbean?
The allowance is per stateroom, not per adult. A couple gets two bottles between them, and so does a family of four sharing one cabin. Bottles must be unopened, standard 750 ml size, and brought aboard by a guest aged 21 or over. Magnums and boxed wine don't qualify.

The window is embarkation day only. Wine bought at a later port, in Cozumel or Juneau, is held like any other bottle.

A small allowance of non-alcoholic drinks is also permitted in carry-on, recently up to 12 standard cans or cartons per stateroom. Confirm both allowances before you pack.

### Getting your wine aboard without a fuss
1. Pack both bottles in your carry-on. Royal Caribbean expects the allowance in hand luggage, and wine in a suitcase is more likely to be pulled.
2. Wrap them in clothing or a padded wine sleeve.
3. Have an adult aged 21 or over carry the bag through security.
4. Chill one in your cabin fridge.

## The Royal Caribbean corkage fee
Corkage of roughly US$15 per bottle applies when a server opens your wine in the main dining room, a specialty restaurant such as Chops Grille, or a bar. Wine poured in your stateroom or on your balcony carries no fee.

> **Example:** a couple from Edmonton flying into Vancouver the day before an Alaska sailing buys two bottles of Okanagan wine near their hotel. One is opened on the balcony during sail-away (free), the other at dinner on formal night (about US$15, roughly C$20–21).

## Beer, spirits and bottles bought in port
Beer, spirits and liqueurs are not permitted in any luggage. Security holds them until the end of the cruise, as it does rum bought in port. Spirits from the ship's duty-free shop are usually delivered to your stateroom on the final night.

## The drinking age on sailings from Canada and the US
On Royal Caribbean sailings from North America, including Vancouver and Seattle, the minimum drinking age on board is 21. That catches out Canadian families with 18- to 20-year-olds who drink legally at home. Some sailings elsewhere set it at 18.

> **Canadian tip:** duty-free bottles collected on the last night count toward your CBSA personal exemption. After 48 hours or more away, each adult who meets the drinking age of the province of entry can bring back 1.5 L of wine, or 1.14 L of spirits, or 8.5 L of beer duty-free. It is one of the three, not all of them.

## Wine allowance or drink package?
The allowance suits couples who like a glass with dinner but won't drink enough to justify a package. ## How Royal Caribbean compares
| Line | Wine you can bring | Corkage (recent) |
|---|---|---|
| **Royal Caribbean** | 2 bottles per stateroom | ~$15 in bars and restaurants |
| [Carnival](/articles/carnival-alcohol-policy/) | 1 bottle per adult (21+) | ~$15 in bars and restaurants |
| [Norwegian](/articles/norwegian-alcohol-policy/) | No fixed limit | ~$15 per bottle, paid at boarding |

Carnival and Norwegian are the lines Canadians most often weigh against Royal Caribbean. Royal Caribbean's per-stateroom rule is the tightest for a cabin of three or four adults, who could bring more on Carnival. Norwegian sets no limit but charges corkage on every bottle. ## Sorting this before you pack
Decide before embarkation day whether to carry your two bottles, buy a package or pay as you go. Wine bought near the port costs far less than on board, but only if it is in your carry-on at the terminal.
