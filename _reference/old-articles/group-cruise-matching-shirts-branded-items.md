Matching shirts are a cheerful group cruise tradition, and they make your crowd easy to spot at a busy terminal or on a tender. Order early, size carefully, dress for the destination and keep it optional.

## Ordering timeline
1. **8 weeks out:** agree the design. Keep it simple, with the group name, the year and perhaps the ship or destination.
2. **7 weeks out:** collect sizes from everyone using a size chart, since unisex and fitted cuts run very differently. Include kids' sizes.
3. **6 weeks out:** place the order and pay. Ask for a digital proof before printing.
4. **3–4 weeks out:** delivery, with time left to swap any wrong sizes.
5. **2 weeks out:** hand shirts out, or post them to people in other cities.

## Canadian ordering tips
> **Canadian tip:** ordering from a US website can bring brokerage fees plus GST or HST at the door, and slower delivery across the border. A Canadian print shop or a supplier that ships from inside Canada usually avoids both, and you pay in Canadian dollars.

If your group is spread across the country, compare shipping everything to the organiser with posting directly to each household. For a reunion with relatives in Halifax, Winnipeg and Kelowna, direct shipping can cost more than the shirts.

> **Quick math:** 20 printed T-shirts at about C$20 each comes to C$400, or C$20 per person. Adding a second print location, such as a design on the back, or switching to performance fabric can push that to C$30–40 each.

## Choosing for the climate
- **Caribbean:** light colours and breathable or moisture-wicking fabric. Dark cotton with a heavy print gets hot fast in the sun.
- **Alaska:** long sleeves, hoodies or even matching toques. They're far more useful on a glacier day than a T-shirt you'll hide under a jacket.
- **Embarkation photos:** bright colours stand out in a crowd, which helps if you're also hiring a photographer, as discussed in [hiring a professional photographer for a group cruise](/articles/hiring-professional-photographer-group-cruise/).

## Other branded touches
- **Cabin door magnets:** most cabin doors are metal, so magnetic signs work and help people find each other's cabins. Check the line's rules before using tape or stickers.
- **Lanyards:** handy on lines that use a physical cruise card, less so where a wearable or phone app does the job.
- **Tote bags and hats:** useful on port days, and they pack flat.

## When to wear them, and when not
Embarkation day, a group excursion and the final group photo are the classics. Your line's dress code still applies at dinner on dressy nights, so leave the shirts in the cabin then. [What to wear during the day](/articles/cruise-ship-dress-code-daytime-wear/) covers daytime norms.

## Keep it optional
Ask who wants a shirt rather than ordering for everyone, and keep the price clearly separate from the trip cost. People who skip the shirt are still part of the group.
