Bring your own mask and snorkel, rent the fins. That split suits most cruisers who plan to snorkel at more than one port. The mask is the piece that matters for comfort and fit, while fins are bulky and the least personal part of the kit.

## Bring, rent or rely on the excursion?

| Option | Best for | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bring mask, snorkel and fins | Keen snorkellers on a reef-heavy itinerary | Best fit, ready anywhere | Fins eat suitcase space and weight |
| Bring mask and snorkel, rent fins | Most cruisers snorkelling two or more times | Good fit, little bulk | Small rental fee per port |
| Rent everything in port | One or two casual snorkels | Nothing to pack | Fit and cleanliness vary |
| Ship snorkel excursion | First-timers, tender ports | Gear included, guided | Less freedom, higher price |

## Why the mask is the part to own
A mask that leaks or fogs ruins a snorkel. Rental masks fit some faces well and others badly, and you only find out in the water. Your own mask, tested at home, removes the guesswork.

To test a mask, hold it to your face without the strap and breathe in gently through your nose. If it stays put, it seals. People with glasses can buy masks with corrective lenses, which is hard to find as a rental.

> **Quick math:** at recent prices, rental sets often cost around US$10–25 a day. Three snorkel days at about US$15 each is roughly US$45, or C$60–65. A decent mask and snorkel bought in Canada for C$50–90 pays for itself within one or two cruises, and you keep it.

## When renting makes more sense
- You are only snorkelling once, on a ship excursion that includes gear.
- You are flying on a tight baggage allowance and need the space.
- Your itinerary is mostly cities and beaches without good reefs.
- You are visiting a private island with rentals near the beach. [Private island stops explained](/articles/private-island-cruise-stops-explained/) covers what each one offers.

## Where it matters most
The decision only really counts on reef-rich routes. The western Caribbean, with Cozumel, Grand Cayman and Roatán, is the classic snorkelling itinerary, and the southern Caribbean has excellent shore snorkelling too. [Eastern vs western vs southern Caribbean](/articles/eastern-vs-western-caribbean-first-cruise/) helps you pick a route if snorkelling is the priority. On Alaska or Mediterranean city itineraries, leave the gear at home.

### Booking independently or through the ship
Booking a snorkel trip independently is often cheaper, and you can bring your own mask either way. The trade-off is timing: a ship tour guarantees you will not be left behind. [Shore excursion through the ship vs booking independently](/articles/cruise-excursion-through-ship-vs-independent/) explains when each makes sense.

## Packing tips for snorkel gear
- Pack the mask inside its hard case, wrapped in clothes, to protect the lens.
- Bring anti-fog drops, and clean a brand-new mask lens before the trip, since factory residue makes it fog.
- Some tour operators do not allow full-face snorkel masks, so a traditional mask is the safer choice.
- Rinse gear in fresh water after each use and let it dry in the cabin bathroom.
- Pack a rash guard and reef-safe sunscreen, since some marine parks restrict ordinary sunscreen.

## Planning your snorkel days
Check which ports on your itinerary have genuinely good reefs before deciding what to pack. If two or more do, bring a mask and snorkel. If none do, save the space, and use the [what not to pack list](/articles/what-not-to-pack-for-a-cruise/) to trim the rest of your suitcase.
