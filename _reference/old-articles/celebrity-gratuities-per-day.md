Celebrity charges US$19.50 per person per day in standard cabins, US$20.50 in Concierge Class and AquaClass, and US$24.50 in The Retreat. Those rates took effect on 29 July 2026, a rise of US$1.50 a day across the board, and they apply to every guest, children included.

## How much are Celebrity gratuities per day?
The charge is added to your onboard account each day unless you prepay it. It scales with cabin tier because the higher tiers carry extra service: Concierge Class and AquaClass have added perks, and Retreat suites come with a butler and their own restaurant, Luminae.

| Cabin and party, 7 nights | Approx. USD | Roughly in CAD |
|---|---|---|
| Couple, inside to balcony | $273 | C$370–380 |
| Couple, Concierge Class or AquaClass | $287 | C$385–400 |
| Couple, The Retreat | $343 | C$465–480 |
| Family of four, balcony | $546 | C$735–765 |
| Family of four, 10 nights | $780 | C$1,055–1,090 |

## Does All Included cover gratuities?
No, not since late 2023. When Celebrity launched All Included, it bundled drinks, wifi and tips. In October 2023 it took gratuities out, so All Included now covers the Classic drinks package and Basic wifi, and Retreat fares cover Premium drinks and wifi. Either way, the daily gratuity is extra. Older blog posts still say otherwise, which makes this a common budgeting mistake.

Celebrity sometimes runs promotions that add prepaid gratuities. If yours does, it will be listed in the offer, so check the wording rather than assuming. [What all-inclusive really means on a cruise](/articles/what-does-all-inclusive-mean-cruise/) explains why "all included" rarely means everything.

> **Watch out:** the drinks service charge and the daily gratuity are separate. All Included builds in the 20% charge on your drinks package, but the US$19.50-a-day gratuity still lands on your account.

## Should Canadians prepay?
Usually, yes. The reasons:
- **Rate lock:** prepaid gratuities stay at the rate in force when you paid, which is how guests who prepaid before July 2026 kept the old rate.
- **Currency:** on a booking priced in Canadian dollars, prepaid gratuities are added in CAD. On board, the charge posts in US dollars and converts at your card's rate, often with a foreign-transaction fee of about 2.5%.
- **A cleaner final bill:** a smaller onboard balance means a smaller hold on your card.

The wider trade-offs are in [cruise gratuities explained for Canadians](/articles/cruise-gratuities-canadians/) and [paying in CAD vs USD for a cruise](/articles/paying-cad-vs-usd-cruise/).

### If you don't prepay
Settle the onboard account with a no-foreign-transaction-fee credit card. Onboard credit from a promotion can usually be put towards the daily charge too; see [onboard credit explained](/articles/onboard-credit-explained/).

> **Example:** a Saskatoon family of four on a 10-night Caribbean sailing from Fort Lauderdale faces about US$780 in gratuities. A 2.5% card fee adds roughly US$20, or about C$26–28, for nothing.

## What the daily gratuity doesn't cover
- The 20% service charge on bar drinks, drink packages, specialty dining such as Fine Cut Steakhouse, room service and minibar items.
- Spa treatments, which carry their own service charge.
- Terminal porters and private tour guides, tipped in cash by convention. Small US bills work in Alaska and Florida; at Canada Place, Canadian cash is fine.

## Can you adjust gratuities on board?
Guest Relations can lower the charge for a genuine service problem, and adjusting for a specific failure is fair. Removing it altogether cuts into crew pay, since it is a real part of how stateroom and dining staff are paid. If someone stood out, name them on the end-of-cruise comment card or add a cash tip.
