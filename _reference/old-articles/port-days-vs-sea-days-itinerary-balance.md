faq1a: Anywhere from none to three. A 7-night Mediterranean cruise may have one or none, a Western Caribbean loop from Florida usually two, and an Eastern Caribbean or Mexican Riviera sailing often three, so check the day-by-day plan rather than assuming.
faq2q: Are sea days boring?
faq2a: Not for most people. Sea days are when the ship's full program runs: talks, trivia, shows, pools, the spa and long lunches with no all-aboard time. Many cruisers say they are the most relaxing days of the trip.
faq3q: Do scenic cruising days in Alaska count as sea days?
faq3a: On the itinerary they do, because the ship doesn't dock. In practice, a Glacier Bay or Hubbard Glacier day feels more like a port day, since you'll spend hours on deck watching ice and wildlife rather than resting.

Two cruises to the same region can feel completely different because of how many days you spend ashore. Before you compare ports, count the port days and sea days. That ratio shapes your energy, your budget and how much of the ship you actually enjoy.

## What does a typical itinerary look like?
These are common patterns for popular Canadian choices. Individual sailings vary, so always check yours.

| Itinerary | Typical ports | Typical sea days | How it feels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7-night Western Caribbean from Florida | 4 | 2 | Busy but balanced |
| 7-night Eastern Caribbean from Florida | 3 | 3 | Relaxed, longer sailing distances |
| 7-night Alaska round trip from Vancouver | 3 | 2, plus a glacier day | Scenic even at sea |
| 7-night Mediterranean | 5–6 | 0–1 | Intense sightseeing |
| 7-night Mexican Riviera from Los Angeles | 3 | 3 | Laid back |
| Hawaii round trip from Vancouver | 4–5 island days | 8–10 in total | Long ocean stretches |
| Transatlantic crossing | 0–3 | 6 or more in a row | A floating holiday |

Alaska sailings from Seattle add a short stop in Victoria, often in the evening, because of US shipping rules. The Inside Passage cruising day still feels scenic, which is why many first-timers find Alaska's "sea days" anything but empty (see [what happens on a glacier day](/articles/alaska-cruise-glacier-day/)).

## How to read an itinerary before booking
1. Count actual docked or tendered port days, not the number of places listed.
2. Check arrival and departure times. A port from 7 am to 1 pm is half a day.
3. Note any tender ports, where getting ashore takes longer (see [cruise ship tenders explained](/articles/cruise-ship-tenders-explained/)).
4. Look for runs of three or more port days in a row.
5. Check where the sea days fall. One in the middle of the week is ideal.

## Which balance suits you?
**Port-heavy suits:** energetic travellers, first-time visitors to a region, people who see the ship as a moving hotel, and anyone on a Mediterranean bucket-list trip.

**Sea-day-rich suits:** couples wanting downtime, families using the kids club, people who've paid for a balcony or a spa package, and retirees who prefer a slower pace.

**A balance suits:** most first-timers. Two sea days in seven lets you recover from early starts and actually use the pools, shows and restaurants you've paid for.

> **Example:** a family from Winnipeg on a Western Caribbean week might plan a big excursion in Cozumel, a beach day in Roatán, and lazy mornings in the other ports. The two sea days become kids club and pool days, so nobody is exhausted by Friday.

## The money side
Port days cost more ashore. Excursions, taxis and lunch in town add up fast, and a port-heavy week can easily mean US$300–600 per person in tours (roughly C$400–850). Use [how many excursions to book per cruise](/articles/how-many-excursions-book-per-cruise/) to set a sensible number.

Sea days cost more on board. Drinks, the spa and specialty dining tempt you when there's nowhere else to be. Some ships discount spa treatments on port days, when it's quieter, so book a massage while others are ashore.

## The fatigue factor
Four or five port days in a row sounds wonderful when booking. In practice, early alarms, tender queues and long tour days wear people down. Many experienced cruisers skip one port on a busy itinerary and stay aboard; the ship is calm, the pools are empty, and the buffet has no queue.
