Leave the wallet in the safe. On a cruise ship, one card or wearable opens your cabin, gets you on and off the ship, and pays for everything from a latte to a spa treatment. Here's how it works and how to keep the final bill free of surprises.

## What your cruise card does
- **Cabin key:** tap or insert it at your door.
- **Boarding pass:** scanned at the gangway every time you leave or return, with your photo on file.
- **Payment:** bars, shops, specialty restaurants, photos and excursions all charge to it.
- **Information:** it may show your muster station, dining time or a drinks-package flag.

## Cards and wearables by line
Most lines hand you a plastic card at your cabin or at check-in. A few offer wearables that do the same job.

| Line | What you get | Wearable option |
|---|---|---|
| Royal Caribbean | SeaPass card | WOWband wristband, sold separately |
| Princess | OceanMedallion disc | It is the wearable, on a lanyard, clip or band |
| Disney | Key to the World card | DisneyBand+, sold separately |
| Carnival | Sail & Sign card | Check the app for digital options |
| Celebrity, Norwegian, Holland America, MSC | Cruise card | Some ships add phone or wearable options |

Features change ship by ship, so check your line's app before you sail. Many lines now let you use your phone as a digital key or to view your account. [Using the cruise line app](/articles/using-cruise-line-app-before-during-trip/) explains what to set up in advance.

## Setting up your account
1. **Link a credit card during online check-in,** or at the terminal. This is the card the final bill settles to.
2. **Or choose a cash deposit** at guest services if you'd rather not link a card. You top it up as you go, and anything unspent is refunded.
3. **Set spending limits for children and teens.** Zero is a valid limit.
4. **Check that onboard credit, prepaid gratuities and any package appear** on the account on day one.

Lines place holds on your card through the week, and the final charge goes through on the last night or morning. How those holds work, and why debit cards can tie up cash, is covered in [how onboard payments work](/articles/how-onboard-payments-work-cruise/).

> **Canadian tip:** onboard accounts settle in US dollars. A credit card with no foreign-transaction fee saves about 2.5% on everything you sign for. If you're offered your bill in Canadian dollars at the end, decline: that conversion rate is usually worse than your card's.

## Keeping the final bill boring
- Check the account in the app midweek. Errors are easier to fix on Wednesday than at 7 a.m. on disembarkation day.
- Remember that most bar and spa purchases add an automatic service charge of about 18–20%.
- Keep the card on a lanyard or in a card holder. Stripe cards can stop working if stored against a phone or magnetic clasp.
- Keep your photo ID with you in port too, alongside the card.

## Where onboard credit fits
Credit attached to your booking loads onto this same account and is used up first. It's also why two fares with the same price aren't equal: one carrying US$200 of credit has effectively prepaid a chunk of your extras. [Onboard credit explained](/articles/onboard-credit-explained/) covers the rules, including what happens to unused credit.

## Ten minutes that set up the week
Link a no-foreign-fee card, cap the kids, confirm your credits and download the app. That small job at check-in scripts the whole financial side of the cruise.
