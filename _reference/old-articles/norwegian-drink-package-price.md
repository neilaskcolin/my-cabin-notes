On Norwegian, the drink package question is usually not "should I buy it?" but "is the service charge worth it?". The Unlimited Open Bar package has a retail value of roughly US$100–120 per person per day at recent pricing, but most guests get it through Free at Sea and pay only the 20% service charge on that value.

## How much is Norwegian's Unlimited Open Bar package?
At retail, roughly US$100–120 per person per day, plus a 20% service charge, which puts the real figure near US$120–145. Buying before you sail is usually cheaper than on board.

Through Free at Sea, the package is included for the first two guests in the cabin, and you pay only the service charge on its value, about US$20–25 per person per day at recent rates. That charge is added to your booking, so it is easy to miss when comparing fares.

## What's included, and what isn't
- Cocktails, spirits, wine by the glass and beer, up to a per-drink price cap (around US$15 at recent policy)
- Sodas, juices and non-alcoholic drinks
- Not in the base package: specialty coffees and bottled water, which an upgraded tier has covered
- Not included: drinks above the cap (you pay the difference) and bottles of wine

### The both-adults rule
At retail, both adults in a cabin must buy the package. Through Free at Sea it applies automatically to the first two guests. Guests under 21 can't use it; a soda package is sold for them.

## The break-even maths
| Couple, 7 nights | Approx. USD | Roughly in CAD |
|---|---|---|
| Free at Sea service charge only | about $310 | C$415–430 |
| Package bought at retail | about $1,850 | C$2,495–2,590 |
| Pay as you go, 3 drinks each a day | about $705 | C$955–990 |

> **Quick math:** with a cocktail at about US$14 plus the 20% charge, each drink costs roughly US$16.80. At retail the package needs around eight drinks a day to pay off; through Free at Sea, about two.

These figures use recent typical prices and will vary by ship and sailing.

## Should you take the drink package on Norwegian?
1. Check whether your fare includes Free at Sea. If it does, the package is already in the bundle and the question is only whether the service charge is worth it.
2. Count honestly. Two drinks a day each, including a glass of wine at dinner, justifies the service charge.
3. If you are on a fare without the perk and drink lightly, pay as you go, or bring wine and pay corkage under the [Norwegian alcohol policy](/articles/norwegian-alcohol-policy/).
4. Only buy at retail if you drink steadily from lunch onward, every day.

> **Canadian tip:** on an Alaska sailing, glacier mornings are when everyone wants a latte or a hot chocolate with Baileys. Check whether specialty coffee is covered by your tier before you assume it is, and settle any extras with a card that has no foreign-transaction fee, since onboard charges post in US dollars.

## How Norwegian compares
| Line | Package | Recent price per person per day | Catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Norwegian** | Unlimited Open Bar | $100–120 USD retail | Usually via Free at Sea (pay the 20% charge) |
| [Royal Caribbean](/articles/royal-caribbean-drink-package-price/) | Deluxe Beverage | $70–105 USD + 18% | All adults in cabin must buy |
| [Carnival](/articles/carnival-drink-package-price/) | Cheers! | $70–75 USD + 20% | 15 alcoholic drinks a day |

On paper Norwegian's package is the priciest of the three, but almost nobody pays retail, because Free at Sea bundles it into so many fares. Royal Caribbean and Carnival sell theirs as straight add-ons, so their true cost is the sticker price plus a service charge of 18% on Royal Caribbean or 20% on Carnival.
