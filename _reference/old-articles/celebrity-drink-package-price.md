Celebrity's Classic drinks package has recently run about US$65–100 per person per day, roughly C$90–140, plus a 20% service charge. It pays off for steady drinkers. Most Celebrity guests never buy it on its own, though, because it comes bundled in the All Included fare.

## What do Celebrity drink packages cost?
Prices vary a lot by ship, sailing length and date, and pre-cruise sales in your Celebrity account are common.

| Package | Recent price per person per day | Per-drink cap | What it adds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classic | about US$65–100 | about US$12 | Beer, spirits, cocktails, wine by the glass, soda, premium coffee |
| Premium | about US$80–125 | about US$19 | Craft beer, premium spirits and wines, fresh juice, premium water |
| Zero Proof | about US$40–45 | none stated | Mocktails, specialty coffee, soda, juices, energy drinks |

All prices exclude the 20% service charge. Classic also takes 15% off bottles of wine, and Premium 20%. Edge-class ships tend to sit at the top of these ranges.

## How many drinks make it worth it?
Bought alone, Classic at US$85 a day becomes about US$102 once the service charge is added. With typical drinks at US$11–12 plus 20%, you need seven to eight drinks every day to come out ahead.

> **Quick math:** a Winnipeg couple on a 7-night Caribbean sailing buying Classic at US$85 a day pays 2 × 85 × 1.2 × 7, about US$1,430 or roughly C$1,930–2,000. Two coffees, a lunchtime beer, a sail-away cocktail, two glasses of wine at dinner and a nightcap is seven drinks each. Drop two of those and pay-as-you-go wins.

## All Included vs buying the package
Celebrity's All Included fare bundles the Classic package and Basic wifi, with the drinks service charge built in at recent terms. Daily gratuities were removed from it in late 2023, so budget for those separately. Suites in The Retreat come with the Premium package and Premium wifi instead.

The step up from cruise-only to All Included has often priced at around US$90 per person per night, though it varies by sailing. Basic wifi isn't sold on its own, and Premium wifi costs about US$27–35 a day per device, so for anyone who wants both drinks and wifi, All Included usually beats buying them separately. [What all-inclusive really means on a cruise](/articles/what-does-all-inclusive-mean-cruise/) explains the wider picture.

### The rules that catch people out
- Every adult in the stateroom must have the same package.
- The drinking age is 21 on North American sailings, Alaska from Vancouver included, even for guests who can drink legally at home in their province.
- Drinks are served one at a time, so you can't buy a round for friends on your package.
- Minibar items and room service aren't covered.

## Deciding in five steps
1. Check whether your sailing offers All Included and what it adds per person per day over cruise-only.
2. Count your honest daily drinks, coffees included, for each adult.
3. If both of you reach seven or more, take All Included or the package; if not, pay as you go.
4. On cruise-only, lighter drinkers can stretch further with the two-bottle wine allowance in the [Celebrity alcohol policy](/articles/celebrity-alcohol-policy/).
5. Watch your Celebrity account for pre-cruise package sales before you sail.

Princess and Royal Caribbean are the lines most often weighed against Celebrity. Celebrity's standalone Classic package is in the same range as Royal Caribbean's Deluxe but carries a higher service charge, so for most Celebrity guests the real comparison is All Included against Princess Plus. ## Getting the package bundled instead
Celebrity regularly runs promotions on All Included fares. For a couple who would buy drinks anyway that can be worth hundreds of dollars, so check your dates before paying for a package separately.
