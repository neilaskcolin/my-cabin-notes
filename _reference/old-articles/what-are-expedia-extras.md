Expedia Extras are perks added to selected cruise bookings made through Expedia Cruises, on top of the cruise line's own fare. They do not lower the ticket price; they add things you would otherwise pay for, such as onboard credit or gratuities.

## How the extras work
Large cruise retailers negotiate with lines at volume. That can mean blocks of cabins at group rates, promotional allocations and value-adds funded jointly with the line. Expedia Cruises packages these as "Extras" on qualifying sailings.

The fare itself is the same as the line's public price for that cabin and date. What changes is what comes attached to it.

## Common types of extra and how to value them
| Type of extra | What it does | How to value it |
|---|---|---|
| Onboard credit | Pre-loads money on your ship account | At what you would spend anyway, in US dollars |
| Prepaid gratuities | Covers the daily service charge | Easy to value: often US$16–20 per person per day |
| Drink or wifi package | Includes a package you might buy | Only at what you would actually use |
| Negotiated cabin rates | Access to cabins at group pricing | Compare the total with the line's public fare |
| Upgrades or bonus perks | Varies by promotion | Value only if you'd pay for it |

On a typical 7-night booking for two, attached extras commonly add up to something in the range of US$100–500 of value, depending on the sailing. How [onboard credit works](/articles/onboard-credit-explained/), including what it can and can't pay for, is worth reading before you count it as savings.

> **Quick math:** prepaid gratuities for two on a 7-night cruise at US$18 per person per day are worth US$252, or roughly C$345. Because tips are charged in US dollars on most ships, having them covered also removes part of your exchange-rate exposure.

## The catches, honestly
- Extras attach to selected sailings, not every departure, and the mix changes often.
- Negotiated cabin blocks are limited and tied to specific dates.
- Some cruise line promotions can't be combined with agency-funded perks. When that happens, take whichever package is worth more in total.
- Onboard credit is usually non-refundable, so unused credit is lost at the end of the cruise.

None of this is hidden; it just means checking your actual dates rather than assuming every booking carries the same perks. If you are choosing between credit and a lower price, our page on [onboard credit versus a cash discount](/articles/cruise-onboard-credit-vs-cash-discount/) helps decide.

## Questions to ask before you book
1. Which departures near my dates carry extras, and what are they?
2. Is the onboard credit per cabin or per person, and is it refundable?
3. Do the extras stack with the line's current promotion, or replace it?
4. If the fare drops before final payment, do the extras stay when the booking is repriced?
5. What is the total value of each option, side by side?

> **Canadian tip:** most extras are denominated in US dollars, so compare them with CAD quotes at about 1.35–1.40. A US$200 credit is worth roughly C$270–280 if you spend it all.

## How to compare fairly
Price the same cabin category on the same sailing both ways, then add the value of any extras you would genuinely use. If the totals are close, choose the option with the better cancellation terms and service, since that matters more than a small perk.
