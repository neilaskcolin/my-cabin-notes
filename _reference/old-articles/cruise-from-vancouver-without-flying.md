Yes, a flight-free cruise holiday from Alberta is entirely doable. The trick is choosing a sailing that starts and ends in Vancouver, then picking the ground route that suits your time and budget: the car, VIA Rail, the Rocky Mountaineer or a coach.

## Step one: choose a sailing that brings you back
The itinerary does most of the work. If the ship returns to Canada Place, your journey home mirrors the journey out.

- **7-night Alaska round trips.** The core no-fly option, sailing weekly from about May to September.
- **Longer Alaska round trips.** Some lines run 10- to 14-night sailings from Vancouver that reach farther north.
- **Pacific coastal repositionings.** In spring and fall, ships move between Vancouver and California. You can come home without flying by Amtrak's Coast Starlight from Los Angeles to Seattle, then the train or a coach to Vancouver, though it takes about two days.
- **Hawaii.** Most Hawaii sailings from Vancouver are one-way and end in Honolulu, which needs a flight. See [Hawaii cruises from Vancouver](/articles/hawaii-cruises-from-vancouver/).

Avoid one-way Alaska sailings ending in Whittier or Seward. Getting home from there without a plane means thousands of kilometres of driving. [Round-trip vs one-way Alaska](/articles/roundtrip-vs-oneway-alaska/) explains the difference.

## Step two: get to the coast
| Option | Starts from | Time to Vancouver | Best for | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Car | Anywhere | About 10–11 hours from Calgary, 12 from Edmonton | Families, flexibility, BC stops | Parking for the cruise, mountain passes in shoulder season |
| VIA Rail's Canadian | Edmonton, Jasper, Saskatoon, Winnipeg | Roughly a day from Edmonton | Sleeper cabins, scenery, no driving | Runs only a couple of times a week and often late |
| Rocky Mountaineer | Banff, Lake Louise or Jasper | Two daylight days, overnight in Kamloops | A trip of a lifetime | Price; runs roughly April to October |
| Coach | Calgary, Edmonton | A long single day | Lowest cost | Changing schedules, luggage limits |

The Canadian arrives at Pacific Central Station, a few SkyTrain stops from Waterfront Station beside the cruise terminal. Rocky Mountaineer trains reach Vancouver at the end of the second day, so plan a hotel night before your ship.

For the car, the route, stops and parking maths are covered in [driving from Calgary to an Alaska cruise](/articles/drive-from-calgary-to-alaska-cruise/), and cheaper alternatives to terminal parking are in [Vancouver cruise ship parking](/articles/vancouver-cruise-terminal-parking/). Edmontonians comparing every option by price should read [the cheapest way from Edmonton to the cruise port](/articles/edmonton-to-vancouver-cruise-port-cheapest/).

> **Good to know:** freight trains have priority on most of Canada's rail network, so the Canadian can arrive hours behind schedule. Treat its timetable as a hope, and plan to reach Vancouver two days before sailing if you take it.

## The day-before rule matters even more
Fly-cruisers can be rebooked when something breaks. Ground travellers can't. Highway 1 and the Coquihalla close occasionally for crashes, avalanche control or wildfire smoke, and trains run late. Whatever you choose, sleep in Vancouver the night before you sail; [where to stay near the terminal](/articles/where-to-stay-vancouver-night-before-cruise/) has options.

## Who a no-fly cruise suits
It works best for people with time: retirees, families who enjoy road trips, rail fans, and anyone with a medical reason not to fly. It suits short holidays less well, since a round trip by land adds two to four days to a 7-night sailing. Budget-wise, the car usually beats the train, and the coach beats both.

Saskatchewan and Manitoba travellers can use the same approach: the Canadian stops in Saskatoon and Winnipeg on its way west.

## Building the whole no-fly package
Rail-and-sail packages pairing the Rocky Mountaineer with an Alaska cruise are sold as single products, and pre-cruise hotels, parking and transfers can often be bundled with the fare. No-fly trips have more moving parts than a fly-cruise, so line up the dates for every leg before you pay for any one of them.
