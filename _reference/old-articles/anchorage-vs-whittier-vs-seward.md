If your one-way Alaska cruise "ends in Anchorage", it really ends in Whittier or Seward, followed by a bus or train to the city. Knowing which port, and how long the transfer takes, is the key to booking a flight home that you will actually catch.

## The three places at a glance
| | Whittier | Seward | Anchorage |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | Small port town on Prince William Sound | Harbour town on Resurrection Bay | Alaska's largest city and main airport |
| Role on your cruise | Where some lines dock | Where other lines dock | Where you fly in or out |
| Road distance to Anchorage airport | about 95 km | about 200 km | n/a |
| Drive time | about 1.5 hours | about 2.5 hours | n/a |
| By train | Scenic rail, roughly 2.5 hours | Scenic rail, roughly 4 hours | n/a |
| Worth a night? | Rarely | Yes, for Kenai Fjords | Yes, before or after |

## Whittier: the short transfer
Whittier is tiny, and famously, most of its residents live in a single high-rise building. The only road in runs through the Anton Anderson Memorial Tunnel, about 4 km long, which is single-lane and shared with the railway. Traffic takes turns in each direction on a schedule, and the tunnel closes overnight. Coaches and trains are timed around it, but it is one reason not to cut transfers fine.

The reward is the shortest trip to Anchorage. Ships here often visit College Fjord, with its string of tidewater glaciers, on the way in or out.

## Seward: the scenic transfer
Seward's transfer is longer, but the Seward Highway is one of Alaska's great drives, running beside Turnagain Arm with mountains on one side and tidal flats on the other. Watch for Dall sheep on the cliffs and, sometimes, beluga whales in the water.

Seward is also worth a night. Kenai Fjords National Park boat tours leave from the harbour, Exit Glacier is a short drive away, and the Alaska SeaLife Center is in town.

## Anchorage: the airport hub
Anchorage is where you fly in or out. Many travellers spend a night here, especially before a southbound cruise, to avoid a same-day flight-and-transfer scramble. It is also the starting point for most Denali land tours; see [Alaska cruisetours explained](/articles/alaska-cruisetours-denali-rail/).

## Disembarkation day, step by step
A typical northbound finish runs roughly like this:

1. **Early morning:** the ship docks in Whittier or Seward.
2. **Breakfast to mid-morning:** you leave the ship in your assigned group, with bags collected on the pier or checked through to the transfer.
3. **Transfer:** coach or train to Anchorage, about 1.5 to 4 hours depending on port and mode.
4. **Airport or hotel:** arrive late morning or afternoon.
5. **Flight home:** usually connecting via Seattle or Vancouver to cities like Calgary, Edmonton or Toronto.

> **Watch out:** a morning flight out of Anchorage on disembarkation day is the most common mistake on one-way Alaska trips. Follow your line's earliest-flight guidance, and if your best flight leaves before then, book a night in Anchorage instead.

For general disembarkation tips, see [what happens on disembarkation morning](/articles/disembarkation-morning-getting-off-ship/).

## Which port will your cruise use?
You don't choose: each line generally uses one port for its one-way Gulf of Alaska sailings. Check your cruise documents. Transfers can be bought from the cruise line, which ties into your luggage handling, or arranged independently, which can be cheaper from Seward but leaves you to manage timing. [Cruise ship transfers explained](/articles/cruise-ship-transfers-explained/) covers the trade-off.

## Getting home to Canada
You clear CBSA at your first Canadian airport. After 48 hours or more outside Canada, each person can bring back up to C$800 in goods duty-free, with set limits on alcohol and tobacco. Southbound sailings flip all of this around, starting in Alaska and ending at Canada Place; see [northbound vs southbound](/articles/alaska-cruise-northern-vs-southbound-embarkation/).

## Confirming your routing
Before booking flights, confirm three things: which port your ship uses, how the transfer works, and the earliest flight time your line recommends. With those settled, the last day of a one-way Alaska cruise becomes part of the scenery rather than a race.
