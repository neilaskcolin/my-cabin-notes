Norwegian's final payment is due about 120 days before departure on most sailings at recent policy. That is a month or more earlier than many lines, and it is the date that turns a refundable deposit into money at risk.

## When is Norwegian's final payment due?
For most cruises, roughly 120 days out. Some short sailings and specific promotions use different dates, so the final payment date printed on your confirmation is the one to trust. Put it in your calendar the day you book, as covered in [cruise deposits and final payment dates explained](/articles/cruise-deposits-final-payment/).

> **Example:** a Calgary couple booking a Seattle–Alaska sailing for 20 June 2027 would owe the balance around 20 February 2027. A March Break Caribbean cruise from Miami in mid-March 2027 would need paying off around mid-November 2026, before the holiday spending starts.

If the balance isn't paid by the deadline, the booking can be cancelled and the deposit kept, so don't rely on a reminder email.

## What you lose if you cancel an NCL cruise
| When you cancel | What you typically lose (standard fare) |
|---|---|
| Before final payment | Nothing; the deposit is refunded |
| Just after final payment | A portion of the fare |
| As sailing gets closer | A rising share, in set bands |
| Final weeks before sailing | Up to 100% of the fare |

The exact bands depend on cruise length and cabin type, and they are set out in your booking terms. Read them once now rather than when you need them.

### Haven suites, holiday sailings and special voyages
The Haven, Norwegian's keycard-access suite complex, has historically carried tighter terms, with deposits that can be non-refundable from booking and penalties that step up sooner. Christmas and New Year sailings have worked the same way. If you are booking either, ask for those terms in writing.

## Refundable vs non-refundable deposit fares
Norwegian sells both side by side. The non-refundable deposit fare is cheaper, but if you cancel before final payment you lose the deposit (on some fares you may be offered a future cruise credit instead). [Cruise fare classes explained](/articles/cruise-fare-classes-refundable/) covers how to weigh the saving against the risk.

## What happens to prepaid extras and Free at Sea?
Prepaid items, including the daily service charge and the service charges on Free at Sea perks (covered in [Norwegian Cruise Line gratuities](/articles/norwegian-gratuities-per-day/)), are generally refunded on cancellation, subject to the same schedule as the fare. The perks themselves have no cash value, so there is nothing to claim back for an unused drinks package. Flights booked through Norwegian can carry their own change rules, so ask about those separately.

## Cancelling one guest, not the whole cabin
Taking one of two guests off a booking after final payment can leave the remaining guest paying a single supplement. On Norwegian it can also change the perks, because Free at Sea applies to the first two guests in a cabin. Ask for a fresh quote before making a partial cancellation.

## Protecting a Norwegian booking from Canada
- Buy trip cancellation insurance soon after paying the deposit; many Canadian policies give full benefits only if bought within a set window.
- Your provincial health plan covers no cancellation costs and very little medical care at sea.
- A cancel-for-any-reason add-on is the only cover for a change of heart; see [cancel for any reason insurance explained](/articles/cancel-for-any-reason-cruise-insurance-explained/).
- Compare Norwegian's own protection plan with a Canadian policy using [travel insurance vs cruise line protection](/articles/travel-insurance-vs-cruise-protection/).

> **Watch out:** if Norwegian cancels or significantly changes your sailing, different rules apply, usually a refund or credit. That is covered in [what happens if the cruise line cancels](/articles/what-happens-cruise-cancelled-by-line/).

## Not getting caught by the early deadline
Write down the 120-day date the day you book, and set a reminder a couple of weeks before it. That gap is your last easy chance to reprice, change cabin or walk away with your deposit, and Norwegian simply reaches it sooner than the lines you may have sailed before.
