No, not the fare itself. Once you have paid a deposit, your cruise fare is locked even if the line raises prices for the same sailing the next day. A few things around the fare can still move, though, and it helps to know which.

## What is locked and what can still change
| Part of your cruise cost | Can it go up after booking? | How to protect yourself |
|---|---|---|
| Cruise fare | No, once the deposit is paid | Nothing needed |
| Government taxes, fees and port charges | Yes, contracts usually allow increases to be passed on | Changes are normally small; budget a little slack |
| Fuel supplement | Only if your contract has the clause and the line invokes it | Rare in recent years; see below |
| Daily gratuities | Yes, if not prepaid | Prepay before any announced increase |
| Drink packages, wifi, dining, excursions | Yes, until you buy them | Prebook items you are sure about |
| CAD cost of a USD booking | Yes, as the exchange rate moves | Book in CAD where offered |
| Your fare after you change the booking | Yes, it reprices at today's rate | Think before switching ship, date or cabin |

## Why your fare is protected
When you book, you accept a contract at a stated price, and the line cannot later ask for more because demand rose. That one-way protection is a big reason early booking works. If prices drop instead, many lines and agents will reprice your booking before final payment, as explained in [price drops after booking](/articles/price-drop-after-booking-cruise/).

The protection starts with the deposit. A quote, a shopping basket or a short courtesy hold is not a booking, and the fare can change until you have paid.

## The small print that can move
**Taxes and port fees.** These are set by governments and ports, and most contracts let the line pass on increases. On a typical sailing any change is modest, but it can appear on your final invoice. [Taxes and port fees explained](/articles/taxes-port-fees-cruise-fare-breakdown/) shows what they cover.

**Fuel supplements.** Some contracts still reserve the right to add one if oil prices spike above a set level. It has rarely been used in recent years. [Cruise fuel surcharges](/articles/cruise-fuel-surcharges-explained/) explains how the clause works.

**Obvious pricing errors.** Most terms let a line correct a clearly mistaken price, such as a balcony advertised at a fraction of its normal cost.

## Things you might do that reprice your booking
- **Changing ship, date or cabin category** usually means paying the current fare, not your old one.
- **Adding a guest** prices that person at today's rate.
- **Switching to a new promotion** can reprice the whole booking, and you may lose perks attached to the old one.
- **Moving a non-refundable fare** can add change fees on top. See [refundable vs non-refundable fares](/articles/cruise-fare-classes-refundable/).

> **Canadian tip:** if your booking is in US dollars, the fare is fixed in USD but the Canadian-dollar cost of your final payment is not. A drop of a few cents in the loonie between deposit and final payment can add C$100 or more to a typical balcony booking. [Paying in CAD vs USD](/articles/paying-cad-vs-usd-cruise/) explains your options.

## The practical takeaway
Book on a refundable fare, prepay gratuities if your line allows it, and lock in any extras you know you want. Then keep an eye on the price until final payment, so a rise costs you nothing and a fall can work in your favour.
