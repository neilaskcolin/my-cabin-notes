The best anniversary cruise is one built around the two of you: an itinerary that suits how you like to travel, a cabin you will actually enjoy, and one or two moments planned for the day itself. Tell the line it's your anniversary at booking, and book the important dinner before you sail.

## Which itineraries feel most romantic?

| Itinerary | Best months | Why couples choose it |
|---|---|---|
| Mediterranean | May–June, September–October | Sunset sail-aways, Greek islands, long dinners |
| Alaska from Vancouver | May–September | Shared balcony, glaciers, quiet mornings |
| Norwegian fjords | June–August | Long evenings of light, dramatic scenery |
| Panama Canal | December–April | A once-in-a-lifetime day for a once-in-decades date |
| Danube river cruise | April–October, December markets | Budapest lit up at night, small ship, Vienna evenings |
| Caribbean with sea days | January–April | Warmth, rest and very little to plan |
| Tahiti and French Polynesia | May–October | Lagoons and small ships, for the very big ones |

The Mediterranean is the classic, and [the best Mediterranean itinerary for couples](/articles/mediterranean-cruise-best-itinerary-couples/) helps you choose a route. Alaska suits couples whose idea of romance is a blanket on the balcony and a glacier in the distance.

> **Canadian tip:** an anniversary in January or February is a natural excuse for the Caribbean or Panama Canal. A summer date points to Alaska or Europe. Avoid Canadian school holidays if you want a calmer ship.

## Choosing the cabin
For a milestone, the balcony is the upgrade most couples are glad they paid for. It is where the private moments happen: coffee at sunrise, a drink before dinner, scenery without the crowd on deck.

- **Aft-facing balconies** give a view of the ship's wake and are often a little more private.
- **Midship balconies** are steadier and usually quieter.
- **Suites** add perks on some lines, such as a private restaurant or priority boarding, which may justify the price for a 40th or 50th.

For Alaska, [the best cabins for Alaska scenery](/articles/best-cabins-alaska-cruise-scenery/) explains which side and deck work best.

## Making the day itself special
Tell the line at booking, and confirm it at guest services on day one. Then plan your own moments rather than relying on the ship.

### Worth paying for
- A specialty restaurant or chef's table on the night itself, booked before you board.
- A private balcony dinner, where offered. Princess, for example, has long sold one.
- A couples treatment in the spa, booked for a sea day. See [couples spa experiences at sea](/articles/couples-spa-experiences-cruise/).
- A short photo session on a dressy night, if you want professional pictures together.

### Often skippable
- Generic decoration packages that mostly add balloons and banners.
- Bundles that include things you would never use, such as a group photo package.

If you want to say your vows again, see [renewing your vows at sea](/articles/renewing-vows-at-sea/).

## A sample anniversary week
Here is how a couple from Edmonton might build a 30th anniversary into a 7-night Alaska sailing from Vancouver:

1. **Embarkation day.** Champagne on deck as the ship passes under the Lions Gate Bridge.
2. **Scenic cruising day.** Breakfast on the balcony through the Inside Passage.
3. **Juneau.** A small-boat whale-watching trip together.
4. **Glacier day.** Blankets and hot chocolate on the balcony, then a specialty dinner that evening, booked weeks earlier for the anniversary date.
5. **Sea day.** A couples massage and a quiet afternoon.
6. **Final night.** A short photo session on the dressy evening.

## Timing it well
Sailing on the exact date matters to some couples. For others, sailing in the right season matters more. Shoulder months often mean softer fares and calmer ships, which can fund the balcony upgrade.
