Yes, often, if the price drops before final payment. Most lines will move your booking to the lower fare when you ask, keeping your cabin. After final payment the door mostly closes, and the best you can usually hope for is goodwill.

## What lines typically do, and when
Final payment is usually due somewhere between about 75 and 120 days before sailing, earlier for long voyages and holiday sailings. That date divides two very different situations.

| When the price drops | What you can usually get | How likely |
|---|---|---|
| Before final payment, same fare type | The new lower fare or better promotion, same cabin | Likely, if you ask |
| Before final payment, new-bookings-only offer | Often nothing, as some promotions exclude existing bookings | Varies |
| After final payment | Onboard credit or a small upgrade as goodwill | Occasional |
| After final payment, cash refund | The difference back to your card | Rare |

Policies differ by line and change, so check the line-by-line pages below before you count on anything.

## The totals trap
Repricing can mean moving onto a different fare, and extras attached to your original booking may not come with you. Taking US$200 off but losing US$350 of onboard credit and prepaid gratuities leaves you worse off.

> **Example:** an Edmonton couple booked a balcony at US$1,499 each with US$150 of onboard credit. The fare drops to US$1,349 with no credit attached. Repricing saves US$300 on fare but loses US$150 of credit, a net gain of US$150, or roughly C$205. Worth doing, but not the C$410 it first looks like.

## How to ask for a price adjustment
1. Find the lower fare for your exact ship, date and cabin category.
2. Take a screenshot showing the date, price and what is included.
3. Contact whoever booked you, your agent or the line, the same day.
4. Ask them to compare the full package, old against new, before switching.
5. Get the adjusted price confirmed in writing or on a new invoice.

Our guide to [asking for cruise price protection](/articles/cruise-price-protection-asking-for-it/) includes what to say. To catch drops without checking every day, set up [cruise price alerts](/articles/how-to-set-cruise-price-alerts/).

> **Canadian tip:** compare in the currency you booked in. If you paid in CAD, a drop on the line's US site may not have reached the Canadian price yet, and a CAD drop can come from an exchange rate update rather than a sale.

## After final payment: goodwill only
Once you have paid in full, the contract favours the line and cash refunds essentially stop. Some lines will offer onboard credit or a category upgrade for part of the difference. One polite request is worth making; arguing rarely helps. This is also why a refundable fare and good insurance matter more than repricing in the final months, as explained in [cruise fare classes and refundability](/articles/cruise-fare-classes-refundable/).

## Keep watching until final payment
Fares move most in the months between booking and final payment, so check your sailing every week or two until then, or have whoever holds your booking watch it for you. Make a note of your final payment date the day you book.
