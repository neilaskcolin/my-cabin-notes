Use the first hour for three things: safety, food and bookings. Get those done while the ship is still half empty and the rest of embarkation day can be completely unhurried.

## Your first 60 minutes, step by step
Timings are a guide, and the order matters more than the minutes.

1. **Minutes 0–5: note your muster station.** As your card is scanned at the top of the gangway, check the station letter or number printed on it or shown in the [cruise line app](/articles/using-cruise-line-app-before-during-trip/).
2. **Minutes 5–15: do the safety check-in.** On most big lines you watch a short video in the app, then walk to your station to be scanned. It's quick when the ship is quiet and slow just before sailing. [What happens at the muster drill](/articles/cruise-ship-safety-drill-what-happens/) explains the process.
3. **Minutes 15–30: eat.** The buffet is the obvious choice and the busiest. On some ships the main dining room or a smaller café also serves lunch on boarding day, with far shorter lines. [How the different venues work](/articles/main-dining-buffet-specialty-restaurants/) helps you choose.
4. **Minutes 30–40: book what sells out.** Specialty dinners, headline shows, spa slots and kids club registration fill fast on popular sailings. Some lines run embarkation-day discounts on specialty dining and spa treatments, so ask.
5. **Minutes 40–50: sort your phone.** Switch to airplane mode or turn off data roaming before the ship leaves port, then buy or activate wifi if you need it. Canadian plans can charge heavily on the ship's maritime network (see [cruise wifi and roaming for Canadians](/articles/cruise-wifi-phone-roaming-canadians/)).
6. **Minutes 50–60: walk the ship.** Find guest services, the medical centre, the forward and aft elevators and your cabin's stairwell. [Finding your way on a big ship](/articles/navigating-large-cruise-ship-without-getting-lost/) has tricks for remembering which end is which.

> **Example:** a Winnipeg family boarding at Canada Place at 12:30 could be cleared for muster by 12:45, eating by 1:00 and holding a kids club registration and a specialty dinner for night three by 1:30, all before their cabin opens.

### If your cabin is already open
Drop your carry-on and spend five minutes checking it over. Make sure the beds are set up the way you booked (two twins or one queen), the safe locks, the thermostat responds and nothing is broken. Report problems now, while the cabin steward has time to fix them before the evening.

## What not to do in hour one
- Don't wait in the corridor for your checked bags. They can take until early evening.
- Don't queue at guest services for something the app handles, such as adding a payment card or checking dining times.
- Don't leave the muster check-in until the last half hour, when every station has a line.

## Does the port change the plan?
Not much. Alaska sailings from Vancouver usually leave in the late afternoon, and US preclearance happens in the terminal, so once you are aboard you can relax. Florida terminals tend to give each passenger an arrival window, so boarding feels more staggered, and the pool deck fills quickly on warm days. The terminal side of a Vancouver departure is covered in the [Canada Place embarkation walkthrough](/articles/canada-place-embarkation-walkthrough/).

## After the first hour
With muster done and bookings made, the rest of the day is yours: explore, nap or find a spot for the sail-away.
