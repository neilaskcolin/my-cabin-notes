The cruise line app is your boarding pass, daily newspaper, map and bill in one place. Set it up at home a few weeks before you sail, and embarkation day goes far more smoothly.

## Before you sail: set it up in order
1. **Download the app at home** on wifi, for every adult in your cabin.
2. **Create an account and link your booking** with your reservation number and the lead passenger's details.
3. **Complete online check-in** when it opens, often 60–90 days before sailing. You'll enter passport details, emergency contacts and a credit card for onboard charges (see [cruise check-in and documents](/articles/cruise-check-in-online-documents/)).
4. **Upload your photo** if the line asks for one, which speeds up boarding.
5. **Pick a boarding time** if your line uses arrival slots.
6. **Book what sells out:** excursions, specialty dining, shows and spa slots.
7. **Save or screenshot your boarding pass**, and print a backup in case your phone dies.

> **Canadian tip:** prices in the app are in US dollars, and the card you link becomes your onboard account. Use a card with no foreign transaction fee, and check whether any pre-cruise purchases are refundable before you buy. [How cruise cards work on board](/articles/cruise-cards-how-you-pay-onboard/) explains holds and deposits.

## On board: what the app does
Once you're on the ship, the app becomes your main tool:

- **Daily program:** shows, talks, trivia, deck parties and opening hours.
- **Deck maps:** some ships show where you are, which helps a lot on a big ship (see [finding your way on a large ship](/articles/navigating-large-cruise-ship-without-getting-lost/)).
- **Reservations:** dining times, shows and excursions, with reminders.
- **Onboard account:** every charge, in close to real time.
- **Chat:** messaging within your group. Free on some lines, a small fee per device on others.
- **Muster drill:** on many lines you watch the safety video in the app before checking in at your station.

### Getting connected without paying for internet
Put your phone in airplane mode before the ship leaves port, then turn wifi on and join the ship's network. On most big lines, the app works on that network free, even without an internet package. Keeping data roaming off avoids expensive charges at sea, as explained in [cruise wifi and roaming for Canadians](/articles/cruise-wifi-phone-roaming-canadians/).

If you want full internet, packages are sold per device per day; [how cruise ship internet works](/articles/cruise-ship-internet-how-it-works/) covers speeds and prices.

## Tips that save hassle
- Turn on notifications so you get reminders for dinner and excursion meeting times.
- Check your bill every day or two, and query anything odd early.
- On the last night, use the app to review your final statement and any self-help disembarkation options.
- Older family members who don't use smartphones can rely on the printed daily program, which many ships still deliver or have at guest services.

## When the app lets you down
Apps can be slow on embarkation day, when thousands of people log in at once. Keep screenshots of your boarding pass and booking confirmations, and don't worry if something won't load at the terminal; staff can check you in without it.
