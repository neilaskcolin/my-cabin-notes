A cruise line protection plan and a travel insurance policy look like the same thing at checkout, but they protect different things. The line's plan is strongest on flexible cancellation. A Canadian travel insurance policy is strongest on medical emergencies, which is where the biggest bills come from.

## Side by side
| | Cruise line protection plan | Canadian travel insurance policy |
|---|---|---|
| Main strength | Flexible cancellation, sometimes for any reason | High medical and evacuation limits |
| Cancel for any reason | Often available, usually refunded as future cruise credit | Limited, offered by only some insurers with conditions |
| Medical limit | Often modest | Commonly C$1 million to C$10 million or more |
| Evacuation from a ship | May be capped | Usually included up to the medical limit |
| Covers flights and hotels you booked yourself | Often not, or only partly | Yes, if included in your trip cost |
| Pre-existing conditions | Varies | Covered if stable under the policy's rules |
| Refund form | Often future cruise credit | Usually cash |

Benefits differ widely between products, so read the actual policy wording, not the sales summary. Plans sold to Canadians can also differ from the US versions you see online.

## What a line's protection plan does well
Its real value is flexibility. Cancel for a covered reason and you typically get money back; on many plans, cancel for any other reason and you get most of your cost back as credit towards a future cruise. That kind of change-of-mind cover is hard to buy elsewhere in Canada.

### The future cruise credit catch
A refund in [future cruise credit](/articles/future-cruise-credit-explained/) has an expiry date and conditions. It is fine if you will definitely cruise again with that line, but poor compensation if illness or a change in circumstances ends your cruising plans. Check whether a refund is cash or credit before you buy.

## What a travel insurance policy does well
Its job is the worst case. A heart problem treated on board, a hospital stay in Juneau or Miami, or an evacuation from the ship by helicopter can run into tens of thousands of dollars or more. Ship medical centres charge you directly, usually in US dollars, as explained in [cruise ship medical care costs](/articles/cruise-medical-care-costs/).

A good policy also covers cancellation for defined reasons with cash refunds, missed connections, and trip interruption. Its weakness mirrors the line plan's strength: changing your mind is not a covered reason.

> **Canadian tip:** your provincial health plan pays very little outside Canada, and nothing towards an evacuation from a ship. Buy travel medical insurance for every cruise, even a short Alaska sailing from Vancouver, since every Alaska port is in the US.

## How experienced cruisers combine them
Many experienced Canadian cruisers:
- Buy a medical-heavy travel insurance policy at the time of deposit
- Book a refundable fare, so the fare itself provides much of the cancellation flexibility
- Add a line's protection plan only when the fare is non-refundable or their plans are genuinely uncertain
- Check whether their credit card's coverage has age limits, trip-length limits or low caps before relying on it

> **Watch out:** pre-existing condition rules are strict. Answer medical questionnaires accurately and check the stability period, or a claim can be refused. Older travellers should read [cruise insurance for seniors with pre-existing conditions](/articles/cruise-insurance-seniors-canada-pre-existing/).

## Which do you need?
Almost everyone needs travel medical insurance. Cancellation cover depends on how much you would lose if you could not go, and how much flexibility your fare already gives you. If you want the option to cancel for any reason, compare a line's plan against the [cancel for any reason insurance options](/articles/cancel-for-any-reason-cruise-insurance-explained/) available to Canadians, and see our full [travel insurance guide for Canadian cruisers](/articles/travel-insurance-cruise-canadians/).

## Matching cover to your booking
Five things decide the right setup: your ages, any health conditions, the countries on the itinerary, whether your fare is refundable, and how tight your flight connections are. Sort out insurance at the same time you pay the deposit, when cancellation cover gives you the most protection.
