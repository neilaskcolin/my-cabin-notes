Some onboard prices really are good, especially on spirits and tobacco. But "duty-free" only describes the sale on the ship. When you land back in Canada, the Canada Border Services Agency's personal exemption rules decide what you pay, and the alcohol limit is lower than most people think.

## What does "duty-free" on a ship really mean?
Once a ship is in international waters, its shops sell without local sales tax or import duty. That's a genuine saving on goods that are heavily taxed at home, which in Canada means alcohol and tobacco above all. On other goods, the advantage is smaller, and prices are in US dollars, so the exchange rate and any card foreign-transaction fee eat into it.

### Where the bargains usually are
- **Spirits and liqueurs:** often well below provincial liquor store prices
- **Cigarettes:** much cheaper than Canadian retail, but tightly limited at the border
- **Some fragrances and cosmetics:** occasionally cheaper, often similar to Canadian sale prices

### Where to be careful
Watches, jewellery and gemstones are hard to price-compare, and returns from a foreign store are difficult. Stores on the port shopping maps often have a commercial arrangement with the promotion, so "recommended" doesn't mean "cheapest".

## Your CBSA limits for a cruise
At recent CBSA rules, per person:

| Time out of Canada | Personal exemption | Alcohol and tobacco |
|---|---|---|
| Under 24 hours | None | None |
| 24 hours or more | Up to C$200 | Not included |
| 48 hours or more | Up to C$800 | Included, within limits |

The alcohol allowance is one of: 1.5 litres of wine (two standard bottles), 1.14 litres of spirits (one large bottle), or 8.5 litres of beer (about 24 cans). You must meet the drinking age of the province where you land. Tobacco limits are 200 cigarettes, 50 cigars or 200 g of tobacco. Exemptions can't be pooled between family members. Confirm the current figures on the [CBSA personal exemptions page](https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/travel-voyage/ifcrc-rpcrc-eng.html).

> **Watch out:** on the 24-hour exemption, if your goods total more than C$200 you can't claim it at all, and duty and taxes apply to the full amount, not just the excess. Over 48 hours, you pay only on the amount above C$800.

### The alcohol catch
A typical 7-night cruise gives you the C$800 exemption, but only one alcohol allowance. Buy two bottles of spirits and the second is over the limit, assessed with duty plus provincial fees. That can wipe out the saving.

> **Quick math:** a 1.14-litre bottle at US$30 on board is roughly C$41–42. If the same bottle is C$55–60 at your provincial liquor store, you save around C$15. A second bottle, assessed at the border with duty, taxes and fees, can cost more than buying it at home.

## How to shop smart and declare properly
1. **Check your exemption tier** before you buy anything, based on how long you'll be out of Canada.
2. **Keep every receipt** in one envelope, including port purchases.
3. **Track a running total in Canadian dollars** on your phone.
4. **Pack bought alcohol** on the final night when the ship delivers it, with checked luggage in mind if you're flying (see [luggage rules for fly-cruises](/articles/luggage-rules-flying-to-cruise/)).
5. **Declare everything honestly** at Canada Place or your arrival airport. Undeclared goods can be seized, and penalties are much worse than duty.

Goods made in the US or Mexico may be free of duty under trade agreements, but GST or HST still applies above your exemption.

## Paying for onboard shopping
Purchases go on your cruise account in US dollars. A card with no foreign-transaction fee saves about 2.5%, as explained in [how onboard payments work](/articles/how-onboard-payments-work-cruise/). For a wider spending plan, see [how much spending money to bring](/articles/how-much-spending-money-cruise/). Cannabis can't come aboard or cross borders on a cruise, even from Canada (see [why cannabis isn't allowed](/articles/cruise-cannabis-canada-why-no/)).

## Shopping smart within your limits
Buy the one bottle that's a real bargain, skip the hard-sell jewellery talk unless you know prices well, and keep receipts together. Know your C$200 or C$800 tier before you shop, and the border is a non-event.
