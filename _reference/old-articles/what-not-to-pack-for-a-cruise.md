The short version: nothing that heats, nothing that burns, nothing illegal, and nothing the ship already gives you. Every bag is X-rayed at the terminal, and banned items are pulled out before your luggage reaches the cabin.

## Items cruise lines ban
Exact lists differ by line, but these turn up on almost all of them:

| Item | Why it's banned | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Clothes irons and steamers | Fire risk in cabins | Use the ship's pressing service or self-serve laundry |
| Candles, incense, diffusers with flames | Open flame | Battery LED candles are usually fine |
| Kettles, coffee makers, hot plates | Heating elements | Coffee and tea are free in the buffet |
| Surge-protected power bars | Can interfere with the ship's electrics | A non-surge USB charging cube |
| Weapons, including large knives | Security | Leave at home |
| Hard liquor in your luggage | Line alcohol rules | Wine is often allowed in small amounts |
| Drones | Restricted or banned on board on most lines | Check the [drone rules](/articles/cruise-ship-drone-photography-rules/) for your line |

Extension cords are a grey area, with exceptions for medical equipment such as CPAP machines, covered in [can you bring an extension cord](/articles/extension-cord-cruise-ship-rules/). Alcohol allowances vary more than anything else on this list; compare them in [cruise line alcohol policies](/articles/cruise-line-alcohol-policies-compared/).

## Canadian items that cause trouble at the terminal
A few things that are normal in a Canadian suitcase are not welcome on a ship.

- **Cannabis.** Legal at home, but cruise lines ban it and it remains illegal under US federal law, even on Alaska sailings from Vancouver. The full explanation is in [why you can't bring cannabis on a cruise](/articles/cruise-cannabis-canada-why-no/).
- **Bear spray.** Albertans heading to Alaska sometimes pack it for hiking. It is banned on cruise ships and on Canadian flights, so buy it at a port outfitter if a guided hike requires it, or rely on the guide.
- **CBD products.** Many lines ban these too, regardless of THC content.

> **Canadian tip:** most ships sailing from North America have standard 110-volt North American outlets, so your Canadian plugs fit. Some European-built ships also have European sockets, which is handy but not something you need to pack for.

## Things you simply don't need
- **Beach and pool towels.** Ships provide them, including for shore days.
- **A hair dryer.** Every cabin has one, though it may be weak. Straighteners and curling irons are generally allowed.
- **Full-size toiletries.** Shampoo and body wash are supplied on most lines.
- **Lots of cash.** Purchases go on your cruise account, so you only need small bills for port tips and taxis (see [how much spending money to bring](/articles/how-much-spending-money-cruise/)).
- **Multiple formal outfits.** One or two dressier looks cover most sailings (see [formal nights explained](/articles/formal-nights-what-to-wear/)).
- **Snorkel gear for a single stop.** Rental is usually easy; [bring or rent snorkel gear](/articles/bringing-snorkel-gear-vs-renting-port/) weighs it up.
- **A week of clothes for a two-week trip.** Plan one load of [laundry on board](/articles/cruise-laundry-cost-alcohol-water/) instead.

## What should never go in checked luggage
Porters take your bags at the curb, and you may not see them until early evening. Keep passports, medication, valuables and chargers with you. Spare lithium batteries and power banks must be in your carry-on anyway if you are flying to the ship.

## How to check your line's current list
Search your line's website for "prohibited items" before you pack, and look again if your sailing is months away, since lists get updated. If something is borderline, such as a medical device or a travel kettle for a specific need, contact the line in advance rather than arguing at security.
