If your Holland America fare drops before final payment, you can generally ask to reprice to the lower advertised fare and keep your cabin. After final payment, the money stays with the line. The catch is comparing like with like, because a cheaper headline fare may come without perks your original booking included.

## Before final payment: repricing
A lower publicly advertised fare for the same ship, sailing and cabin category can generally be requested as a reprice. You keep your cabin number, and the difference comes off your balance or back to your card if you have paid more than the new total.

The request has to come through whoever holds the booking, whether that is Holland America directly or a travel advisor. Policies change, so confirm the current terms before relying on this.

### The window is getting shorter
Holland America has moved many newer bookings, and all longer voyages, to a final payment date 120 days before sailing. Many earlier bookings of 13 nights or less still use 90 days. That means the window for catching a drop closes about a month earlier than it used to on a lot of sailings. The [Holland America cancellation policy](/articles/holland-america-cancellation-policy-final-payment/) page sets out which schedule applies.

## After final payment
No refunds for later drops. Occasionally the line offers a paid upgrade at the fare difference, and very occasionally onboard credit. One polite ask is reasonable; repeated pushing is not. The wider picture is in [cruise price drop after final payment](/articles/price-drop-after-booking-cruise/).

## Have It All, Early Booking Bonus and cruise-only
Holland America sells many cabins two ways: cruise-only, and Have It All, which adds a drinks package, specialty dining, Surf wifi and shore excursion credit for a premium. Its Early Booking Bonus version has gone further, with upgraded drinks and wifi and prepaid crew appreciation.

Before repricing, check what the new fare actually includes:
- Is it the same fare type, or a cruise-only fare that drops Have It All?
- Would you lose Early Booking Bonus perks such as prepaid crew appreciation?
- Does the new promotion change the deposit to non-refundable?
- Is it priced in the same currency as your booking?

> **Example:** a Calgary couple books a 7-night Alaska verandah for C$4,800 in the fall. In February the same category shows C$4,300. If the old fare carried an Early Booking Bonus with prepaid crew appreciation worth about C$345 and the new one does not, the real saving is closer to C$155 than C$500.

[Cruise Line Sales Decoded](/articles/cruise-line-sales-decoded/) explains how to value bundled perks.

## When Holland America fares tend to move
Holland America's core products, Alaska and long voyages, price on a seasonal rhythm. Wave season, roughly January to March, brings the biggest promotions, and Alaska shoulder weeks in May and September often see later pushes. Peak July sailings rarely get cheaper close in. [Wave season explained](/articles/wave-season-explained-canada/) and [when Alaska cruise prices drop](/articles/when-do-alaska-cruise-prices-drop/) cover the timing.

The book-early-then-reprice approach in [booking 2027 sailings now vs waiting](/articles/booking-2027-now-vs-waiting/) suits the line well, as long as you hold a refundable deposit.

## How to ask for a Holland America reprice
1. Check the fare for your exact ship, sailing date and cabin category, not a similar one.
2. Note what the new fare includes and its deposit terms.
3. Compare totals in the same currency. Holland America quotes Canadian residents in CAD at its own exchange rate, so convert any US-dollar fare before treating it as lower.
4. Ask whoever holds the booking to reprice, and get the new total in writing.
5. Keep checking until final payment, using [cruise price alerts](/articles/how-to-set-cruise-price-alerts/).

## Keeping a Holland America booking watched
Promotion windows are easy to miss on a booking made a year ahead. Set a reminder for each wave-season sale and for a few weeks before final payment, and check your sailing each time.
