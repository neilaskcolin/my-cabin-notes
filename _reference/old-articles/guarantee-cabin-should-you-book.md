A guarantee cabin is a discount for letting the cruise line choose your room. You pick the category and pay a bit less; the line picks the deck and position. It can be a smart way to save, or a week above the nightclub, depending on who you are.

## How a guarantee booking works
1. You book a category, such as inside, oceanview or balcony, at the guarantee fare.
2. You get a booking confirmation with no cabin number.
3. The line assigns a cabin in that category or higher, whenever its inventory planning suits. That can be weeks before sailing or at check-in.
4. The cabin number appears in your booking or app. You generally cannot swap it for free.

The discount varies by sailing. Sometimes it is a modest saving per person, sometimes it is enough to pay for a shore excursion, and sometimes there is no gap at all.

> **Quick math:** say a 7-night Alaska balcony from Vancouver is US$1,850 per person to choose your own cabin and US$1,750 as a guarantee. A Calgary couple saves US$200, roughly C$270–280. That is a whale-watching trip in Juneau for one, or most of the gratuities.

## Will you really get an upgrade?
Occasionally. If the cheaper cabins sell out and higher categories have gaps, guarantee guests can be placed up a level. It happens often enough to fuel online stories, but not reliably enough to plan around.

The more likely result is the category you paid for in one of its less popular spots: far forward, right aft, under the pool deck, or near the theatre. Plenty of those cabins are perfectly fine. Some are not.

> **Watch out:** a balcony guarantee can be assigned any cabin within that category, and categories can include balconies with partly blocked views or smaller balcony sizes if the category description allows it. Read exactly what the category covers.

## Who should take the deal
- Flexible travellers who would rather spend the saving ashore
- Last-minute bookers, when guarantees may be most of what is left
- Inside cabin bookers, since location matters less without a view
- Anyone on a round-trip itinerary where both sides of the ship see the scenery

## Who should not
- Light sleepers and anyone prone to seasickness, who want a midship cabin on a lower deck (see [the quietest cabins and where to book](/articles/quietest-cabins-where-to-book/))
- Groups and families who want cabins together, since guarantees scatter people across the ship
- Balcony bookers on one-way scenic routes who care which side they face
- Anyone needing an accessible cabin, who should always book the specific cabin

## What to ask before you book
- How big is the price gap on this exact sailing, per person, with taxes?
- Which categories could I be placed in?
- Does the fare include perks, and do they apply to guarantee fares too?
- If I need to change later, can a guarantee booking be switched to a chosen cabin?

## The middle path
When the price gap is small, choosing your own cabin is cheap insurance against a noisy week. When the gap is large, the gamble can pay for an excursion. Check the spread on your actual sailing, and look at the deck plan for the category so you know the best and worst cabins you could get, using [how to read a deck plan](/articles/reading-cruise-ship-deck-plans/).
