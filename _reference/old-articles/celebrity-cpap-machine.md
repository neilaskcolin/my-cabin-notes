Yes, you can bring a CPAP on any Celebrity ship, from the newest Edge-class ships to the older Millennium class. The line provides an approved extension cord and distilled water on request. Your part is to tell its Access Department ahead of time and keep the machine in your hand luggage.

## Before you sail: the steps in order
1. Book the cabin, then contact Celebrity's Access Department, directly or through your travel agent, to note the CPAP on the reservation.
2. Ask for the medical extension cord, especially on a Solstice- or Millennium-class ship or if you don't know where the bedside outlets are.
3. Request distilled water and ask whether your ship charges for it.
4. Check your airline's medical-device rules for the flight to Vancouver, Seattle or Fort Lauderdale. Canadian airlines generally let a CPAP travel as an extra carry-on, but confirm for your fare.
5. Pack your prescription or a doctor's letter, which helps most on Celebrity's European and Asian itineraries with several customs checks.

## Why Celebrity supplies the extension cord
Celebrity, like sister line Royal Caribbean, bans guests' own extension cords and power bars as a fire risk. One packed in your bag is likely to be held at the terminal until the cruise ends, so use the approved cord instead. The general rules are in [extension cords on cruise ships](/articles/extension-cord-cruise-ship-rules/).

## Edge class vs older ships: where the power is
On Celebrity, which ship you are on makes a real difference to CPAP setup.

### Edge-class ships
Celebrity Edge, Apex, Beyond, Ascent and Xcel were designed with modern cabin electrics, including bedside outlets and USB charging in most staterooms. Many guests can plug in beside the bed without needing the cord at all.

### Solstice and Millennium classes
Solstice, Equinox, Eclipse, Silhouette and Reflection, and the smaller Millennium-class ships such as Summit, are older designs. Some cabins have few outlets, often at the desk rather than the bed, which is when the medical cord matters. Solstice and Summit both sail Alaska in 2026, so western Canadians meet this often.

Celebrity cabins have standard North American outlets, so a Canadian plug fits without an adapter.

> **Good to know:** distilled water is the piece most often forgotten. Bring a small bottle for the first night, since water ordered ahead isn't always in the cabin when you board. Medical liquids are generally allowed through terminal security in reasonable amounts. Each line's approach is in [distilled water for a CPAP on a cruise](/articles/cpap-distilled-water-cruise-ships/).

## What to put in your hand luggage
- The CPAP, mask, hose and power supply, never in checked bags. Luggage can reach your cabin late in the evening or, rarely, miss the ship, as covered in [lost or delayed luggage on a cruise](/articles/lost-delayed-luggage-cruise/).
- Spare filters and a spare mask cushion, since the ship's shops won't stock them.
- Your prescription or doctor's letter.
- Any CPAP battery, which has to travel in carry-on on your flight anyway.

> **Canadian tip:** provincial health plans pay little or nothing for care on board, and the ship's medical centre bills privately. Declare sleep apnea on your travel insurance application if the policy asks about it, so a related claim isn't refused. Typical costs are in [cruise medical care costs](/articles/cruise-medical-care-costs/).
