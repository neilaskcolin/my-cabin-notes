For the sailings most Canadians want in 2027, booking now on a refundable fare is the safer bet. You lock in the best cabin choice and today's price, and you can still walk away or reprice until final payment. Waiting only clearly pays off if your dates and cabin are flexible.

## What is actually on sale for 2027?
Most major lines have their 2027 Alaska, Caribbean and Europe sailings open, and some have started releasing 2028. For a July 2027 sailing, you are about nine months out today, which is roughly the point where the most popular cabins on peak sailings start getting scarce.

## The ledger: book now or wait
| | Book now (refundable fare) | Wait |
|---|---|---|
| Cabin choice | Full: midship balconies, connecting and accessible cabins still available | Whatever is left |
| Price | Locked at today's fare | Could fall on soft sailings, often rises on popular ones |
| If the fare drops | Ask to reprice before final payment | You get the lower fare |
| If the fare rises | Not your problem | You pay more |
| Cash tied up | A deposit, usually refundable | None |
| Flexibility | Free cancellation until final payment | Total |
| Flights | Can book early and align | May face higher fares |

The pattern is lopsided. If you book early and prices fall, you can usually capture the drop. If you wait and prices rise, which is what tends to happen on popular weeks, you pay the difference and get a worse cabin. [Can a cruise line raise your fare after booking](/articles/can-cruise-line-raise-fare-after-booking/) explains why a booked fare is protected.

> **Quick math:** a family of four holding US$250 per person in deposits has about US$1,000 (roughly C$1,350–1,400) tied up. Left in a savings account for six months, that might earn C$20–40. A single price rise of US$100 per person on the balcony you wanted costs US$400. The opportunity cost of an early deposit is tiny.

## Who should book 2027 now, and who can wait
### Book now
- **Families tied to school calendars.** July, August and March Break 2027 are fixed for you, and connecting cabins sell early.
- **Anyone who needs a specific cabin.** Accessible cabins, solo cabins, aft wraparound balconies and Alaska balconies on the scenic side go first.
- **Groups.** Holding several cabins together is far easier a year out. See [group cruise deadlines and deposits](/articles/group-cruise-deadlines-deposits/).
- **Western Canadians flying to Florida.** Early booking lets you watch for seat sales over many months.

### Wait if you are flexible
- Couples with fully flexible dates who are happy with an inside cabin.
- Anyone targeting May or September 2027 shoulder sailings, which see more discounting.
- Retirees within driving distance of Vancouver who can pounce on a late deal. [Last-minute cruise deals for Canadians](/articles/last-minute-cruise-deals-canada/) covers how that works.

## The currency question
If you book in US dollars, your deposit is converted now but the balance is converted at final payment, so a weaker loonie by spring 2027 raises your CAD cost. Booking in Canadian dollars, where the line or your agent offers it, removes that uncertainty. [How exchange rates affect cruise pricing](/articles/exchange-rate-cruise-pricing-canadians/) runs the numbers.

## How to book 2027 the smart way
1. **Shortlist two or three sailings** and price them in full, including taxes, fees and gratuities.
2. **Book your favourite on a refundable fare**, not a non-refundable deposit. The trade-off is in [refundable vs non-refundable fares](/articles/cruise-fare-classes-refundable/).
3. **Buy travel insurance within the window** your insurer requires after the first deposit, so any pre-existing condition or cancellation benefits apply.
4. **Put the final payment date in your calendar**, with a reminder two weeks before.
5. **Watch the price through wave season**, January to March 2027, when new promotions land, and ask to reprice if the total improves.
6. **Book flights** once airlines open seats for your dates, typically up to about a year ahead.
