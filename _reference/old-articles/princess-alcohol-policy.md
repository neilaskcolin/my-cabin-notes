Princess is one of the friendlier big lines for wine drinkers. Each adult can carry one 750 ml bottle of wine or champagne aboard free at embarkation, and more bottles are allowed if you pay corkage, around US$20 each at recent policy. Beer, spirits and liqueurs stay off the ship.

## What can you bring on a Princess cruise?
At recent policy, the terminal rules are:

- One 750 ml bottle of wine or champagne per guest aged 21 or over, free.
- Additional bottles of wine or champagne, each charged a corkage fee of roughly US$20 (about C$27–28) to your onboard account.
- No beer, spirits or liqueurs, in carry-on or checked luggage. Anything found is held and handed back at the end of the cruise.
- Pack bottles in your carry-on so they clear security with you.

The minimum drinking age on Princess sailings from North America is 21, and the wine allowance follows the same rule. Check the current wording before you pack.

### How corkage works in practice
Extra bottles are logged when you board and the fee is posted to your account. Drinking your own wine in the cabin costs nothing more. If you want to take a bottle to the dining room, Crown Grill or Sabatini's, ask on board whether a charge applies to it, since practice has varied and it is easier to know before the bottle is opened.

## Wine and spirits bought in port
Princess treats wine bought ashore much like extra bottles at embarkation: you can bring it back and pay corkage instead of having it held. Spirits and beer bought in port are collected at the gangway and returned near the end of the cruise, as are bottles from the onboard shops. A bottle bought in Juneau comes back on the last evening or disembarkation morning.

> **Canadian tip:** sailing from Canada Place, a bottle of BC wine bought in Vancouver can be your free bottle. It is usually far cheaper than the ship's wine list.

## Bringing bottles home to Canada
Anything you carry off the ship counts towards your CBSA allowance. After 48 hours or more outside Canada, each adult who meets the provincial drinking age can bring back one of the following duty-free: 1.5 litres of wine, 1.14 litres of spirits, or up to 8.5 litres of beer (about 24 standard cans). The fuller picture, including the personal exemption, is in [cruise duty-free limits for Canadians](/articles/cruise-shopping-onboard-duty-free/).

> **Watch out:** spirits the ship held for you are handed back just before you disembark, and they still count. Two bottles of Alaska whisky per person will put you over the limit.

## Is the wine allowance enough, or should you buy a package?
For a couple from Calgary on a 7-night Alaska sailing who drink a bottle with dinner every other night, the sums are simple. Two free bottles plus two extra at about US$20 corkage comes to roughly US$40, or C$55, on top of the wine itself. That is a fraction of a drinks package for two.

Steady drinkers are a different case. The [Princess drink package price](/articles/princess-drink-package-price/) page runs the break-even, including when Princess Plus makes more sense. ## How Princess compares
| Line | Wine you can bring | Corkage (recent) |
|---|---|---|
| **Princess** | 1 free per adult; more with corkage | ~$20 for extra bottles |
| [Holland America](/articles/holland-america-alcohol-policy/) | 750 ml bottles; limits apply (confirm any free bottle) | ~$20 per bottle |
| [Celebrity](/articles/celebrity-alcohol-policy/) | 2 bottles per stateroom | ~$25 in bars and restaurants |

Holland America charges a similar US$20 or so in corkage, but its current terms list that fee per bottle and no longer confirm a free one, so Princess's free bottle per adult is the clearer deal. Celebrity counts per stateroom instead, allowing two bottles, and charges corkage of about US$25 when you open one in a bar or restaurant. All eight lines are compared in [Cruise Line Alcohol Policies Compared](/articles/cruise-line-alcohol-policies-compared/).
