The extra money on a luxury cruise buys three things: a fare that includes almost everything, a much smaller ship, and more staff per guest. Whether that's worth two or three times the price depends on how much you value quiet, space and never signing a bar bill.

## What luxury fares actually include
Mainstream lines advertise a low fare and charge for drinks, gratuities, wifi, specialty dining and excursions. Luxury lines fold most of that in. That's why the sticker price looks so much higher and why the real gap is smaller than it seems.

## The main luxury and upper-premium lines compared
Inclusions change often and vary by fare type, so treat this as a guide and confirm current details for your sailing.

| Line | Guests per ship (roughly) | Typically included | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regent Seven Seas | 490–750 | Drinks, gratuities, wifi, all dining, unlimited shore excursions, often flights | The most inclusive, spacious suites |
| Silversea | 100–730 | Drinks, gratuities, wifi, butler in every suite, flights on some fares | Refined, strong expedition fleet |
| Seabourn | 260–600 | Drinks, gratuities, wifi, most dining | Relaxed and social, expedition ships too |
| Explora Journeys | about 900 | Drinks, gratuities, wifi, most dining | Contemporary, resort-style |
| Viking (ocean) | 930–1,000 | One tour per port, beer and wine with meals, wifi, specialty dining | Adults only, no casino, destination-focused |
| Oceania | 670–1,250 | Specialty restaurants, with drinks and excursions often in a bundle | Food-first, more traditional |

Viking and Oceania are often called upper premium rather than full luxury, but many couples cross-shop them. For smaller names, see [quiet luxury: small-ship lines Canadians overlook](/articles/quiet-luxury-small-ship-lines-overlooked/).

## The real cost difference
> **Quick math:** an illustrative 7-night mainstream balcony at US$2,000 per person, plus a drink package (about US$600), gratuities (about US$125), wifi (about US$140), two specialty dinners (about US$120) and three excursions (about US$360), comes to about US$3,350. A comparable luxury sailing might be US$6,000–7,000 with most of that included. The gap shrinks from three times to about twice.

At roughly 1.37, that luxury fare is around C$8,200–9,600 per person. Many luxury lines price in US dollars, so check whether a Canadian-dollar fare is available, and pay with a card that has no foreign-transaction fee.

### Flights from Canada
Where flights are included, check which Canadian airports qualify, whether you can choose your airline, and whether business class costs extra. Included flights can save C$1,000–3,000 per couple on a European cruise, but only if the routing works for you.

## What you notice on board
- **No lines.** Embarkation takes minutes, there are no buffet queues, and tenders run smoothly.
- **Service that remembers.** Crew ratios are often close to one crew member per guest, against roughly one per two or three guests on big ships.
- **Specialty-level food everywhere,** without reservation fees. Some ships add hands-on [cooking classes](/articles/cruise-ship-culinary-classes-cooking-demos/).
- **Space.** Most cabins are suites, often with a walk-in closet and a real bathtub.
- **Smaller ports.** Small ships can dock in town centres and visit harbours big ships can't, which also means fewer crowds ashore.

## Who it suits, and who it doesn't
Luxury suits couples celebrating a big milestone, travellers who've done several mainstream cruises and want a calmer pace, and anyone who hates adding up extras. It's a strong choice if you [don't like crowds](/articles/cruise-for-couples-who-dont-like-crowds/).

It suits people less if you want waterslides, Broadway shows and a lively nightlife, if you're travelling with young children, or if you drink little and rarely book excursions, since you'd be paying for inclusions you won't use.

> **Watch out:** "all-inclusive" rarely means everything. Premium wines, spa treatments, some excursions and laundry can still cost extra. [What all-inclusive actually means on a cruise](/articles/what-does-all-inclusive-mean-cruise/) explains the fine print.

## Common mistakes when comparing
1. Comparing headline fares instead of totals with extras added back.
2. Assuming every ship in a fleet is the same. Expedition and classic ships within one line can feel very different.
3. Forgetting that included flights may not leave from your nearest airport.
4. Booking a luxury line for one feature, like a butler, without checking the rest suits your style.

## Is luxury worth it for your trip?
Price the same itinerary on a mainstream line with all the extras you'd actually buy, then compare it with a luxury fare.
