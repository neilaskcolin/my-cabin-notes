A big ship stops feeling like a maze once you know its logic. Ships are built around a few fixed reference points, and once you can name them, you can find almost anything in a minute or two.

## The three reference points that matter
**Forward, midship and aft.** The bow is forward, the stern is aft, and the middle is midship. Most ships have three sets of elevators and stairs: one forward, one midship, one aft. Learn which set is closest to your cabin and you'll always have a way home.

**Deck numbers go up as you climb.** Cabins are usually numbered by deck, so cabin 9214 is on deck 9. Pools and the buffet sit near the top; the theatre, dining rooms and guest services are lower down.

**Port and starboard.** Facing forward, port is left and starboard is right. On many ships, odd and even cabin numbers sit on opposite sides. Check which on your ship, because it's a quick way to tell which corridor you're in. The [cruise terminology guide](/articles/cruise-terminology-decoded/) covers the rest of the vocabulary.

## Your 20-minute orientation walk
Do this on boarding day, before the crowds settle in:

1. Start at your cabin and note the nearest stairwell, forward, midship or aft.
2. Walk to your muster station, so you know the route without signs.
3. Find the main atrium and guest services. This is the ship's "town square".
4. Go to the top deck and walk end to end: pools, buffet, any adults-only area.
5. Drop down to the theatre and main dining room, and notice where you have to change decks to get past them.
6. Take a photo of the deck plan at your cabin's elevator lobby.

> **Good to know:** on some ships, artwork, carpet colours or stairwell themes differ between forward and aft. Ask your cabin steward whether yours has a trick like this. It saves a lot of guessing.

## Use the app and the lobby maps
Most big lines include a deck map in their app, and on some ships it shows where you are. It works on the ship's wifi without an internet package on many lines, so connect to the network with roaming off. Setup is covered in [using the cruise line app](/articles/using-cruise-line-app-before-during-trip/).

The posted maps at elevator lobbies are often quicker. Check the "you are here" dot and which way the map is facing before you set off.

### Stairs or elevators?
Elevators queue badly before dinner, after shows and on disembarkation morning. For two or three decks, the stairs are usually faster. More timing tips are in [cruise elevators and stairs on big ships](/articles/cruise-elevators-stairs-big-ship-navigation/).

## Tips for families and groups
Pick a meeting point that's easy to find, such as a named bar in the atrium, rather than "the pool deck". Kids clubs often issue wristbands or app tracking, but a fixed meeting spot still helps. If your phones aren't on a wifi plan, agree on times as well as places.

Anyone with limited mobility should note which elevators reach every deck, since some only serve part of the ship. The [deck plan reading guide](/articles/reading-cruise-ship-deck-plans/) shows how to spot those before you book.

## Still turned around?
Ask any crew member. They are used to it, and most will walk you part of the way. By day two, the ship will feel like a small town rather than a maze.
