For most Canadians, the Royal Caribbean Deluxe Beverage Package is worth it only if each adult will have six to eight alcoholic drinks a day. At roughly US$70–105 per person per day plus an 18% service charge, it rewards steady sea-day drinking and punishes anyone who switches to water by mid-afternoon.

## How much is the Royal Caribbean drink package?
The Deluxe Beverage Package has recently run about US$70–105 per person per day before the 18% service charge. The price varies by ship and sailing, and it moves. Royal Caribbean runs frequent sales in the Cruise Planner before you sail, and the price on board is usually higher than the best pre-cruise price.

> **Quick math:** at US$85 a day plus 18%, the package costs about US$100 per person per day, or roughly C$135–140. For a couple on a 7-night sailing that is about C$1,900–1,960 before a single drink is poured.

## What the Deluxe Beverage Package includes
- Cocktails, spirits, beer and wine by the glass, up to a per-drink price cap (around US$14 at recent policy). Pricier drinks cost the difference.
- Sodas, specialty coffees, teas, fresh juices and bottled water.
- A discount on bottles of wine, rather than free bottles.
- Use on Royal Caribbean's private island, Perfect Day at CocoCay, as on the ship.

There is no published daily drink limit, though bartenders can refuse service. Minibar items are generally excluded.

### Cheaper Royal Caribbean packages
The Refreshment Package covers non-alcoholic drinks, including mocktails, specialty coffee and fresh juice. The Classic Soda Package covers fountain soft drinks only. Both cost a fraction of the Deluxe, and they suit kids, teens and the non-drinker in a couple.

## Break-even: a sea day for one person
Here is a fairly typical sea day at recent onboard prices, before the 18% service charge that applies to individual drinks too.

| Drink | Approx. price (USD) |
|---|---|
| 2 specialty coffees | $9–10 |
| 2 beers by the pool | $18–20 |
| 2 cocktails | $28–30 |
| 2 glasses of wine at dinner | $24–28 |
| **Total** | **$79–88** |

That's eight drinks, and it roughly matches a mid-priced package day. Port days are where the package loses: a long day in Juneau or St. Maarten might mean two drinks on board.

## The every-adult rule
Royal Caribbean requires all adults in a stateroom to buy the Deluxe package if one does. A non-drinking partner is sometimes allowed the Refreshment Package instead on request, but don't assume it.

> **Watch out:** the package is sold for every day of the sailing, port days included. You can't buy it for sea days only.

## Who the package suits on Royal Caribbean
It works best for couples who drink steadily on Oasis- or Icon-class ships with several sea days and a CocoCay call. It rarely pays on port-heavy Alaska itineraries, short sailings with long port days, or for anyone who mainly drinks wine at dinner. Those guests do better with pay-as-you-go plus the two-bottle wine allowance in the [Royal Caribbean alcohol policy](/articles/royal-caribbean-alcohol-policy/). The 18% charge sits alongside the [Royal Caribbean daily gratuities](/articles/royal-caribbean-gratuities-per-day/), so budget for both.

## How Royal Caribbean compares
| Line | Package | Recent price per person per day | Catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Royal Caribbean** | Deluxe Beverage | $70–105 USD + 18% | All adults in cabin must buy |
| [Carnival](/articles/carnival-drink-package-price/) | Cheers! | $70–75 USD + 20% | 15 alcoholic drinks a day |
| [Norwegian](/articles/norwegian-drink-package-price/) | Unlimited Open Bar | $100–120 USD retail | Usually via Free at Sea (pay the 20% charge) |

Carnival and Norwegian are the lines most often weighed against Royal Caribbean. Royal Caribbean's package has no published daily cap, unlike Carnival's 15-drink limit, but it can cost more and binds every adult in the cabin. Norwegian usually bundles its package into the fare and charges only the service fee.
