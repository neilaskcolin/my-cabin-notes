Pay in whichever currency gives the lower total on the day you pay, after converting at your card's real rate. For most Canadians that comparison takes a minute, and the bigger savings come later, on board, where almost everything is charged in US dollars.

## Choice one: which currency for the fare?
Most major lines show Canadians a CAD price on their Canadian websites. That price is set using the line's internal exchange rate, refreshed on its own schedule, often with a margin built in.

To compare properly:
1. Find the same cabin and fare in USD, either on the line's US site or by asking for both quotes.
2. Multiply the USD fare by today's exchange rate.
3. Add your card's foreign transaction fee, usually 2.5% on Canadian cards, unless you have a no-fee card.
4. Compare that total with the CAD quote, taxes and fees included on both.
5. Book whichever is lower, then stick with that currency for the booking.

| Example, 7-night fare for two | Amount |
|---|---|
| USD fare | US$2,000 |
| Converted at 1.37 | C$2,740 |
| Plus 2.5% card fee | C$2,809 |
| CAD quote from the line | C$2,790 |
| Cheaper option that day | CAD, by about C$19 |

With a no-fee card, the USD route would cost C$2,740 and win by C$50. Small differences like this flip from week to week, so run the numbers when you pay.

> **Good to know:** booking in CAD fixes your rate for the fare. Booking in USD means each payment, from deposit to final payment, converts at whatever the rate is that day, so you carry the exchange risk until you've paid in full.

## Choice two: everything on board
Even if you paid the fare in CAD, your shipboard account on most major lines runs in US dollars. Gratuities, drinks, specialty dining, wifi and ship-booked excursions all build up in USD and settle at the end of the cruise. On some European sailings the onboard currency is the euro instead.

### The two habits that save money
- **Use a credit card with no foreign transaction fee.** On a C$1,500 onboard bill, that saves about C$37 per sailing, every sailing.
- **Decline the conversion offer.** At check-in or when you settle, the ship may offer to bill you in CAD. That rate is set by the line or its payment provider and is usually worse than your card's. Choose USD.

How the account, card holds and final statement work is covered in [how onboard payments and card holds work](/articles/how-onboard-payments-work-cruise/), and linking a card to your account is explained in [how cruise cards work on board](/articles/cruise-cards-how-you-pay-onboard/).

> **Watch out:** debit cards can tie up money in holds, and some charge fees on top. A credit card with no foreign transaction fee is the safer choice for the onboard account.

## Choice three: cash ashore
US dollars work in Alaska, most of the Caribbean, the Bahamas and Mexican ports. Bring a modest amount of small bills for tips and taxis, bought before you leave or withdrawn once from a bank-owned ATM. In Europe you need euros, or kroner in Norway, though cards are widely accepted.

## Should you wait for a better exchange rate?
Nobody can predict the loonie. What you can control is certainty: a CAD-fixed fare, gratuities prepaid in CAD, and packages bought before sailing all lock in today's rate. For anyone on a set budget, that certainty is worth more than guessing. ## The Canadian setup in one list
Register a no-fee credit card at check-in, book the fare in whichever currency totals lower, prepay gratuities in CAD where you can, and decline every conversion offer. Those four small choices are commonly worth C$100 or more per sailing for a couple. Ask for both currency quotes side by side before paying the deposit.
