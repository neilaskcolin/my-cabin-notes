Fly in the day before. Same-day flying works most of the time, but when it fails, the cost is enormous: a ship that won't wait and a scramble to reach Alaska. One hotel night turns a delayed flight from a disaster into a shrug.

## How risky is a same-day flight from where you live?
| Flying from | Flight to YVR | Clock change | Same-day verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calgary or Edmonton | About 1.5 hours, many nonstops | Gain 1 hour | Possible, but one cancellation can sink it |
| Winnipeg | About 2.75 hours | Gain 2 hours | Risky; fewer nonstops |
| Toronto | About 5 hours | Gain 3 hours | Not advisable |
| Montreal or Ottawa | About 5–5.5 hours; Ottawa often connects | Gain 3 hours | Not advisable |

Ships typically close boarding 60–90 minutes before sailing, earlier than the departure time on your ticket. A morning flight from Calgary lands with hours to spare on paper, but one weather hold, a mechanical issue or a de-icing queue can eat the buffer. Alaska season opens in May, when a spring snowstorm in Calgary is still possible.

## What missing the ship actually costs
The ship doesn't wait, and "catch it at the next port" means somewhere like Ketchikan. Expect last-minute flights through Seattle, perhaps a hotel night, a port agent's help to get aboard, and lost days of a cruise you have already paid for. The full scenario is in [what happens if your flight is delayed](/articles/cruise-flight-delay-what-happens/).

> **Quick math:** a downtown Vancouver hotel the night before runs about C$180–300 for two at recent summer prices. Catching an Alaska ship at its first port can cost over C$1,000 per person, plus one or two lost cruise days that might be worth C$200–400 each. The hotel is the cheapest insurance in travel.

## The day-before plan
1. **Book a flight that lands by early evening.** That leaves room for a delay and still gets you to dinner.
2. **Stay within walking distance of Canada Place or near a Canada Line station.** [Where to stay the night before](/articles/where-to-stay-vancouver-night-before-cruise/) compares neighbourhoods and budgets.
3. **Enjoy the evening.** Walk the seawall at Coal Harbour, eat in Gastown, or watch other ships at the terminal.
4. **Sleep in and have breakfast.** There is no reason to be at the terminal before your assigned check-in time.
5. **Walk or ride to the terminal.** Getting from the hotel to the ship is covered in [cruise transfers explained](/articles/cruise-ship-transfers-explained/), and the terminal process in the [Canada Place embarkation walkthrough](/articles/canada-place-embarkation-walkthrough/).

## If you really must fly the same day
Take the first nonstop of the morning, not a connection, and avoid the last flight that could possibly work. Carry essentials in your cabin bag in case checked luggage misses the ship. Avoid ultra-low-cost carriers, whose thin schedules may have no later flight. Long weekends such as Victoria Day are when flights are fullest and rebooking is hardest.

## The exception that changes the math
If your flights are booked as part of the cruise package rather than separately, the cruise line takes responsibility when the flight is delayed. It will rebook you and, if needed, get you to the next port at its cost. That protection only applies to air bought through the cruise, which is why it is worth pricing against DIY flights; see [cruise line air vs your own flights](/articles/cruise-line-air-vs-own-flights/).

Even with line air, arriving the day before is more relaxing. Protection fixes the cost of a missed ship; it doesn't give you back the first evening on board.
