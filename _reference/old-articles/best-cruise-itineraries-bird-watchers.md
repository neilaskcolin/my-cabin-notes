Most cruises pass birds, but a few itineraries are worth choosing for them. For Canadians, Alaska is the easy win, northern Europe's seabird colonies are the summer showpiece, and expedition voyages are where birding becomes the main event.

## Where are the best birding cruises?
| Region | Signature birds | Best months | Cruise type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Southeast Alaska | Bald eagles, ravens, murrelets, gulls | May–September | Mainstream or small ship from Vancouver or Seattle |
| Seward and Kenai Fjords | Horned and tufted puffins, murres, kittiwakes | June–August | Day boat before or after a one-way Alaska cruise |
| Orkney and Shetland | Atlantic puffins, gannets, great skuas | May–July | British Isles cruise |
| Iceland | Atlantic puffins, seabird cliffs | Late May to early August | Iceland or Iceland-Greenland cruise |
| Northern Norway | Puffins, gannets, sea eagles | June–August | Longer fjords route to the North Cape |
| Galápagos | Blue-footed boobies, frigatebirds, Darwin's finches | Year-round | Small expedition ship |
| Antarctica and South Georgia | Penguins, albatrosses, petrels | November–March | Expedition ship |

### Alaska: eagles everywhere
Bald eagles are so common in Southeast Alaska that by day three you stop pointing. Look along shorelines, river mouths and harbour pilings, especially when salmon are running in late summer. If your cruise starts or ends in Seward, a Kenai Fjords boat trip adds puffins and seabird cliffs, and is worth a night on land.

A birder from Edmonton, for example, could book a one-way June sailing from Vancouver to Seward, spend two nights in Seward for a full-day Kenai Fjords trip, then fly home from Anchorage. That covers forest, shoreline and seabird-cliff species in one trip. [Round-trip vs one-way Alaska](/articles/roundtrip-vs-oneway-alaska/) explains how one-way sailings work.

### Northern Europe's seabird colonies
Atlantic puffins nest on cliffs in Iceland, Shetland, Orkney and northern Norway from roughly May into early August, then leave for open ocean. Plan an early-summer sailing if puffins are the goal. Our guides to [British Isles cruises](/articles/british-isles-cruise-canadians/) and [Iceland and Greenland cruises](/articles/iceland-greenland-cruises-canadians/) cover routes and ports.

### Expedition voyages
In the Galápagos, birds nest so close to the trails that a long lens is optional. Antarctica and South Georgia bring penguin colonies and albatrosses following the ship. These trips cost far more, but naturalists and landings make them the serious birder's choice. See the [Galápagos expedition cruise guide](/articles/galapagos-expedition-cruise-canadians/) and [small-ship lines for wildlife lovers](/articles/best-small-ship-lines-wildlife-nature/).

> **Canadian tip:** closer to home, New England and Canada fall cruises often call at Gaspé, near Percé and Bonaventure Island's huge northern gannet colony. The gannets are usually present into September, but check whether your port time allows the trip.

## Birding from the ship on sea days
Sea days are free pelagic birding. On Pacific crossings, such as Hawaii sailings from Vancouver, watch for albatrosses gliding in the ship's wake. Transatlantic and repositioning cruises bring shearwaters and storm-petrels.

1. Get out early, when decks are empty and birds are active.
2. Pick a low open deck, forward or aft, out of the wind.
3. Scan the wake and the bow wave, where birds feed on disturbed fish.
4. Log sightings in a free app such as Merlin or eBird, which also helps with identification.
5. Ask whether the ship has a naturalist or birding group on board.

> **Good to know:** 8x42 binoculars are the best all-round choice on a moving ship. Higher magnification is harder to hold steady. Photographers will want at least a 300mm lens, as covered in [Alaska cruise photography gear and settings](/articles/best-alaska-cruise-photography/).

## Birding responsibly
Keep your distance from nesting colonies, stay on marked paths, and never flush birds for a photo. Most cruise lines also ban flying drones from the ship, and many parks ban them entirely.

## Choosing your birding cruise
Casual birders will be very happy with Alaska or a British Isles route in June. Keen birders with a life list should look at an early-summer Iceland or Shetland call, or save for an expedition voyage where birds set the schedule.
