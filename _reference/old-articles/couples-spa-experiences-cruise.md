Yes, a couples spa day at sea is easy to arrange: nearly every large ship has at least one double treatment room and a thermal area. The trick is booking the right day, knowing what the bill really adds up to, and not paying for a pass you'll use once.

## What couples treatments are on offer?
Most ship spas sell side-by-side versions of their standard massages, usually 50 or 75 minutes, in a room set up for two. Some add a private soaking tub or a couples ritual that combines a scrub, wrap and massage. Menus vary by line and ship, so look at the spa section of your cruise app before you sail.

### The thermal suite
This is the quieter, less obvious option. You get heated ceramic loungers facing the sea, a sauna, a steam room and sometimes a hydrotherapy pool. There is no therapist and no appointment, so it suits couples who want an hour of shared calm every afternoon rather than one big treatment. Many lines cap the number of passes sold, which keeps it uncrowded but means passes can disappear by day two.

## How much does a couples spa day cost?
Spa prices are set in US dollars and change often, so treat these as rough ranges at recent prices on mainstream lines.

| Item | Typical price (USD) | Rough CAD |
|---|---|---|
| 50-minute couples massage | US$300–450 per couple | C$410–630 |
| Thermal suite day pass | US$40–60 per person | C$55–85 |
| Thermal suite cruise pass, 7 nights | US$150–300 per person | C$205–420 |
| Couples thermal pass, 7 nights | US$250–500 per couple | C$340–700 |
| Automatic service charge | 18–20% of treatment price | |

> **Quick math:** a US$380 couples massage plus a 20% service charge comes to US$456. At 1.37, that is about C$625, before any foreign-transaction fee on your card.

## The port-day pricing trick
Spas are busiest on sea days, when everyone is on board and looking for something to do. On port days most passengers are ashore, so many spas offer discounts on treatments to fill empty slots. If you skip a port you have seen before, or come back early from a short stop, that is the ideal time to book.

Embarkation day often has specials too, plus spa tours with prize draws. Sea-day afternoons are the hardest slots to get and rarely discounted.

## Booking without the upsell
1. Pre-book through the cruise line app or website if your line allows it. Anniversary and honeymoon sailings fill the couples rooms fast.
2. Confirm the price includes or excludes the service charge before you sign.
3. Decide in advance that you won't buy products. Treatments often end with a pitch for creams and serums, and a polite "no thanks" is fine.
4. Arrive early and use the thermal area or relaxation room if your treatment includes access.

> **Canadian tip:** spa charges post to your onboard account in US dollars. A credit card with no foreign-transaction fee saves around 2.5%, which adds up on a C$600 spa bill. Our guide to [paying in CAD or USD](/articles/paying-cad-vs-usd-cruise/) explains the wider picture.

## Is it worth it for a couple?
A single couples massage is a lovely way to celebrate an anniversary, and it pairs well with the ideas in [romantic cruise ideas for a big anniversary](/articles/romantic-anniversary-cruise-ideas/). For couples who mainly want quiet time together, the thermal pass usually gives more hours per dollar, especially on a cold-weather route like Alaska where a warm lounger beats a windy deck.

Some suite and spa-class cabins include thermal access, so check before buying a pass you already have.
