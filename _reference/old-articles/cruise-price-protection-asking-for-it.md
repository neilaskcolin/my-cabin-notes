Cruise fares move after you book, and when they fall, you can often have the difference. Most lines do not advertise this, but a polite, specific request before final payment frequently succeeds.

## How does cruise price protection work?
Lines reprice sailings constantly as cabins sell (see [how cruise pricing works](/articles/how-cruise-pricing-works/)). If the fare for your exact category on your sailing drops, the line or your agent can usually apply the new fare to your existing booking. You keep the cabin you chose, and the difference comes off your balance.

It is not a formal guarantee on most fares. It is a routine adjustment that has to be requested, and each line sets its own rules, which is why it is worth knowing the windows below.

## When can you ask?

### Before final payment, on a refundable fare
This is the sweet spot. Final payment is commonly around 75–120 days before sailing, depending on line and length. Until then, a standard refundable fare can usually be repriced, because in theory you could cancel and rebook anyway.

### On a non-refundable deposit fare
Results vary. Some lines will reprice these, some give the difference as onboard credit instead, and some decline. [Refundable vs non-refundable fares](/articles/cruise-fare-classes-refundable/) explains the trade-off you made at booking.

### After final payment
Cash adjustments become rare. You might get onboard credit or nothing, as covered in [price drops after final payment](/articles/price-drop-after-booking-cruise/). Line-specific rules are in pages such as [Royal Caribbean's repricing policy](/articles/royal-caribbean-price-drop-after-booking/).

## A worked example
> **Example:** a couple books a balcony on a 7-night Alaska sailing in February at US$1,499 per person on a refundable fare. In late April a spring promotion drops the same category to US$1,299 with US$100 of onboard credit. Final payment isn't until June, so one message with a screenshot gets the booking repriced. They save US$400 as a couple, roughly C$550, and gain the credit.

Had they been on a non-refundable fare, the line might have declined. Had they asked in July, after final payment, the best realistic outcome would have been goodwill credit rather than money back.

## How to ask, step by step
1. Find the booking number and confirm your fare type and final payment date.
2. Screenshot the lower fare showing the ship, date, category code and price.
3. Contact whoever holds the booking: your agent if you used one, otherwise the line.
4. Ask plainly: "I'm booked in category X on this sailing. The same category is now showing at Y. Could you reprice my booking?"
5. Ask whether any current extras would be lost or gained in the switch.
6. Get the new balance confirmed in writing or as an updated invoice.

## What to check before you ask
- **Same category, not just same cabin type.** A "from US$1,299" headline is often an inside cabin or a lower balcony grade.
- **New-booking-only offers.** Some promotions exclude existing reservations.
- **Bundled extras.** If your original booking came with prepaid gratuities or a drinks package, the cheaper fare may be worth less overall.
- **Currency.** If you booked in Canadian dollars, compare the CAD fare, not the USD one.
- **Occupancy.** Make sure the quote you saw was for the same number of guests.

## Make it a habit
Put your final payment date in your calendar with a reminder a week earlier, and check your sailing every week or two until then. If a lower fare appears, ask. The worst answer is no, and the cabin stays yours either way.
