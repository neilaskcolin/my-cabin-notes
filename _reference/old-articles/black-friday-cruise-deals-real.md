Some Black Friday cruise deals are genuinely better, but plenty are standing promotions wearing a November label. The quick way to tell is to price your actual sailing before the sale starts, then compare totals, not percentages.

In 2026, US Thanksgiving falls on Thursday, November 26, so Black Friday is November 27 and Cyber Monday is November 30. Expect cruise lines to start "early Black Friday" emails up to a couple of weeks before.

## What Black Friday genuinely brings
Cruise lines do compete during the retail rush week, and the improvements tend to come as extras rather than lower stickers:

- **Reduced deposits**, sometimes as low as a token amount per person, which lowers the cost of committing early.
- **Bonus onboard credit** stacked on an existing promotion.
- **Third and fourth guest deals** that help families sharing a cabin.
- **Included gratuities, drinks or wifi** on selected fares.
- **Targeted fare cuts** on specific sailings that are selling slowly.

The fare cuts cluster on the same soft inventory that sales always chase: shoulder dates, older ships and less popular cabin types.

## What is usually recycled
"Up to 60% off" claims are often measured against a brochure price nobody actually paid, or apply only to the second guest. Countdown clocks are common, yet many Black Friday offers roll into Cyber Monday, then holiday sales, then wave season in January. The urgency is mostly staging. [Cruise line sales decoded](/articles/cruise-line-sales-decoded/) explains how to read the percentages.

| Headline you will see | Often means | Real value? |
|---|---|---|
| "Up to 60% off" | Discount on the second guest only, on selected cabins | Check the per-couple total |
| "$1 deposit" | Lower upfront payment; fare may be non-refundable | Real if the fare type suits you |
| "Bonus $200 onboard credit" | Added to an existing offer | Real if nothing else was removed |
| "Kids sail free" | Third and fourth guests pay taxes and fees only | Real for families; see [the kids sail free catch](/articles/kids-sail-free-catch/) |
| "Lowest prices of the year" | Marketing language | Prove it against your baseline |

## The three-step audit
1. **Set a baseline in October or early November.** Price your shortlisted sailing with everything included: fare, taxes and fees, gratuities and any extras you would buy.
2. **Price the same sailing during the sale.** Same ship, same date, same cabin category, every cost added up.
3. **Compare what is bundled.** A deal that swaps US$400 of prepaid gratuities for US$200 of onboard credit is a downgrade, even if the fare looks lower.

> **Example:** a Toronto couple prices a 7-night March 2027 balcony from Fort Lauderdale in October at US$3,100 for two with gratuities included. On Black Friday the same cabin shows "50% off the second guest" at US$2,950, but gratuities are no longer included, worth roughly US$250 for the week. The "deal" is about US$100 worse, roughly C$140.

## Canadian points worth checking
Prices on US cruise sites are in US dollars. At about 1.35–1.40 CAD per USD, a US$200 saving is roughly C$270–280, and your card's foreign transaction fee can eat some of that. [Paying in CAD vs USD](/articles/paying-cad-vs-usd-cruise/) shows how to compare properly.

Check the offer applies to Canadian residents too, because some promotions are restricted by country. And if you are eyeing a March Break 2027 sailing, popular cabins may already be thin by late November, so a modest bonus is not worth gambling the cabin.

## How to make the countdown irrelevant
Book a refundable fare when you are ready, whenever that is. If Black Friday then produces a genuinely better total, ask to move your booking onto it before final payment. You keep your cabin and bank the improvement. If the sale turns out to be smoke, you have lost nothing. ## Black Friday vs wave season
Black Friday is a useful early look, but the bigger promotional push for most lines still arrives in January to March. If you have not booked yet and your dates are flexible, [wave season explained for Canadians](/articles/wave-season-explained-canada/) shows what to expect next, and [last-minute cruise deals](/articles/last-minute-cruise-deals-canada/) covers the other end of the calendar.
