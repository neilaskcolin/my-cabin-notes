faq1q: When should Canadians buy travel insurance for a cruise?
faq1a: At or soon after you pay your deposit. Cancellation cover only protects you from the day the policy starts, and some Canadian policies give fuller cover, such as for pre-existing conditions, only if you buy within a few days of your first payment.
faq2q: What documents do Canadians need for a cruise?
faq2a: A valid Canadian passport for any sailing that visits or flies through another country, including Alaska and the Caribbean, plus the boarding pass from online check-in. Exotic itineraries may need visas, and a parent travelling alone with a child should carry a consent letter.
faq3q: How much US cash should I bring on a cruise?
faq3a: Onboard spending goes on your cruise account, so you mainly need small US bills for porters, taxis and tips ashore. Around US$100–200 per couple in fives and ones covers most week-long sailings.

A first cruise rarely goes wrong on board. When it does go wrong, it is usually in the months before: an expired passport, insurance bought too late, or a flight booked for the morning of sailing. Work through this list in the order the decisions arrive.

## Your timeline at a glance

| When | Main jobs |
|---|---|
| 6 months or more out | Passports, book cruise, buy insurance |
| 3 months out | Flights, hotel, excursions, final payment |
| 1 month out | Restaurants, shows, money, medication |
| 2 weeks out | Online check-in, phone plan, packing |
| 2 days out | Carry-on, documents, travel to the port |

## Six months or more before
### Documents
- Check every traveller's passport expiry today. Aim for six months of validity past your return date, which some Caribbean ports expect.
- Renewal queues grow in spring, before summer travel, so allow several weeks.
- Children need their own passports. A parent sailing alone with a child should carry a signed consent letter. The [passports, NEXUS and ID guide](/articles/passports-nexus-id-cruise-documents/) covers what counts.

### Booking and insurance
- Book early for the best cabin choice, especially for March break or July and August, when Canadian families crowd the same sailings.
- Ask whether the deposit is refundable and note the final payment date.
- Buy travel insurance at deposit. Your provincial health plan covers very little outside Canada and nothing useful at sea. See [travel insurance for Canadian cruisers](/articles/travel-insurance-cruise-canadians/).

## Three months before
- Book flights that land the day before sailing. A Toronto or Montreal flight to Florida, or an Edmonton or Winnipeg flight to Vancouver, can be delayed by weather in any season.
- Book a hotel near the port for the night before.
- Make the final payment, usually 60 to 90 days before sailing.
- Book the two or three excursions you care most about. Whale watching, the White Pass railway and glacier flights sell out first.

> **Watch out:** the final payment date is when most deposits stop being refundable. Put it in your calendar with a reminder a week earlier.

## One month before
- Reserve specialty restaurants and shows if your line opens bookings before sailing.
- Ask your pharmacist about a vacation supply of prescriptions. Keep medication in original packaging.
- Get some small US bills for porters and tips ashore.
- Check your credit card's foreign-transaction fee, since the onboard account bills in US dollars.

> **Canadian tip:** a card with no foreign-transaction fee saves about 2.5% on everything you charge on board. On a C$1,000 onboard bill, that is around C$25 back in your pocket.

## Two weeks before
- Complete online check-in: passport details, photo, payment card and arrival time. You will get your boarding pass and check-in window.
- Download the line's app and log in.
- Decide on a phone plan, or plan to use airplane mode at sea, as in [cruise wifi and roaming for Canadians](/articles/cruise-wifi-phone-roaming-canadians/).
- Start packing from a list, such as the [printable cruise packing list](/articles/cruise-packing-list-canadians-printable/).

## The last 48 hours
Your carry-on is the bag that saves trips. Checked luggage can take until evening to reach your cabin. Pack these in the bag you hold:
- Passports and boarding passes
- All medication
- Phone, charger and battery pack
- Valuables and the credit card linked to your account
- A swimsuit, a layer and a change of clothes

Check the weather at your departure airport, reconfirm your flight, and print a backup of your boarding pass. If you are sailing from Vancouver, the [Canada Place boarding walkthrough](/articles/canada-place-embarkation-walkthrough/) shows what happens at the terminal.

## The setup underneath it all
Most of this list gets easier when the booking itself is set up well: a refundable fare, flights with a buffer, insurance that matches the trip and any perks attached. For the money side, read [first cruise mistakes that cost money](/articles/first-cruise-mistakes-cost-money/) before you pay the deposit.
