faq1q: How far in advance do cruise excursions sell out?
faq1a: The most popular tours in high-demand destinations like Alaska can sell out one to three months before sailing, and sometimes earlier on peak July and August departures. Larger-capacity tours often stay available right up to embarkation.
faq2q: Can you book excursions after boarding the ship?
faq2a: Yes, subject to availability. The shore excursion desk and the line's app handle bookings on board for anything with space left, but the small-group and limited-capacity tours are usually long gone by the time the ship sails.
faq3q: Can you cancel a cruise excursion if you change your mind?
faq3a: Usually, for tours booked through the ship. Many lines allow a full refund if you cancel a set number of days or hours before the port call, and refund automatically if the ship misses the port. Independent operators set their own rules, so read them before paying.

Book the tours that have very few seats the moment your cruise line opens them, and relax about the rest. The difference between booking early and booking late is rarely price. It is whether the one tour you really wanted still has space.

## Why timing matters for some tours and not others
Capacity decides everything. A helicopter landing on a glacier might carry six people per flight. A small whale-watching boat might carry a few dozen. A 50-seat coach tour on a big-ship day has far more room. Once a small tour is full, no amount of money gets you on.

## What to book when

| Type of excursion | Capacity | When to book |
|---|---|---|
| Helicopter, floatplane, glacier dog sledding | Very limited | As soon as the planner opens |
| Small-boat whale watching, kayaking, bear viewing | Limited | As soon as possible, especially July and August |
| Scenic railways such as the White Pass in Skagway | Fixed by train departures | Early, on peak sailings |
| Private island cabanas and premium beach clubs | Limited | Early |
| City coach tours, beach days, standard snorkel trips | Large | Can wait, even until you board |
| Walking on your own | Unlimited | No booking needed |

## When does excursion booking open?
It varies by line. Many big lines let you browse and book in their app or online planner soon after you book the cruise, or once the booking is paid in full; others open closer to sailing. Check the planner as soon as you have a booking number, and use the line's app to see when each port's tours go on sale. [Using the cruise line app before your trip](/articles/using-cruise-line-app-before-during-trip/) shows where to find it.

> **Good to know:** ship excursion prices are usually charged in US dollars to your onboard account or card. A credit card with no foreign transaction fee saves about 2.5%, which adds up on a C$600 helicopter tour.

## A booking timeline that works
1. **After booking the cruise:** list your ports and research one priority tour per port. The [shore excursion vs independent tour comparison](/articles/cruise-excursion-through-ship-vs-independent/) helps you decide who to book with.
2. **As soon as the planner opens:** book the limited-capacity tours you would be genuinely disappointed to miss.
3. **Around final payment:** fill in the rest, and check your total spend. [How many excursions to book per cruise](/articles/how-many-excursions-book-per-cruise/) helps avoid overbooking.
4. **In the final weeks:** watch for planner sales, since some lines discount tours before sailing. Rebook if the price drops and the cancellation terms allow it.
5. **On board:** check the app or the excursion desk for cancellations if a sold-out tour still tempts you.

## Alaska: the port where timing matters most
Alaska has the most capacity-limited tours of any common Canadian cruise destination, and sailings from Vancouver and Seattle fill with several ships in port at once. On a peak summer sailing, book these first:

- Small-group whale watching in Juneau, ranked in [Juneau shore excursions by value](/articles/juneau-shore-excursions-ranked/).
- The White Pass railway, weighed up in [Skagway and the White Pass railway](/articles/skagway-white-pass-railway-worth-it/).
- Any helicopter or floatplane trip, which depends on the weather; ship-booked flights cancelled for weather are normally refunded.

## Ship tours vs independent tours on timing
Independent operators often sell out more slowly and can be cheaper, but they set their own cancellation terms and will not hold the ship if you are late. A ship tour guarantees the ship waits or gets you to the next port, as explained in [what happens if you miss the ship](/articles/what-happens-miss-ship-port/). For tight ports or long tours, that guarantee is worth paying for.

## The simple rule
Book scarce tours the day the planner opens, leave the big ones for later, and keep an eye out for cancellations once you are aboard. That way you get the trip you planned, not the leftovers.
