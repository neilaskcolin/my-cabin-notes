Back-to-back cruising means booking two separate sailings on the same ship, one straight after the other, and staying aboard on the changeover day. It is a well-established way to get a longer trip, combine two itineraries or avoid an extra set of flights.

## How does a back-to-back cruise work?
You book two sailings as two separate reservations, for example a 7-night northbound Alaska cruise followed by the same ship's 7-night southbound return. On turnaround day, most passengers leave and new ones board. You stay, though you may have a few formalities first.

Lines usually call you a "back-to-back" or "in-transit" guest, and many have a set process for you.

## What happens on turnaround day?
Details vary by line and port, but the day often looks like this:

1. **The night before:** you receive a letter or app message with instructions and a meeting time.
2. **Morning:** you may meet in a lounge, or walk through the terminal with your passport for an immigration check, then re-board.
3. **Free time:** if allowed, you can often go ashore for a few hours. The ship is quiet and it is a good chance to enjoy it.
4. **Afternoon:** you collect a new cruise card for the second sailing, if needed.
5. **Before departure:** you may be asked to complete the safety briefing again.

If you are changing cabins, crew often help move your luggage. Ask about this when booking.

## What resets between sailings?

| Item | Usually carries over? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Your cabin | Only if booked on both | Request the same cabin when you book |
| Onboard account | No | Settled at the end of the first sailing |
| Drink and wifi packages | No | Usually bought again for the second sailing |
| Onboard credit | No | Tied to each booking; use it before it expires |
| Specialty dining bookings | No | Reserve for each sailing separately |
| Gratuities | No | Charged for each sailing |
| Loyalty credit | Yes | Nights from both sailings normally count |

## Why people book back-to-back cruises
- **A longer trip without a longer itinerary.** Many routes only offer 7-night sailings.
- **Two different itineraries.** Eastern and Western Caribbean, or Inside Passage and Gulf of Alaska.
- **Fewer flights.** One set of flights for two weeks at sea.
- **Separate promotions.** Each sailing may come with its own offers.

> **Canadian tip:** from Vancouver, a northbound sailing to Whittier or Seward followed by the same ship's southbound return makes a 14-night Alaska trip that starts and ends at Canada Place. You see Hubbard Glacier or Glacier Bay and skip the flight home from Anchorage. ## How to book it properly
1. Check both sailings are on the same ship and that the second starts the day the first ends.
2. Book both at the same time if you can, in the same cabin.
3. Ask the line, or whoever books for you, to link the two bookings and note you as back-to-back.
4. Check each booking's deposit, final payment and cancellation dates; they may differ. See [cruise deposits and final payment](/articles/cruise-deposits-final-payment/).
5. Buy one travel insurance policy that covers the full trip dates, including both sailings.

> **Good to know:** occasionally a line will not allow a particular combination because, taken together, the two sailings would break US rules on foreign-flagged ships carrying passengers between US ports. The line checks this at booking, so ask early if your combination involves two different US ports.

## What to watch for
Prices for the two sailings are set separately, so a later fare drop on one doesn't affect the other. Onboard credit from the first sailing is usually lost if you don't spend it. And if one sailing is cancelled or changed, ask how the other is affected; see [what happens if the cruise line cancels](/articles/what-happens-cruise-cancelled-by-line/).

## Is a back-to-back right for you?
If you love sea time and a ship you know, two weeks aboard feels easy. If you get restless, a single longer itinerary with more variety may suit you better; [longer cruises for retirees](/articles/longer-cruises-retirees-14-nights/) covers what to expect from a two-week trip.
