For Canadians used to all-inclusive resorts in Cancún or Punta Cana, the word suggests the bar is open and the tips are covered. On most cruises, neither is true. "All-inclusive" in cruise marketing can mean almost anything, so the only safe approach is to check the list.

## What's included on a mainstream cruise?
On mainstream lines such as Carnival, Royal Caribbean, Norwegian and MSC, the base fare includes:

- Your cabin and housekeeping.
- Meals in the main dining room, buffet and some casual counters.
- Basic drinks: water, regular coffee and tea, and juice at breakfast.
- Most entertainment, pools, the gym and kids clubs.

It usually doesn't include alcohol, pop, specialty coffee, bottled water, gratuities, wifi, specialty restaurants, shore excursions or spa treatments. Disney is a partial exception, including fountain pop. The full checklist is in [what's included in a cruise fare](/articles/whats-included-cruise-fare/).

## How inclusive is each type of fare?

| | Mainstream base fare | Premium bundled fare | Luxury fare |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main meals | Included | Included | Included, often more venues |
| Alcohol | Extra | Usually included up to a price limit per drink | Included, often premium brands |
| Gratuities | Extra, about US$16–20 per person per day | Included in some bundles | Usually included |
| Wifi | Extra | Usually included, basic tier | Usually included |
| Specialty dining | Extra | Some bundles include a few meals | Usually included |
| Shore excursions | Extra | Some include a credit | Some lines include most |

Premium bundles have names like Celebrity's All Included, Princess Plus and Premier, Holland America's Have It All and Norwegian's Free at Sea. What each covers changes regularly, so check the current terms for your sailing date.

### What "inclusive" bundles often leave out
Even a bundle labelled "inclusive" can exclude:

- Drinks above a per-drink price cap, or premium wines and spirits.
- An automatic service charge on the drink package itself, often around 18–20%.
- Wifi beyond one device or a basic speed.
- Gratuities, on some bundles; Celebrity's All Included, for example, stopped covering them in late 2023.
- Specialty coffee or bottled water on cheaper tiers.

Many lines also require every adult in a cabin to buy the same drink package. [Are drink packages worth it](/articles/drink-packages-worth-it-first-cruise/) runs the numbers.

## What does "all-inclusive" really cost on a mainstream ship?

> **Quick math:** a couple on a 7-night mainstream sailing who buy a drink package, pay standard gratuities, get wifi for one phone and book one specialty dinner might add about US$1,600–2,200 to the fare at recent prices. That's roughly C$2,200–3,100, before any excursions.

That's why a luxury fare that looks two or three times higher can be less of a leap than it seems. On a like-for-like basis, mainstream usually still works out cheaper, but the gap narrows. Luxury lines charge more for smaller ships, more space and more staff per guest as well, as covered in [luxury lines compared](/articles/luxury-lines-compared-extra-spend/).

## Who should pay for an inclusive fare?
**It suits** people who would drink several cocktails a day anyway, couples who want a fixed holiday budget, and anyone who hates watching a bill grow. It's also worth it on sea-day-heavy itineraries, where you spend more time on board.

**It doesn't suit** light drinkers, families with young kids, or anyone on a port-heavy itinerary who'll be ashore most days. Paying as you go is often cheaper.

## How to compare fares fairly
Total what you'd actually spend: drinks, gratuities, wifi, dining, excursions. Add it to each fare, then compare the totals in Canadian dollars. A cheaper fare plus extras can beat a pricier bundle, or vice versa.

Excursions are often the biggest variable. A luxury line that includes them saves real money in expensive ports like Juneau, while on a mainstream line you can cut costs by choosing carefully between [ship tours and booking independently](/articles/cruise-excursion-through-ship-vs-independent/). [Cruise vs a flight and hotel holiday](/articles/cruise-vs-flight-hotel-vacation-cost-compare/) applies the same method to resort holidays.

Remember that onboard charges post in US dollars. A credit card with no foreign transaction fee saves around 2.5% on everything that isn't bundled.

## The bottom line
Treat "all-inclusive" as a phrase to check, not a promise. Read the inclusion list for your exact fare, note what's capped, and budget for the rest. Understanding how fares are built, covered in [how cruise pricing works](/articles/how-cruise-pricing-works/), makes those lists much easier to read.
