Go if you enjoy a bit of theatre and a free glass of bubbly on a sea day. Just treat it as entertainment first and shopping second, because the "investment" angle rarely stands up and the final bill is always higher than the hammer price.

## What happens at an onboard art auction?
Auctions are usually run by a third-party gallery company rather than the cruise line itself. The format is similar on most ships:

- A preview, where works are displayed in a lounge or along a gallery corridor, often with free champagne.
- A lively auctioneer who talks about artists, editions and "today's special" pricing.
- Raffle tickets or a free small print for attending, especially early in the cruise.
- Bidding by paddle, with works ranging from inexpensive prints to pricier originals and limited editions.

It runs for an hour or two, usually on sea days. Nobody is obliged to register or bid.

## Why the investment pitch deserves caution
You may hear that an artist's work has risen in value, or that a piece is appraised well above the bid. Appraisals from the seller are not the same as an independent valuation, and the resale market for many shipboard prints and editions is thin. Serious art buyers research provenance, compare auction records and take time. A fast-paced sea-day sale isn't set up for that.

> **Watch out:** "giclée", "serigraph" and "hand-embellished" describe types of print, not original paintings. Ask exactly what you are buying, how large the edition is, and whether the signature is hand-signed.

## What does a piece really cost?
The number on the paddle is just the start. Here is an illustrative example. Fees vary by company and sailing, so ask for the exact terms before you bid.

| Item | Example (USD) |
|---|---|
| Hammer price | US$1,000 |
| Buyer's premium, if charged (say 15%) | US$150 |
| Framing, if you choose it | US$300–700 |
| Shipping to Canada | US$50–150 |
| GST/HST and brokerage on import | varies by province |
| **Possible total** | **about US$1,500–2,000+** |

> **Quick math:** at around 1.37 CAD per USD, that US$1,000 print can easily become C$2,000–2,800 by the time it reaches your door in Calgary or Halifax, before any foreign-transaction fee on your card.

Works are typically shipped several weeks after the cruise. Keep every receipt and the certificate of authenticity in case the frame arrives damaged or the piece isn't what you expected.

## If you do want to buy
### Before the auction
Look up the artist and edition on your phone using ship wifi. Set a firm, all-in limit in Canadian dollars with your partner before you sit down.

### During and after
Ask whether you can cancel or return, and how long you have. Pay with a credit card, which gives you some protection if the item never arrives or doesn't match the description. ## The sensible way to enjoy it
Go for the spectacle, enjoy the champagne, and leave your credit card in the cabin unless a piece genuinely moves you. If it does, buy it for the wall, not the portfolio.
