Most advice about flying to a cruise covers your own flight being late. Wider disruption is a different problem: when an airline cancels hundreds of flights, every remaining seat fills with rebooked passengers, and even unaffected flights become harder to change.

## How is a wider disruption different from a delayed flight?
A single late flight can usually be fixed with the next departure. A strike, a winter storm across Central Canada, or an airline systems outage can take days to clear. Airports get crowded, phone lines jam and alternative airlines fill up fast.

In Canada, a union or employer must give 72 hours' notice before a strike or lockout at a federally regulated airline. That usually means you hear about labour disruption a few days ahead, which is enough time to act if you are watching.

## What protections do you have?

### Canada's air passenger rules
For flights to, from and within Canada, the Air Passenger Protection Regulations generally treat strikes and severe weather as outside the airline's control. That usually means no cash compensation, but large airlines must still rebook you, eventually on another carrier if they can't do it themselves within a set time, or refund you if the trip no longer makes sense. Rebooking can still come too late for a ship.

### Cruise line air
When flights are booked through the cruise line's air program, the line generally takes some responsibility for getting you to the ship, including helping you join at a later port if you miss sailing. Terms vary, so read them, but this is the strongest protection for complex itineraries. [Cruise line air vs booking your own flights](/articles/cruise-line-air-vs-own-flights/) compares the two.

### Travel insurance
Trip interruption and travel delay benefits can pay for extra hotels, new flights and catching the ship at its next port. The catch is timing: once a strike or storm is publicly announced, policies bought afterwards usually exclude it. Buying cover at deposit is what makes it useful here (see [cruise insurance in Canada](/articles/travel-insurance-cruise-canadians/)).

> **Canadian tip:** fly in the day before as standard, and two days before in winter or when a strike vote is in the news. Travellers from Edmonton, Winnipeg or smaller airports have fewer daily flights to fall back on, so they benefit most from an extra buffer. [Should you fly the day before](/articles/fly-day-before-cruise/) covers the basics.

## What to do when disruption is in the news
1. **Check your airline's travel notices** every day in the week before departure.
2. **Look for a travel waiver.** Airlines often let affected passengers change dates for free during an announced disruption. Use it to move earlier, not later.
3. **Call your insurer before changing anything** and ask what is covered and what receipts they need.
4. **Price a backup.** Another airline, a different routing, or ground transport. Albertans heading to a Vancouver sailing can drive in about ten to eleven hours from Calgary, and VIA Rail runs from Edmonton on a limited weekly schedule.
5. **Tell the cruise line early** if you might miss embarkation, and ask about joining at the next port.
6. **Keep every receipt**, boarding pass and cancellation notice for a claim.

> **Good to know:** a ship sails on schedule for the thousands already aboard. If you miss it, joining at the next port can mean an expensive flight to Ketchikan or Juneau on an Alaska sailing, or a Caribbean island on a Florida sailing, so carry your passport either way.

## Weather is the other big disruptor
Winter storms at Toronto, Montreal or Ottawa regularly delay flights to Florida, and de-icing queues can stretch for hours. [Snow delay risk on winter flights to a Caribbean cruise](/articles/snow-delay-winter-flights-caribbean-cruise/) explains how to plan around it. In late summer and fall, hurricanes can close Florida airports and ports at short notice, as covered in [what happens if a hurricane threatens your cruise](/articles/what-happens-hurricane-cruise-caribbean/).

## If you miss the ship anyway
Stay calm and work the problem in order: airline for rebooking, cruise line for the next port, insurer for costs. The steps are in [what happens if your flight to Vancouver is delayed](/articles/cruise-flight-delay-what-happens/) and [what happens if you miss the ship](/articles/what-happens-miss-ship-port/).

## Build the buffer before you need it
Most disruption problems are solved months earlier: insurance bought at deposit, flights that arrive with a day to spare, and a clear idea of who is responsible for getting you to the ship. If those are in place, a strike headline becomes an inconvenience rather than a lost cruise.
