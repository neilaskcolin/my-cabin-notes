A Canadian couple should budget roughly C$3,400–7,000 all-in for a 7-night Caribbean cruise in an inside cabin, flying from Toronto or Montreal. Choose a balcony and it is closer to C$4,500–8,700. The headline fare is usually well under half of the real total.

## What does a 7-night Caribbean cruise cost from Canada?
Figures are per couple at recent prices, for a 7-night sailing from Fort Lauderdale or Miami. Fares and onboard charges are usually in US dollars, so they are shown with a rough CAD equivalent.

| Item, per couple | Inside cabin | Balcony cabin |
|---|---|---|
| Cruise fare | US$1,000–1,600 (C$1,350–2,250) | US$1,800–2,800 (C$2,450–3,900) |
| Taxes, fees and port charges | C$270–560 | C$270–560 |
| Return flights from Toronto or Montreal | C$600–1,200 | C$600–1,200 |
| One hotel night in Florida | C$200–350 | C$200–350 |
| Airport and port transfers | C$40–120 | C$40–120 |
| Gratuities (about US$16–20 per person per day) | C$300–390 | C$300–390 |
| Drinks without a package | C$200–500 | C$200–500 |
| Wifi, if you need it | C$0–280 | C$0–280 |
| Excursions and beach days | C$300–900 | C$300–900 |
| Travel insurance | C$150–450 | C$150–450 |
| **Rough total** | **C$3,400–7,000** | **C$4,500–8,700** |

> **Example:** a Montreal couple sailing in early December 2026, on a seat sale to Fort Lauderdale, with one beach day each and no wifi, lands near C$3,500 in an inside cabin. The same couple sailing over March Break with a balcony, a drinks habit and two excursions each can pass C$8,000.

## What moves the fare
### Season and school holidays
Caribbean pricing peaks when Canadians and Americans most want sun: Christmas, New Year and the March Break weeks. January and February are also strong. The cheapest windows are usually late April to early June, early December, and the hurricane season from June to November, with its own trade-offs covered in [what happens to a Caribbean cruise in a hurricane](/articles/what-happens-hurricane-cruise-caribbean/).

### Ship and itinerary
Newer mega-ships with headline attractions charge more than older ships on the same route. Eastern and Western Caribbean routes price similarly, and [Eastern vs Western Caribbean for a first cruise](/articles/eastern-vs-western-caribbean-first-cruise/) helps you choose. Balconies matter less here than in Alaska, since most days are spent ashore or by the pool.

## Getting to Florida from across Canada
- **Toronto:** the most choice, with frequent nonstops to Fort Lauderdale, Miami and Orlando. See [Toronto to Fort Lauderdale cruise flights](/articles/toronto-fort-lauderdale-cruise-flights/).
- **Montreal:** good nonstop options to South Florida, especially in winter. [Montreal to a Caribbean cruise](/articles/montreal-caribbean-cruise-us-port/) covers the routes.
- **Calgary, Edmonton, Vancouver and Winnipeg:** fewer nonstops and longer days. Add roughly C$200–400 per couple to the flight line above, or more in peak weeks.

Fly the day before. In winter, two days before is safer, because a single snowstorm can wipe out a day of flights.

## Onboard spending and excursions
Gratuities are added daily to your account, about US$16–20 per person depending on the line. Without a drink package, a couple having a cocktail at sail-away and a glass of wine at dinner can spend US$150–350 across the week. [How much spending money to bring](/articles/how-much-spending-money-cruise/) breaks this down further.

Caribbean excursions are usually cheaper than Alaska's. Snorkel trips run roughly US$60–120 per person, beach clubs and day passes US$80–150, and town tours US$50–100. Many ports, such as Cozumel or Nassau, are easy to explore on foot or by taxi, and a line's private island day costs nothing extra unless you buy add-ons.

> **Canadian tip:** onboard charges post in US dollars, so a card with no foreign transaction fee saves about 2.5% on everything you sign for. Coming home after a week away, each traveller can bring back up to C$800 of goods duty-free, including limited alcohol, so keep receipts from port shopping.

## Ways to trim the total
- Sail in early December or late spring rather than March Break.
- Book flights early, or watch for seat sales out of your nearest airport.
- Choose a sailing that includes gratuities or onboard credit in the fare.
- Skip the drink package unless the numbers clearly work.
- Do at least one port on your own rather than on a ship tour.

Insurance is not optional. Your provincial health plan covers very little in the US or the Caribbean, and medical evacuation can cost tens of thousands of dollars. [Travel insurance for Canadian cruisers](/articles/travel-insurance-cruise-canadians/) explains what to buy.
