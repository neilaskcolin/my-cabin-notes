Alaska suits solo travellers better than most cruise regions, because so much of the trip is shared outdoors: on deck, on small boats and in naturalist talks. The tricky part is price. Dedicated solo cabins are limited and often on Seattle sailings, so plan your cabin strategy first.

## Why Alaska works well alone
On a Caribbean cruise, much of the day can revolve around pools and beaches, which can feel couple-heavy. Alaska days revolve around looking at things. When a humpback surfaces or a glacier calves, strangers at the rail start talking.

- **Naturalist lectures** draw a curious crowd and give you something to talk about at dinner.
- **Small-boat excursions** like whale watching seat everyone together.
- **Scenic cruising days** mean hours on deck, where conversation comes easily.
- **Solo meet-ups** are held on many ships early in the voyage; look in the daily programme or app.

If you like to dine with others, ask for a shared table in the main dining room. It is the simplest way to meet people on night one.

## Your cabin options as a solo traveller

| Option | Rough cost | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Studio cabin | Often well below a full double fare | Designed for one, sometimes with a solo lounge | Few ships, few cabins, mostly from Seattle |
| Standard cabin, full supplement | About 200% of the per-person fare | Any ship, any departure | Expensive |
| Standard cabin, reduced supplement | About 150–175% | Same choice of ships | Usually only on slower-selling dates |
| Share with a friend | 100% each | Cheapest option | Requires the right cabin-mate |

> **Good to know:** studio cabins have appeared on Norwegian ships such as Bliss and Encore and on some Royal Caribbean Quantum-class ships in Alaska, mostly sailing from Seattle. A few newer Holland America ships include a small number of single cabins. Ship deployments change every year, so check what is sailing in your season.

For general strategies, see [solo cruising from Canada and single supplements](/articles/solo-cruising-canada-supplements/) and [how to dodge the single supplement](/articles/single-supplement-how-to-dodge/).

## Vancouver or Seattle?
If you live in BC or Alberta, Vancouver is the easier departure, with no border crossing before boarding and no US flight. But studio cabins are more common on Seattle ships. A solo saving of several hundred dollars on a studio can outweigh the cost of getting to Seattle, so price both. [Calgary to Seattle vs Vancouver](/articles/calgary-vs-seattle-vs-vancouver-alaska/) compares the travel side.

## Balcony or not?
For a couple, the balcony is often the best Alaska upgrade. For a solo traveller paying a supplement, it doubles an already high premium. An inside or oceanview cabin, combined with time in the ship's forward observation lounge and open decks, gives you most of the scenery for far less.

## Excursions and staying safe alone
Shore excursions are priced per person, so solo travellers pay the same as anyone else. Alaska excursions are pricier than most, with whale watching often around US$150–250 and flightseeing US$400 and up at recent prices.

### Sensible solo habits ashore
- Book guided hikes rather than walking trails alone, especially in bear country.
- Note the all-aboard time and carry the ship's port agent number.
- Tell someone on board, or a tablemate, your plans for the day.
- Walk free routes in town; Ketchikan is easy on your own with [this free walking plan](/articles/ketchikan-free-walking-plan/).

## Choosing the right sailing
For solo travellers, three things matter most: cabin price, an onboard culture that encourages mixing, and enrichment. Lines with strong naturalist programmes are a good fit; see [enrichment at sea](/articles/enrichment-at-sea-lectures/). May and September sailings often bring lower prices and the best chance of a reduced supplement, though weather is cooler.

## Before you book
Your provincial health plan won't cover much once you are in Alaskan ports, and as a solo traveller you have nobody to handle problems for you. Buy travel medical insurance, carry a copy of your passport, and keep an emergency contact on file with guest services.
