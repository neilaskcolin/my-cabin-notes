Princess calls its gratuity "crew appreciation". At recent rates it runs roughly US$17–20 per person per day depending on your cabin, and you can either let it post to your onboard account daily or prepay it. If you book Princess Plus or Premier, it is already included.

## How much is Princess crew appreciation per day?
The charge applies to every guest in the cabin for every night of the cruise. Rates step up with cabin category, and Princess revises them periodically, so confirm the current figure before you set a budget.

| Cabin tier | Approx. per person per day | Couple, 7 nights (USD) | Roughly in CAD |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interior, oceanview, balcony | US$17–18 | $238–252 | C$320–355 |
| Mini-suite and Reserve Collection | US$18–19 | $252–266 | C$340–375 |
| Suites | US$19–20 | $266–280 | C$360–390 |
| Family of four, standard cabin | US$17–18 each | $476–504 | C$640–705 |

The money is shared among dining, bar and stateroom teams and the crew behind the scenes. For many of them it is a real part of their pay. The wider picture is in [cruise gratuities explained for Canadians](/articles/cruise-gratuities-canadians/).

## Does Princess Plus include gratuities?
Yes. Princess Plus and Princess Premier both include crew appreciation, along with a drinks package and MedallionNet wifi. Premier adds more on top. For a couple who would buy all three anyway, the bundle often costs less than the pieces, which is why a "cheaper" standard fare can turn out to be the worse deal. [Cruise line sales decoded](/articles/cruise-line-sales-decoded/) explains how to compare the two.

> **Example:** a couple from Calgary in a balcony on a 7-night Vancouver round trip would pay roughly US$245 in crew appreciation on a standard fare. On Princess Plus, at around US$60 each per day, that is already covered, so the real question is whether the drinks and wifi are worth the remaining US$595 or so.

The drinks side of that sum is worked through on the [Princess drink package price](/articles/princess-drink-package-price/) page.

## Should Canadians prepay Princess gratuities?
Usually, yes. If you booked in Canadian dollars, prepaid crew appreciation is added to the booking in CAD. Left to post on board, it is charged in US dollars and converted at your card's rate, often with a foreign-transaction fee of around 2.5%. Prepaying also locks the rate in force when you pay, so a later increase does not touch you. The currency question is covered in [paying in CAD vs USD for a cruise](/articles/paying-cad-vs-usd-cruise/).

How to prepay on a standard fare:
1. Check the current rate for your cabin tier and count every guest.
2. Ask whoever holds your booking to add prepaid crew appreciation, at booking or before the cut-off shortly before sailing.
3. Check your next invoice shows guests times nights times rate.
4. On board, look in the Princess app or ask Guest Services to confirm gratuities show as prepaid.

> **Watch out:** if you prepay gratuities and later switch to Princess Plus, ask for the prepaid amount to be removed. Otherwise you may end up paying for crew appreciation twice.

## What crew appreciation doesn't cover
A service charge, around 18% at recent rates, is added separately to bar purchases, specialty dining and spa services bought outside a package. Terminal porters and shore excursion guides are tipped in cash by convention.

On a cruisetour, the daily charge stops at the gangway. Tour directors, motorcoach drivers and guides on the Denali and Fairbanks land days are usually tipped separately, so take small US bills. The trip structure is in [Alaska cruisetours explained](/articles/alaska-cruisetours-denali-rail/).

## Adjusting it on board
Guest Services can adjust crew appreciation for a genuine service problem, and reducing it for a specific failure is fair. Removing it wholesale is discouraged, because it shortchanges crew who did their jobs well. A note naming someone who stood out is always welcome.
