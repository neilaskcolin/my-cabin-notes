Taking a CPAP on a cruise is routine, and many passengers on every sailing do it. The rules are simple once you know them. Problems only come from discovering them at the terminal.

## Can you bring a CPAP on a cruise? The short answer
Yes, on every major cruise line, with no fee and no special approval. CPAP, BiPAP and APAP machines are classed as medical devices. What catches people out is not the machine itself but the extension cord, the distilled water and where the bedside outlet is.

## The four things to do before you sail
1. **Notify the line's accessibility desk** once you have booked, so the CPAP is noted on your reservation and the ship can prepare a cord and water.
2. **Sort out the extension cord.** On most lines, request the ship's medical cord and don't pack your own; on Carnival, pack a plain cord with no surge protector.
3. **Arrange distilled water**, and pack a first-night supply anyway.
4. **Carry the machine on** in your hand luggage, never in checked bags, which can reach your cabin hours late.

## Power, outlets and the extension cord rule
Cabins on ships sailing from North America have standard North American outlets, so most machines plug straight in. The issue is placement. On older ships the only outlet is often at the desk, across the cabin from the bed, which is exactly what the medical extension cord solves. Newer ships usually have bedside outlets and USB ports. MSC ships built for European markets may have European sockets alongside North American ones, so pack a simple plug adapter, not a power strip.

Every major line except Carnival bans guests' own extension cords and power strips as a fire risk, and all of them ban surge protectors. Security holds banned items at the terminal and returns them at the end of the cruise. On those lines the ship's approved cord, lent on request, is the medical exception; Carnival simply allows plain cords and power strips without surge protection. [Can you bring an extension cord on a cruise](/articles/extension-cord-cruise-ship-rules/) covers what you can bring instead.

## How much distilled water do you need?
Supply, delivery and any charge vary by line and ship.

> **Quick math:** a humidifier uses roughly 250–350 ml a night, so a 7-night cruise needs about 2–2.5 litres. Pack one or two litres in your carry-on to cover the first nights while the ship's supply is arranged.

Medical liquids are routinely allowed through cruise terminal security in reasonable quantities. Line-by-line detail is in [Distilled Water for a CPAP on a Cruise Ship](/articles/cpap-distilled-water-cruise-ships/).

## Flying to the port, letters and batteries
The cruise lines do not require a doctor's letter. On itineraries that cross borders, such as Alaska from Vancouver, the Caribbean or Europe, a short letter stating the device is medically necessary smooths any customs or security question. Air Canada, WestJet and most airlines treat a CPAP as a medical device that does not count towards your carry-on allowance; confirm with your airline before flying, and see [luggage rules when flying to your cruise](/articles/luggage-rules-flying-to-cruise/) for the rest of your bags.

Ships provide power, so batteries are only useful for cruisetour land nights or pre-cruise hotels with awkward outlets. Lithium batteries must travel in carry-on luggage on flights, within airline watt-hour limits. Replacement parts are hard to find mid-cruise, so a spare mask cushion and tubing are worth packing; the [cruise packing list for Canadians](/articles/cruise-packing-list-canadians-printable/) has room for both.

## Travel insurance with sleep apnea
Sleep apnea is a pre-existing condition for insurance purposes. Declare it, and check the stability period on your policy before sailing. The detail for travellers over 60 is in [Cruise Insurance for Seniors in Canada](/articles/cruise-insurance-seniors-canada-pre-existing/).

## Getting it all noted at booking
The CPAP note, the cord request and the water arrangement can all go in one message to the line's accessibility desk. Send it the day you book, keep the reply, and nothing is left to embarkation-day luck.
