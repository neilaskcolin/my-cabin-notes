Speak up early, be specific, and be realistic. A noise problem reported on night one can often be fixed or escaped. The same problem endured for a week and raised on disembarkation morning usually ends with an apology and nothing more.

## What to do, step by step
1. **Identify the noise.** Note what it is and when: scraping chairs at 6 a.m., a rattling vent, bass from a lounge until 1 a.m., or a door slamming in a stairwell.
2. **Call or visit guest services that night or the next morning.** Ask for the issue to be logged on your account.
3. **Ask for a specific fix.** A maintenance visit, a cabin change, or earplugs and a fan as a stopgap.
4. **Keep a simple record.** Times, a short phone audio clip, and the names of staff you spoke to.
5. **Follow up after 24 hours** if nothing has changed. Ask to speak to the guest services manager or hotel director's office.
6. **Write to the line after the cruise** only if the problem was never resolved, attaching your notes.

> **Example:** "We're in cabin 8240. From about 5:45 a.m. there's loud scraping directly overhead, every morning so far. Could someone check what's above us, and is there a comparable cabin we could move to?" Specific, calm and with a clear ask.

## What the ship can realistically do
| Noise source | Likely fix | Compensation chance |
|---|---|---|
| Rattling vent, creaking panel, faulty door closer | Maintenance can usually fix it | Low, once fixed |
| Plumbing or machinery hum that shouldn't be there | Maintenance, or a move if it can't be fixed | Moderate if it persists |
| Deck chairs being set out overhead at dawn | Earplugs; a move if space allows | Low to moderate |
| Nightclub, theatre or bar below or above | A move if space allows | Low; the location was on the deck plan |
| Anchor or bow thrusters on port mornings | None; it's normal ship operation | Very low |
| Noisy neighbours | Security can have a word | None from the line |

Cabin moves depend on availability: often possible off-peak, unlikely on a full March break or July sailing.

Compensation, when offered, is typically a modest onboard credit or a small gesture rather than a refund. It's more likely when the ship is at fault and you raised it promptly. A cabin that's simply in a busy spot you picked is unlikely to earn anything.

## Quick fixes while you wait
- Ask your cabin attendant for earplugs; many ships keep them.
- Use a white-noise app on your phone.
- Ask for a fan, which also helps if the cabin runs warm (see [cabin thermostats explained](/articles/cruise-cabin-temperature-control-thermostat/)).

## How to avoid a noisy cabin next time
Prevention beats any complaint. Before booking, check what is directly above, below and beside the cabin on the deck plan, and favour cabins sandwiched between other cabin decks. [How to read a cruise ship deck plan](/articles/reading-cruise-ship-deck-plans/) and [the quietest cabins and where to book them](/articles/quietest-cabins-where-to-book/) walk through it.

Be wary of guarantee fares if you're a light sleeper, since the line picks your cabin. And think twice about a connecting cabin next to people you don't know; the shared door carries more sound than a wall, a trade-off covered in [connecting cabins vs one big cabin](/articles/connecting-cabins-one-big-cabin-families/).

## Keep expectations fair
Ships are working machines, and some sound is part of the deal. Raise real problems quickly and politely, accept a fix where one exists, and save the deck-plan homework for your next booking.
