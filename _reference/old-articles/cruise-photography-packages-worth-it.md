Cruise photographers take genuinely good pictures, and the pricing is designed to catch you on day six when you are looking at a wall of flattering portraits. Decide on a budget before you board, and a package becomes an easy yes or no.

## How cruise ship photography works now
Photographers work the busiest moments of the voyage: boarding day, formal or dressy nights, staged backdrops around the atrium, and sometimes the gangway in port. You are never obliged to pose or to buy.

On many ships the photos no longer hang on a gallery wall. They appear in the cruise line's app or on gallery screens, matched to you by a cruise-card scan or facial recognition. You browse, favourite and buy from there, often as digital downloads rather than prints.

## What do cruise photo packages cost?
Pricing varies by line and changes often, so treat these as rough ranges at recent prices:

- **Single photos:** often around US$15–30 each, print or digital.
- **Small packages:** a set number of photos, commonly from about US$150.
- **Large or unlimited digital packages:** often US$300–400 or more for the voyage.
- **Portrait sessions:** some ships sell a private studio session, priced separately and usually higher.

> **Good to know:** lines often discount photo packages when bought before the cruise through the online planner, and sometimes run a sale late in the voyage. If you know you want one, compare those prices with the onboard rate.

## When a package is worth it
- A honeymoon, big anniversary or vow renewal where you want a proper set of portraits. See [romantic cruise ideas for a big anniversary](/articles/romantic-anniversary-cruise-ideas/).
- A multi-generation family trip where getting everyone in one good photo matters.
- Two or more formal nights, when you are dressed up anyway (see [what people wear on formal nights](/articles/formal-nights-what-to-wear/)).
- You genuinely want ten or more of the photos once you have browsed them.

## When to skip it
- You mostly want scenery. Glaciers, sail-away and tropical beaches are easy with a phone, and [Alaska cruise photography from the deck](/articles/best-alaska-cruise-photography/) covers getting the best of them.
- You are a short-sailing light spender who will love two or three shots at most.
- You are travelling as a large group. A hired photographer for one session can cost less per family than everyone buying packages, as explained in [hiring a photographer for a group cruise](/articles/hiring-professional-photographer-group-cruise/).

## How to decide without overspending
1. Set a photo budget before you sail, alongside the rest of your [onboard spending money](/articles/how-much-spending-money-cruise/).
2. Pose when it is easy and free to do so, especially on formal nights.
3. Favourite the shots you love in the app as the week goes on, without buying.
4. On the second-last day, count your favourites and multiply by the single-photo price.
5. Compare that figure with the package price, and only buy the package if it is clearly cheaper for what you actually want.
6. Confirm the package includes digital downloads with permission to print, so you can make your own prints at home for much less.

> **Canadian tip:** photo charges post to your onboard account in US dollars. A US$300 package is roughly C$405–420 before your card's foreign-transaction fee, which is commonly around 2.5% on Canadian cards unless you use a no-fee card.

## Getting good photos yourself
Most memorable cruise photos are casual ones: the sail-away, a port lunch, the kids on deck. Ask another passenger or a crew member to take one of the whole group, and be considerate about who ends up in your frame (see [photo etiquette with crew and other guests](/articles/cruise-ship-photography-etiquette-crew-guests/)).

What a professional adds is lighting and a posed, polished portrait. That is the one thing worth paying for, and it rarely requires a whole package.

## The verdict
Buy the moments you can't recreate, not the volume. For most Canadian travellers, two or three formal-night portraits bought individually are the sweet spot, and a package only makes sense when the occasion calls for a full set.
