Friends with very different budgets cruise together well when the group shares a ship and a few meals, not a standard of spending. Choose a sailing where the cheapest cabin suits the tightest budget, then let every family decide its own cabin, extras and excursions without comment.

## Start with the ship and dates, not the cabins
The biggest price lever for a mixed group is when you sail, not which cabin you pick. The same 7-night Caribbean itinerary can cost far less in early December or late April than over March break or Christmas. Families with school-age kids are tied to holidays, so compare the cheapest holiday week rather than defaulting to the obvious one.

Line choice matters too. A mainstream line with genuinely affordable inside cabins gives everyone a way in. A premium line where the baseline is already a stretch for one family puts the whole trip on shaky ground.

## What the budget tiers look like
Per person, two to a cabin, on a 7-night Caribbean sailing from Florida, at recent prices before taxes. Confirm for your sailing.

| Tier | Cabin | Rough fare per person | What the family gets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline | Inside | US$600–1,000 (about C$830–1,400) | Same ship, food, shows and pools as everyone |
| Step up | Oceanview | about US$100–300 more | Daylight and a sea view |
| Comfort | Balcony | US$1,000–1,700 (about C$1,400–2,350) | Private outdoor space |
| Splurge | Suite | US$2,500 and up (about C$3,500 and up) | Space and priority perks |

Third and fourth guests in a cabin often pay much less than the first two, which can make one inside cabin for a family of four surprisingly economical. Taxes and port fees, often US$120–250 per person for a week, apply to everyone, including kids. [Inside, oceanview, balcony and suite explained](/articles/cruise-cabins-explained-inside-oceanview-balcony-suite/) covers what each tier is really like.

> **Quick math:** a family of four in one inside cabin, at US$800 each for the adults, about US$400 each for the kids and US$200 each in taxes, pays about US$3,200, roughly C$4,300–4,500. A couple in a balcony at US$1,400 each plus taxes pays almost the same. Spending looks different between families more often than it actually is.

## The real gap is onboard spending
Cabin prices are visible from day one. The friction usually comes from what people spend once on board.
- **Drink packages:** roughly US$60–110 per adult per day at recent prices. On several lines, if one adult in a cabin buys one, all adults in that cabin must. [Are drink packages worth it](/articles/drink-packages-worth-it-first-cruise/) helps families decide.
- **Specialty dining:** about US$40–80 per person, before drinks.
- **Excursions:** commonly US$80–200 per person per port for ship tours.
- **Gratuities:** about US$16–23 per person per day on most big lines, usually including children. See [cruise gratuities for Canadians](/articles/cruise-gratuities-canadians/).
- **Flights:** families from Toronto or Montreal usually pay less to reach Florida than families flying from Calgary, Edmonton or Vancouver.

## Ground rules that keep it friendly
- Share one baseline price in the first message: "Inside cabins from about US$X per person; everything else is optional."
- Make the included main dining room the nightly meeting point, not a specialty restaurant.
- Give every family its own onboard account. No group tab, and no splitting bar bills.
- Offer a free option every port day, such as a beach or a walking route, alongside paid tours.
- Never comment on anyone's cabin, package or excursion choices.

### Handling shared costs
For a group excursion or celebration dinner, agree the price per person upfront, with children at a lower rate and an easy opt-out. Collect it by Interac e-Transfer before sailing so nobody settles up in US dollars on board. A private group tour can bring the per-person cost below the ship's version, as explained in [group shore excursions](/articles/group-cruise-shore-excursions-private/).

> **Canadian tip:** decide early whether the group books in Canadian or US dollars. Some lines and agencies quote Canadian residents in CAD, which removes exchange-rate risk for the family on the tightest budget. In USD, a few cents' swing in the loonie over a year can change a family's cost by a few hundred dollars.

## Where should the group perks go?
Eight or more cabins in one block usually earns perks such as a free or reduced berth and onboard credit, as explained in [the 8-cabin rule](/articles/group-cruise-8-cabin-rule/). The fairest approach is to agree openly on their use before booking. Many friend groups put the value toward the shared dinner or excursion, which lowers everyone's cost without singling anyone out. [What organisers get free](/articles/group-cruise-perks-organiser/) lists what to ask for.

## Who this approach suits
It works for friend groups whose bond is stronger than their spending habits, and for families who are happy to meet up for dinner and go their own way by day. It works less well if one family expects everyone in suites or a group drink package. Have that conversation before deposits, not at the pool bar.

## A trip everyone can say yes to
The goal is a trip every family can join without stretching. Set the baseline, keep shared costs small and separate, and let everyone spend as they like.
