Fare tracking takes about ten minutes to set up and five minutes a week to run. The payoff is catching a price drop before final payment, when it can usually be applied to your booking.

## What exactly should you track?
Track your booking, not a general idea of it. Note these details from your confirmation:

- Ship, sailing date and itinerary
- The cabin category code, not just "balcony"
- Fare type, such as refundable or non-refundable deposit
- Number of guests in the cabin
- Currency, CAD or USD
- Any extras attached, such as onboard credit or prepaid gratuities

Those details matter because a lower price is only useful if it is for the same thing. If you are still choosing a sailing, track two or three candidates the same way.

## How to set up tracking, step by step
1. **Copy your booking details** into a note or spreadsheet with today's date and price.
2. **Bookmark the booking path** on the line's website with your category selected, or save the sailing in the line's app if it offers favourites.
3. **Turn on any alerts available**, from a cruise-tracking website or the line's own email offers set to the Canadian region.
4. **Add calendar reminders**: a weekly check, extra checks in January to March and around Black Friday, and one a week before final payment.
5. **Log each check** in one line: date, fare, extras. A pattern appears within a few weeks.
6. **Track your flights separately**, since airfare often moves more than the cruise.

## Which tools actually help?

### Cruise-tracking websites
Several independent sites follow fares on popular sailings and send emails when prices change. They are useful for a second pair of eyes, but they don't always pick up every promotion or every category, so treat an alert as a prompt to check, not as proof.

### The cruise line's own site and app
This is the source of truth. Log in if you have a past-guest account, since loyalty or resident rates may only show when you are signed in. The [cruise line app guide](/articles/using-cruise-line-app-before-during-trip/) covers what the apps can do before you sail.

### Flight trackers
Google Flights and similar tools let you set price alerts on routes such as Calgary to Vancouver or Toronto to Fort Lauderdale. If you are flying to the port, [how early to book flights for an Alaska cruise](/articles/how-early-book-flights-2027-alaska/) explains the timing.

> **Canadian tip:** check the line's Canadian site if you booked in Canadian dollars. Switching to the US site shows USD fares, which may move differently because the CAD price uses the line's own exchange rate. Compare in the currency you paid in.

## How to compare like with like
When a lower number appears, confirm the quote engine has the same guest count, since many default to two adults. Check that the fare type matches, as a cheaper non-refundable fare is not the same product. Look at what extras the new price includes compared with yours. A small saving can disappear if it costs you US$200 of onboard credit.

The reasons fares jump around in the first place are covered in [how cruise pricing works](/articles/how-cruise-pricing-works/).

## What to do when you spot a drop
Screenshot it with the date, category and price visible, then contact whoever holds your booking and ask for a reprice. Do it the same day, because promotional fares can disappear within hours. The full script is in [how to ask for cruise price protection](/articles/cruise-price-protection-asking-for-it/), and what happens later in the cycle is in [price drops after final payment](/articles/price-drop-after-booking-cruise/).

Tracking one sailing is easy; tracking several lines and every promotion is real work. If that sounds like a chore, a licensed cruise consultant can watch the booking for you.

## The five-minute weekly habit
Open your bookmark, compare the fare and extras with your log, and act on any genuine drop. Keep going until final payment, then stop: after that point, the chances of getting money back fall sharply.
