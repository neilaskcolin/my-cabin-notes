Private islands are the cruise lines' answer to the perfect beach day: a stretch of sand they control, with no taxis to haggle over and no crowds from other lines. For many Canadians escaping February, they become the highlight of a Caribbean week.

## What is a private island stop?
It's an island or beach that a cruise line owns or leases and develops just for its passengers. Only that line's ships call there, and sometimes ships from a sister line. Facilities are built around a day visit: beaches, loungers, bars, restaurants and, on the biggest, waterparks.

Most private islands are in the Bahamas, so they appear on short Bahamas sailings and on Eastern and Western Caribbean itineraries from Florida.

| Island | Line | Where | Known for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Perfect Day at CocoCay | Royal Caribbean | Bahamas | Paid waterpark, beach club, huge pool |
| Great Stirrup Cay | Norwegian | Bahamas | Beaches, cabanas, water sports |
| Harvest Caye | Norwegian | Belize | Zip line, lagoon, mainland tours |
| Half Moon Cay | Carnival and Holland America | Bahamas | Long crescent beach |
| Celebration Key | Carnival | Grand Bahama | Newer, lagoon-style beaches |
| Castaway Cay and Lookout Cay | Disney | Bahamas | Family beaches, adults-only areas |
| Ocean Cay | MSC | Bahamas | Marine reserve, evening lighthouse show |
| Princess Cays | Princess | Eleuthera, Bahamas | Beach bungalows |

Facilities change often, so check what your island offers when you book.

## What's included, and what costs extra
Included on almost every island:
- Beach access, standard loungers and umbrellas.
- A buffet or barbecue lunch, plus water, iced tea and lemonade.
- Swimming areas and some free activities, such as volleyball.

Usually extra:
- **Cabanas and bungalows.** Shade, private loungers and sometimes a server. Expect roughly US$150–500 and much more for the largest, often sold out well ahead.
- **Waterparks and beach clubs.** Day passes are priced by demand and can pass US$100 per person on busy sailings.
- **Water sports.** Snorkel tours, kayaks, jet skis, parasailing.
- **Alcohol and pop.** Charged at bar prices unless you have a drink package.

### Do you need to book the extras ahead?
Popular cabanas and waterpark passes sell out, especially on March break sailings. Prices in the app before sailing are sometimes lower than on board. If you just want a lounger and a swim, you don't need to book anything.

> **Canadian tip:** Canadian school March breaks fill Florida ships and private islands. If you can sail in late January, early February or after Easter, the beach is calmer and the cabanas cheaper. Prices on board post in US dollars, so a no-foreign-transaction-fee card helps.

## Is it worth it compared with a real port?
It depends on what you want from the day.

**A private island suits** families with young children, first-time cruisers, and anyone who wants a beach without organizing taxis or tours. Everything is close, clean and predictable.

**A real port suits** travellers who want local food, history and a sense of place. A private island is pleasant, but it is a resort, not a destination.

Many itineraries include one of each, which gives a good balance. [Port days vs sea days](/articles/port-days-vs-sea-days-itinerary-balance/) explains how to weigh the mix.

## Making the most of the day
Get off early. The first hour is the best time to choose loungers, especially in shade. If your island uses tender boats, expect a queue mid-morning (see [cruise ship tenders explained](/articles/cruise-ship-tenders-explained/)).

Pack reef-safe sunscreen, water shoes and a hat; the sun is stronger than a Canadian winter has prepared you for. Bring your cruise card, since that's how you pay. Snorkel gear can be rented, but bringing your own saves money, as [bringing snorkel gear vs renting](/articles/bringing-snorkel-gear-vs-renting-port/) explains.

## Weather and cancellations
Wind and swell can cancel tender stops at short notice, and storms can wipe out a whole day. The ship usually turns it into a sea day. If you sail between August and October, read [what happens if a hurricane threatens your cruise](/articles/what-happens-hurricane-cruise-caribbean/) first.
