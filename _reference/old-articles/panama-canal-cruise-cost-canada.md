A full Panama Canal transit typically costs a Canadian couple C$5,800–10,500 all-in for an inside cabin, while a partial transit round trip from Florida runs about C$4,500–8,000. The difference is less about the fare and more about one-way flights and extra nights.

## Full transit or partial transit?
Both formats take the ship through canal locks. They differ in length, flights and how much of the canal you see.

| | Full transit | Partial transit |
|---|---|---|
| Route | Ocean to ocean, usually Florida to California or the reverse | Round trip from Florida, entering the Gatun locks and turning in Gatun Lake |
| Typical length | 10–16 nights, longer from Vancouver | Commonly about 10 nights |
| Fare per person, inside to balcony | About US$1,100–1,900 | About US$900–1,600 |
| Flights | Open-jaw, C$400–700 per person | Simple return to Florida, C$180–400 per person |
| Sea days | Many | Several |
| Rough total per couple, inside | C$5,800–10,500 | C$4,500–8,000 |

Fares are at recent prices and vary a lot by line, ship and season. Canal tolls are included in the cruise fare.

## Building the full transit budget
For a couple in an inside cabin on a 14-night Fort Lauderdale to Los Angeles sailing, a realistic budget looks like this:

- **Fare with taxes:** US$2,200–3,800, or about C$3,000–5,200
- **Open-jaw flights:** C$800–1,400 for two
- **Hotel the night before:** C$250–450
- **Gratuities:** about C$600–800, since 14 nights at US$16–20 per person per day adds up
- **Drinks and extras:** C$400–900
- **Excursions:** C$400–1,000 across the ports in Central America and Mexico
- **Travel insurance:** C$300–700, as longer trips cost more to insure
- **Transfers:** C$100–200

### The flight logistics
Book a multi-city ticket rather than two one-ways where you can: for example, Toronto to Fort Lauderdale, then Los Angeles to Toronto. From Calgary or Vancouver, flying home from Los Angeles or San Diego is short and usually cheap, which is why westbound transits suit western Canadians. Eastern Canadians may prefer an eastbound sailing so the long flight is at the start.

> **Canadian tip:** look for spring and fall transits that start or end in Vancouver. Ships moving between the Caribbean and the Alaska season sometimes sail Fort Lauderdale to Vancouver, so a BC or Alberta couple flies only once.

## Building the partial transit budget
A partial transit is simpler. A couple on a 10-night round trip from Fort Lauderdale in an inside cabin might spend:

- **Fare with taxes:** US$1,800–3,000, about C$2,450–4,100
- **Return flights to Florida:** C$500–1,000 for two from Toronto or Montreal, more from the West
- **Hotel the night before:** C$200–400
- **Gratuities:** C$440–560
- **Drinks, excursions and insurance:** C$800–2,000

That totals roughly C$4,500–8,000. Partial transits often also visit Caribbean islands, Colombia or Costa Rica, so they feel like a longer Caribbean cruise with a canal highlight.

## What transit day is like
A transit through the locks takes most of a day, and the best viewing spots fill early. A balcony is less essential than on Alaska because much of the action is at the bow and stern, but a forward-facing public deck early in the morning is worth the alarm. Some larger ships use the newer, wider locks, which look different from the original century-old chambers, so ask which set your ship uses if that matters to you. Our [Panama Canal cruise guide for couples](/articles/panama-canal-cruise-couples/) covers the day in more detail.

> **Quick math:** at US$18 per person per day, a 16-night voyage carries US$576 in gratuities for a couple, close to C$800. Prepaying them in Canadian dollars, where the line allows it, locks the rate.

## When to sail
Most transits run in the dry season, roughly December to April, which conveniently matches the Canadian winter. Repositioning transits in spring and fall add more choice. Our guide to [repositioning cruises](/articles/repositioning-cruises-explained/) explains why those one-way sailings are often good value per night.

## Who each format suits
A full transit suits retirees and anyone with two to three weeks free who enjoys sea days. A partial transit suits travellers with less time, families during March break, and anyone wary of the open-jaw flight puzzle. ## Choosing your version
Start with how many days you have and where you live. A western Canadian with three weeks might pick a Vancouver-ending full transit; a Toronto couple with ten days will usually get the most for their money from a partial transit out of Fort Lauderdale.
