For most Canadians on a first Alaska trip, the round trip from Vancouver is the better deal: one simple return flight and a calm, sheltered route. The one-way to Whittier or Seward, the ports for Anchorage, is the better trip if you want Hubbard Glacier, College Fjord or a Denali land tour, and are willing to pay for the flights.

## How the two shapes work

A **round trip** leaves Canada Place, sails the Inside Passage to Alaska's port towns and a glacier, and returns to Vancouver seven nights later. You fly or drive to Vancouver and back.

A **one-way** sails between Vancouver and Whittier or Seward, in either direction. It covers most of the same ports, then crosses the Gulf of Alaska. You fly into one city and out of another, which travel agents call an open jaw. The scenery and sea differences are covered in [Inside Passage vs Gulf of Alaska](/articles/inside-passage-vs-gulf-of-alaska/).

## The whole-trip cost for a Calgary couple

The fare is only part of it. This compares the travel costs around a 7-night sailing, per couple, at recent prices. Your cruise fare goes on top.

| Cost, per couple | Round trip from Vancouver | One-way, Vancouver to Seward or Whittier |
|---|---|---|
| Flight to Vancouver | C$300–700 return | C$150–400 one-way |
| Flight home from Anchorage | Not needed | C$800–1,600, usually with a connection |
| Ship-to-Anchorage transfer | Not needed | About US$100–200 (C$140–280) |
| Hotel night before the cruise | C$250–450 | C$250–450 |
| Extra hotel night in Anchorage, if flights don't line up | Not needed | C$200–400 |
| **Rough travel total** | **C$550–1,150** | **C$1,540–3,130** |

> **Quick math:** at those ranges, the one-way usually adds roughly C$800–1,600 per couple before any land tour, and more if you need a hotel night in Anchorage. That's not far off the cost of upgrading from an inside cabin to a balcony on many Alaska sailings, so the question is whether Hubbard and College Fjord are worth more to you than a balcony.

Flight prices swing a lot with season and seat sales. Price your actual dates; [how early to book flights for an Alaska cruise](/articles/how-early-book-flights-2027-alaska/) helps with timing.

## The case for the round trip

- **Simple flights.** One return from Calgary, Edmonton, Winnipeg or Toronto, and no US connection.
- **Calm water.** You stay in the sheltered Inside Passage almost the whole way, which matters if anyone gets seasick.
- **Easy to drive.** From Alberta or the BC Interior, you can skip flying entirely and park near the terminal, as covered in [cruising from Vancouver without flying](/articles/cruise-from-vancouver-without-flying/).
- **Customs at home.** You come back through Canada Place and clear Canadian customs there.

## The case for the one-way

- **Bigger ice.** Hubbard Glacier and College Fjord are out of reach for most round trips.
- **Denali.** A one-way is the natural base for a cruisetour, adding rail and lodge nights inland. Which order works best is in [Denali before or after the cruise](/articles/denali-add-on-before-after-cruise/).
- **No repeat scenery.** You see each stretch of coast once, rather than going up and back down.
- **Good for a second Alaska trip.** It shows you a different side of the state.

### What about Seattle?

Round trips from Seattle are another option. By US law, foreign-flagged ships sailing round trip from a US port must call at a foreign port, which is why Seattle sailings stop in Victoria. They also spend more time in open water off Vancouver Island. For most Canadians, Vancouver is simpler; the comparison is in [Seattle vs Vancouver from Calgary](/articles/calgary-vs-seattle-vs-vancouver-alaska/).

## The deciding question

Is Denali, or interior Alaska, part of the dream? If yes, choose the one-way and plan the land tour around it. If no, choose the round trip and put the flight savings towards a better cabin or a standout excursion. Where the one-way ships finish is covered in [Anchorage vs Whittier vs Seward](/articles/anchorage-vs-whittier-vs-seward/).

## Price it as a whole

Compare complete trips, including the fare, flights at both ends, transfers and any land segment, not just cruise fares. Promotions can tilt things either way, and a cheap one-way fare can disappear once the Anchorage flight is added. Getting both shapes quoted for the same week is the only comparison that tells the truth.
