River and ocean cruises share a name but not much else. A river cruise is a small hotel gliding through Europe's town centres; an ocean cruise is a floating resort that links coastlines. Most couples over 50 know which they prefer within a paragraph.

## River vs ocean at a glance

| | River cruise | Ocean cruise |
|---|---|---|
| Ship size | About 130–190 guests in Europe | About 100 to 6,000+ guests |
| Where you dock | Usually in the town centre | Cruise ports, sometimes a bus ride from town |
| Sea days | None; scenery all day | Often several |
| Evening entertainment | Pianist, local performers, lectures | Theatres, shows, bars, casinos |
| Included | Daily tours, wine and beer with meals, wifi on many lines | Food and basic drinks; most extras cost more |
| Per-night price | Often US$400–800+ per person | Often US$150–350 per person, mainstream |
| Medical care | Usually no doctor on board; local doctors in port | Medical centre with a doctor |
| Motion | Virtually none | Some, depending on the sea |

## What a river cruise feels like
A European river ship docks in places like Vienna, Budapest or Cologne, and you step off into town. A guided walk is usually included in each port, there is one main restaurant where staff learn your name quickly, and the scenery passes your window all day. On stretches like Austria's Wachau Valley or the Rhine Gorge, castles and vineyards appear almost every few minutes.

### River realities to know
- **Water levels.** Low water in a dry late summer, or high water after spring floods, can force ship swaps or coach transfers between towns.
- **Bridges.** On some rivers the sun deck closes at low bridges.
- **Rafting.** Ships often moor side by side, so your view may be another ship's windows.
- **Balconies.** Many cabins have a "French balcony", a sliding door with a railing rather than a space to sit out.

## What an ocean cruise feels like
An ocean ship gives you choice: dozens of regions, cabins from inside to suites, many dining options and an evening programme. Sea days are a feature, with time for the spa, lectures and long lunches. The ship is part of the holiday, not just the way between towns.

The trade-off is that ports can be busy and some cruise piers are far from the centre. On a Mediterranean cruise, Rome and Florence are long coach trips from the ship.

> **Quick math:** take an illustrative 7-night river cruise at US$500 per person per night, or US$3,500. A comparable 7-night Mediterranean ocean cruise at US$1,400, plus about US$120 in gratuities, US$450 for five shore tours, US$300 in drinks and US$100 for wifi, comes to about US$2,370. River still costs more, by roughly US$1,100 per person (around C$1,500), but nowhere near the two or three times the brochures suggest.

## The question that sorts most couples
Ask yourselves: is the ship the destination, or the transport?

1. **If the ship is transport** and you want to wake up in a new European town each day, choose river.
2. **If the ship is part of the holiday** and you enjoy sea days, shows and variety, choose ocean.
3. **If you can't decide**, alternate. Plenty of couples sail the Danube one year and the Mediterranean the next.

## Getting there from Canada
Both formats in Europe mean a transatlantic flight. River cruises are usually one-way, for example Budapest to Amsterdam, so book open-jaw flights and add a pre-night to recover from jet lag. From Calgary, Edmonton, Vancouver or Winnipeg, expect at least one connection; Toronto and Montreal have more nonstop options to major European hubs.

Your provincial health plan covers very little abroad. Because river ships usually have no doctor on board, make sure your travel insurance includes medical care and transport home.

## Which suits you better?
River usually suits couples who love history, food and wine, dislike motion and want everything arranged. Ocean usually suits couples who like variety, want lower per-night costs, need more accessible cabins, or want warm-weather itineraries.

## When to book
River ships are small, so the best cabins on spring and fall dates often sell close to a year ahead. December Christmas-market sailings are popular with Canadians and sell early too. Ocean ships have more cabins but the same rule applies: book early for the cabin you want.
