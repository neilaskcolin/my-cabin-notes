A missing bag on embarkation day is stressful, but it is fixable. The ship will not wait for it, so the trick is to start the paperwork at the airport, tell the ship early, and have a carry-on that gets you through the first two days.

## Where do bags actually go missing?
Almost always on the flight, not at the cruise terminal. A tight connection in Toronto, Montreal or a US hub is the classic culprit, because the bag misses the transfer while you sprint for the gate. Bags handed to cruise porters at the pier occasionally go astray too, but those usually turn up on board within hours.

## What to do if your bag doesn't come off the plane
1. **Stay in the baggage hall.** Go to the airline's baggage desk and file a delayed baggage report before you leave. You will get a file reference; photograph it.
2. **Give the airline the ship, not your home.** List the ship name, cruise line, sailing dates, the ports in order and the line's port agent if you can find it in your documents. Ask whether they will forward the bag to a later port.
3. **Tell guest services as soon as you board.** Hand over the file reference. The ship's team can liaise with the port agent at upcoming ports, which an airline call centre cannot do on its own.
4. **Buy only essentials, and keep receipts.** Toiletries, underwear, a swimsuit, a layer for the deck.
5. **File the written claim on time.** For international flights, you have 21 days from getting the bag back to claim delay costs, and 7 days for damage.

> **Watch out:** a bag forwarded to a foreign port may need to clear local customs, and on a short Caribbean run there may be only one or two realistic chances to catch the ship. On a 7-night Alaska sailing, Juneau or Skagway mid-week is often the practical target.

## What the ship can and can't do
Guest services cannot hold the ship, and they cannot pay for your replacement clothes. What they can do is coordinate with the airline and port agents, deliver the bag to your cabin if it arrives, and point you to the onboard shops. Many ships sell basic toiletries, logo T-shirts and swimwear, though at resort prices.

Ask about laundry too. A quick wash of what you're wearing can fill the gap, and many lines have self-service launderettes or a pressing service (see [how cruise ship laundry works](/articles/cruise-ship-laundry-self-service-walkthrough/)).

## Who pays for what?

| Source | What it typically covers | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| The airline | Reasonable interim expenses; lost bags up to the Montreal Convention limit (1,519 SDR, about C$2,780, for flights since late 2024) and a refund of the bag fee | Claim deadlines in the airline's terms |
| Travel insurance | Essentials once a bag is delayed past a set number of hours; lost items up to a per-item and total limit | The delay threshold and receipts needed |
| Credit card | Delayed and lost baggage cover on some premium Canadian cards | Whether the flight was charged to the card |
| Home insurance | Some policies cover belongings while travelling | Your deductible, which may exceed the claim |

Claim from the airline first, then insurance for any gap. Insurers usually want the airline's file reference and a record of what the airline paid. The full picture of what cruise policies include is in [cruise insurance for Canadians](/articles/travel-insurance-cruise-canadians/).

## The carry-on that saves the week
Pack your first 48 hours in a carry-on: medication, passports, chargers, a swimsuit, one change of clothes, basic toiletries and, for Alaska, a fleece. Our [printable cruise packing list](/articles/cruise-packing-list-canadians-printable/) flags what belongs in it.

> **Canadian tip:** a cheap Bluetooth tracker in each checked bag lets you tell the airline exactly where it is, which speeds things up. Just check your airline's rules on where trackers and their batteries can go.

## How to make it less likely
- Fly the day before, so a missed bag has a whole day to catch up before the ship sails (see [flying in the day before vs same day](/articles/fly-day-before-cruise/)).
- Choose a direct flight or a generous connection.
- Put a name and phone number inside the bag, not just on the tag.
- Know your airline's limits, covered in [luggage rules when flying to a cruise](/articles/luggage-rules-flying-to-cruise/).

## Missing bags at the cabin door
If your flight bags arrived but never made it from the pier to your cabin, check with your cabin steward first, then guest services. Bags with a prohibited item, such as a power bar or an iron, are held for security, and you'll be asked to collect them and surrender the item.
