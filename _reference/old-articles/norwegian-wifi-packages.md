Wifi on Norwegian costs roughly US$25–40 per device per day at recent pricing, depending on whether you want a basic plan or one fast enough for streaming. Before buying anything, check your fare: Norwegian's Free at Sea promotion often includes wifi already.

## How much is wifi on Norwegian Cruise Line?
Norwegian sells two main tiers. The basic unlimited plan covers browsing, email, social media and messaging apps. The premium plan adds enough speed for streaming video and video calls. Both are priced per device per day, and a plan works on one device at a time.

Prices vary by ship and sailing, and pre-cruise prices are usually 10–30% lower than buying on board.

> **Quick math:** a couple who both want to stay connected for 7 nights at about US$30 per device per day pays around US$420, roughly C$565–590. One plan shared by logging in and out of a single account costs half, if you can take turns.

## Is wifi included with Free at Sea?
Often. Free at Sea, Norwegian's rotating bundle of extras, frequently includes wifi alongside drinks, specialty dining and excursion credit. Depending on the fare, that can be a block of minutes or a full package for the voyage.

Minutes go faster than you'd think, because the clock runs while you are logged in. If the bundle gives you minutes and you want to stay online every day, the paid upgrade to unlimited is usually the better buy. The service charges on Free at Sea perks are covered in [Norwegian Cruise Line gratuities](/articles/norwegian-gratuities-per-day/).

## What speeds to expect on Norwegian
Speeds have improved a lot since Norwegian moved to low-orbit satellite internet across much of the fleet, and newer ships generally have noticeably faster connections than older ones. Busy evenings and remote stretches can still slow things down. [How cruise ship internet actually works](/articles/cruise-ship-internet-how-it-works/) explains why.

On Alaska sailings, the Inside Passage's mountains and fjords can briefly interrupt the signal, so download maps and podcasts before you reach Glacier Bay or Tracy Arm.

## Should you buy wifi before you sail?
1. Check whether your Free at Sea perks include wifi, and whether it is minutes or unlimited.
2. Decide who needs it: one shared plan for messaging, or one plan each.
3. Choose basic unless someone must stream or take video calls.
4. Buy before sailing through your booking or the Norwegian app to lock the lower price.
5. Log in on embarkation day and test it before the ship leaves port.

### Phone roaming for Canadians
Put your phone in airplane mode with wifi on as soon as you board. The ship's own cellular network can bill your Canadian carrier at steep rates, even for background data. In Alaska ports such as Juneau and Ketchikan, a US roaming add-on from your carrier works. Seattle round trips usually call at Victoria, where your home plan works as normal. [Cruise wifi and phone roaming for Canadians](/articles/cruise-wifi-phone-roaming-canadians/) has the carrier details, and [using the cruise line app](/articles/using-cruise-line-app-before-during-trip/) covers what works without a paid plan.

## How Norwegian compares
| Line | System | Recent price per device per day | Included with |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Norwegian** | Free at Sea-linked | $25–40 USD | Often the Free at Sea bundle |
| [Royal Caribbean](/articles/royal-caribbean-wifi-packages/) | VOOM | $20–30 USD | Some promotions; some suites |
| [Carnival](/articles/carnival-wifi-packages/) | Hub plans | $20–30 USD | Occasional sales and bundles |

Norwegian's list price is the highest of the three, but it is also the line most likely to include wifi in the fare, through Free at Sea. Royal Caribbean and Carnival sell theirs mostly as an add-on, so compare what you would really pay, not just the daily rate.
