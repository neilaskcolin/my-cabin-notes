If wine is the main draw, choose a river cruise through a European wine region: you'll visit vineyards most days and drink regional wines at dinner. If food is the draw, look at premium ocean lines that build their reputation on dining, then add ports with strong food cultures. Either way, the table can genuinely be the point of the trip.

## Which river cruises are best for wine?
River ships dock close to vineyards and wine towns, and tastings are often part of the included excursions.

| Region | River | What you'll taste | Best months |
|---|---|---|---|
| Douro, Portugal | Douro | Port and Douro reds among terraced vineyards | April to October; harvest in September |
| Bordeaux, France | Garonne, Dordogne, Gironde | Médoc and Saint-Émilion reds | April to November |
| Burgundy and the Rhône | Saône and Rhône | Burgundy, Beaujolais, Côtes du Rhône; Lyon's food | April to October |
| Mosel and Rhine, Germany | Moselle and Rhine | Riesling from steep riverside slopes | April to October |
| Wachau, Austria | Danube | Grüner Veltliner and apricot specialties | April to October |

Harvest time in September and October is especially atmospheric, though also popular. For more on the format itself, see [river cruise vs ocean cruise for couples over 50](/articles/river-cruise-vs-ocean-cruise-over-50/) and [Danube and Rhine river cruises for Canadians](/articles/danube-rhine-river-cruises-canadians/).

## Which ocean lines put food first?
On premium and luxury lines, food is a main reason people book, not a bonus. Oceania is especially known for its dining, and several of its ships have hands-on cooking schools. Viking, Regent and Seabourn are also strong, with more of the experience included in the fare.

On mainstream lines, the best food is usually in the specialty restaurants, which cost extra. Compare dining options in [main dining room vs buffet vs specialty restaurants](/articles/main-dining-buffet-specialty-restaurants/).

### What to look for
- **Wine programmes:** sommelier-led tastings, pairing dinners or a dedicated wine bar.
- **Cooking classes:** hands-on kitchens rather than demonstrations only. [Cruises with real culinary programmes](/articles/cruise-ship-culinary-classes-cooking-demos/) covers who runs them.
- **Culinary excursions:** market tours, cooking classes ashore and winery visits instead of standard sightseeing.
- **Themed sailings:** guest chefs or winemakers on board for a specific voyage. These sell out, so watch for announcements.

## Pick ports with great food too
The ship is only half the story. Mediterranean itineraries through Spain, Italy, France and Portugal are an easy choice. Closer to home, a fall cruise through Atlantic Canada and New England means lobster, oysters and seafood chowder in almost every port; see [New England and Canada fall cruises](/articles/new-england-canada-fall-cruises/). Alaska has wild salmon and crab shacks in Juneau and Ketchikan.

## Bringing wine on board
Most lines let each adult bring a limited amount of wine on embarkation day, often one or two bottles, and some charge a corkage fee if you drink it in a restaurant. Lines differ on whether wine bought in port goes straight to your cabin or is held until the last night. Check [cruise line alcohol policies compared](/articles/cruise-line-alcohol-policies-compared/) before you pack.

> **Good to know:** coming home, the duty-free allowance is 1.5 litres of wine per adult after at least 48 hours away, roughly two bottles. You can bring more, but duty and provincial charges on the extra can make a bargain bottle expensive, so price it before you buy a case.

## Getting there from Canada
Most wine-region river cruises start in Europe, so you'll need a transatlantic flight. Toronto and Montreal have the most nonstop options, including seasonal flights to Portugal; travellers from Calgary, Edmonton, Vancouver and Winnipeg usually connect. River cruises are often one-way, so book open-jaw flights and add a night or two in Porto, Bordeaux or Lyon to eat well before boarding.

## Who these trips suit
They suit couples who plan holidays around restaurants, enjoy learning about wine and like a slower pace. They suit people less well if they want beach time, nightlife or lots of choice in the evening. If you are non-drinkers or have dietary needs, ask how menus adapt; [dietary needs at sea](/articles/dietary-needs-cruise-ships/) explains how ships handle them.

## Questions to ask before booking
Ask which wines are included with meals and which cost extra, how many tastings are in the excursion programme, whether a sommelier or guest winemaker is on your specific sailing, and how many guests each cooking class takes.
