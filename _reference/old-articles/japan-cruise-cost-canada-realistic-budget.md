A Japan cruise is one of the bigger trips a Canadian can take, and the fare is often the smaller part of the bill. Flights across the Pacific, a few nights in Tokyo or Yokohama, and the season you choose decide most of the total.

## How much does a Japan cruise cost from Canada?
Rough per-couple figures at recent prices for a 9-night round trip from Yokohama in an inside cabin, converted at about 1.37. Balconies typically add 50–80% to the fare line.

| Per couple, CAD | Lower end | Upper end |
|---|---|---|
| Cruise fare | C$2,750 | C$4,950 |
| Taxes and port fees | C$550 | C$1,100 |
| Return flights from Canada | C$2,200 | C$4,600 |
| Two hotel nights before sailing | C$400 | C$800 |
| Gratuities | C$400 | C$450 |
| Excursions, trains and admissions | C$600 | C$1,500 |
| Drinks and extras on board | C$300 | C$700 |
| Travel insurance | C$250 | C$700 |
| **Rough total** | **C$7,450** | **C$14,800** |

The lower end assumes a shoulder-season sailing, flights from Vancouver bought early, and exploring ports by train. The upper end reflects cherry blossom dates, flights from Eastern Canada and ship-run tours in most ports.

## The fare itself
At recent prices, inside cabins on a 7–10 night Japan sailing commonly run around US$1,000–1,800 per person, with cherry blossom departures at or above the top of that range. Itineraries usually start in Yokohama for Tokyo, often call at Kobe or Osaka for Kyoto, and visit smaller ports, sometimes with a stop in South Korea. [Japan cruises for Canadians](/articles/japan-cruise-canadians-what-to-know/) covers the typical routes.

### What changes the price
Season matters most. Late March to early April brings the blossoms and the highest fares, autumn foliage in November is a second peak, and Japan's Golden Week holidays from late April into early May make hotels pricey. Summer is hot and humid, and typhoon season, which peaks around August and September, can force port changes.

## Getting there from Canada

### From Western Canada
Vancouver has the most nonstop service to Tokyo, and Calgary has had nonstop flights too, at least seasonally. The flight is roughly ten hours westbound. Return economy fares often fall around C$1,100–1,800 per person when booked early.

### From Central and Eastern Canada
Toronto has nonstop flights to Tokyo, and travellers from Montreal, Edmonton or Winnipeg will usually connect. Expect roughly C$1,400–2,300 per person in economy, more in peak weeks.

### Skipping one flight
Ships moving between Alaska and Asia occasionally sail transpacific repositioning voyages between Vancouver and Japan in spring or fall. They usually run two weeks or more with many sea days, but replace one long flight and often price softly per night. [Repositioning cruises explained](/articles/repositioning-cruises-explained/) covers what to expect.

> **Canadian tip:** you cross the international date line flying west, so you land a calendar day later than you might expect. Book two hotel nights before the cruise. [Planning an overnight before a cruise](/articles/overnight-layover-before-cruise/) and [ship's time and jet lag](/articles/cruise-cabin-jet-lag-time-zones/) help with the timing.

## What you spend ashore
Japan is easier and often cheaper to explore independently than first-timers expect. Local trains and buses are efficient, and a rechargeable transit card covers most fares and convenience-store purchases. Cash is still used more than in Canada, and convenience-store ATMs widely accept foreign cards.

Ship tours to Kyoto or Mount Fuji are the convenient option and cost more, commonly US$100–250 per person. The national rail pass rarely pays off on a cruise, since the ship covers the long distances. [Booking excursions through the ship vs independently](/articles/cruise-excursion-through-ship-vs-independent/) explains the trade-off on timing and return guarantees.

> **Example:** a Vancouver couple on a 9-night November sailing pays about US$1,300 each for an inside cabin, finds flights at C$1,300 each, spends two nights in Yokohama and explores most ports by train. With tips, drinks and insurance they land around C$9,000–9,500. The same trip in cherry blossom season with a balcony could pass C$14,000.

## Insurance and money
Your provincial health plan covers very little in Japan or at sea, so buy travel medical cover that includes cruising and evacuation (see [cruise insurance in Canada](/articles/travel-insurance-cruise-canadians/)). Onboard accounts on most lines run in US dollars, so a no-foreign-transaction-fee card saves around 2.5% on everything you sign for. The currency choices are set out in [paying in CAD or USD](/articles/paying-cad-vs-usd-cruise/).

## Where the budget goes
Flights and season move the total far more than the cabin. If cherry blossoms are the dream, book a year ahead and accept the premium. If you simply want Japan, a November or early-summer sailing with flights bought early can save several thousand dollars per couple.
