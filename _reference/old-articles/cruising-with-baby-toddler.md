Yes, babies and toddlers can cruise, and many families find it easier than a hotel holiday because the room, food and entertainment are all within a few decks. What decides whether the week is lovely or long is a handful of rules about age, pools and childcare that you need to know before you book.

## How old does a baby have to be to cruise?
The usual minimum is 6 months on the day you sail. Itineraries with long stretches at sea, often three or more sea days in a row, raise that to 12 months on most lines. For Canadians, the practical split is:
- **Alaska from Vancouver or Seattle:** usually fine from 6 months.
- **Caribbean from Florida:** usually fine from 6 months on 3- to 7-night sailings.
- **Hawaii from Vancouver and transatlantic crossings:** typically 12 months, because of the long sea run.

Confirm the rule for your exact sailing, because lines word it differently.

## The pool rule that reorders everything
Children who are not toilet trained cannot use regular pools or hot tubs, even in a swim diaper. Crew do enforce it. Some family-focused ships have a separate splash zone designed for diapered babies; on the rest, water play means the cabin shower or a beach day in port. If your toddler loves water, this one feature should steer your choice of ship.

## Childcare for under-3s
Most kids clubs start at age 3, and many require children to be toilet trained. Below that, you are looking at a nursery or a parent-and-child programme.

| Line | What exists for under-3s |
|---|---|
| Royal Caribbean | Paid nursery for roughly 6 to 36 months on many ships |
| Disney | Paid nursery for roughly 6 months to 3 years |
| MSC | Paid Baby Care drop-off from 6 months on many ships, plus free parent-and-child Baby Time |
| Norwegian | Parent-and-child play sessions on some ships |
| Carnival | No nursery, but the main kids club accepts 2-year-olds |
| Princess, Celebrity, Holland America | Nothing formal until age 3 |

Nursery spots are limited and hourly fees apply, so book sessions as soon as your line opens reservations. For older children, see [kids clubs by age and cost](/articles/kids-clubs-cruise-ships-ages-costs/).

> **Good to know:** in-cabin babysitting is rare on big ships. The [cabin babysitting guide](/articles/cruise-ship-babysitting-in-cabin-service/) explains which lines offer any.

## Setting up the cabin
### What to request at booking
Cribs and bed rails are free but limited, so ask when you book and again a week before sailing. Not every cabin has a bathtub, and most do not, so ask if you rely on one.

### Which cabin works
A balcony gives parents somewhere to sit once the baby is asleep in a dark cabin. For families of four or more, two [connecting cabins](/articles/connecting-cabins-one-big-cabin-families/) often cost less than a suite and give everyone a bathroom.

## Packing and getting there
Onboard shops carry few diapers and little formula, at high prices. Bring the full supply plus extra for delays.

1. Pack diapers, wipes, formula or baby food for every day plus two spare.
2. Bring a soft carrier. Gangways and tender steps are hard with a stroller (see [bringing a stroller and car seat](/articles/bringing-stroller-car-seat-cruise/)).
3. Carry infant pain reliever, a thermometer and any prescriptions.
4. Get your baby a Canadian passport in good time, and a consent letter if one parent travels alone.
5. Fly in the day before. A Toronto–Fort Lauderdale or Winnipeg–Vancouver morning flight with a baby is stressful enough without a deadline.

> **Canadian tip:** your provincial plan covers almost nothing abroad. Make sure your travel medical policy names the baby, and check how it handles a child under one.

## A pace that works
Pick one anchor activity a day and protect nap times. Use the buffet or room service on cranky evenings, choose an early fixed dining time, and treat sea days as the real holiday. Docked ports beat tender ports for families, since you can wander back for a nap.

## Book it like a parent
Five booking decisions shape the week: a ship with the childcare you need, a crib requested early, a cabin with room to sit while the baby sleeps, dinner matched to bedtime, and an itinerary of docked ports. Get those right, and much of the stress goes away.
