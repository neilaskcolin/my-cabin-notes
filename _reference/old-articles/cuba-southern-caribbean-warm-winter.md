If your goal is guaranteed warmth in January or February, head south. The southern Caribbean sits closer to the equator than the Bahamas or Cozumel, so winter cold fronts that can cool Florida and Nassau rarely reach it. Cuba is a different kind of trip, and harder to find on a cruise than many Canadians expect.

## Southern Caribbean vs Cuba vs the classic routes
| Route | Typical length | Where it starts | Average January high | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Southern Caribbean | 7 nights from San Juan or Barbados, 10–14 from Florida | San Juan, Bridgetown, Fort Lauderdale, Miami | about 29–30°C | Dutch, French and British island mix, fewer mega-resorts |

Temperatures are long-term averages and vary year to year. For the classic routes, see [eastern vs western vs southern Caribbean](/articles/eastern-vs-western-caribbean-first-cruise/).

## Why the southern Caribbean wins in deep winter
The ports are spread out, so itineraries mix island days with sea days, which suits couples who like an unhurried pace. Typical calls include:

- **Aruba, Bonaire and Curaçao:** dry, sunny, and south of the main hurricane track.
- **Barbados and St. Lucia:** beaches, rum and dramatic volcanic peaks.
- **Grenada, Antigua, St. Kitts, Dominica and Martinique:** greener, smaller, often less crowded.

Many repeat cruisers who've done Cozumel and Grand Cayman find this the natural next step. You keep the reliable warmth without the long flight to Hawaii or the South Pacific.

> **Canadian tip:** starting in Barbados or San Juan avoids the long sail from Florida. Toronto has nonstop flights to Barbados in winter, and seasonal options from other Canadian cities come and go. Fly in the day before, and see [snow delay risk on winter flights](/articles/snow-delay-winter-flights-caribbean-cruise/) for why that buffer matters in January.

## Cruising to Cuba: what's realistic
Canadian rules don't restrict visits, and Cuba is a hugely popular resort destination for Canadians. Cruises are another matter. Since 2019, US rules have kept US-based lines out, which rules out most ships sailing from Florida. Cuban calls now appear mainly on a small number of European-owned or small-ship itineraries, and they change from season to season.

If you find one, Havana's old town, classic cars and music scene make a very different port day from a Caribbean beach stop.

> **Watch out:** Cuba has faced serious power cuts, fuel and food shortages in recent years, and the rules on money and cards change often. Check the Government of Canada's travel advice for Cuba before booking, bring enough cash, and assume cards linked to US banks won't work.

## When to go
Hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30, with peak risk from August to October. December through April is the reliable window, and it lines up with the months Canadians most want to escape. Mid-December and March break sailings cost more, so late January and early February are often the best-value weeks. Read [what happens if a hurricane threatens your cruise](/articles/what-happens-hurricane-cruise-caribbean/) if you're sailing near the edges of the season.

## What to check before booking
- Total vacation days: a 10–14 night southern loop from Florida needs nearly two weeks off plus travel days.
- Whether the Cuba stop is a full day or a few hours, and whether you'll need a visa or tourist card for it.
- How your flights work from your home city, especially for a San Juan or Barbados start.
- Travel insurance with emergency medical cover, since your provincial plan covers very little abroad.

## The warm-winter play in one line
For dependable heat with a slower pace, choose a southern Caribbean sailing between late January and April. Treat a Cuba call as a bonus if you can find one, not the reason for the trip.
