For Canadians, a cruise is usually a US-dollar purchase made in instalments over a year or more. The exchange rate on each payment date decides what you really pay, and the Canadian dollar has swung by more than 20 cents against the US dollar within the past five years.

## How much can the exchange rate change your cruise cost?
A simple rule helps: every one-cent move in the rate changes the cost by about C$10 for every US$1,000 you spend. Here is a couple with a US$3,000 fare and US$800 of onboard spending, US$3,800 in all.

| Exchange rate (CAD per USD) | Cost in Canadian dollars |
|---|---|
| 1.30 | about C$4,940 |
| 1.35 | about C$5,130 |
| 1.40 | about C$5,320 |
| 1.45 | about C$5,510 |

If the rate moves from 1.35 at booking to 1.42 by the time the balance and onboard account are settled, the same trip costs about C$270 more. Nothing the cruise line charges has changed. Add a typical card foreign-transaction fee of around 2.5% on top if your card charges one.

## When does your money actually convert?
A cruise is not one transaction. Each payment converts on its own date, which is why the timing matters.

| Payment | Roughly when | How to fix the rate |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit | At booking | Book in CAD, or pay from US dollars you already hold |
| Final balance | Commonly 75–120 days before sailing | A CAD booking fixes it; otherwise it floats until paid |
| Prepaid extras | Any time before sailing | Prepay gratuities, packages and excursions, in CAD where offered |
| Onboard account | Settled at the end of the cruise | Only partly; it converts when the charge posts to your card |
| Refunds | After a cancellation or unused refundable credit | Not fixable; they convert back at the rate on the refund date |

Deposit and final payment timing by line is covered in [cruise deposits and final payment dates](/articles/cruise-deposits-final-payment/).

> **Watch out:** refunds of US-dollar charges on a Canadian card convert back at the refund date's rate, and banks use different buy and sell rates. You can get back less than you paid even if the rate hasn't moved, and the foreign-transaction fee on the original charge is often not returned. Booking in CAD avoids that loss if you might cancel.

## How to manage currency risk, step by step
1. **Ask for both quotes.** Get the fare in CAD and in USD on the same day and compare at your card's real rate. The method is in [paying for a cruise in CAD or USD](/articles/paying-cad-vs-usd-cruise/).
2. **Decide how you will fund USD payments.** Options include a no-foreign-transaction-fee credit card, a US-dollar credit card or a US-dollar bank account, all widely offered by Canadian banks.
3. **Prepay what you are certain to buy.** Gratuities are the obvious one, and [prepaying gratuities as a Canadian](/articles/cruise-gratuities-canadians/) explains the options.
4. **Add a buffer to your onboard budget.** About 5% on the USD part covers a moderate swing without stress. [How much spending money to bring](/articles/how-much-spending-money-cruise/) helps set the base figure.
5. **Decline currency conversion on board.** If the ship offers to bill your account in CAD, say no and let your card convert, as explained in [how onboard payments work](/articles/how-onboard-payments-work-cruise/).

## Should you wait for a better exchange rate?
Nobody can reliably predict currency moves, and the cruise fare itself is likely to shift more than the rate over the same months. A popular sailing can rise by more than any exchange-rate gain while you wait. Book when the cruise suits you, then lock in what you can.

If you hold US dollars from work, a US property or a past trip, using them for USD payments removes the conversion entirely. Buying USD gradually ahead of a big trip is another way to average out the rate without trying to time it.

## The goal is certainty, not a bet
Fix the big pieces (fare and prepaid extras) at a rate you can live with, keep the floating part small, and budget a little slack for the onboard bill. That way a moving dollar changes your trip by tens of dollars, not hundreds.
