Canadian citizens can skip the visa and ESTA question entirely. What you do need for a cruise from Miami, Fort Lauderdale, Seattle or any other US port is a valid Canadian passport book, and the exceptions mostly affect people who aren't Canadian citizens.

## Why ESTA doesn't apply to you
ESTA is the online authorization for travellers from Visa Waiver Program countries, such as the UK, Australia and much of Europe. Canada isn't in that program because Canadian citizens have their own long-standing visa exemption for US visits. Cruise guides mentioning ESTA are written for international passengers.

The same exemption covers Alaska sailings from Vancouver, where US officers preclear you at Canada Place, and Caribbean sailings that call at the Bahamas, Mexico or other islands. Canadian passport holders don't need visas for the usual Caribbean stops.

## Which document do you need?
| Who you are | What you need to cruise from a US port |
|---|---|
| Canadian citizen flying to the port | Valid Canadian passport book |
| Canadian citizen with NEXUS | Passport still required; NEXUS may speed some lines |
| Canadian citizen driving to the port | Passport recommended; some lines list an enhanced driver's licence, but check |
| Permanent resident of Canada | Passport from your country of citizenship plus PR card, and an ESTA or US visa if your nationality needs one |
| Dual citizen | Use the passport you'll be travelling on consistently, and check it meets the line's validity rule |

Royal Caribbean, for example, states that a Canadian passport is required for guests travelling to the US by air. Other lines word it differently, so read your own line's document page.

## The NEXUS trap
NEXUS speeds up land and air crossings, so many cardholders assume it covers the ship. It doesn't replace a passport on cruise documents lists. A passport also matters if something goes wrong: if you miss the ship or need to fly home from a foreign port, you can't board a flight without one (see [what happens if you miss the ship](/articles/what-happens-miss-ship-port/)).

## Exceptions that catch people
- **Criminal records.** Some convictions, including impaired driving, can make a Canadian inadmissible to the US even though no visa is normally needed. A US waiver takes months, so sort it out before booking.
- **Long stays.** If you combine a cruise with a Florida winter, the US limits Canadian visitors to about six months, and recent rules require Canadians staying 30 days or more to register with US authorities. Check CBP's current guidance.
- **Unusual itineraries.** Sailings to Brazil, parts of South America or Asia may need visas even for Canadians.

> **Good to know:** Canadian children need their own passports to fly to a US port, whatever their age. Some lines accept a birth certificate for young children on certain sailings, but a passport avoids any doubt.

## A five-minute document check
1. Confirm every traveller's passport is valid for at least six months after the cruise ends.
2. Make sure the name on the booking matches the passport exactly.
3. Read your line's travel documents page for your departure port.
4. If anyone isn't a Canadian citizen, check their US entry requirements and apply for an ESTA or visa early.
5. Complete online check-in with passport details (see [cruise check-in documents for Canadians](/articles/cruise-check-in-online-documents/)).
