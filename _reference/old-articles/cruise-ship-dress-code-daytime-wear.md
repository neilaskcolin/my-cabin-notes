During the day, a cruise ship dresses like a beach resort. You won't be judged for shorts at breakfast or a sundress at lunch. There are only a handful of real rules, and they're about swimwear, footwear and what you wear ashore.

## What do people actually wear during the day?
On a warm-weather sailing, most passengers spend the day in shorts, T-shirts, linen shirts, sundresses and sandals, with swimwear underneath. Sea days look like a pool party. Port days look like any tourist town. On cooler routes, jeans and a fleece are the uniform.

Daytime is the easiest part of the cruise wardrobe. The [formal and dressy night guide](/articles/formal-nights-what-to-wear/) covers evenings, which is where the real decisions are.

## Where the rules apply, venue by venue
Policies vary a little by line, but these norms hold on almost every mainstream ship.

| Venue | Fine | Not fine |
|---|---|---|
| Pool deck and sun loungers | Swimwear, bare feet | Rarely an issue |
| Buffet and pool grill | Swimwear with a cover-up, sandals | Dripping wet swimwear, bare feet |
| Main dining room at lunch | Shorts, T-shirts, sundresses, sandals | Swimwear, bare chests, bare feet |
| Casino, shops, theatre | Casual clothes and footwear | Wet swimwear |
| Spa and gym | Workout gear, trainers | Sandals in the gym |
| Afternoon tea or specialty lunch | Smart casual | Swimwear, gym gear |

### The cover-up rule
The most common daytime reminder is about swimwear indoors. A shirt, wrap or loose dress thrown over your swimsuit solves it. Pack one light cover-up per person and keep it in your pool bag.

### Footwear
Bare feet belong on the pool deck. Inside, wear flip-flops at minimum. On a rolling sea day, choose sandals with a back strap: wet stairs and pool deck tiles get slick.

## What to wear on port days
Comfort beats style ashore. Wear broken-in walking shoes, bring a hat, and dress for the weather at the port rather than on the ship.

### Religious and cultural sites
Some churches, cathedrals and temples ask for covered shoulders and knees, particularly in Mediterranean ports. A light scarf or a pair of long trousers in your day bag avoids being turned away. Some Caribbean islands also frown on swimwear in town centres, so cover up when you leave the beach.

### Excursions with gear rules
Ziplines, ATVs and hiking tours need closed-toe shoes. Snorkelling trips need reef-friendly sunscreen at some sites. Read the excursion description the night before.

## Dressing for Alaska or a chilly morning
Daytime in Alaska is about layers, not dress codes. A waterproof jacket, a fleece and a toque cover scenic cruising on deck, and you peel layers off in port. [What people actually wear on an Alaska cruise](/articles/alaska-cruise-dress-code/) has the specifics.

> **Canadian tip:** leaving Toronto or Calgary in February for a Florida sailing, you'll want shorts by day two. Pack them in your carry-on, since checked bags often reach your cabin only in the evening.

## Packing appropriately for relaxed daytime wear
Daytime clothing is the part of a cruise you can plan in five minutes: casual clothes for half the trip, one cover-up, sandals plus walking shoes, and a layer for the air conditioning. Save the thinking for dinner.
