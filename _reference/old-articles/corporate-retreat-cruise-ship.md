A cruise can be a surprisingly practical retreat venue. Rooms, meals and evening entertainment come in one price, nobody wanders off to their own house at night, and the ship does the travelling between activities. The trade-offs are limited meeting space, patchier internet than an office, and a tax treatment you need to sort out before you book.

## Cruise vs resort: what a retreat costs
Here's an illustrative comparison for a 4-night retreat, per person, at recent prices. Your numbers will depend on the ship, the resort and the season.

| Cost line | 4-night cruise | 4-night resort |
|---|---|---|
| Room | Included in fare | C$800–1,800 |
| Main meals | Included in fare | C$400–700 |
| Evening entertainment | Included | Extra, or none |
| Cruise fare, inside or oceanview, shared | US$500–1,000 (roughly C$700–1,400) | Not applicable |
| Taxes, port fees, gratuities | Roughly C$200–350 | Resort fees, taxes and tips extra |
| Meeting room | Often available to booked groups; AV may cost extra | Daily room hire plus AV |
| Drinks | Extra, unless packaged | Extra |
| Getting there | Flights to the port | Flights plus transfers |

> **Quick math:** a 20-person team in shared balcony cabins on a 4-night sailing might pay around US$900 per person for the fare and fees. A comparable resort with meals and meeting room hire can easily pass C$1,500 each before drinks and transport. The cruise often wins on price, but compare real quotes for your dates.

Sharing cabins cuts the cruise cost sharply. Single occupancy, which many companies prefer for colleagues, can nearly double the fare because of the single supplement.

## What meeting space ships actually have
- **Conference rooms** on many larger ships, sized from small boardrooms to rooms for 100 or more.
- **Private dining rooms**, which can work as boardrooms between meals.
- **Lounges and bars** that are quiet in the morning and can sometimes be reserved before they open.
- **Theatres**, available to large groups at times when no show is scheduled.

Space is first come, first served on each sailing, so reserve it when you book the block, not later. Ask what AV is included, whether you can bring your own, and what private coffee breaks cost.

Ship wifi has improved a lot on many ships, but plan for video calls to be possible rather than guaranteed. [How cruise ship internet works](/articles/cruise-ship-internet-how-it-works/) explains the limits.

## A sample retreat day
### Sea day
- 8:00 a.m. breakfast together in a reserved section
- 9:00 a.m. to noon: working session in the conference room
- Afternoon free: pool, spa or a team activity like trivia
- 7:00 p.m. team dinner, then a show

### Port day
Leave it open, or book one group excursion. Trying to hold sessions while the ship is docked somewhere beautiful rarely works.

## Who a cruise retreat suits, and who it doesn't
It suits teams of roughly 10 to 60 who want strategy sessions in the morning and real time together in the afternoon. Being in one place with no commuting does a lot for team bonding.

It suits less well a team that needs full days of workshops, reliable video links to head office, or a big plenary room for several hundred people every day. It's also a hard sell if many staff have young children at home, get seasick, or would rather not share a cabin. Ask before you book.

## Sailings that suit Canadian teams
Short 3–5 night sailings are the sweet spot for retreats. Western Canadian teams can look at spring and fall coastal sailings from Vancouver, or a 7-night Alaska round trip for a combined retreat and reward. Eastern teams usually fly to Florida for Bahamas sailings, where flights from Toronto and Montreal are about three hours. Shoulder seasons give lower fares and quieter ships. Every guest will need a valid passport for sailings that call at US or Caribbean ports.

## The business-expense question
> **Watch out:** don't promise anyone a full write-off. Canadian rules on meals, entertainment and conferences held outside Canada can limit what's deductible, and personal extras never are. Keep a written agenda, attendance records and a clear split between business and leisure costs, and get your accountant's view before you book.

If the trip is partly a reward for top performers, the tax picture changes again. Rewards are covered in [booking a cruise as a corporate incentive](/articles/corporate-incentive-reward-cruise-booking/).
