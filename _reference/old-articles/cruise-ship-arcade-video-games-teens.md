The arcade is the one place on a cruise ship where a teenager can quietly run up a bill. Games cost money on every mainstream line, they're charged to a card that doesn't feel like cash, and nobody sees the total until the final statement.

## How do cruise ship arcades charge?
Most arcades work like a mall arcade with the coin slot replaced by a card reader. Depending on the ship, you either tap the teen's cruise card on each machine, or buy a game card loaded with credit that's billed to your onboard account. Each play is typically around US$1–3 at recent prices, with bigger racing or VR games at the top end.

Some ships run ticket-redemption games where winnings trade for small prizes. As at home, the prizes are worth far less than the credit spent to win them.

## What's free for teens on board?
Plenty, which is the best argument for not living in the arcade:
- **Teen club:** usually has consoles, a lounge and organized events, all included (see [kids clubs on cruise ships](/articles/kids-clubs-cruise-ships-ages-costs/))
- **Sports and activities:** basketball and pickleball courts, mini golf, and on some big ships laser tag, waterslides or climbing walls
- **Trivia and game shows:** the daily programme lists teen-only and family sessions
- **Movies and pool time:** outdoor screens and late-night teen events

The casino is off limits. The minimum age is typically 18, and higher on some sailings.

## How to set a teen spending limit
1. **Agree a total before you sail**, written down, so there's no debate on day three.
2. **Set the limit at guest services or in the app** on the first afternoon. Most big lines let parents cap a minor's card or switch off charging.
3. **Check the onboard account daily** in the app. Arcade charges show as separate lines.
4. **Top up deliberately**, if at all, rather than lifting the cap.

> **Quick math:** at US$2 a play, 10 games a day for 7 days is US$140, or roughly C$190–200 once the exchange rate and any card foreign-transaction fee are added. A US$70 cap halves it without banning the arcade.

## Bringing their own games
A handheld console is a good sea-day backup and costs nothing on board. Download games, updates and films at home, because ship wifi is slow and expensive for large files (see [how cruise ship internet works](/articles/cruise-ship-internet-how-it-works/)). Pack a charger, but not a power bar, since most lines ban them.

## Planning teen entertainment budgets for your cruise
Arcade size varies a lot. Big family ships on Royal Caribbean, Carnival, Norwegian and Disney have full arcades, while adult-leaning ships may have a few machines or none. Check your ship's deck plan, agree a budget, and let the free activities do most of the work.
