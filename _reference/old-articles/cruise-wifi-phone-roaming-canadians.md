The phone left on at sea is the classic surprise bill. Turn on airplane mode at sail-away, switch wifi back on, and decide whether you need the ship's internet package or can wait for port days.

## Why your Canadian roaming plan stops working at sea
Once land falls away, phones connect to the ship's own cellular-at-sea system, which relays calls and data by satellite. The daily travel passes from Rogers, Bell, Telus and their sister brands, typically around C$12–16 a day for the US, are built for countries, not ships. Maritime use is usually billed separately, per minute and per megabyte.

A phone quietly backing up photos overnight can run up hundreds of dollars before breakfast. Some carriers do sell cruise or maritime add-ons, so call yours and ask directly before you leave.

> **Watch out:** on an Alaska sailing from Vancouver or a Caribbean sailing near the coast, your phone can flip between land towers and the ship's network without warning. Airplane mode is the only reliable protection.

## The two-minute routine
1. Before boarding, download maps, playlists, shows and your boarding documents.
2. At sail-away, switch airplane mode on and leave it on whenever the ship is at sea.
3. Turn wifi and Bluetooth back on manually. They work in airplane mode.
4. Install the cruise line's app, which usually offers free onboard messaging or a cheap chat plan (see [using the cruise line app](/articles/using-cruise-line-app-before-during-trip/)).
5. In port, turn cellular back on only if your plan or eSIM covers that country. Alaska ports count as the US.

Many big lines have moved to low-orbit satellite service such as Starlink, which has made browsing and video calls far more usable than a few years ago. Tiers still matter: a basic plan may handle messaging and email but block or throttle streaming.

> **Quick math:** a family from Edmonton buying two streaming plans at US$22 a day for a 7-night cruise pays about US$308, or roughly C$420–430. One shared plan, rotated between phones, halves that.

## Cheaper ways to stay connected
- **Book a fare that bundles wifi.** Celebrity All Included, Princess Plus, Holland America Have It All and Norwegian's Free at Sea offers can include it. On the lines above, Disney is the one where it is not bundled.
- **Buy one device and share it.** Most plans allow one login at a time, so log out and swap.
- **Use port wifi.** Cafés, libraries and cruise terminals are fine for a daily upload and messages home.
- **Use an eSIM for port days.** A US or Mexico data eSIM can cost less than a daily pass if you have several port stops.
- **Go offline.** A week with only port-day check-ins costs nothing.

## Before you sail
Wifi is one of the extras that appears in booking promotions, and free wifi sailings come up on several lines. Ask whether your dates carry one before paying the onboard price, and check your carrier's maritime rates so nothing arrives on your next bill.
