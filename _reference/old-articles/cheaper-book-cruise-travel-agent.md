Not usually cheaper on the fare itself. Cruise lines set the price and pay agents a commission out of their own share, so the sticker is generally the same everywhere. The difference shows up in what comes attached to that fare and in who looks after the booking afterwards.

## Why the fare is usually the same
Cruise lines keep tight control over how their fares are advertised, so agencies compete mainly on extras and service rather than the headline price. The agent's commission comes from the line's margin, not from a fee added to your bill.

That means "cheaper" is the wrong test. The useful question is what each channel adds to the same fare.

## What each channel typically offers
| | Booking direct with the line | Booking through an agent |
|---|---|---|
| Base fare | Line's published price | Usually the same |
| Extras | Line-wide promotions | Same promotions, sometimes plus agency onboard credit or group perks |
| Group rates | Through the line's group desk | Agencies often hold group space on popular sailings |
| Repricing after booking | You must spot drops and ask | A good agent may watch for you |
| Problems and changes | Call centre | A named contact, if the agent is good |
| Casino and targeted loyalty offers | Usually direct only | Sometimes not available |
| Fees | None | Usually none; some charge service fees |
| Loyalty points | Yes | Yes |

## Where an agent can save you money
### Extras on the same fare
Agencies sometimes add onboard credit, prepaid gratuities or other perks on top of the line's own promotion. On a 7-night sailing, US$100–300 of extra onboard credit is roughly C$135–420 of real spending money.

### Group space
Agencies often reserve blocks of cabins on popular sailings. Those blocks can carry perks or a slightly better rate, and occasionally still have cabins when a category looks scarce online. [How the 8-cabin group rule works](/articles/group-cruise-8-cabin-rule/) explains group perks.

### Watching the price
Fares move after booking. On many fares, you can move onto a lower price before final payment, but only if somebody notices. [Asking for price protection](/articles/cruise-price-protection-asking-for-it/) covers how to request it, whoever booked you.

## When booking direct makes sense
- **Casino and personal loyalty offers** sent to you by name, which often must be booked with the line.
- **Simple bookings** where you are happy to handle every change yourself.
- **Occasional direct-only promotions**, which some lines run from time to time.
- **People who enjoy the admin** and check prices regularly.

All are legitimate reasons. Direct booking is not a mistake; it just puts the after-sale work on you.

> **Good to know:** Ontario (TICO), Quebec (Office de la protection du consommateur) and British Columbia (Consumer Protection BC) license travel sellers, and Ontario and Quebec also run compensation funds that can help if a registered seller fails. Check registration before paying anyone you don't know.

## The downsides of agents, honestly
Quality varies widely. Some agents know cruising deeply; others are generalists. Some agencies only sell a handful of lines they have commercial deals with, so ask which lines they work with. And a few charge service fees, which should be stated up front.

## How to compare quotes properly
1. **Choose one sailing**, one cabin category and the same fare type, refundable or not.
2. **Get the total from the line's site**, including taxes, fees and any included extras.
3. **Ask an agent for a quote on exactly the same booking.**
4. **Compare what is bundled**: onboard credit, gratuities, drinks, wifi, and whether any fee applies.
5. **Ask how each handles price drops and changes** before final payment.
6. **Check the currency.** Canadian agencies may quote in CAD, while some line sites default to USD. See [paying in CAD vs USD](/articles/paying-cad-vs-usd-cruise/).

If you already booked direct, many lines let you move the booking to an agent within a set period, often 30–60 days, and keep your cabin and fare. [Can you transfer a cruise booking to a travel agent](/articles/transfer-cruise-booking-to-travel-agent/) explains the rules.

## The honest test
Price the same sailing both ways and compare totals, not fares. If the agent's quote matches the line's price and adds extras or help you value, it wins. If it doesn't, booking direct is perfectly sensible. For a related trick on fares, see [what a cruise consolidator fare is](/articles/what-is-cruise-consolidator-fare/).
