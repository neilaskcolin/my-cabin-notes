For years, every trip was planned around school calendars, kids' clubs and someone's picky eating. Your first cruise as empty nesters can be planned around just two people. The best choice is usually a calmer ship, an itinerary you've been postponing, and a date nobody with school-age children can take.

## Start with timing: the hidden perk
The first real freedom is the calendar. Families are locked into March break, July, August and the winter holidays, which are the most expensive weeks to cruise. You aren't.

September is a natural fit: the kids have just left for school, residence or a new job, and fares fall once Canadian schools are back. May works well too. The ships carry far fewer children, and you'll often pay noticeably less for the same cabin than in peak summer.

> **Example:** a couple from Winnipeg who used to cruise every July with the kids book a 7-night Alaska sailing from Vancouver in mid-September instead. A balcony costs noticeably less than the same cabin would in July, and the passengers are mostly couples. ## Rethinking the cabin and the budget
After years of connecting cabins or a family suite, one well-placed cabin for two feels like a luxury. Put the savings into a balcony on a scenic route, or an extra specialty dinner or two.

Some empty nesters find the budget is tighter than expected, with tuition or a child's first apartment in the mix. If so, an inside cabin on a good ship in shoulder season beats a balcony on a peak-season sailing you're anxious about paying for.

> **Canadian tip:** if the kids are away for the first time, they may want to reach you. Check your phone plan's cruise roaming rates, or buy a small wifi package for messaging, rather than switching on data at sea. [Cruise wifi and phone roaming for Canadians](/articles/cruise-wifi-phone-roaming-canadians/) explains the options.

## Easing into travelling as two again
The first trip without children can feel oddly quiet at first. A few habits help:

- **Plan one new thing together,** such as a cooking class, a wine tasting or a kayak excursion, that neither of you has done before.
- **Plan one thing each,** and meet up afterward with stories to share.
- **Leave empty time.** Lingering over breakfast or reading on deck is the point, not a gap to fill.
- **Agree on a check-in plan with the kids** before you go, so you aren't watching your phone all day.
- **Sort the house,** pets and mail early. Adult children home for the summer can sometimes house-sit.

## Choosing your first trip as two
Start with the calendar, pick the itinerary you've been postponing, then choose a ship that matches your idea of a good evening. Talk it through together first: the best choice is often the one neither of you would have suggested while the kids were coming along.
