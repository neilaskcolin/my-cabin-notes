Two things decide what a Royal Caribbean cancellation costs you: the type of deposit you paid and how close you are to sailing. Final payment falls about 75 days out on short sailings and about 90 days out on longer ones, and that date is the line between a free exit and a sliding penalty.

## When is Royal Caribbean final payment due?
At recent policy, the balance on a 3- or 4-night Bahamas getaway is due about 75 days before sailing, and on a 7-night Caribbean or Alaska cruise about 90 days before. Some holiday sailings and suite bookings can run earlier. The date printed on your confirmation is the one that counts, so put it in your calendar the day you book, as suggested in [cruise deposits and final payment dates explained](/articles/cruise-deposits-final-payment/).

For Canadian families this matters most around March break. A 7-night sailing in mid-March has a final payment date in mid-December, right when holiday spending peaks.

## Refundable vs non-refundable deposit fares
Royal Caribbean usually offers both fare types on the same cabin, and the choice shapes everything that follows.

- **Refundable deposit:** cancel any time before final payment and get everything back. You can usually move to another sailing before final payment too.
- **Non-refundable deposit (NRD):** a lower fare, but the deposit is lost if you cancel, and switching ship or date costs a per-person change fee.
- **Both:** once final payment passes, the same penalty schedule applies.

The trade-off is laid out in [cruise fare classes explained](/articles/cruise-fare-classes-refundable/). If your plans could still change, the refundable fare is usually worth the small premium.

## Royal Caribbean cancellation penalties after final payment
Penalties are a percentage of the total fare and step up as sailing approaches. The bands below show the typical shape for a 7-night sailing; exact dates differ for shorter cruises, so check the schedule on your booking.

| When you cancel (7-night sailing) | Typical penalty |
|---|---|
| Before final payment (about 90+ days) | None on refundable fares; deposit on NRD fares |
| First weeks after final payment | The deposit, rising to about a quarter of the fare |
| About 60 to 31 days out | About half |
| About 30 to 15 days out | About three-quarters |
| 14 days or fewer | 100% |

Government taxes and fees are generally refunded even when the fare is not.

> **Quick math:** a couple from Winnipeg with a C$5,000 March sailing who cancel 40 days out would typically lose about half, roughly C$2,500. With trip-cancellation insurance and a covered reason, such as a hospital stay, that amount is claimable.

## Insurance for Canadians: the real safety net
The penalty window is exactly what trip-cancellation cover is for. Canadian policies from your bank card, employer or a standalone insurer often include it, but check the limit covers your full fare. Provincial health plans pay almost nothing outside Canada, so medical cover matters too. ## Cancelling one guest, or missing the payment date
Removing one person from a two-person cabin after final payment can leave the other paying a single supplement, so ask for a quote before cancelling half a booking.

> **Watch out:** if the balance isn't paid by the deadline, Royal Caribbean can cancel the booking and release the cabin. Any promotion attached goes with it, and on an NRD fare so does the deposit.

Fares booked in Canadian dollars are refunded in Canadian dollars. If you paid in US dollars on a Canadian card, a refund converts back at the day's rate, which may not match what you paid.

## Choosing the fare with cancellation in mind
The refundable deposit costs a little more and buys a free exit until final payment. If a year of life sits between booking and sailing, it is usually the better choice, and pairing it with insurance bought at deposit time covers the stretch after final payment too.
