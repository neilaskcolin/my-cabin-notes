Yes, you can bring snacks on a cruise, as long as they are sealed, store-bought and do not need cooking. With so much food included on board, most people bring very little, but there are good reasons to pack a few favourites.

## What you can and can't bring
Exact rules vary by line and change from time to time, so treat this as the typical pattern and confirm with your own line.

| Item | Usually allowed? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Granola bars, crackers, nuts, chips | Yes | Sealed and commercially packaged |
| Candy and chocolate | Yes | Keep chocolate out of the sun on deck |
| Baby food and formula | Yes | Bring enough for the trip |
| Medically required foods | Yes | Mention them to the line in advance |
| Tea bags, instant coffee sachets | Usually | Cabins often have no kettle |
| Homemade baking or sandwiches | Usually not | Food safety rules |
| Fresh fruit, vegetables, meat, cheese | Usually not | Food safety and agricultural rules |
| Kettles, hot plates, rice cookers | No | Fire risk; confiscated at security |

Alcohol follows separate, stricter rules on every line, so do not assume the snack allowance covers it. [Cruise line alcohol policies compared](/articles/cruise-line-alcohol-policies-compared/) sets them out.

## Good reasons to pack your own
- **Dietary needs.** Ships handle allergies and special diets well when told in advance, but a few safe snacks of your own add peace of mind. See [how ships handle dietary needs](/articles/dietary-needs-cruise-ships/).
- **Children.** A familiar cereal or snack can save a meltdown, especially on embarkation day. [Cruising with a baby or toddler](/articles/cruising-with-baby-toddler/) covers what else to pack.
- **Excursion days.** A long tour may not include lunch, and port food costs extra.
- **Late nights in the cabin.** Many ships have a 24-hour venue, but a bar in your bag beats a walk across the ship. [Room service on cruises](/articles/room-service-cruise-free-cost/) explains what is free.

## Taking food ashore
This is where people get caught out. Many ports, including Hawaii and many Caribbean islands, restrict bringing fresh fruit, meat and plant products ashore. An apple from the buffet in your day bag can mean a fine.

> **Watch out:** packaged snacks are fine for port days, but eat buffet fruit on board. When you fly home, declare any food you are carrying to the CBSA, and check that it is allowed back into Canada.

## How much to bring
For most people, a small bag is plenty: a few bars and a packet of crackers per person for excursion days. Remember that the fare already includes the buffet, main dining room and several casual venues, set out in [what's included in a cruise fare](/articles/whats-included-cruise-fare/). Luggage space is limited, and the [what not to pack list](/articles/what-not-to-pack-for-a-cruise/) explains which items will be taken away at security.

## Checking your line's current policy
If you rely on your own food for medical or dietary reasons, check your line's policy before you pack and tell the line about your needs in advance. That way security sees exactly what it expects, and the dining team can cover the rest.
