A cruise fare buys more than a hotel room and less than the brochure photos suggest. The cabin, most of the food and nearly all the entertainment are covered; tips, drinks, wifi and anything ashore are not, on mainstream lines at least.

## What's included in a mainstream cruise fare?
This applies to lines such as Royal Caribbean, Carnival, Norwegian, MSC and Princess on a standard fare. Prices are at recent rates and vary by ship.

| Category | Included | Usually extra |
|---|---|---|
| Cabin | Your cabin, daily housekeeping, towels, toiletries | Upgrades, late check-out |
| Food | Main dining room, buffet, pizza, grill and deli-style venues | Specialty restaurants, about US$30–80 per person |
| Drinks | Tap water, drip coffee, tea, some juices and iced tea | Alcohol, soda, specialty coffee, bottled water |
| Entertainment | Theatre shows, live music, comedy, trivia, movies | A few premium attractions on the biggest ships |
| Facilities | Pools, hot tubs, gym, jogging track, daytime kids clubs | Spin or yoga classes, thermal suites, late-night group babysitting |
| Room service | Continental breakfast on many lines | Most other items, by delivery fee or per item |
| Service | | Gratuities, about US$16–25 per person per day |
| Connectivity | | Wifi, about US$15–40 per device per day |
| Ashore | Getting on and off the ship, tenders | Excursions, port meals, taxis |

For the finer print, see [which drinks are included by default](/articles/cruise-drink-menu-what-included-basics/) and [what room service costs](/articles/room-service-cruise-free-cost/).

## The extras that add up fastest
- **Gratuities.** Added daily per person, children included on most lines. On a 7-night sailing that is US$110–175 each. [Prepaying gratuities as a Canadian](/articles/cruise-gratuities-canadians/) explains when it helps.
- **Drinks.** A cocktail runs about US$12–16 plus an automatic service charge of around 18–20%. A couple who each have two drinks a day spend US$300–400 over a week.
- **Wifi.** Easy to skip on a sea-day-light itinerary, harder if you work or have kids at home.
- **Specialty dining.** One or two dinners out is normal; the main dining room is good enough that many people never pay for one.
- **Shore excursions.** Usually the biggest single extra. In Alaska, a whale-watching trip alone can be US$150–250 per person.

## Taxes and port fees: in the price or not?
Port fees and government taxes are charged per passenger and are often shown separately from the cruise fare. On a 7-night sailing they commonly add US$150–300 per person, depending on the ports. Always check whether a quote is "fare only" or "fare plus taxes and fees", as explained in [why your cruise costs more than the advertised price](/articles/taxes-port-fees-cruise-fare-breakdown/).

> **Quick math:** a couple on a 7-night mainstream cruise who skip packages but pay standard tips, buy a few drinks, take two excursions each and get one specialty dinner can add US$1,000–1,600 to the fare. That is roughly C$1,400–2,200, before flights.

## How inclusions change by type of line
The further up the price ladder you go, the more is folded into the fare.

- **Mainstream lines** include the essentials listed above and sell everything else separately, with frequent promotions bundling drinks, wifi or onboard credit.
- **Premium lines** such as Celebrity, Princess and Holland America offer bundled fares that add drinks, wifi and sometimes gratuities for a daily premium. Check exactly what each bundle contains, since names and contents change.
- **Adults-only Virgin Voyages** includes gratuities, basic wifi, soft drinks and all its restaurants in the fare.
- **Luxury lines** such as Regent Seven Seas, Seabourn and Silversea generally include drinks, gratuities and wifi, and Regent includes many excursions too. [What the luxury premium buys](/articles/luxury-lines-compared-extra-spend/) breaks this down.

"All-inclusive" means very different things depending on who is saying it, which is why [what all-inclusive means on a cruise](/articles/what-does-all-inclusive-mean-cruise/) is worth reading before you compare a mainstream deal with a luxury fare.

> **Canadian tip:** onboard charges post in US dollars. A credit card with no foreign transaction fee saves about 2.5% on everything you sign for, which adds up on a week's worth of tips and drinks.

## Compare the week, not the ticket
Price each option the same way: fare, taxes and fees, gratuities, your realistic drinks, wifi and excursions, side by side. A pricier fare with tips and drinks included regularly beats a cheap fare plus extras, and the same cabin with prepaid gratuities or onboard credit attached is a different product from the bare fare.
