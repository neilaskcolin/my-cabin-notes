Disney's dress code is relaxed: cruise casual every evening, one optional dress-up night on most sailings, and Pirate Night, which is about fun rather than formality. Only the adult-only restaurants ask for anything smarter.

## What does cruise casual mean on Disney?
Cruise casual is the baseline every night in the rotational dining rooms. Think trousers, jeans, capris, skirts, sundresses, polo shirts and nice tops. Swimwear, tank tops and bare feet are out, and shorts are more accepted on children than adults.

Because you rotate between three themed restaurants, the setting changes but the dress code does not. On the Disney Wish, a night in Worlds of Marvel feels very different from a night in 1923, yet the same polo shirt works in both.

## The optional dress-up night
Most sailings list one evening as formal or semi-formal. Guests who enjoy it wear a suit, sports jacket or cocktail dress, and kids often appear in princess dresses or little tuxedos. Ship photographers set up backdrops that night, so it is the evening for a family portrait.

Nobody is turned away in cruise casual. Many families skip the formal clothes entirely.

> **Good to know:** princess dresses and character costumes are welcome at dinner for children any night. Disney's Bibbidi Bobbidi Boutique, found on several ships, does paid princess and knight makeovers, often booked for the dress-up night.

## Pirate Night
Pirate Night is the evening most families remember. The dining rooms serve a pirate-themed menu, and later there is a deck party with characters. On many sailings it ends with fireworks at sea. A pirate bandana is usually left in your stateroom, and costumes run from that bandana to full pirate regalia. Adults join in as much as children.

On an Alaska sailing from Vancouver, plan for a cool evening on deck. A striped shirt under a dark fleece or rain jacket still reads as pirate.

## Dress codes for Palo, Remy and Enchanté
The adult-only restaurants have their own code, every night of the cruise.

| Venue | What to wear |
|---|---|
| Rotational dining rooms | Cruise casual; optional dress-up on the formal night |
| Palo (every ship) | Collared shirt and dress trousers, or a dress or smart separates; no shorts, flip-flops or swimwear |
| Remy (Dream and Fantasy) and Enchanté (Disney Wish) | Dressiest on board; a jacket fits in well |
| Buffet and pool deck | Casual; cover-up over swimwear |

Rules on items such as dark jeans have changed over time, so check the current code when you book your adult dining reservation.

## How to pack for a Disney week
A typical 7-night sailing has five or six plain cruise-casual evenings, one optional dress-up night and one Pirate Night. Your Navigator app shows which nights are which.

1. Check the theme nights for your sailing in the app or your booking.
2. Pack cruise casual for every evening as the base.
3. Add one smarter outfit per adult for the dress-up night or Palo.
4. Add something pirate for everyone, even if it is just a striped top.
5. For Alaska, swap some summer clothes for layers and a waterproof jacket.

Disney also runs Halloween on the High Seas sailings in the autumn, which overlap Canadian Thanksgiving, and costumes are part of the fun.
