The right line for a reunion depends less on the itinerary than on who's coming: their ages, their budgets, and how much time everyone wants to spend together. Here's how the main lines stack up for groups of 15 to 40 relatives.

## Family reunion cruise lines at a glance
| Line | Best for | Kids clubs from about | Big-table dining | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Royal Caribbean | Mixed ages, 15–40 people | Age 3 (nursery on some ships) | Set-time dining handles large tables | Busy ships; extras add up |
| Carnival | Budget-minded, younger families | Age 2 | Set times available | Fewer quiet spaces for older relatives |
| Disney | Families with children under 12 | Age 3 (nursery from 6 months) | Rotational dining keeps the group together | Highest fares |
| Princess and Holland America | Grandparent-led, Alaska | Age 3 | Tables of 8–10 comfortably | Fewer teen attractions |
| Norwegian | Groups happy to eat in clusters | Age 3 | No set times; big tables need booking | Hard to guarantee one long table |

> **Good to know:** kids club ages, hours and nursery availability change by ship and season. Confirm on the line's own page before booking, or compare them in [kids clubs on cruise ships](/articles/kids-clubs-cruise-ships-ages-costs/).

## How each line handles a reunion
### Royal Caribbean
Royal Caribbean has big ships with the widest cabin range, from interiors to multi-bedroom family suites, plus excellent kids and teen clubs. Main dining rooms handle tables of 10–12 at a set time. It's best for mixed-age reunions of 15–40. The full profile is in [Is Royal Caribbean right for you](/articles/royal-caribbean-honest-guide-canadians/).

### Carnival
Carnival has the lowest entry price for younger families, kids clubs from age 2, and relaxed evenings. Dining rooms can seat big groups with some notice. It's best for budget-conscious reunions where nobody wants formal nights.

### Disney
Disney is the gold standard for families with children under 12, with a nursery, kids clubs and characters, at fares well above the others. Rotational dining moves the whole group through different restaurants with the same serving team all week. It's best when young kids are the centre of the reunion and the budget allows. See [Is Disney Cruise Line right for you](/articles/disney-cruise-line-honest-guide-canadians/).

### Princess and Holland America
These lines suit reunions anchored by grandparents: calmer ships, strong Alaska programmes from Vancouver, and dining rooms that seat 8–10 comfortably. They have fewer facilities for teenagers. They're best for three-generation Alaska reunions where the elders are hosting.

### Norwegian
With no set dining times, there's no guaranteed big table. That's the trade-off of freestyle dining. It works for groups happy to eat in smaller clusters and frustrates groups who want one long table every night. Reserving ahead helps, as explained in [seating a group of 20 together](/articles/group-dining-cruises-20-people/).

## Which lines suit reunions less well?
Adults-only lines such as Virgin Voyages rule out anyone under 18, so they only work for reunions of grown-up siblings and cousins. Luxury and small-ship lines offer calm and fine dining, but have few family cabins, no kids clubs, and fares that can split a family by budget. River cruises have the same limits, plus very small ships. They're lovely for a milestone anniversary, but rarely right for a 25-person reunion with toddlers.

## Alaska or the Caribbean for a Canadian reunion?
Alaska from Vancouver is the obvious Canadian choice: BC relatives can skip flying altogether, Albertans have a short flight, and the scenery gives grandparents something they'll talk about for years. July and August line up with school holidays across the country, but they're also the priciest weeks. For younger kids, the [Alaska cruise with kids by age](/articles/alaska-cruise-with-kids-ages/) guide helps judge the fit.

The Caribbean from Florida suits families who want pools, waterslides and beaches. March break works well, but break dates differ by province, so check every household's dates before choosing a week.

## Cabins that keep everyone close
Connecting cabins suit families with young children, and a block of cabins on one corridor suits adult siblings who want their own space. Family suites on Royal Caribbean and Disney can house grandparents and grandkids together. The options are compared in [best cabins for large groups to stay connected](/articles/large-group-cabins-stay-connected/).

## Making any line work
1. **Book as one group of eight or more cabins** to unlock the organiser perks explained in [the 8-cabin rule for group cruises](/articles/group-cruise-8-cabin-rule/).
2. **Request big or adjoining dining tables early**, ideally when the block is created.
3. **Hold cabins near each other** before the best locations sell.
4. **Plan one anchor event**, such as a private cocktail hour or group excursion, rather than scheduling every day.
