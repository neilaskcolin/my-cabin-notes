Glacier Bay, Hubbard and Endicott Arm are three different glacier days. Glacier Bay is the long, narrated national park day, Hubbard is the biggest wall of ice, and Endicott Arm is the dramatic fjord approach. Which one you get depends mostly on your itinerary, not on choice.

## The three glacier days compared

| | Glacier Bay | Hubbard Glacier | Endicott Arm (Dawes Glacier) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where | National park west of Juneau | Yakutat Bay, on the Gulf of Alaska | Fjord south of Juneau |
| Typical sailings | Round trips and one-ways on permit-holding lines | Mostly one-way Gulf sailings, some longer round trips | Common on 7-night round trips from Vancouver and Seattle |
| Time at the ice | Most of a day inside the park | A few hours | A few hours, including the long approach |
| The show | Several tidewater glaciers, Margerie the star | One enormous face, around 10 km wide | Narrow walls, waterfalls, icebergs, then Dawes |
| Commentary | Park rangers come aboard | Ship's naturalist or officers | Ship's naturalist or officers |
| Main risk | Not on every sailing | Ice can keep ships well back | Ice can stop ships short of the face |

## Glacier Bay: the full-day national park

Glacier Bay is a [national park](https://www.nps.gov/glba/index.htm) and part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site shared with Kluane in Yukon and Tatshenshini-Alsek in BC. Rangers board in the morning and narrate as the ship cruises roughly 100 kilometres up the bay, passing smaller glaciers before reaching Margerie and the rock-covered Grand Pacific at the head of Tarr Inlet.

Wildlife is a big part of the day: harbour seals on the ice, sea otters, mountain goats on the cliffs and sometimes brown bears on the shore. Entry is by permit: the park's 2019 contracts went to Holland America, Princess, Norwegian, Royal Caribbean, Viking, Seabourn and Cunard, with Holland America and Princess sailing in most often. If Glacier Bay is on your list, filter for it explicitly.

## Hubbard Glacier: the giant

Hubbard is often described as North America's largest tidewater glacier. Its face stretches around 10 kilometres across and rises higher than most cruise ships, and the ice starts its journey in the mountains of Yukon. Unusually, it has been advancing rather than retreating.

You'll see it mainly on one-way sailings between Vancouver and Whittier or Seward, which cross the Gulf of Alaska. Floating ice in the bay sometimes keeps ships several kilometres back, but the scale still impresses.

### What about College Fjord?

Many Gulf sailings also include College Fjord near Whittier, a fjord lined with glaciers named after US colleges. Paired with Hubbard, it makes the one-way route the best ice itinerary of the season, as covered in [Inside Passage vs Gulf of Alaska](/articles/inside-passage-vs-gulf-of-alaska/).

## Endicott Arm: the fjord drama

Endicott Arm is a long, narrow fjord lined with granite walls and waterfalls, ending at Dawes Glacier, an active calver. It usually falls on the morning before a Juneau call on round trips from Vancouver or Seattle. Icebergs drifting past the ship are part of the show.

> **Good to know:** in heavy ice years, ships can't always get close to Dawes. The approach is still spectacular, but if reaching the face matters to you, know that this is the glacier day most likely to stop short.

Neighbouring Tracy Arm, with its twin Sawyer Glaciers, is similar but narrower, and it is visited more often by smaller ships.

## How much should the glacier pick your cruise?

- **Choose for Glacier Bay** if you want the ranger-led, all-day experience. It is the one distinction worth paying for or changing dates to get.
- **Choose a one-way for Hubbard and College Fjord** if you want the most ice and are also open to a Denali land tour.
- **Accept Endicott Arm happily** if the rest of the sailing suits you. It is a superb day, and calving at Dawes can be excellent.
- **Don't pay a premium** to swap between Hubbard and Endicott alone. Pick on dates, ship and price.

Wherever you end up, plan your time on deck with [the best glacier viewing spots](/articles/best-spot-cruise-ship-glacier-viewing/), and if you are booking specifically for Glacier Bay, read [best cabins for Glacier Bay day](/articles/best-cabins-glacier-bay-viewing-day/).

## Whatever the glacier, prepare the same way

Every version rewards an early cabin choice and warm layers. Bring a hat, gloves and binoculars, get on deck an hour before arrival, and stay outside when the ice starts to crack. Itinerary changes for ice or weather are at the captain's discretion, as explained in [the cruise contract of carriage](/articles/cruise-contract-of-carriage-explained/).
