Retirement opens up a different cruise market. Longer voyages often cost less per night, sail to places a one-week trip can't reach, and attract fellow travellers who aren't rushing. The planning changes too, mostly around insurance, medication and home.

## Why longer cruises cost less per night
Week-long sailings during school holidays carry the most demand, so they carry the highest prices. Voyages of two weeks or more, especially outside peak months, have to appeal to people with time, and they're often priced that way. Repositioning cruises, when ships change regions, are often the best value of all (see [repositioning cruises explained](/articles/repositioning-cruises-explained/)).

Length also stretches fixed costs. You pay for flights and a pre-cruise hotel once, whether you sail for 7 nights or 21.

> **Quick math:** C$1,400 in return flights for two works out to C$200 a night on a 7-night cruise, about C$100 on a 14-night voyage and about C$67 on a 21-night one. The longer the trip, the less those flights weigh.

## Where longer voyages go
| Length | Typical voyages |
|---|---|
| 10–13 nights | Southern Caribbean from Florida, British Isles, one-way Vancouver to Hawaii |
| 14–18 nights | Full Panama Canal transits, Hawaii round trips from Vancouver, transatlantic crossings with ports |
| 19–35 nights | South America, Australia and New Zealand, combined back-to-back sailings |
| 35 nights or more | World cruise segments and full world voyages |

Two weeks or more lets you do the [Panama Canal end to end](/articles/panama-canal-cruise-couples/), [cross the Atlantic](/articles/transatlantic-crossings-what-like/) or sail [to Hawaii from Vancouver](/articles/hawaii-cruises-from-vancouver/) without a flight one way. Combining two sailings is another option, covered in [back-to-back cruises](/articles/back-to-back-cruises-how-booking-works/).

## What life aboard feels like
Long voyages have a slower rhythm. Sea days become a routine rather than a gap between ports: a morning lecture, lunch with the same friends, an afternoon nap, dinner at your regular table. Crew get to know you, and you unpack once for weeks. Enrichment programs tend to be stronger on longer sailings, too (see [enrichment at sea](/articles/enrichment-at-sea-lectures/)).

Cabin choice matters more when you'll live in it for a month. A balcony and a quiet location are worth more over 21 nights than over 7.

## The practical shifts
### Insurance and provincial health rules
Many policies cover 30 or 60 days per trip, and pre-existing conditions must meet stability rules. Each province also sets how long you can be away while keeping coverage, often expressed as a minimum time at home each year. If you winter in Florida and then cruise, add the days up. [Cruise insurance for seniors](/articles/cruise-insurance-seniors-canada-pre-existing/) explains age bands and stability periods.

> **Watch out:** many Canadian home insurance policies require someone to check an empty house regularly, especially in winter when pipes can freeze. Read your policy's vacancy wording before leaving for a month.

### Before you leave, in order
1. Confirm insurance covers the full trip, including pre- and post-cruise nights.
2. Ask your pharmacist about a vacation supply of every prescription, plus a buffer, and pack medicines in original containers with a doctor's letter. Ship medical centres stock a limited range and charge privately.
3. Arrange home checks, and use Canada Post's Hold Mail service or a neighbour.
4. Set up automatic bill payments and check your credit card won't expire mid-trip.
5. Buy an internet package or plan for ship wifi, so family can reach you.
6. Pack for half the trip and plan to do laundry (see [self-service laundry on cruise ships](/articles/cruise-ship-laundry-self-service-walkthrough/)).

## Who long voyages don't suit
Anyone who gets restless after a week, needs regular specialist appointments, or can't leave home, pets or family responsibilities for long. Try a 10- or 12-night sailing first. ## Making the most of the time you've earned
Start with a two-week voyage on a route you've always wanted, such as Panama or a crossing. Sort insurance and medication early, look after the house, and pack light. Many retirees find they never go back to one-week cruises.
