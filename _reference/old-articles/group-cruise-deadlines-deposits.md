A group booking doesn't have one deadline. It has three or four, spread over the months before sailing, and missing any of them quietly shrinks the group. The fix is simple: publish every date on day one and track them in one place.

## The group cruise calendar at a glance
Exact timings depend on the line and your group contract. This is the common pattern for a sailing booked about a year ahead.

| Deadline | Typical timing | If it's missed |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit to hold a cabin | At booking, often 10–14 months out | The cabin never joins the block |
| Release date for unsold cabins | Set in the contract, often a few months before final payment | Unclaimed cabins return to the line |
| Names and passport details | Before or at final payment | The cabin can be released, or name changes cost more |
| Final payment | Commonly 75–120 days before sailing | Unpaid cabins are cancelled and may lose their deposit |
| After final payment | Until sailing | Cancellations face steep penalties, often the full fare close to departure |

## Deadline one: the deposit
When the block opens, each cabin pays a deposit to hold its place at the group rate. Group deposits are often lower than regular ones and may be refundable until a set date, which helps separate real commitments from "maybe". Confirm the refund terms before you tell people it's risk-free.

> **Canadian tip:** check what currency the deposit and balance are charged in. Many group fares are priced in US dollars, so a weaker loonie between deposit and final payment raises the cost in Canadian dollars. Some lines and agencies offer pricing in Canadian dollars, compared in [paying in CAD vs USD](/articles/paying-cad-vs-usd-cruise/).

## Deadline two: names and passport details
Every cabin needs each guest's full legal name exactly as it appears on their passport, plus date of birth and nationality. This catches more groups than people expect. A cousin who goes by her middle name, or someone whose passport has expired, can turn into a scramble a week before the cut-off.

Ask everyone to check their passport the day the block opens. Canadian passport renewals can take weeks, and many cruise lines want passports valid well beyond the return date. [Passports, NEXUS and ID](/articles/passports-nexus-id-cruise-documents/) covers what's accepted.

## Deadline three: final payment
This is the big one: the full balance for every cabin, due on one date. Any cabin not paid in full is cancelled. That can drop the group below its perk threshold and cost the organiser their free berth. A cabin lost late is also much harder to fill again than one lost early.

> **Watch out:** cancellation penalties usually start or jump sharply at final payment. Everyone should buy travel insurance with cancellation cover when they pay their deposit, not at final payment. [Group cruise insurance](/articles/group-cruise-insurance-one-policy-everyone/) explains why one policy rarely covers everyone.

## A simple organiser routine
1. **Day one:** send every date in one message, and add them to a shared calendar.
2. **Set internal deadlines** two weeks before each real one, and tell people the earlier date.
3. **Keep a tracker** with columns for cabin, lead guest, deposit paid, names submitted and balance paid.
4. **Send reminders** at 30, 14 and 7 days before each internal deadline.
5. **Phone anyone still missing** a week before the real deadline. A call works better than a fourth group message.
6. **Decide in advance** what happens to an unpaid cabin, whether it's released or offered to a waiting list, and tell the group that rule at the start.

If someone pulls out, [what happens if someone drops out of a group cruise](/articles/what-if-someone-drops-out-group-cruise/) covers the options for refunds, replacement guests and keeping the group's perks.
