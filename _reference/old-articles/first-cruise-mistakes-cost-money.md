faq1q: What is the biggest onboard bill surprise for new cruisers?
faq1a: Phone roaming at sea. The ship's maritime network bills outside normal Canadian travel passes, sometimes by the megabyte. Switching to airplane mode at sail-away and using wifi instead prevents the worst bills.
faq2q: Is travel insurance really necessary for a cruise?
faq2a: Yes. Provincial health coverage pays very little outside Canada, a medical evacuation from a ship can cost tens of thousands of dollars, and cancellation cover protects the fare itself. Buy it when you pay the deposit, not just before you leave.
faq3q: Should I let the cruise line charge my card in Canadian dollars?
faq3a: Compare first. Some lines offer to convert your onboard bill into your card's currency at their own exchange rate, which can be worse than your card's rate. Many Canadians come out ahead paying in US dollars on a no-foreign-transaction-fee card.

First cruises come with a learning curve, and it is measured in dollars. Most of the expensive mistakes happen before you board, not at the bar, and nearly all of them have a cheap fix.

## The mistakes, priced
Costs below are rough ranges at recent prices, for a couple, to show the scale. Your numbers will vary.

| Mistake | What it can cost | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Flying in on sailing day | C$800–2,500+ to catch up at the next port, or the whole trip | Fly in the day before |
| No travel insurance | Tens of thousands for a medical evacuation | Buy cover at deposit |
| Phone left roaming at sea | Hundreds of dollars | Airplane mode at sail-away |
| Drink package you do not drink through | C$500–1,000 over a week | Run the maths first |
| Foreign-transaction fees | About 2.5% of every charge | No-fee card |
| Ship tour in a walkable port | US$100–250 for two | Walk it yourself |
| Missing a price drop | Hundreds of dollars | Watch the fare until final payment |
| Not checking the onboard account | Surprise charges at the end | Check the app mid-week |

## Before you book
1. **Comparing headline fares instead of totals.** Two identical cabins can sail with or without onboard credit, prepaid gratuities or a drink package, depending on when and how they were booked. Ask what is included in each quote, and read [what's included in a cruise fare](/articles/whats-included-cruise-fare/).
2. **Forgetting taxes and fees.** Some quotes show them separately, adding a few hundred dollars per couple. The [taxes and port fees breakdown](/articles/taxes-port-fees-cruise-fare-breakdown/) shows where they hide.
3. **Booking without insurance.** Buy it at deposit, so cancellation cover starts early. Check how it handles pre-existing conditions.

> **Quick math:** a missed ship from Fort Lauderdale might mean a last-minute flight to the next port, a hotel night and a taxi, easily C$1,000 or more per couple, on top of missed days you have already paid for. A pre-cruise hotel night costs C$200–400.

## Between booking and sailing
4. **Ignoring price drops.** If the fare falls before final payment, many bookings can be repriced or given onboard credit. Someone has to notice. See [what happens if the price drops after booking](/articles/price-drop-after-booking-cruise/).
5. **Buying the drink package by default.** Most first-timers do not drink enough to break even. Read [are drink packages worth it](/articles/drink-packages-worth-it-first-cruise/) before paying.
6. **Booking ship tours everywhere.** Some ports are the excursion: Ketchikan, Skagway and Cozumel's waterfront are easy on foot. Use [how many excursions to book](/articles/how-many-excursions-book-per-cruise/) to choose.

## On board
7. **Leaving data roaming on.** Switch to airplane mode before the ship leaves port, then use wifi.
8. **Paying the foreign-transaction fee.** Your account bills in US dollars. A no-fee card saves around C$25 on every C$1,000 spent.
9. **Not watching the account.** Cashless spending adds up quickly. Check the app halfway through the trip, and query anything odd before the last night. [How onboard payments work](/articles/how-onboard-payments-work-cruise/) explains the deposit holds and final charge.
10. **Impulse spending.** Photo packages, casino ATMs, art auctions and spa add-ons are priced for holiday mood. Decide what you want before you sail.

> **Example:** a Saskatoon couple on a first Caribbean sailing who fly in a day early, skip the drink package, walk two ports and pay with a no-fee card could easily keep C$1,000 or more in their pocket compared with doing the opposite.

## Small habits that add up
Bring a refillable water bottle, since tap water on board is free. Eat in the included restaurants most nights and book one specialty dinner you are excited about. Set a daily spending number and check it against the app. For a realistic budget, see [how much spending money to bring](/articles/how-much-spending-money-cruise/).

## The habit that prevents most of this
Book like a veteran: a day-before flight, insurance at deposit, extras checked before paying, and someone watching the fare afterwards. A licensed cruise consultant can do that last part at no extra cost.
